⚖️ Cases

  • Satender Kumar Antil “Charge sheet is not a jail ticket” Bail is the rule, jail the exception; a charge sheet does not require arrest; courts must follow the arrest safeguards strictly.
  • Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia “Liberty is not boxed in” The restrictions were struck down: anticipatory bail is a wide discretion to protect liberty, decided case by case.
  • Sushila Aggarwal “No expiry date on liberty” It need not be time-bound; it can continue till the end of the trial, unless the court limits it for special reasons.

🧩 Part C routes

  • Bail refused in a bailable offenceS.478S.47(2)S.483 A must be released at once; he can apply to the Magistrate under S.478, then to the Sessions Court or High Court under S.483, and if detained illegally, file habeas corpus.
  • Accused on bail threatens witnessesS.480(5)S.483 The prosecution or witnesses can apply for cancellation of bail; the court will cancel it and send A to custody. The witnesses may also seek protection under the Witness Protection Scheme (S.398).
  • Bail granted, but too poor to payS.478(1)S.484Art. 21 She should apply to modify the conditions; the court can reduce the amount and release her on a personal bond without sureties.
  • Refused bail by two courtsS.483Art. 136S.187(3) Apply to the Supreme Court (Art. 136), or renew the application on changed circumstances, default bail or S.479.
  • Terror suspect: bail possible?S.480(1)S.43D(5) Yes, he should be released, if necessary on a personal bond; if no evidence remains, the police should also seek his discharge.

★ Golden rules

  • Bailable = right; non-bailable = discretion. The First Schedule to the BNSS tells you which.
  • "Bail is the rule, jail the exception"; arrest is not automatic even after a charge sheet.
  • Anticipatory bail is not limited in time; it can last till the end of the trial.
  • Poverty is no reason to keep a man in jail: an indigent accused can be released on a personal bond without sureties (S.478).
  • If investigation is not complete within 60 / 90 days, the accused gets default bail as of right (S.187(3)).

  • Non-cognizable: no investigation without a Magistrate's order (S.174(2)).
  • Courts can quash an FIR only in the clear categories laid down by the Supreme Court.

★ Golden rules

  • Age on the date of the offence decides juvenility, not the age when caught or tried.

  • Only a heinous offence (minimum 7 years) by a child of 16–18 can lead to trial as an adult, after preliminary assessment.
  • No joint trial of a child with an adult (S.23).

★ Golden rules

  • An offender under 21 should not be imprisoned unless the court records reasons (S.6).
  • Probation removes the disqualification of a conviction (S.12), e.g. for government jobs.
  • Parole is not a right; time on parole is usually not counted in the sentence; furlough time is counted.

  • Custodial violence is never allowed; illegal detention gives a right to compensation.
  • A judicial officer is arrested only with immediate intimation to the District Judge / High Court; no handcuffs.
  • A confession to the police is inadmissible; only one recorded by a Judicial Magistrate under S.183 can be used.

  • Search without warrant, nothing foundS.185S.103S.105 Legal only if S.185, S.103 and S.105 were followed; otherwise illegal, and A may complain to the Magistrate and the SP. Finding nothing does not by itself make it illegal.
  • Illegal search and seizure: remedyS.185S.103S.105 B can apply to the Magistrate for return of the property and action, file a complaint against the officer, and approach the High Court under Art. 226; the evidence is not automatically excluded.
  • Accused vanishes after a warrantS.84S.85S.356 Proclamation (S.84) → attachment and sale of property (S.85) → trial in absentia (S.356).
  • CCTV footage and WhatsApp chatsS.94S.96S.105 Yes: an order under S.94 to produce, and if refused, a search warrant (S.96) and seizure recorded on video (S.105).

★ Golden rules

  • A search must have two independent respectable witnesses of the locality, a list of seized things, and a copy to the occupant (S.103).
  • The BNSS requires audio-video recording of searches and seizures (S.105).
  • A police officer searching without warrant (S.185) must record his grounds in writing first and send copies to the nearest Magistrate.
  • Illegal search does not make the evidence inadmissible; the court only scrutinises it more carefully.
  • Search and seizure do not violate Art. 20(3), because they do not compel the accused to be a witness.

★ Golden rules

  • A fair trial is fair to the accused, the victim and society; a trial vitiated by intimidation can be held again.
  • Speedy trial is part of Art. 21.
  • Every distinct offence needs a separate charge (S.241); exceptions in S.242–246.
  • An error or omission in the charge is fatal only if it caused a failure of justice (S.238).
  • Double jeopardy bars a second trial on the same facts for the same offence, even under a different law.

★ Golden rules
