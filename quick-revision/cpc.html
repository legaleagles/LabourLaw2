🧠 Visual notes

Every right to go to court comes with a clock. It starts, it runs, it can pause or restart, and when it rings, the court door shuts.

⚖️ Cases

  • Katiji “4 days late, 6 golden rules” Condone it. "Sufficient cause" must be read liberally; substantial justice beats technicality, and the State is treated like any other litigant.
  • N. Balakrishnan “883 days, but an honest reason wins” Condoned. "Length of delay is no matter, acceptability of the explanation is the only criterion." The other side was paid ₹10,000.
  • Shapoor Mazda “Admit the debt, restart the clock” Still a valid acknowledgment. It must admit a present, subsisting liability; a promise to pay is not needed. The clock restarts from the date it is signed.
  • PNB v. Surendra Prasad Sinha “The door closes, the debt stays” Complaint quashed. Limitation bars only the remedy (the court route); the debt itself survives, so the bank could adjust money it already held.
  • Ravinder Kaur Grewal “12 years: sword and shield” Both. After 12 years the owner's right is extinguished (S.27) and the possessor gets title, usable as a sword (to sue) and a shield (to defend).

🧩 Part C routes

  • Jailed for the whole periodS.6S.5S.3 No. The suit is barred and will be dismissed under S.3. (S.14 would help only if he had meanwhile pursued the claim in good faith in a wrong court.)
  • Firm: B insane, C minorS.7 Time runs against all three (B, C and D) from the date the debt became due. B's insanity and C's minority give no extension.
  • Minor silent 3 years after majorityS.6(1)S.8Art. 65 He can sue only if the ordinary 12 years from accrual have not yet run out. If they have, the suit is barred: S.8 allows no more than 3 years after majority. Show both outcomes with ages for full marks.
  • Last day: court closedS.4S.12(2) Filing on the first day the court reopens is within time. No condonation is needed.
  • Pro-note of 2020, suit in 2025Art. 35S.18S.19 A suit filed in early 2025 may just be in time because of the Covid exclusion; one filed later in 2025 is time-barred and must be dismissed under S.3. If G acknowledged in writing or paid part before expiry, a fresh 3 years runs from that date. The debt itself survives PNB v. Surendra Prasad Sinha.
  • Possession since 1998, suit in 2012Art. 65S.27 If Y proves hostile possession from 1998, the 2012 suit is barred and X's title is extinguished (S.27); Y becomes owner Ravinder Kaur Grewal. If Y's possession was permissive or not proved adverse, the suit is maintainable.
  • Paid a time-barred debt: refund?S.3S.25(3) No; the suit is not maintainable. Limitation bars the remedy, not the right, so the payment was a valid discharge PNB v. Surendra Prasad Sinha.
  • Ill, missed the ex parte deadlineArt. 123S.5 He should file the O.IX r.13 application with an S.5 application and a medical certificate; the delay can be condoned Katiji N. Balakrishnan, possibly on payment of costs to the other side.

★ Golden rules

  • Limitation bars the remedy, not the right. The debt still exists; only the court route is closed.
  • S.5 is never available for a suit. It saves only appeals and applications (and not O.XXI execution applications).
  • Only three legal disabilities: minor, insane, idiot ("MII"). Imprisonment, illness and poverty are not legal disabilities.
  • Once time starts running, it never stops (S.9).
  • Acknowledgment counts only if it is before expiry, in writing, and signed.

★ Golden rules

  • Necessary party = without him no effective decree can be passed. Proper party = his presence helps decide everything completely.
  • Non-joinder of a necessary party is fatal (r.9 proviso); misjoinder or non-joinder of a proper party never is.
  • No suit against Government without 2 months' notice (S.80), unless the court gives leave for urgent relief.
  • "Person" in O.XXXIII includes a company; a company too can sue as an indigent person.
  • An agent cannot interplead against his principal, nor a tenant against his landlord (O.XXXV r.5).

⚖️ Cases

  • Jolly George Varghese “No jail for being poor” Set aside. Arrest needs bad faith or present means plus refusal; poverty alone is no ground (Art. 21, Art. 11 ICCPR).

  • Attachment and arrest togetherO.XXI r.11O.XXI r.21S.51 He can ask for both, but the court may refuse simultaneous execution (O.XXI r.21), and arrest will be ordered only after notice and a finding under the S.51 proviso.

★ Golden rules

  • A decree conclusively determines rights; an order does not. Rejection of a plaint is a decree; dismissal for default is not.
  • The executing court cannot go behind the decree, unless it is a nullity.
  • No civil prison for honest poverty. Arrest needs bad faith or means to pay plus refusal.
  • All questions between the parties about execution are decided by the executing court under S.47, not by a separate suit.
  • Execution should normally end within 6 months of filing.

★ Golden rules

  • S.10 stays the later suit; S.11 bars it. Sub judice = pending; judicata = decided.
  • Constructive res judicata (Expl. IV): a ground that might and ought to have been raised is treated as decided.
  • Res judicata applies to writ petitions too: a High Court decision on merits bars an Art. 32 petition on the same grounds.
  • "Same title" means the same capacity: losing as an individual does not bar a suit as a trustee.
  • A foreign judgment is conclusive except in the six cases of S.13; a foreign decree on a ground unknown to our law is not recognised.
