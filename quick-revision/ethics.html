⚖️ Cases

  • V.C. Rangadurai “Punish to reform, not to ruin” Gross professional misconduct was proved; but punishment should reform as well as deter, so the Court reduced the long suspension ordered below and required him to do free legal-aid work.

★ Golden rules

  • "Misconduct" is not defined in the Act: it covers any conduct that makes an advocate unworthy of the profession, in or outside his work.
  • The Act punishes "professional or other misconduct" (S.35): even conduct outside court can count.
  • Disciplinary proceedings aim to protect the public and reform the advocate, not merely to punish.
  • A State Bar Council must decide a complaint within one year, or the BCI takes it over (S.36B).
  • Only the Bar Councils can suspend or remove an advocate from the roll; courts punish contempt.

⚖️ Cases

  • Harish Chandra Tiwari “Client's money is a sacred trust” Retaining a client's money is grave professional misconduct; the Supreme Court replaced the three-year suspension with removal of the advocate's name from the roll.

★ Golden rules

  • An advocate must keep accounts of client's money showing receipts, expenses and fees, with dates (BCI Rule 25).
  • Money received for expenses cannot be diverted to fees during the case without the client's written consent (Rule 26).
  • Keeping or misusing a client's money is grave misconduct: it can end a career.
  • An advocate shall not lend money to his client for the litigation, nor turn client funds into loans (Rules 31, 32).
  • Accounting = recording, classifying, summarising, interpreting and communicating financial information.

⚖️ Cases

  • A.K. Balaji “Indian law is for Indian advocates” "Practice of law" covers litigation and non-litigation work; foreign lawyers cannot practise Indian law in India. They may advise on foreign law on a casual "fly in and fly out" basis, and appear in international commercial arbitrations, subject to rules.
  • D.K. Gandhi “A lawyer is not a shopkeeper” The legal profession is sui generis; an advocate's service is a contract of personal service and is outside the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Remedies lie in civil courts and Bar Council proceedings.

★ Golden rules

  • The Advocates Act, 1961 created an All-India Bar with a single class of advocates (S.29).
  • Practising law is a right of an enrolled advocate under the Act, subject to reasonable restrictions on the freedom of profession (Art. 19(1)(g), 19(6)).
  • Foreign lawyers and firms cannot practise Indian law in India; only advising on foreign law on a casual basis is allowed.
  • Lawyers' services are not covered by consumer law: the legal profession is sui generis.
  • A senior advocate is designated for ability and standing; he cannot file a vakalatnama or take instructions directly from a client.

★ Golden rules

  • Lawyers have no right to strike or boycott courts; abstaining from work harms clients and justice.
  • An advocate is bound to accept a brief in his field at his usual fee, unless there are special reasons (Rule 11).
  • No contingency fees, no buying interest in litigation, no soliciting work (Rules 20, 22, 36).
  • Bar and Bench are two wheels of the same chariot: mutual respect keeps justice moving.

⚖️ Cases

  • Gaurav Kumar “₹750 means ₹750” State Bar Councils and the BCI cannot charge enrolment fees or other charges beyond those fixed by S.24(1)(f) (₹750; ₹125 for SC/ST). Excessive fees violate equality and the right to practise. The ruling applies prospectively.
  • Bonnie Foi Law College “Degree opens the door, AIBE opens the court” The BCI can conduct the All India Bar Examination as a condition for practice; the earlier contrary view was overruled; final-year law students may take the AIBE.

★ Golden rules

  • Qualifications (S.24): Indian citizen, 21 years, recognised law degree, enrolment fee and the State Council's other conditions.
  • Bar Councils cannot charge more than the enrolment fee fixed by S.24(1)(f): ₹750 (₹125 for SC/ST).
  • The BCI may hold the All India Bar Examination, and final-year students may take it.
  • Disqualification (S.24A): conviction for moral turpitude or untouchability offences, or dismissal from State service for moral turpitude; it ends two years after release or dismissal.
  • A State Bar Council cannot refuse enrolment on its own: it must refer the case to the BCI (S.26).

⚖️ Cases

  • In re Vinay Chandra Mishra “The robe does not shield contempt” He was guilty of criminal contempt; the Court imposed a suspended sentence and also suspended him from practice for three years (the last part was later held to be beyond its contempt power).
  • SCBA v. Union of India “Court punishes contempt; Bar Council punishes misconduct” The Supreme Court cannot, in contempt jurisdiction or under Art. 142, suspend or cancel an advocate's licence; that power belongs to the Bar Councils under the Advocates Act. The Court can punish contempt and refer the matter to the Bar Council.

★ Golden rules

  • Supreme Court and High Courts are courts of record with power to punish contempt (Art. 129, 215).
  • Fair criticism of a judgment is not contempt; scandalising the judge is.
  • No punishment unless the contempt substantially interferes with justice; truth is a defence if in the public interest and bona fide (S.13).
  • A court can punish an advocate for contempt, but only the Bar Council can suspend his licence.
  • Every advocate must remember that anyone genuinely in need of a lawyer is entitled to legal assistance (Rule 46).
