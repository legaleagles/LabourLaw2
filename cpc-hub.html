CPC in Motion — Chalchitra notes NEW

The whole syllabus as 77 short animated comic scenes — one rule per picture, with music and sound effects. Each episode ends with a quick quiz and can also be watched as a video.

Best for
First contact with a unit, revising on the phone, anyone who finds bare reading heavy.
How to use it
  1. Watch the episode for a unit before you read its notes — the pictures make the reading much faster.
  2. Answer the quiz at the end; replay the scenes you got wrong.
  3. Rewatch all six episodes (about 12 minutes) on the day before the exam.

Nyaya Metro — the subject as a metro map

Units are metro lines and key topics are stations, sized by how often they were asked. Tap a station for its one-line meaning, section, leading case and memory hook. The repeat Part C problems are drawn as yes/no route maps.

Best for
Seeing the whole subject at once, linking topics, quick revision, Part C problem practice.
How to use it
  1. Open the map on your phone and ride one line (unit) at a time.
  2. Big stations were asked the most — learn those tickets first.
  3. Before the exam, walk each Part C route map and say the answer aloud.
  4. Print the PDF and pin it near your desk.

CPC & Limitation — Super Notes (complete book) NEW

The complete book: all 52 topics of the five units in the order a suit actually moves, in colour. Every topic has a 'think first' question, the topic in one line, a picture (flow, checklist, comparison), simple points, the key bare-act section decoded phrase by phrase, a real-life story, case cards (story → held → why it matters), golden rules, and how it was asked with marks. It opens with a two-page guide on how to read a bare act and ends with a ranked past-paper analysis, a Part C problem solver, Latin maxims, numbers to remember, a last-minute ★★★ list and a section finder.

Best for
Anyone who wants one book instead of many — regular students reading the whole syllabus, and non-regular students who need to understand from scratch.
How to use it
  1. Read the 'How this book works' and 'How to read a Bare Act' pages first (5 minutes).
  2. Go topic by topic, in order — try to answer the 'Think first' question before reading.
  3. Draw the picture of each topic once in your own notebook — you will remember it in the hall.
  4. Last week: golden-rule boxes + Part C problem solver + numbers to remember. Last day: the ★★★ list.

Final Exam Cluster Notes

The exam-writing notes. The syllabus grouped into 16 clusters of topics that are asked together, with ready-to-write Part A (6-mark), Part B (15-mark) and Part C (10-mark) answers, 45+ cases told as short stories, and the key points in bold.

Best for
The last two weeks, writing practice, students who want answers in exam shape.
How to use it
  1. Start with the clusters the Analysis marks as asked most.
  2. Read a model answer, close the PDF, write it from memory, then compare.
  3. Use the cases exactly as given — name + one line on why it matters.

Story Notes — the whole subject as one neighbour dispute

All five units told as 17 comic episodes of one dispute between neighbours: suit → summons → pleadings → trial → decree → execution → appeal, review, revision → Limitation Act. Ends with practice MCQs with answers.

Best for
Understanding how the parts connect, first reading, a relaxed revision.
How to use it
  1. Read it in one or two sittings before you start the detailed notes.
  2. Whenever a topic feels abstract later, find its episode in the story.
  3. Do the MCQs at the end to check yourself.
