Emergency Treatment Rights
Cannot be refused · Golden hour · Government & private hospitals
▼
FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT
No Hospital Can Refuse Emergency Treatment
The Supreme Court in Parmanand Katara v. Union of India (1989) held that no hospital — government or private — can refuse emergency treatment for want of money or documentation. This is part of the right to life under Article 21. For road accident victims — golden hour cashless treatment is specifically mandated under Motor Vehicles Amendment Act 2019 Section 162.
💬 What to Say
→"You cannot refuse emergency treatment. This is a Fundamental Right under Article 21 and the Supreme Court judgment in Parmanand Katara v. Union of India."
→For road accident: "Section 162 Motor Vehicles Act 2019 mandates cashless treatment in the golden hour. Proceed immediately."
→Call 108 (ambulance) to transfer to nearest government hospital if private refuses.
→File complaint with District Collector and State Medical Council immediately after treatment.
Legal basis: Article 21 Constitution; SC: Parmanand Katara v. Union of India (1989); MVA 2019 Section 162 — golden hour cashless; Clinical Establishments Act 2010

Patient Rights — Records, Consent & Second Opinion
Records in 72 hrs · Informed consent · Right to refuse treatment
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Medical Records Within 72 Hours
Every patient has the right to access their own medical records within 72 hours of request. Hospitals cannot withhold records — not even for unpaid bills. Records include: case sheets, investigation reports, discharge summaries, operation notes, scan reports. File consumer complaint if records are withheld.
WHAT TO DO
1
Request records in writing — state date, patient name, admission number.
2
Hospital must provide within 72 hours under MCI/NMC Regulations.
3
If refused — file complaint with State Medical Council and Consumer Commission.
4
Consumer Forum: hospitals cannot hold records hostage for pending bills — file deficiency complaint.
5
For death cases — family has the right to post-mortem report and all records.
Legal basis: NMC (Medical Council of India) Professional Conduct Regulations 2002, Regulation 1.3; Consumer Protection Act 2019; SC: Samira Kohli v. Dr. Prabha Manchanda (2008)
ENTITLEMENT
Informed Consent is Mandatory Before Any Procedure
No doctor can perform any surgery, procedure, or invasive test without your informed written consent. Informed means: you understood the nature, risks, alternatives, and chose freely. Operating without consent is assault in criminal law and medical negligence in civil law. You have the right to refuse treatment at any time.
Legal basis: SC: Samira Kohli v. Dr. Prabha Manchanda (2008) — informed consent mandatory; NMC Regulations 2002; Consumer Protection Act 2019

Medical Negligence Rights
Consumer complaint · IPC 304A · No-win no-fee lawyers
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Medical Negligence — Consumer Complaint Without Lawyer
Medical negligence is covered under Consumer Protection Act 2019 — you can file at District Consumer Commission without a lawyer for claims up to ₹50 lakhs. Criminal negligence (causing death) is covered under IPC Section 304A. Get an independent medical opinion first to document the negligence.
WHAT TO DO
1
Collect all records: case sheet, prescriptions, operation notes, discharge summary, scan reports.
2
Get second opinion from a qualified doctor — get written report documenting negligence.
3
File Consumer Complaint at District Consumer Commission. No lawyer needed up to ₹50 lakh.
4
For death by negligence — file FIR under IPC Section 304A. Cognisable offence.
5
File with State Medical Council for professional misconduct — parallel remedy.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection Act 2019 — SC: IMA v. VP Shantha (1995) — medical services covered; IPC Section 304A; NMC Act 2020 — disciplinary proceedings

Mental Health Rights
Right to treatment · Decriminalised suicide attempt · No forced admission
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Mental Illness is Not a Crime — Right to Dignified Treatment
Under the Mental Healthcare Act 2017 — every person with mental illness has the right to: free treatment at government mental health facilities · legal representation · confidentiality · advance directives about treatment · live in community (not be forced into institutional care). Forced admission requires specific procedural compliance.
💬 What to Say
→Attempt to suicide is decriminalised — Section 115 MHCA: police must facilitate care, not prosecute.
→Free treatment at government hospitals is mandatory — no payment can be demanded.
→You can make an advance directive specifying how you want to be treated if you lose capacity.
→Forced admission: requires examination by two psychiatrists and compliance with MHCA Section 89.
→Helplines: iCall 9152987821 · Vandrevala Foundation 1860-2662-345 (24/7 free)
🆘 Crisis Support
If you or someone you know is in mental health crisis — call iCall: 9152987821 or Vandrevala Foundation: 1860-2662-345. Both are free, confidential, 24/7. You are not alone.
Legal basis: Mental Healthcare Act 2017, Sections 18-28, 89, 115; Article 21 Constitution; NIMHANS helpline
