Anti-Ragging Rights
Criminal offence · Expulsion · UGC helpline 24/7 · Anonymous complaint
▼
CRIMINAL OFFENCE
Ragging is a Crime — Zero Tolerance Under Law
Ragging is a criminal offence and a UGC-prohibited act. Punishment ranges from expulsion and suspension to up to 3 years imprisonment. Every institution must have an Anti-Ragging Committee and Squad. Complaints can be made anonymously. Institution must act within 24 hours.
WHAT TO DO
1
Call UGC Anti-Ragging Helpline: 1800-180-5522 — free, 24/7, can be anonymous.
2
File written complaint with Anti-Ragging Committee of your institution.
3
File complaint on UGC Anti-Ragging Portal: antiragging.in.
4
File FIR at nearest police station — ragging is a cognisable offence.
5
Institution must act within 24 hours or face UGC penalty including withdrawal of affiliation.
Legal basis: UGC Regulations on Curbing Ragging 2009; SC: University of Kerala v. Council of Principals (2009); IPC Sections 339-342, 506; IT Act 2000 — for cyber ragging

Fee Refund Rights
UGC mandates refund · ₹1,000 max deduction · Before cut-off date
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Full Fee Refund If You Withdraw Before Cut-Off
UGC mandates full fee refund (minus maximum ₹1,000 processing charge) if a student withdraws before the last date notified by the institution. Colleges cannot forfeit the entire fee. This applies to all UGC-regulated institutions — engineering, medical, law, arts and science. Osmania University, JNTU, all Telangana/AP state colleges are covered.
WHAT TO DO
1
Submit written withdrawal request to Principal/Registrar before notified cut-off date. Keep copy.
2
Attach fee receipts and demand full refund within 15 days.
3
If refused — file complaint with UGC at ugc.gov.in within 30 days.
4
Also file Consumer Complaint at District Consumer Commission — education is a service.
5
For TS colleges — also file with TSCHE (Telangana State Council for Higher Education).
⚠️ Cut-Off Date is Critical
The refund right is only for withdrawals before the notified cut-off. After that date, refund entitlement reduces. Act early — check the specific date notified by your institution.
Legal basis: UGC (Refund of Fees) Regulations 2018; AICTE Refund Policy; Consumer Protection Act 2019; SC: P.T. Koshy v. Ellen Charitable Trust — education is a consumer service
PROHIBITED
Original Certificate Retention is Illegal
Institutions cannot retain original certificates (10th/12th marksheets, degree certificates) of students as security. This is explicitly prohibited under UGC Regulations. If your institution is holding your originals — demand them back in writing. File UGC complaint if they refuse.
💬 What to Say
→"Under UGC (Non-Retention of Original Certificates) Regulations — you are legally prohibited from retaining my original certificates. Return them immediately."
→Send registered post letter to Principal. Keep proof of posting.
→File FIR for wrongful retention if certificates are not returned.
Legal basis: UGC (Refund of Fees and Non-Retention of Original Certificates) Regulations 2018; IPC Section 405 — criminal breach of trust

Examination Rights
Revaluation · Answer copy · RTI · Malpractice hearing
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Revaluation & Copy of Answer Script
Students have the right to apply for revaluation of answer scripts within the deadline specified in results notification. Under a Supreme Court landmark judgment — answer scripts are accessible under RTI. Osmania University and many others now provide personal copies. If malpractice is alleged — you have the right to a hearing before Malpractice Enquiry Committee before any punishment.
WHAT TO DO
1
Apply for revaluation immediately after results — usually 15-30 days window.
2
Apply for photocopy of answer script through university examination section.
3
If university refuses photocopy — file RTI application to university Public Information Officer.
4
If malpractice alleged — attend the hearing. Do not accept punishment without being heard.
5
For arbitrary result cancellation — file Writ Petition in High Court.
Legal basis: RTI Act 2005 — SC: CBSE v. Aditya Bandopadhyay (2011) — answer scripts accessible; University Acts; Article 14 Constitution — fair hearing before adverse action

Scholarship Rights
SC/ST/BC/OBC/Minority · Not a favour — legal entitlement
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Government Scholarships are Legal Entitlements
SC/ST Post Matric, BC, Minority, Disabled, and Merit scholarships are legal entitlements under government schemes — not favours. Delay in disbursement is actionable. Colleges cannot withhold scholarship amounts received in their accounts. File RTI to track status. District Social Welfare Officer is the nodal authority.
WHAT TO DO
1
Apply through official portal: TS — scholarships.telangana.gov.in; AP — apepass.apcfss.in.
2
Check status on portal. If processed but not credited — file RTI to District Social Welfare Officer.
3
If college holding amount — file complaint with District Collector immediately.
4
For Central scholarships — file grievance at scholarships.gov.in.
5
File Consumer Complaint against college for withholding disbursed amounts.
Legal basis: SC/ST Post-Matric Scholarship Scheme; Articles 15(4), 46 Constitution; RTI Act 2005; Consumer Protection Act 2019
