🚢Letters of Credit and Bank Guarantees

Banks deal in documents, not goods. A letter of credit pays against the right papers; a bank guarantee pays on demand. Only fraud or irreparable harm can stop either.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

A textile exporter in Tiruppur sells shirts to a buyer in Germany. The exporter fears: "What if I ship and he does not pay?" The buyer fears: "What if I pay and he does not ship?" A letter of credit solves both fears: the buyer's bank promises to pay the seller as soon as the seller hands over the right shipping documents. The bank looks only at the papers, never at the shirts.

A bank guarantee works the other way round: a contractor building a bridge for a State corporation gives a guarantee from his bank, which promises to pay the corporation on demand if he fails to perform. Courts treat both as promises the bank must keep, whatever the quarrel between buyer and seller.

1

Meaning

A letter of credit (documentary credit) is a written undertaking by a bank (the issuing bank), at the request of the buyer, to pay the seller a stated sum, provided the seller presents the stipulated documents within the stated time. It replaces the buyer's credit with the bank's credit. It is usually governed by the ICC's UCP 600.

🎨 Picture it

A paper bridge between a seller and a buyer in two countries: cross it with the right documents and the bank pays.

🌍 Real life

A Tiruppur shirt exporter fears non-payment; the German buyer fears non-shipment. The LC removes both fears.

2

Parties

Applicant (buyer/importer); issuing bank (buyer's bank); beneficiary (seller/exporter); advising bank (informs the seller); confirming bank (adds its own promise to pay); nominated/negotiating bank (pays or buys the documents); reimbursing bank.

🧠 Memory trick

Applicant · Issuing bank · Beneficiary · Advising · Confirming · Negotiating · Reimbursing: buyer, buyer's bank, seller, and the helpers.

3

How it works

Sale contract → buyer applies for the LC → issuing bank opens it → advising bank informs the seller → seller ships and presents documents (bill of exchange, bill of lading, invoice, insurance policy, certificate of origin) → the bank checks them (within five banking days) and pays, accepts or negotiates → the issuing bank reimburses → the buyer pays the issuing bank and gets the documents to take delivery.

🧠 Memory trick

Contract → apply → open → advise → ship → present → check → pay → reimburse.

4

Autonomy

The credit is separate from the sale contract; banks deal with documents, not goods (UCP 600 Arts. 4, 5). A quarrel about the quality of goods is no ground for the bank to refuse, or for a court to stop, payment against conforming documents UCB v. Bank of India. Only established fraud is an exception.

🧠 Memory trick

Documents, not goods. A quarrel about quality is no reason to refuse payment against conforming documents.

🎬 Case link

UCB v. Bank of India: an LC is independent of the sale; no injunction except for fraud or irretrievable injustice.

5

Strict compliance

The documents must conform exactly to the terms of the credit: "there is no room for documents which are almost the same, or which will do just as well" (Equitable Trust Co. of New York v. Dawson Partners, 1927). The bank may refuse discrepant documents, unless the buyer waives the discrepancy.

🎨 Picture it

"Almost the same" will not do (Equitable Trust v. Dawson, 1927).

🌍 Real life

The LC requires shipment by 31 March; the bill of lading is dated 2 April: the bank may refuse.

🔢 Number trick

5 banking days to examine the documents (UCP 600, Art. 14).

6

Kinds

Irrevocable (UCP 600: all credits are irrevocable) / revocable; confirmed / unconfirmed; sight (pay on presentation) / usance (pay later: acceptance or deferred payment); transferable (seller can transfer to a supplier); back-to-back (a second LC backed by the first); revolving (renews automatically); red clause (advance to seller before shipment) and green clause (advance plus storage); standby LC (works like a guarantee); documentary / clean.

Example: a Tiruppur exporter ships shirts under an irrevocable LC confirmed by an Indian bank. He presents a clean bill of lading and invoice exactly as required and is paid in India within days, even if the German buyer later complains about the colour.
🧠 Memory trick

Every credit under UCP 600 is irrevocable, even if it doesn't say so.

📖 Word origin

Sight = pay on presentation; usance = pay later. Back-to-back = a second LC backed by the first.

7

Merits

Seller: assured payment, can get pre-shipment finance. Buyer: pays only against proof of shipment, gets credit time. Demerits: cost, paperwork, and payment can be refused for a small discrepancy; it does not guarantee the quality of goods.

🧠 Memory trick

Seller: assured payment. Buyer: pays only against proof of shipment. But: cost, paperwork, and no guarantee of quality.

🔁 Recall: An LC is a paper bridge: the seller crosses it with the right documents, and the bank on the other side pays without looking into the shirts.

3Bank guarantee

Exam question: Bank guarantee: meaning, kinds, nature, invocation and injunction; guarantee v. letter of credit

1

Meaning

S.126

A bank guarantee is a contract by which a bank (surety) promises to pay a beneficiary if its customer (principal debtor) fails to perform or pay (S.126 Contract Act). In practice most are unconditional "on demand" guarantees: the bank pays on a written demand within the validity period.

🎨 Picture it

A locked safe that opens on a written demand.

🔢 Number trick

S.126 Contract Act: surety (bank), principal debtor (customer), creditor (beneficiary).

2

Kinds

Performance guarantee (completion of a contract); financial guarantee (payment of a debt); advance payment guarantee; bid bond / earnest money guarantee; deferred payment guarantee; guarantees for customs, excise and courts; conditional and unconditional.

🧠 Memory trick

Performance · Financial · Advance payment · Bid bond · Deferred payment.

3

Independent promise

An unconditional guarantee is independent of the underlying contract. The bank must pay according to its terms when the beneficiary demands; it cannot look into the dispute between its customer and the beneficiary Hindustan Steelworks.

🎬 Case link

Hindustan Steelworks: an independent contract; the bank pays on its terms, whatever the dispute under the main contract.

4

When courts stop payment

Only in two exceptional cases: (1) fraud of an egregious nature, of which the bank has notice; (2) irretrievable injury or injustice (special equities) U.P. State Sugar Corp.. A mere dispute, or even a strong case on the main contract, is not enough.

Example: a contractor builds a canal for a State corporation and gives a ₹1 crore performance guarantee. The corporation says the work is defective and invokes it. The contractor says the corporation itself caused the delay. The bank must still pay; the contractor must fight the dispute in arbitration or court and recover later if he wins.
🧠 Memory trick

Only two things jam the lock: egregious fraud known to the bank, or irretrievable injustice.

🎬 Case link

U.P. State Sugar Corp.: the injunction was set aside; an unconditional guarantee must be honoured.

🌍 Real life

A canal contractor's ₹1 crore performance guarantee is invoked; he blames the corporation for the delay. The bank must still pay.

5

Guarantor's rights

S.129S.130S.133S.134S.141

A continuing guarantee may be revoked for future transactions (S.129, S.130); the surety is discharged by a variance in the contract made without his consent (S.133), by release of the principal debtor (S.134), or by the creditor losing a security (S.141); on paying, he steps into the creditor's shoes (S.140). A bank guarantee may validly fix a claim period of not less than one year after the event (S.28, Exception 3).

🔢 Number trick

Surety discharged by: variance without consent (S.133) · release of the debtor (S.134) · loss of security (S.141).

6

Guarantee v. LC

(1) An LC is a means of payment in a sale; a guarantee is security against default. (2) Under an LC the bank pays against documents showing performance; under a guarantee, against a demand alleging non-performance. (3) LC: bank's primary obligation; traditional guarantee: secondary (but on-demand guarantees work like primary obligations). Both are independent of the underlying contract.

🧠 Memory trick

LC = payment in a sale. Guarantee = security against default.

🔁 Recall: A bank guarantee is a locked safe that opens on a written demand; only fraud or irreparable harm can jam the lock.

5Case stories

United Commercial Bank v. Bank of India

(1981) 2 SCC 766 · AIR 1981 SC 1426

📖 What happenedTwo nationalised banks quarrelled over reimbursement under a documentary letter of credit after a dispute arose about the underlying sale; an interim injunction froze the payment.
❓ The questionShould courts restrain a bank from paying under a letter of credit because of disputes about the underlying contract?
⚖️ HeldA letter of credit is independent of the sale contract; courts should not grant injunctions restraining a bank from meeting its obligations under it, except for fraud or irretrievable injustice.

U.P. State Sugar Corporation v. Sumac International Ltd.

(1997) 1 SCC 568

📖 What happenedA supplier who had given bank guarantees obtained an injunction stopping a State corporation from encashing them because of a contract dispute.
❓ The questionWhen can a court restrain the encashment of an unconditional bank guarantee?
⚖️ HeldAn unconditional bank guarantee must be honoured on its terms; injunction only for egregious fraud known to the bank or irretrievable injustice. The injunction was set aside.

⚖️ HeldA bank guarantee is an independent contract between the bank and the beneficiary; the bank must pay on its terms, and disputes under the main contract are no ground for an injunction.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1Banks deal in documents, not goods: an LC is independent of the sale contract (UCP 600, Arts. 4–5). UCB v. Bank of India
  • ★ 2Strict compliance: documents that are "almost the same" will not do.
  • ★ 3A bank guarantee is an independent promise; a dispute in the main contract does not stop it. Hindustan Steelworks
  • ★ 4Courts stop payment only for egregious fraud known to the bank or irretrievable injustice. U.P. State Sugar Corp.
  • ★ 5Under UCP 600, every credit is irrevocable even if it does not say so.

7Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. Company A and Company B enter into a contract for which B furnishes two bank guarantees. A dispute arises about completion of the contract. Will the guarantees be affected?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a dispute under the main contract stop the encashment of a bank guarantee?
  2. 📜 LawA bank guarantee is an independent contract; the bank must pay according to its terms Hindustan Steelworks. Injunction only for egregious fraud known to the bank or irretrievable injustice U.P. State Sugar Corp. (also U.P. Co-operative Federation v. Singh Consultants, 1988; Svenska Handelsbanken v. Indian Charge Chrome, 1994).
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe dispute concerns completion of the contract: an ordinary contractual dispute. Unless B can show fraud of the kind described, or irreparable injustice, the guarantees stand.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo. If A invokes the guarantees according to their terms, the bank must pay; B must resolve the dispute separately and claim the money back if it succeeds.

2. Under an LC requiring shipment by 31 March, the seller presents a bill of lading dated 2 April. The bank refuses to pay. The seller says the two-day delay is immaterial. Decide.

  1. 🎯 IssueMust the bank pay against documents that do not strictly comply?
  2. 📜 LawDoctrine of strict compliance (Equitable Trust Co. v. Dawson Partners, 1927); UCP 600: banks examine documents on their face; a complying presentation alone obliges payment; the applicant may waive discrepancies.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe latest shipment date is a term of the credit. A bill of lading dated after it is a discrepancy, however small.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe bank is right to refuse. The seller can ask the buyer to waive the discrepancy (or amend the credit); otherwise he must recover the price from the buyer under the sale contract.

3. After an LC is opened, the buyer finds that the goods shipped are of poor quality and asks the court to restrain the bank from paying the seller. Advise.

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a court restrain payment under an LC because of a dispute about the goods?
  2. 📜 LawAutonomy principle: an LC is independent of the sale contract UCB v. Bank of India; courts interfere only in cases of established fraud known to the bank, or irretrievable injustice.
  3. 🔍 ApplyPoor quality is a breach of the sale contract, not fraud in the documents.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe court will not restrain payment. The buyer must pay the bank and sue the seller for breach of contract. If the seller had forged the shipping documents (no goods shipped at all), the fraud exception could apply.
