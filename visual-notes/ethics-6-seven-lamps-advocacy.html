🪔Seven Lamps of Advocacy and Elements of Advocacy

Seven lamps light an advocate's path (Parry, 1923), and four verbs make up his toolkit: know, prepare, perform, behave.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

In 1923, an English judge, Sir Edward Abbott Parry, wrote a small book called "The Seven Lamps of Advocacy" (echoing Ruskin's Seven Lamps of Architecture). He said a good advocate's path is lit by seven lamps: Honesty, Courage, Industry, Wit, Eloquence, Judgment and Fellowship. A century later, examiners still ask about them in almost every paper.

Think of Bhulabhai Desai in 1945, defending officers of the Indian National Army at the Red Fort against the might of the British Empire: courage to take the brief, industry in preparation, eloquence in argument, judgment in strategy, and honesty throughout. That is advocacy at its best.

1

Origin

Judge Edward Abbott Parry (England) in The Seven Lamps of Advocacy (1923) described seven qualities that light the path of a good advocate. Indian courts and the BCI Rules reflect the same values.

🔢 Number trick

1923: Judge Edward Abbott Parry, The Seven Lamps of Advocacy, echoing Ruskin's Seven Lamps of Architecture.

🧠 Memory trick

HCIWEJF: "Honest Courageous Industrious advocates Win by Eloquence, Judgment and Fellowship."

2

Honesty

The foundation of advocacy. An advocate must never mislead the court, misstate facts or law, or deceive his client. He may defend a person he believes guilty, but must not invent a false defence. Honesty earns the trust of the court, which is the advocate's greatest asset.

🎨 Picture it

Honesty is the first lamp: without it, the other six only make a clever cheat.

🌍 Real life

An advocate may defend a man he believes guilty, but must not invent a false defence.

3

Courage

Fearlessness to take up unpopular causes, stand up to powerful opponents and even to the judge (respectfully), and tell the client unpleasant truths. Example: Bhulabhai Desai's defence of INA officers (1945); lawyers defending persons in custody against the State. BCI Rule 15 asks advocates to uphold clients' interests fearlessly.

🌍 Real life

1945, Red Fort: Bhulabhai Desai defends INA officers against the British Empire.

🧠 Memory trick

Courage = unpopular briefs · powerful opponents · unpleasant truths to the client (BCI Rule 15).

4

Industry

Hard work and thorough preparation: knowing every fact, every document, every precedent. "There is no royal road to success at the Bar." Briefs are won in chambers before they are won in court.

🗣️ Jargon buster

"There is no royal road to success at the Bar."

🎨 Picture it

Briefs are won in chambers before they are won in court.

5

Wit

Quick thinking and presence of mind, with a touch of humour, to handle an unexpected question from the Bench or a turn in cross-examination. It must never become rudeness or ridicule.

🎨 Picture it

Wit is a quick reflex: a sudden question from the Bench, a turn in cross-examination. Humour, never ridicule.

6

Eloquence

The ability to speak clearly, simply and persuasively. Not flowery speech, but the right words, well ordered, suited to the court and the case.

🧠 Memory trick

Eloquence = the right words, well ordered, not flowery speech.

7

Judgment

Sound sense and wisdom: choosing the right points to argue, the right questions to ask (and not ask), when to settle, and how to advise the client realistically.

🧠 Memory trick

Judgment = choosing: which points · which questions (and which not to ask) · when to settle.

8

Fellowship

Comradeship and courtesy towards fellow advocates: fight hard in court, but stay friends outside; respect juniors and seniors; help colleagues. It keeps the Bar united and dignified.

Why they matter today: clients judge lawyers by results, but courts judge them by character. A young advocate who is honest, well prepared and courteous earns the Bench's confidence; that, more than eloquence alone, wins cases over a career.
🗣️ Jargon buster

"Opponents in court, friends at the Bar."

🧠 Memory trick

Respect juniors and seniors; help colleagues. Legal aid (Rule 46) extends fellowship to society.

🔁 Recall: Light seven lamps with "HCIWEJF": Honest Courageous Industrious advocates Win by Eloquence, Judgment and Fellowship.

3Elements of advocacy

Exam question: Elements of advocacy

1

Meaning

Advocacy is the art of presenting a client's case persuasively before a court or tribunal, within the law and ethics. Its elements are the skills and qualities that make an effective advocate.

📖 Word origin

Advocacy, from Latin advocare, "to call to one's aid": the art of persuading a court within law and ethics.

2

Law

Sound grasp of substantive and procedural law, the latest judgments, and the rules of the court. An advocate must know where to find the law quickly.

🧠 Memory trick

Know the substantive law · procedural law · latest judgments · court rules, and where to find them fast.

3

Facts

Mastery of the facts and documents of the case, often more important than the law: most cases are won or lost on facts.

🎨 Picture it

Most cases are won or lost on facts, not law.

4

Preparation and research

Case analysis, identifying issues, preparing a list of dates, compilations and short written notes; anticipating the other side's points.

🧠 Memory trick

Issues · List of dates · Compilations · Short notes · Anticipate the other side.

5

Drafting

Clear, precise pleadings, petitions, affidavits and written submissions: a well-drafted pleading wins half the battle.

🗣️ Jargon buster

A well-drafted pleading wins half the battle.

6

Presentation and examination

Clear and brief oral argument, answering the Bench's questions directly, skilful examination-in-chief and cross-examination of witnesses, and persuasion through logic and precedent.

Example: in a cheque-bounce trial, the advocate who has studied the bank memo dates, the notice and the postal receipt (facts), knows the presumption cases (law), and cross-examines the complainant crisply on the debt, will do better than one relying only on eloquence.
🌍 Real life

Cheque-bounce trial: the advocate who knows the bank-memo dates (facts) and the presumption cases (law), and cross-examines crisply on the debt, wins.

7

Ethics and temperament

Honesty, courtesy to the court and opponents, patience, punctuality, self-control and tact: the seven lamps in daily practice. Without ethics, skill becomes a danger.

🧠 Memory trick

Honesty · courtesy · patience · punctuality · self-control · tact: the seven lamps in daily practice.

🔁 Recall: An advocate's toolkit: know (law, facts), prepare (research, drafting), perform (argument, cross-examination), and behave (ethics).
