🧠Mind map

1The big picture

A widow has been fighting an insurance company for motor accident compensation for six years. On a Saturday, at a National Lok Adalat, a retired judge and a social worker sit with both sides; the company offers a fair amount, she agrees, and by evening she holds an award that is final, with no appeal and the court fee refunded. That is Indian ADR at its most familiar: the "people's court".

The same idea runs through this cluster. The Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 gives free lawyers to the poor and runs Lok Adalats; Family Courts must first try to reconcile husband and wife; and S.89 CPC tells every civil judge to ask, before trial, "can this be settled outside?"

2Lok Adalat

Exam question: Lok Adalat: features, jurisdiction, procedure and award; Permanent Lok Adalat

1

Meaning and origin

"Lok Adalat" means people's court: a forum where disputes pending in court or at the pre-litigation stage are settled amicably. Begun as camps in Gujarat (1982), it was given statutory status by the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (Chapter VI).

📖 Word origin

Lok Adalat = "people's court".

🔢 Number trick

Began as camps in Gujarat, 1982; statutory under the LSA Act, 1987.

2

Organisation (S.19)

S.19

Organised by the State, District and Taluk Legal Services Authorities and the Supreme Court and High Court Legal Services Committees. A bench usually has a serving or retired judicial officer and other members such as a lawyer and a social worker. National Lok Adalats are held on fixed days across India.

🎨 Picture it

A bench of a judge and a social worker sitting with both sides around a table.

3

Jurisdiction

S.19(5)

Any case pending before any court, or any dispute at the pre-litigation stage, within the jurisdiction of the court. Not for non-compoundable offences. Typical matters: motor accident claims, bank recovery, cheque cases, matrimonial (other than divorce itself), electricity and water bills, compoundable offences.

🧠 Memory trick

Pending or pre-litigation disputes; never non-compoundable offences.

🌍 Real life

Motor accident claims, bank recovery, cheque cases and family disputes fill a National Lok Adalat day.

4

Cognizance (S.20)

S.20

A court refers a case if (i) the parties agree, or (ii) one party applies and the court sees a chance of settlement, or (iii) the court thinks it fit, after hearing the parties. The Lok Adalat acts with utmost speed, guided by justice, equity and fair play. If no settlement, the case goes back to the court (or the parties are told to approach a court).

🧠 Memory trick

Three routes in (S.20): both agree · one asks and the court sees a chance · the court thinks fit.

5

Award (S.21)

S.21

Every award is deemed a decree of a civil court; it is final and binding on all parties and no appeal lies from it. The court fee paid is refunded. An award without real consent is invalid and can be challenged only by a writ petition Jalour Singh.

Example: a bank sues a farmer for ₹3 lakh. At a Lok Adalat, the bank waives part of the interest and the farmer agrees to pay ₹2.2 lakh in instalments. The award ends the suit; the farmer gets back his court fee, and neither side can appeal.
🧠 Memory trick

The three Fs: Fast · Free (court fee refunded) · Final (no appeal).

🎬 Case link

Jalour Singh: no consent, no award; it can only record a settlement, never decide.

🌍 Real life

A bank waives part of the interest and a farmer agrees to pay ₹2.2 lakh in instalments: the award ends the suit, and the farmer gets his court fee back.

6

Powers (S.22)

S.22

The powers of a civil court for summoning and examining witnesses, discovery, receiving evidence on affidavits and requisitioning records; its proceedings are judicial proceedings.

🧠 Memory trick

A civil court's powers to summon, discover, take affidavits, call for records; its proceedings are judicial.

7

Permanent Lok Adalat

S.22BS.22C(8)S.22E

For public utility services (transport, post, telephone, electricity, water, hospitals, insurance, banking and the like). It first tries conciliation; if that fails, it can decide the dispute on merits (S.22C(8)). Its award is final and binding (S.22E). Not for non-compoundable offences; there is a monetary ceiling.

🎨 Picture it

For public utilities (transport, post, phone, power, water, hospitals, insurance, banks): conciliation first, then it can decide.

🔁 Recall: Lok Adalat in three Fs: Fast (one day), Free (court fee refunded), Final (no appeal). But only if both say "yes".

3Free legal aid

Exam question: Free legal aid and the Legal Services Authorities

🧠 Say it: “Children Should Always Want Water”

  1. CChildrenConstitutional base
  2. SShouldSupreme Court on legal aid
  3. AAlwaysAuthorities
  4. WWantWho is entitled (S.12)
  5. WWaterWhat is given
1

Constitutional base

Art. 39AArt. 14Art. 22

Art. 39A (added in 1976): the State shall provide free legal aid so that justice is not denied by reason of economic or other disabilities. Art. 14 and Art. 22(1) (right to consult a lawyer) support it.

🔢 Number trick

Art. 39A, added in 1976: justice must not be denied because of poverty.

2

Supreme Court on legal aid

Art. 21

Free legal aid to a poor accused is part of the fair procedure under Art. 21: M.H. Hoskot v. State of Maharashtra (1978) (copy of judgment and a lawyer for appeal), Hussainara Khatoon (1979), Khatri v. State of Bihar (1981) (from the first production before the Magistrate), Suk Das v. Union Territory of Arunachal Pradesh (1986) (the accused need not even ask: the court must tell him).

🧠 Memory trick

Hoskot (1978) · Hussainara (1979) · Khatri (1981): free legal aid is part of a fair procedure under Art. 21.

🎬 Case link

Hussainara Khatoon: undertrials and free legal aid.

3

Authorities

NALSA (national, headed by the Chief Justice of India as Patron-in-Chief), State Legal Services Authorities, High Court Legal Services Committees, District Legal Services Authorities and Taluk Legal Services Committees. They provide legal aid, run Lok Adalats, legal literacy camps, mediation centres, and victim compensation schemes.

🧠 Memory trick

NALSA → SLSA → HCLSC → DLSA → TLSC, national to taluk.

4

Who is entitled (S.12)

S.12

A member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe; a victim of trafficking or begar; a woman or a child; a person with disability; a victim of mass disaster, ethnic or caste violence, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster; an industrial workman; a person in custody; and a person whose annual income is below the prescribed limit.

Example: a daily-wage worker arrested for theft has no money for a lawyer. At his first production, the Magistrate must tell him he can have a free legal-aid lawyer, and the District Legal Services Authority assigns one at no cost.
🧠 Memory trick

Who walks in free (S.12): SC/ST · trafficking victims · women and children · persons with disability · disaster victims · industrial workmen · persons in custody · low income.

🌍 Real life

A daily-wage worker arrested for theft must be told at first production that he can have a free lawyer.

5

What is given

A lawyer, court fee and process fees, drafting of pleadings, copies of documents, and legal advice. The legal-aid defence counsel system now provides full-time defence lawyers in many districts.

🧠 Memory trick

Lawyer · fees · drafting · copies · advice.

🔁 Recall: "Poverty must not lock the court door": Art. 39A opens it, NALSA keeps the key, S.12 lists who walks in free.

4Family Courts

Exam question: Family Courts: object, jurisdiction, procedure and special features

🧠 Say it: “Our Elders Just Don't Like Prying”

  1. OOurObject
  2. EEldersEstablishment and judges
  3. JJustJurisdiction (S.7)
  4. DDon'tDuty to settle (S.9)
  5. LLikeLawyers (S.13)
  6. PPryingProcedure and appeal
1

Object

The Family Courts Act, 1984 sets up special courts to promote conciliation and secure speedy settlement of disputes about marriage and family affairs, in a less formal, more humane setting than ordinary courts.

🎨 Picture it

A family room, not a courtroom.

2

Establishment and judges

S.3S.4S.5S.6

The State Government, in consultation with the High Court, must set up a Family Court for every city or town with a population over ten lakh, and may elsewhere. Judges should be committed to protecting the institution of marriage and promoting the welfare of children; women are given preference. Counsellors and social welfare agencies are attached (S.5, S.6).

🔢 Number trick

Must be set up for every city or town with a population over 10 lakh (S.3).

3

Jurisdiction (S.7)

S.7

Suits and proceedings for nullity, restitution of conjugal rights, judicial separation and divorce; validity of a marriage or a person's matrimonial status; disputes about the property of the spouses; injunctions arising out of the marital relationship; legitimacy; maintenance; guardianship and custody of children; and maintenance proceedings under the BNSS (Chapter X).

🧠 Memory trick

Marriage · Status · Property of spouses · Injunctions · Legitimacy · Maintenance · Guardianship.

4

Duty to settle (S.9)

S.9

The Family Court must, in the first instance, make efforts to persuade the parties to a settlement, and may adjourn proceedings for that purpose.

🎨 Picture it

Settlement before judgment (S.9): the counsellor first.

5

Lawyers (S.13)

S.13

No party is entitled as of right to be represented by a legal practitioner; the court may allow it, and may appoint a lawyer as amicus curiae. This keeps proceedings simple and less adversarial.

Example: a couple files for divorce in a Family Court in Hyderabad. The judge first sends them to the court's counsellor; they agree on child custody and maintenance, and the court then records a mutual-consent decree.
🧠 Memory trick

No lawyer as of right (S.13); the court may allow one or appoint an amicus curiae.

📖 Word origin

Amicus curiae = "friend of the court".

6

Procedure and appeal

S.10(3)S.11S.12S.14S.15

The court may lay down its own procedure for settlement (S.10(3)); proceedings may be held in camera (S.11); it may take help of medical and welfare experts (S.12); strict rules of evidence are relaxed (S.14) and only a memorandum of evidence is recorded (S.15). Appeal lies to the High Court within 30 days; no appeal from a decree passed with consent (S.19).

🧠 Memory trick

Own procedure · in camera · experts · relaxed evidence (S.10–14).

🌍 Real life

A couple filing for divorce in Hyderabad is sent to the counsellor; they agree on custody and maintenance, and the court passes a mutual-consent decree.

🔁 Recall: A Family Court is a family room, not a courtroom: counsellor first, lawyer only if allowed, doors closed if needed, settlement before judgment.

5Section 89 CPC

Exam question: Section 89 CPC: settlement of disputes outside court

🧠 Say it: “The Four People Saw Elephants”

  1. TTheText and object
  2. FFourFour methods
  3. PPeopleProcedure (O.X r.1A–1C)
  4. SSawSalem Bar and Afcons
  5. EElephantsEffect
1

Text and object

Inserted by the CPC (Amendment) Act, 1999, in force from 1 July 2002. Where it appears to the court that there exist elements of a settlement acceptable to the parties, the court shall formulate the terms of a possible settlement, give them to the parties for observations, and then refer the dispute to an ADR method.

🔢 Number trick

Inserted in 1999, in force from 1 July 2002.

🎨 Picture it

A "Settle first?" signboard at every civil court door.

2

Four methods

(a) Arbitration (then governed by the 1996 Act); (b) conciliation (1996 Act); (c) judicial settlement, including Lok Adalat (LSA Act); (d) mediation (the court effects a compromise under prescribed procedure).

🧠 Memory trick

A-C-L-M: Arbitration · Conciliation · Lok Adalat (judicial settlement) · Mediation.

3

Procedure (O.X r.1A–1C)

O.X r.1

After framing issues, the court directs the parties to opt for one of the modes (r.1A); they appear before the chosen forum (r.1B); if the effort fails, they come back to court (r.1C).

🧠 Memory trick

Opt → Appear → Return if it fails (O.X r.1A, 1B, 1C).

4

Salem Bar and Afcons

S.89

Salem Advocate Bar Association v. Union of India (2005) upheld S.89 and approved model mediation rules. In Afcons Infrastructure the Supreme Court explained that the definitions of "judicial settlement" and "mediation" in S.89 had been mixed up and must be read interchanged; that considering ADR is mandatory after pleadings; that reference to arbitration or conciliation needs consent; and listed cases unsuitable for ADR (representative suits, election disputes, fraud and forgery, cases against the Government, criminal matters).

Example: in a partition suit between three siblings, the judge sees that they mostly agree, refers them to the court's mediation centre, and they sign a settlement dividing the house; the court passes a decree in terms of it.
🎬 Case link

Afcons: considering ADR is mandatory; arbitration and conciliation need consent; read "judicial settlement" and "mediation" interchanged.

🎬 Case link

Salem Advocate Bar: S.89 upheld; model mediation rules approved.

5

Effect

S.21S.16

A settlement through Lok Adalat becomes an award (S.21 LSA Act); through mediation or judicial settlement, the court records it and passes a decree; through arbitration or conciliation, it is governed by the 1996 Act. The plaintiff gets a refund of court fee (S.16 Court-fees Act).

🧠 Memory trick

Lok Adalat → award · mediation or judicial settlement → decree in terms · arbitration or conciliation → the 1996 Act.

🔁 Recall: S.89 is the "Settle first?" signboard at every civil court door, pointing to four rooms: Arbitration, Conciliation, Lok Adalat, Mediation ("A-C-L-M").

7Case stories

State of Punjab v. Jalour Singh

(2008) 2 SCC 660

📖 What happenedIn motor accident claims, a Lok Adalat recorded an "award" increasing compensation though one party had not agreed.
❓ The questionCan a Lok Adalat decide a dispute when the parties do not agree to a settlement?
⚖️ HeldA Lok Adalat has no power to adjudicate; it can only record a settlement agreed by the parties. An award made without consent is invalid and can be challenged by a writ petition.

(2010) 8 SCC 24

📖 What happenedA trial court sent a construction dispute to arbitration under S.89 even though one party objected.
❓ The questionCan a court refer a suit to arbitration under S.89 without the consent of both parties? How should S.89 be applied?
⚖️ HeldConsidering ADR under S.89 is mandatory, but arbitration and conciliation need consent; "judicial settlement" and "mediation" must be read interchanged; the Court listed cases fit and unfit for ADR.

8Golden rules

  • ★ 1A Lok Adalat can only record a settlement; it cannot decide a case when the parties do not agree. Jalour Singh
  • ★ 2A Lok Adalat award is a decree, final and binding, with no appeal (S.21).
  • ★ 3Free legal aid is a fundamental right of an accused who cannot afford a lawyer (Art. 21, 39A).
  • ★ 4Family Courts must first try to settle the dispute (S.9) and lawyers appear only with leave (S.13).
  • ★ 5Under S.89, courts must consider ADR in every suit; arbitration and conciliation need the parties' consent. Afcons Infrastructure
