⚖️Arbitration Agreement, Types of Arbitration and the Arbitral Tribunal

Every arbitration is born from a promise made before the quarrel. Then the parties pick a private judge, who must be independent and fair to both.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

Every arbitration is born from a promise made before the quarrel: a clause in the contract saying "any dispute shall be referred to arbitration". That clause is the arbitration agreement. Without it, nobody can be dragged into arbitration; with it, a party who runs to a civil court is sent back to arbitration (S.8).

Next comes the arbitral tribunal: a private judge (or three) chosen by the parties. Because the parties hand over their dispute to someone they picked, the law insists on one thing above all: the arbitrator must be independent, impartial and fair to both sides (S.12, S.18). A clause that lets one party alone pick the judge is no longer allowed.

1

Meaning (S.7)

S.7(1)S.7(2)

An agreement by the parties to submit to arbitration all or certain disputes which have arisen or may arise between them in respect of a defined legal relationship, whether contractual or not. It may be an arbitration clause in a contract or a separate agreement (S.7(2)).

🎨 Picture it

A "Plan B" signed before the fight: a clause in the contract, or a separate agreement.

2

Essentials

(1) A present or future dispute; (2) a defined legal relationship; (3) a clear intention to refer to arbitration and be bound by the decision; (4) in writing; (5) parties competent to contract; (6) the dispute is arbitrable. Words like "may be referred to arbitration if the parties so agree" show no binding intention Jagdish Chander.

🧠 Memory trick

Dispute · Defined relationship · Clear intention to be bound · Written · Competent parties · Arbitrable.

🎬 Case link

Jagdish Chander: "may refer, if the parties agree" is not an arbitration agreement.

3

Written form

S.7(3)

It must be in writing: a document signed by the parties; an exchange of letters, telex, telegrams or electronic communications (including e-mail) that records it; an exchange of statements of claim and defence in which one alleges it and the other does not deny it; or a reference in a contract to a document containing an arbitration clause, if the reference makes it part of the contract.

🌍 Real life

An exchange of e-mails that records the agreement to arbitrate is "in writing" (S.7(4)).

4

Separability (S.16)

S.16(1)

The arbitration clause is treated as an agreement independent of the other terms of the contract; a finding that the contract is null and void does not by itself invalidate the clause. The tribunal can rule on its own jurisdiction (competence-competence).

🎨 Picture it

The clause is a lifeboat: if the contract sinks (is void), the clause still floats (S.16(1)).

📖 Word origin

Kompetenz-Kompetenz (German, "competence-competence"): the tribunal rules on its own jurisdiction first.

5

Reference by courts (S.8)

S.8S.9S.11

If a party files a suit on a matter covered by an arbitration agreement, the court shall refer the parties to arbitration if the other party applies no later than its first statement on the substance, unless it finds that prima facie no valid agreement exists. Courts also give interim protection (S.9) and appoint arbitrators (S.11).

Example: a software contract says "All disputes shall be finally settled by arbitration in Hyderabad". When the client sues for damages in a civil court, the vendor applies under S.8 and the court must send the dispute to arbitration.
🧠 Memory trick

Apply no later than your first statement on the substance, and the court must refer (S.8).

🌍 Real life

A client sues his software vendor in a civil court despite an arbitration clause; the vendor applies under S.8 and the court sends it to arbitration.

6

Arbitrability

Not every dispute can be arbitrated. The Supreme Court's four-fold test excludes disputes that are actions in rem, affect third-party rights, involve sovereign or public functions, or are made non-arbitrable by statute: e.g. criminal offences, matrimonial status, guardianship, insolvency, testamentary matters, tenancies under rent control laws Vidya Drolia. An unstamped agreement is not void; stamp objections are for the tribunal (In re Interplay between Arbitration Agreements and the Stamp Act, 7 judges, 2023).

🧠 Memory trick

Not arbitrable if it is: in rem · hits third parties · sovereign or public · barred by statute.

🎬 Case link

Vidya Drolia: landlord-tenant disputes under the TPA are arbitrable; rent-control disputes are not.

7

Significance

S.40

It is the foundation of arbitration: it gives the tribunal its power, fixes the scope of disputes, the number of arbitrators, the seat and the rules. It binds legal representatives (S.40) and makes the award enforceable.

🧠 Memory trick

It gives the tribunal its power, scope, number, seat and rules, and binds legal representatives (S.40).

🔁 Recall: An arbitration agreement is a "Plan B" signed before the fight: written, clear, binding, and it survives even if the rest of the contract falls.

3Types of arbitration

Exam question: Types of arbitration: ad hoc, institutional, statutory, domestic, international and fast-track

🧠 Say it: “All Indians Should Do It Fast”

  1. AAllAd hoc
  2. IIndiansInstitutional
  3. SShouldStatutory
  4. DDoDomestic
  5. IItInternational commercial
  6. FFastFast-track
1

Ad hoc

S.11

Conducted by the parties and the arbitrators themselves, without any institution; the parties fix the procedure and fees. Flexible and cheap, but may suffer delays and disputes about appointment (then S.11 applies).

📖 Word origin

Ad hoc, Latin "for this": arranged for this dispute only, without an institution.

2

Institutional

Administered by an arbitral institution under its own rules: e.g. Indian Council of Arbitration, Mumbai Centre for International Arbitration, Delhi International Arbitration Centre, ICC, SIAC. The institution appoints arbitrators, fixes fees and supervises timelines. More predictable; costlier.

🧠 Memory trick

The institution appoints, administers, charges fixed fees: ICA, MCIA, DIAC, ICC, SIAC.

3

Statutory

S.3GS.2(4)

Imposed by a statute, whether or not the parties agree: e.g. the arbitrator appointed by the Central Government for compensation under the National Highways Act, 1956 (S.3G), disputes under co-operative societies laws, or the Electricity Act, 2003. Part I of the 1996 Act applies so far as it is consistent with that statute (S.2(4)).

🌍 Real life

Compensation for land acquired for a National Highway is fixed by an arbitrator the Central Government appoints (S.3G, NH Act).

4

Domestic

S.34S.34(2A)

Arbitration seated in India between parties who are not foreign (not an international commercial arbitration). Governed by Part I; the award can be set aside under S.34, including for patent illegality (S.34(2A)).

🧠 Memory trick

Seated in India + no foreign party = domestic; S.34 challenge includes patent illegality.

5

International commercial

S.2(1)(f)

A commercial dispute where at least one party is foreign: an individual habitually resident abroad, a body corporate incorporated abroad, an association managed abroad, or a foreign government. If seated in India, Part I applies (but patent illegality is not a ground to set aside); if seated abroad, Part II governs enforcement of the award in India.

Example: a Hyderabad hospital and a German equipment maker agree to ICC arbitration in Singapore: this is institutional and international commercial arbitration, seated abroad, so the award will be enforced in India as a foreign award under Part II.
🧠 Memory trick

At least one foreign party = international commercial arbitration.

🌍 Real life

A Hyderabad hospital and a German maker choose ICC arbitration in Singapore: institutional, international, seated abroad.

6

Fast-track

S.29BS.89

Fast-track arbitration (S.29B): on written pleadings without oral hearing (unless needed), award within six months. Documents-only arbitration; online arbitration; court-referred arbitration under S.89 CPC (with consent).

🔢 Number trick

Fast-track: written pleadings, award within six months (S.29B).

🔁 Recall: Ask three questions: who runs it? (parties = ad hoc, institution = institutional, statute = statutory), who are the parties? (Indian = domestic, foreign = international), how fast? (fast-track = 6 months).

4The arbitral tribunal

1

Composition (S.10)

S.10

The parties are free to fix the number of arbitrators, but it shall not be an even number; if they fail, there is a sole arbitrator.

🧠 Memory trick

Never an even number; no agreement? A sole arbitrator.

2

Appointment (S.11)

S.11S.11(6A)

The parties may agree on a procedure. Failing that, in a three-member tribunal each party appoints one and the two appoint the third (presiding) arbitrator within 30 days. If a party or the two arbitrators fail, on application the Supreme Court (international) or the High Court (domestic), or an institution designated by it, appoints. The court only examines whether an arbitration agreement exists (S.11(6A)).

🧠 Memory trick

One each, then the two pick the third, within 30 days; failing that, the court or an institution appoints (S.11).

3

Independence and challenge (S.12, S.13)

S.12S.13S.12(5)S.34

A proposed arbitrator must disclose in writing any circumstances likely to give justifiable doubts about his independence or impartiality (Fifth and Sixth Schedules). A person with a relationship listed in the Seventh Schedule (e.g. an employee or adviser of a party) is ineligible unless the parties expressly waive it in writing after the dispute arises (S.12(5)). Challenge within 15 days to the tribunal; if rejected, the tribunal continues and the point can be raised under S.34. A party with an interest in the dispute cannot unilaterally appoint a sole arbitrator, nor force the other side to choose from its own panel CORE (Railways).

🧠 Memory trick

Disclose → doubt → challenge: Fifth and Sixth Schedules for disclosure; Seventh Schedule relationships make a person ineligible.

4

Natural justice (S.18)

S.18S.24S.19S.31(3)S.34(2)(a)(iii)

"The parties shall be treated with equality and each party shall be given a full opportunity to present his case." This is audi alteram partem (hear the other side) and nemo judex in causa sua (no one judges his own cause) in statutory form. Also: notice of hearings and documents (S.24), the tribunal not bound by CPC or Evidence Act but by fairness (S.19), a reasoned award (S.31(3)). Breach is a ground to set aside the award (S.34(2)(a)(iii) and public policy).

Case story CORE (Railways): a Railways contract let the Railways prepare a panel of its own retired officers from which the contractor had to choose. A Constitution Bench held that such clauses violate equal treatment (S.18) and Art. 14: the party with an interest in the result cannot control who judges it.
📖 Word origin

Audi alteram partem: hear the other side. Nemo judex in causa sua: no one judges his own cause.

🎬 Case link

CORE (Railways): no unilateral appointments and no curated panels of one party's retired officers.

5

Duties and relationship with parties

S.42AS.29AS.14S.15

The arbitrator is not the agent of the party who appointed him; he is a quasi-judicial decision-maker owing duties to both: disclosure, impartiality, confidentiality (S.42A), diligence and finishing within time (S.29A: award within 12 months of completion of pleadings, extendable by 6 months by consent), deciding only within the reference, and giving reasons. Fees follow the agreement or the Fourth Schedule. His mandate ends if he cannot act or withdraws (S.14, S.15).

🎨 Picture it

The arbitrator is not the appointing party's agent: a referee picked by both teams, fair to both.

6

Court intervention (S.5)

S.5S.8S.9S.11S.14

Notwithstanding any other law, no judicial authority shall intervene except where Part I so provides: reference (S.8), interim measures (S.9), appointment (S.11), termination of mandate (S.14), assistance in taking evidence (S.27), setting aside (S.34), enforcement (S.36) and appeals (S.37). This "minimal intervention" principle comes from the UNCITRAL Model Law.

🎨 Picture it

The court is the stadium owner: it steps in only where Part I says so (S.5): S.8, 9, 11, 14, 27, 34, 36, 37.

🔁 Recall: A good arbitrator is a referee chosen by both teams: odd number on the field, both teams pick, no player's cousin, both teams get the ball, and the stadium owner (court) steps in only when the rulebook says so.

6Case stories

Jagdish Chander v. Ramesh Chander

(2007) 5 SCC 719

📖 What happenedA partnership deed said disputes would be decided by the partners mutually "or shall be referred for arbitration if the parties so determine".
❓ The questionDoes a clause saying disputes "shall be referred for arbitration if the parties so determine" amount to an arbitration agreement?
⚖️ HeldNo binding arbitration agreement: the clause only contemplated a possible future agreement to arbitrate. The Court laid down the attributes of an arbitration agreement.

Vidya Drolia v. Durga Trading Corporation

(2021) 2 SCC 1 · decided 14 Dec 2020

📖 What happenedA landlord-tenant dispute under a lease raised the question whether such disputes can be arbitrated at all.
❓ The questionWhen is a dispute not arbitrable, and are tenancy disputes arbitrable?
⚖️ HeldThe Court laid down a four-fold test for non-arbitrability and held that landlord-tenant disputes under the Transfer of Property Act are arbitrable (those under rent control laws are not).

⚖️ HeldA party cannot unilaterally appoint a sole arbitrator or force the other side to pick from its own curated panel; this violates equal treatment (S.18) and Art. 14. The ruling applies to appointments after the judgment.

7Golden rules

  • ★ 1No written arbitration agreement, no arbitration (S.7); "may refer, if parties agree" is not enough. Jagdish Chander
  • ★ 2The arbitration clause survives even if the main contract is said to be void (S.16: separability).
  • ★ 3A civil court must refer the parties to arbitration if a valid agreement exists (S.8).
  • ★ 4Most civil disputes are arbitrable; rights in rem, criminal, matrimonial, insolvency and some public-law matters are not. Vidya Drolia
  • ★ 5A party with an interest cannot unilaterally appoint the arbitrator; equal treatment applies from day one. CORE (Railways)
