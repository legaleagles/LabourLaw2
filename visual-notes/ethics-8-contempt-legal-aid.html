🔨Contempt of Court and Legal Aid

1The big picture

Courts have no army and no purse. Their orders are obeyed because people respect their authority. When someone wilfully disobeys a court order, or scandalises a judge so as to shake public confidence in justice, the court can punish him for contempt. But the power is used carefully: fair criticism of a judgment is not contempt, and since 2006 truth told in the public interest is a defence.

The other side of the same coin is legal aid. Justice must be not only respected but reachable: a person who cannot afford a lawyer must still get one. For an advocate, legal aid is not charity; it is a professional duty (BCI Rule 46).

1

Object and base

Art. 129Art. 19

To protect the authority and dignity of courts and the administration of justice, not the personal dignity of judges. The Supreme Court and High Courts are courts of record with inherent power to punish contempt (Art. 129, 215); contempt is a ground for restricting free speech (Art. 19(2)). The Act of 1971 (on the Sanyal Committee's report) defines and limits the power.

💬 In simple words
  • Contempt of court: disobeying a court or insulting or obstructing it.
🎨 Picture it

Contempt guards the court's authority, not the judge's ego.

🔢 Number trick

Art. 129 (Supreme Court) · Art. 215 (High Courts): courts of record · Art. 19(2): a ground to restrict speech · Sanyal Committee → 1971 Act.

2

Civil contempt

S.2(b)

Wilful disobedience of any judgment, decree, direction, order, writ or other process of a court, or wilful breach of an undertaking given to a court.

🧠 Memory trick

Civil = disobey: a decree, order or writ, or break an undertaking given to court (S.2(b)). The key word: wilful.

🌍 Real life

A builder promises the court to stop construction and keeps building: wilful breach of an undertaking.

3

Criminal contempt

S.2(c)

Publication (by words, signs, visible representations or otherwise) or any other act which: (i) scandalises or lowers the authority of any court; (ii) prejudices or interferes with the due course of any judicial proceeding; or (iii) interferes with or obstructs the administration of justice in any other way.

🧠 Memory trick

Criminal = Scandalise · Prejudice · Obstruct (S.2(c)).

4

Defences

S.3S.4S.5S.6S.13

Innocent publication or distribution without knowledge of pending proceedings (S.3); fair and accurate report of judicial proceedings (S.4); fair criticism of a decided case (S.5); a good-faith complaint about a subordinate judge to a superior authority (S.6). Since 2006: no punishment unless the contempt substantially interferes with justice, and truth is a defence if in the public interest and the request is bona fide (S.13).

🧠 Memory trick

Four shields: innocent publication (S.3) · fair report (S.4) · fair criticism (S.5) · good-faith complaint (S.6).

🔢 Number trick

2006: S.13 adds the substantial interference test and truth in the public interest.

5

Punishment and apology

S.12

Simple imprisonment up to six months, or fine up to ₹2,000, or both. A bona fide apology may lead the court to discharge the contemner or remit the punishment; an apology must not be qualified or merely tactical.

🔢 Number trick

Up to 6 months' simple imprisonment, or ₹2,000, or both (S.12).

🗣️ Jargon buster

An apology is a shield, not a weapon: it must be bona fide, not qualified or merely tactical.

6

Procedure

S.14S.15S.15(2)S.19S.20

Contempt in the face of the court is dealt with summarily (S.14). Other criminal contempt: on the court's own motion, or on a motion by the Advocate-General (or Attorney/Solicitor-General for the Supreme Court), or by any person with his written consent (S.15); a subordinate court refers it to the High Court (S.15(2)). Appeals lie under S.19. No proceedings after one year from the contempt (S.20).

Case story In re Vinay Chandra Mishra: a senior advocate (then Chairman of the Bar Council of India) shouted at and threatened a High Court judge in open court. The Supreme Court held him guilty of criminal contempt. Its further order suspending his licence to practise was later held to be beyond its contempt power SCBA v. Union of India.
💬 In simple words
  • Bar Council: the elected body that regulates advocates in a State or in India.
  • Senior advocate: an advocate given special designation for ability and standing at the Bar.
🧠 Memory trick

In the face of the court: summary (S.14). Elsewhere: own motion, the Advocate-General, or anyone with his written consent (S.15).

🔢 Number trick

Clock: one year (S.20). Appeal: S.19.

🎬 Case link

Vinay Chandra Mishra: a senior advocate shouted at and threatened a judge in open court: criminal contempt.

7

Advocates and contempt

S.35

An advocate who commits contempt may be punished by the court and separately disciplined by the Bar Council (S.35, Advocates Act). Courts may also bar a contemnor-advocate from appearing before them until he purges the contempt (R.K. Anand v. Registrar, Delhi High Court, 2009). Recent example: In re Prashant Bhushan (2020): an advocate fined ₹1 for tweets held to be contempt.

🎨 Picture it

Two separate whips: the court punishes contempt; only the Bar Council can suspend the licence (S.35).

🎬 Case link

SCBA: the Supreme Court cannot suspend an advocate's licence, even under Art. 142; it can refer the matter to the Bar Council.

🌍 Real life

R.K. Anand (2009): a court may bar a contemnor-advocate from appearing until he purges the contempt. Prashant Bhushan (2020): fined ₹1 for tweets.

🔁 Recall: Contempt has two faces: civil (disobeying an order) and criminal (scandalising or obstructing). Shield: fair report, fair criticism, truth. Sword: 6 months or ₹2,000. Clock: one year.

1

Meaning

Legal aid means free legal services (advice, a lawyer, court fees, copies) to persons who cannot afford them, so that justice is not denied for want of money or because of other disabilities.

💬 In simple words
  • Legal aid: free legal help for those who cannot afford a lawyer.
🧠 Memory trick

Free advice · lawyer · court fees · copies, so that justice is not denied for want of money.

2

Constitution and courts

Art. 39AArt. 21

Art. 39A (1976): equal justice and free legal aid. The Supreme Court made it part of Art. 21: M.H. Hoskot (1978), Hussainara Khatoon (1979), Khatri v. State of Bihar (1981, from the first production before a Magistrate), Suk Das (1986, even without asking). A trial without offering legal aid to a poor accused may be vitiated.

🔢 Number trick

Art. 39A (1976) + Art. 21: Hoskot 1978 · Hussainara 1979 · Khatri 1981 · Suk Das 1986.

🧠 Memory trick

Khatri: from the first production before a Magistrate. Suk Das: even without asking.

3

Legal Services Authorities Act

S.4S.7S.10

NALSA (national), State and District Legal Services Authorities, High Court and Taluk committees. Functions: provide legal services, organise Lok Adalats, legal literacy and awareness camps, mediation centres, the legal aid defence counsel system, victim compensation schemes, and supervise legal aid clinics in law colleges (S.4, S.7, S.10).

🧠 Memory trick

Three tiers: NALSA → State → District authorities, plus High Court and Taluk committees (LSA Act, 1987).

🧠 Memory trick

Lok Adalats · literacy camps · mediation · defence counsel · victim compensation · law-college clinics.

4

Who is entitled

S.12

SC/ST members; victims of trafficking or begar; women and children; persons with disability; victims of disasters and violence; industrial workmen; persons in custody; and persons with annual income below the prescribed limit.

Example: an under-trial prisoner who has been in jail for two years without a lawyer writes to the District Legal Services Authority through the jail. A panel lawyer is assigned, files a bail application, and he is released the next month.
🧠 Memory trick

S.12 list: SC/ST · trafficking or begar victims · women and children · disability · disaster or violence victims · workmen · persons in custody · low income.

🌍 Real life

An under-trial in jail for two years writes to the District Legal Services Authority; a panel lawyer gets him bail within a month.

5

Advocate's duty

S.9A

Every advocate shall bear in mind that anyone genuinely in need of a lawyer is entitled to legal assistance even if he cannot pay fully or adequately, and within his means the advocate shall render legal aid. Bar Councils run legal aid committees (S.9A, Advocates Act). A legal-aid lawyer owes the same diligence as to a paying client; neglecting such a case is misconduct.

💬 In simple words
  • Professional misconduct: behaviour unfit for an advocate, punishable by the Bar Council.
🧠 Memory trick

Rule 46: within his means, every advocate gives legal aid. Same diligence as a paying client; neglect is misconduct.

🔁 Recall: Legal aid is a bridge with three pillars: the Constitution (Art. 39A, 21), the statute (LSA Act, S.12) and the profession (BCI Rule 46).

5Case stories

In re Vinay Chandra Mishra

(1995) 2 SCC 584 · decided 10 Mar 1995

📖 What happenedA senior advocate, then Chairman of the Bar Council of India, shouted at and threatened a High Court judge in open court when questioned during a hearing.
❓ The questionWas the advocate guilty of criminal contempt, and what punishment could the Supreme Court impose?
⚖️ HeldHe was guilty of criminal contempt; the Court imposed a suspended sentence and also suspended him from practice for three years (the last part was later held to be beyond its contempt power).

Supreme Court Bar Association v. Union of India

(1998) 4 SCC 409 · decided 17 Apr 1998

📖 What happenedAfter the Supreme Court suspended a senior advocate's licence for contempt, the Supreme Court Bar Association questioned whether the Court had that power.
❓ The questionCan the Supreme Court suspend an advocate from practice as a punishment for contempt of court?
⚖️ HeldThe Supreme Court cannot, in contempt jurisdiction or under Art. 142, suspend or cancel an advocate's licence; that power belongs to the Bar Councils under the Advocates Act. The Court can punish contempt and refer the matter to the Bar Council.

🪝 Court punishes contempt; Bar Council punishes misconduct · Full story →

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1Supreme Court and High Courts are courts of record with power to punish contempt (Art. 129, 215).
  • ★ 2Fair criticism of a judgment is not contempt; scandalising the judge is.
  • ★ 3No punishment unless the contempt substantially interferes with justice; truth is a defence if in the public interest and bona fide (S.13).
  • ★ 4A court can punish an advocate for contempt, but only the Bar Council can suspend his licence. SCBA v. Union of India
  • ★ 5Every advocate must remember that anyone genuinely in need of a lawyer is entitled to legal assistance (Rule 46).
