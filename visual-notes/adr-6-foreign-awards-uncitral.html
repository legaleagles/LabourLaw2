1The big picture

A German company wins an arbitration in London against an Indian buyer: ₹20 crore. The award is useless unless it can be enforced where the buyer's money is: in India. That is what Part II of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act does. India and 170-odd other countries have joined the New York Convention, 1958, promising to enforce each other's arbitral awards with only a few, narrow grounds for refusal.

The Indian court does not re-hear the case. It checks the papers, asks a few basic questions (was there a valid agreement, a fair hearing, is the matter arbitrable, is enforcement against India's public policy?) and, if satisfied, treats the foreign award as its own decree.

1

Meaning (S.44)

S.44

A "foreign award" is an arbitral award on differences between persons arising out of legal relationships, contractual or not, considered commercial under Indian law, made on or after 11 October 1960, in pursuance of a written arbitration agreement to which the New York Convention applies, in a territory that the Central Government has notified as a reciprocating territory.

💬 In simple words
  • Arbitration: parties choosing a private judge (arbitrator) whose decision binds them.
  • Arbitral award: the final decision of the arbitrator.
🧠 Memory trick

India's two reservations: Commercial relationships only, and notified (reciprocating) countries only.

🔢 Number trick

Covers awards made on or after 11 October 1960.

2

Conventions and reservations

The Geneva Protocol (1923) and Geneva Convention (1927) were the first international arrangements; the New York Convention (1958) replaced them for most countries and is now near-universal. India enforced them first through the Arbitration (Protocol and Convention) Act, 1937 and the Foreign Awards (Recognition and Enforcement) Act, 1961; today Part II of the 1996 Act does so. India made two reservations: reciprocity and commercial relationships only.

💬 In simple words
  • Foreign award: an arbitral award made outside India under the New York or Geneva Convention.
🔢 Number trick

1923 Geneva Protocol → 1927 Geneva Convention → 1958 New York Convention (India ratified 1960).

3

Referral to arbitration (S.45)

S.45

If a party sues in India on a matter covered by an agreement under the Convention, the court shall refer the parties to arbitration unless it prima facie finds the agreement null and void, inoperative or incapable of being performed.

🧠 Memory trick

Sued in India despite a Convention arbitration agreement? The court shall refer, unless it is null, inoperative or incapable of performance (S.45).

4

Application and evidence (S.47)

S.47

The party seeking enforcement applies to the High Court having jurisdiction (over the subject matter or the debtor's assets) and produces: (a) the original award or a duly authenticated copy; (b) the original arbitration agreement or a certified copy; (c) evidence that it is a foreign award; with translations if not in English.

🧠 Memory trick

Three papers (S.47): the award · the agreement · proof it is a foreign award, before the High Court.

5

Grounds for refusal (S.48)

S.48

Enforcement may be refused only if the party resisting proves: (a) incapacity or an invalid agreement; (b) no proper notice of the arbitrator's appointment or proceedings, or he was otherwise unable to present his case; (c) the award goes beyond the submission (severable); (d) the composition or procedure was not as agreed or as per the law of the seat; (e) the award is not yet binding, or has been set aside or suspended in the country where it was made. Or the court finds: (a) the subject matter is not arbitrable in India; or (b) enforcement is contrary to the public policy of India (fraud or corruption, fundamental policy of Indian law, most basic notions of morality or justice), with no review on merits.

Case story Renusagar Power: General Electric won an ICC award against Renusagar Power. Renusagar argued that enforcing it, with interest, broke Indian foreign-exchange law and so public policy. The Supreme Court enforced it: public policy means the fundamental policy of Indian law, the interests of India, or justice and morality, not every breach of an Indian law.
💬 In simple words
  • Seat of arbitration: the legal home of the arbitration, which decides which courts supervise it.
  • Setting aside: a court cancelling an award on limited grounds.
🎨 Picture it

A checklist, not a re-trial: incapacity or invalid agreement, no notice, beyond scope, wrong tribunal, award not yet binding or set aside, not arbitrable, public policy.

🎬 Case link

Renusagar: public policy means the fundamental policy of Indian law, the interests of India, or justice and morality; mere breach of a law is not enough.

6

Deemed decree and appeal (S.49, S.50)

S.49S.50S.45S.48

If the court is satisfied that the award is enforceable, it is deemed to be a decree of that court and executed like one. An appeal lies only against an order refusing to refer the parties to arbitration (S.45) or refusing to enforce the award (S.48); no second appeal, though an appeal to the Supreme Court is possible by special leave.

🧠 Memory trick

Once satisfied, the award is a deemed decree (S.49). Appeal only against a refusal (S.50).

7

Geneva awards (S.53–60)

S.53S.56S.57S.58S.59

Awards under the Geneva Protocol/Convention (from countries not party to the New York Convention but notified under the Geneva Convention) are enforced on similar lines: evidence (S.56), conditions for enforcement (S.57), deemed decree (S.58) and appeal (S.59). In practice, almost all foreign awards now come under the New York Convention.

🧠 Memory trick

Geneva awards follow the same road: S.56 evidence, S.57 conditions, deemed decree.

🔁 Recall: Foreign award in three Ps: Prove it (S.47 papers), Pass the S.48 checklist (no merits review), then it becomes a Plain decree (S.49).

1

What UNCITRAL is

The United Nations Commission on International Trade Law, set up by the UN General Assembly in 1966 to harmonise and modernise the law of international trade.

📖 Word origin

UNCITRAL = United Nations Commission on International Trade Law (set up 1966).

2

Model Law, 1985

The UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration (adopted 1985, amended 2006) is a template for national arbitration laws: party autonomy, minimal court intervention, competence-competence, equal treatment, limited grounds to set aside, and recognition of awards. Many countries have adopted it.

🧠 Memory trick

Model Law's five pillars: party autonomy · minimal court intervention · competence-competence · equal treatment · limited grounds of challenge.

3

Arbitration and Conciliation Rules

The UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules (1976, revised 2010 and 2013) are widely used in ad hoc arbitration, including investor-State disputes; the UNCITRAL Conciliation Rules (1980) shaped Part III of the 1996 Act. Newer texts include the Model Law on International Commercial Mediation (2018) and the Singapore Convention on Mediation (2019), which India has signed.

💬 In simple words
  • Conciliation: a neutral person helping the parties and suggesting terms to settle.
  • Mediation: a neutral person helping the parties talk and reach their own settlement.
🔢 Number trick

Arbitration Rules 1976 (revised 2010, 2013); Conciliation Rules 1980, which shaped Part III of the 1996 Act.

4

Influence on India

S.5S.16S.18S.34S.48

The Preamble of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 expressly says it takes into account the Model Law and the UNCITRAL Conciliation Rules. Hence S.5 (minimal intervention), S.16 (competence-competence), S.18 (equal treatment), S.34 (limited grounds) and S.48 all follow the Model Law.

🎨 Picture it

The UN's recipe book: India cooked the 1996 Act from the Model Law and the Conciliation Rules.

🔢 Number trick

Model Law fingerprints in the 1996 Act: S.5 minimal intervention · S.16 competence-competence · S.18 equal treatment · S.34 limited challenge.

🎬 Case link

BALCO: Part I (and S.34) applies only to arbitrations seated in India; awards made abroad are resisted only at enforcement.

🔁 Recall: UNCITRAL is the UN's recipe book for trade law: India cooked the 1996 Act from its Model Law (arbitration) and Conciliation Rules.

5Case stories

Renusagar Power Co. Ltd. v. General Electric Co.

1994 Supp (1) SCC 644 · AIR 1994 SC 860

📖 What happenedGeneral Electric won an ICC award against an Indian power company, which resisted enforcement in India as contrary to public policy.
❓ The questionWhen can an Indian court refuse to enforce a foreign award as being contrary to public policy?
⚖️ HeldA foreign award can be refused as contrary to public policy only if it offends the fundamental policy of Indian law, the interests of India, or justice or morality; the award was enforced.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1A foreign award must come from a New York Convention country notified by India and arise from a commercial relationship (S.44): India's two reservations.
  • ★ 2An Indian court cannot review the merits of a foreign award; refusal is only on the S.48 grounds.
  • ★ 3Public policy for foreign awards is narrow: fundamental policy of Indian law, interests of India, justice or morality. Renusagar Power
  • ★ 4If the seat is abroad, Indian courts cannot set aside the award under S.34; they only decide enforcement. BALCO
  • ★ 5The 1996 Act is modelled on the UNCITRAL Model Law (1985).
