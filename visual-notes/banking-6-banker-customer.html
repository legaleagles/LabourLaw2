🤝Banker and Customer

A deposit is not safe custody: it is a loan to the bank. One relationship, many hats: debtor, agent, bailee, landlord, friend, guarantor.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

When Lakshmi deposits ₹1 lakh in a bank, she feels she has kept her money "in safe custody". In law, she has lent it to the bank. The bank becomes her debtor and she its creditor; the bank may use the money as it likes, so long as it repays her on demand.

But the relationship has many hats. When the bank collects her cheque, it is her agent; when it keeps her jewellery in safe custody, it is a bailee; when it rents her a locker, it is a lessor; and it must always keep her affairs secret and deal with her in good faith. One relationship, many roles.

2Banker and customer

Exam question: Banker and customer: meaning and the general (debtor-creditor) relationship

🧠 Say it: “Big Cats Don't Steal Rotis”

  1. BBigBanker
  2. CCatsCustomer
  3. DDon'tDebtor and creditor
  4. SStealSpecial features of the debt
  5. RRotisRoles reverse on overdraft
1

Banker

S.5(b)S.3

One who carries on the business of banking: accepting deposits from the public for lending or investment, repayable on demand and withdrawable by cheque (S.5(b) BR Act; S.3 NI Act).

🧠 Memory trick

Two tests again: public deposits + lending or investment, repayable on demand.

2

Customer

Not defined by statute. A customer is a person who has an account with the bank, or for whom the bank has agreed to open one, and whose dealings are of a banking nature. Duration does not matter: one becomes a customer when the account is opened (Commissioners of Taxation v. English, Scottish and Australian Bank, 1920). A person who merely cashes cheques at the counter without an account is not a customer (Great Western Railway v. London and County Banking Co., 1901).

🧠 Memory trick

One account makes a customer: how long or how often he deals does not matter.

3

Debtor and creditor

When a customer deposits money, the bank becomes his debtor and he its creditor. The money becomes the bank's property; it can use it as it likes and need not tell the customer how; it only promises to repay an equal sum Foley v. Hill.

Example: Lakshmi deposits ₹1 lakh in a savings account. The bank lends it to a trader the same day. Lakshmi cannot object; she can only demand ₹1 lakh (plus interest) back.
🎨 Picture it

Once deposited, the money is the bank's; the customer owns only a right to demand it back.

🎬 Case link

Foley v. Hill: the bank is a debtor, not a trustee or agent; it need not account for how it uses the money.

4

Special features of the debt

Art. 22

(1) The customer must make a demand before the bank is bound to repay; the demand must be at the branch where the account is kept (Joachimson v. Swiss Bank Corporation, 1921). (2) So limitation runs from the date of demand (Art. 22 Limitation Act), not from the deposit. (3) Demand must be in the proper form (cheque, withdrawal slip). (4) The bank is not a trustee of the money.

🧠 Memory trick

An unusual debt: demand first, at the branch (Joachimson, 1921). So limitation runs from the demand (Art. 22: 3 years).

5

Roles reverse on overdraft

When the customer borrows or overdraws, the bank becomes the creditor and the customer the debtor, usually secured by pledge, mortgage or hypothecation, with the bank's rights of lien and set-off.

🎨 Picture it

On an overdraft the roles flip: the bank becomes the creditor, with lien and set-off.

🔁 Recall: Ask "Whose money is it now?" Once deposited, it is the bank's; the customer owns only a right to demand it back.

3Special relationships, rights, duties

Exam question: Special relationships, and the rights and duties of a banker

🧠 Say it: “Tall Aunty Brought Lemon For Grandpa, Ready Daily”

  1. TTallTrustee
  2. AAuntyAgent
  3. BBroughtBailee
  4. LLemonLessor (locker)
  5. FForFiduciary
  6. GGrandpaGuarantor

1

Trustee

When money or securities are given to the bank for a specific purpose (e.g. "pay this to my supplier", "buy shares for me"), the bank holds them in trust until the purpose is carried out; it cannot treat them as its own or set them off.

🌍 Real life

"Pay this ₹50,000 to my supplier": the bank holds it in trust for that purpose and cannot set it off.

2

Agent

When the bank collects cheques, dividends or bills, pays insurance premia under standing instructions, or buys and sells securities for the customer, it is his agent and owes him an agent's duties.

🎨 Picture it

The agent hat: collecting cheques, dividends, paying premiums on standing instructions.

3

Bailee

S.148S.151

Valuables (jewellery, documents) left for safe custody make the bank a bailee (S.148 Contract Act); it must take the care a prudent man takes of his own goods (S.151) and has no lien on them.

🎨 Picture it

The bailee hat: jewels in safe custody; the care of a prudent man (S.151), and no lien on them.

4

Lessor (locker)

A locker is rented: the bank does not know the contents. Under RBI locker rules (2022), its liability for loss due to fire, theft, building collapse or fraud by its employees is up to 100 times the annual rent.

🔢 Number trick

A locker is rented; the bank doesn't know what is inside. Loss through its own fault: liability up to 100 times the annual rent.

5

Fiduciary

The relationship rests on trust and good faith: secrecy, honest advice, no misuse of confidential information. Where a customer relies on the bank's advice, the bank must not take unfair advantage (Lloyds Bank v. Bundy, 1975: an elderly farmer's guarantee and mortgage given on the bank manager's advice were set aside).

🎨 Picture it

The friend hat: honest advice, no unfair advantage.

🗣️ Jargon buster

Lloyds Bank v. Bundy (1975): an elderly farmer's guarantee was set aside because he relied on the bank.

6

Guarantor

The bank issues bank guarantees and letters of credit on the customer's behalf; the customer counter-indemnifies it.

🎨 Picture it

The guarantor hat: bank guarantees and letters of credit, backed by the customer's counter-indemnity.

7

Rights of banker

S.171

General lien (S.171 Contract Act); set-off; appropriation of payments; to charge interest, commission and service charges; to close an account by reasonable notice.

🧠 Memory trick

Lien · Set-off · Appropriation · Charges · Close with notice.

8

Duties of banker

S.31

To honour cheques (S.31 NI Act); to keep secrecy; to collect cheques with diligence; to follow the customer's mandate and instructions; to keep proper accounts; to give notice before closing an account; KYC and anti-money-laundering duties; fair dealing (RBI codes).

🧠 Memory trick

Honour cheques · Secrecy · Collect · Follow the mandate · Accounts · Notice · KYC · Fair dealing.

🔁 Recall: Imagine the banker changing hats through a day: trustee for a specific purpose, agent for collection, bailee for jewels, landlord for a locker, friend for advice, guarantor for trade.

4Secrecy of accounts

Exam question: Secrecy of customer accounts: the duty and its exceptions

🧠 Say it: “The Cat Did Bite Cousin Priya”

  1. TTheThe duty
  2. CCatCompulsion of law
  3. DDidDuty to the public
  4. BBiteBank's own interest
  5. CCousinConsent
  6. PPriyaPrivacy today
1

The duty

S.44S.13

A banker must not disclose the state of a customer's account or his dealings. It is a legal duty arising from the contract, continuing even after the account is closed Tournier. Statutes add oaths of secrecy for public sector banks (SBI Act S.44; Banking Companies (Acquisition) Act, 1970, S.13). Breach makes the bank liable for damages.

🎨 Picture it

Secrecy is a locked diary, and it stays locked even after the account is closed.

🎬 Case link

Tournier: a legal duty of secrecy, with four exceptions.

2

Compulsion of law

S.94S.131S.133(6)

Court orders and summons (BNSS S.94; CPC); certified copies under the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891; income-tax (S.131, S.133(6) IT Act); PMLA reporting; RBI returns; credit information companies.

🌍 Real life

A court summons, a certified copy under the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, an income-tax notice under S.133(6): the law opens the diary.

3

Duty to the public

Where there is a danger to the State or public (e.g. a customer financing the enemy or terror), the bank may disclose.

🌍 Real life

A customer financing the enemy or terror: the bank may disclose to protect the public.

4

Bank's own interest

E.g. when the bank sues the customer for an overdraft, or a guarantor must be told the amount due.

🌍 Real life

The bank sues the customer for an overdraft, or tells a guarantor how much is due.

5

Consent

Express (customer asks the bank to give a reference) or implied (bankers' opinions on creditworthiness in general terms).

🧠 Memory trick

Express consent (a reference he asked for) or implied (general opinions on creditworthiness).

6

Privacy today

The Supreme Court has held that a customer's records in a bank are part of his right to privacy; a law letting officials inspect bank records without safeguards is invalid Canara Bank (privacy). Privacy is now a fundamental right (K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India, 2017), and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 adds data duties.

🎬 Case link

Canara Bank (privacy): bank records are part of the customer's privacy; inspection without safeguards was struck down.

🔁 Recall: Secrecy is a locked diary with four keys: the law, the public, the bank itself, and the customer's own permission.

6Case stories

Foley v. Hill

(1848) 2 HLC 28 · 9 ER 1002 · House of Lords

📖 What happenedA customer deposited money with bankers, was never paid the agreed interest, and years later sued in equity for an account, claiming the bankers were his trustees.
❓ The questionIs a banker a trustee or agent of the money deposited by a customer?
⚖️ HeldThe bankers were his debtors, not trustees or agents: money deposited becomes the bank's money. His remedy was an ordinary claim for debt.

🪝 Deposit = loan to the bank · Full story →

Tournier v. National Provincial and Union Bank of England

[1924] 1 KB 461 · Court of Appeal

📖 What happenedA bank manager telephoned a customer's employers and revealed that he was overdrawn and was dealing with bookmakers; he lost his job.
❓ The questionDoes a banker owe a legal duty to keep a customer's affairs secret, and when may he disclose them?
⚖️ HeldA banker has a legal duty of secrecy, subject to four exceptions: compulsion of law, duty to the public, the bank's own interest, and the customer's consent.

🪝 Four keys to the locked diary · Full story →

District Registrar and Collector, Hyderabad v. Canara Bank

(2005) 1 SCC 496 · decided 1 Nov 2004

📖 What happenedA State law let any person authorised by the Collector enter a bank and inspect and impound customers' documents to check stamp duty.
❓ The questionCan officials be empowered to inspect and seize customers' documents held by a bank without safeguards?
⚖️ HeldThe provision was struck down: customers' records with a bank are part of their privacy, and the power had no safeguards.

🪝 Bank records are private · Full story →

7Golden rules

  • ★ 1A deposit is a loan to the bank; the money becomes the bank's, and it need not account for how it uses it. Foley v. Hill
  • ★ 2The bank's debt is unusual: it is payable only on demand by the customer, at the branch.
  • ★ 3Secrecy is a legal duty with four exceptions: law, public duty, bank's interest, customer's consent. Tournier
  • ★ 4Customers' bank records are part of their privacy; officials cannot rummage through them without safeguards. Canara Bank (privacy)
  • ★ 5A customer is a person with an account; how long or how often he deals does not matter.

8Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. Mr P, a customer of Banban Bank, demands that the bank show how his deposit is being used. He says he is the principal and the bank his agent. Can he compel it?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs a bank the agent or trustee of its depositor?
  2. 📜 LawThe relationship is debtor and creditor: deposited money becomes the bank's money; it need not account for its use Foley v. Hill. The bank is an agent only for specific services such as collection.
  3. 🔍 ApplyP made an ordinary deposit, not a deposit for a specific purpose. He is a creditor, entitled to repayment on demand with interest.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo. The bank is not his agent for the deposit and need not show how the money is used; P can only demand repayment. (Public information about the bank comes through its published accounts and RBI disclosure rules.)

2. An income-tax officer writes directly to a bank asking for details of a customer's account. Advise the bank.

  1. 🎯 IssueCan the bank disclose without breaching secrecy?
  2. 📜 LawException of compulsion of law Tournier. S.133(6) IT Act: the assessing officer (and certain higher authorities) may require any person, including a banking company, to furnish information relevant to an enquiry or proceeding. S.131: powers of a civil court to summon documents.
  3. 🔍 ApplyIf the letter is a written requisition under the proper section, by an authorised officer, the bank must comply; disclosure is then not a breach. A mere informal request is not enough.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe bank should verify that the request is a lawful written notice under the IT Act from an authorised officer, then disclose only the information asked for. Without such authority, it must refuse.

3. X and Y have a joint savings account. Y informs the bank that X has died, produces the death certificate and asks for the whole balance. What would you do as banker?

  1. 🎯 IssueWho is entitled to the balance on the death of one joint holder?
  2. 📜 LawThe account mandate decides. "Either or survivor" / "former or survivor": the survivor is paid. Without a survivorship clause: the survivor and the legal heirs of the deceased jointly. A nominee under S.45ZA comes in only after all joint holders die. Payment to the survivor or nominee discharges the bank but does not decide ownership among the heirs (Ram Chander Talwar v. Devender Kumar Talwar, 2010).
  3. 🔍 ApplyVerify the death certificate and the mandate. If it is "either or survivor", pay Y. If the mandate is "jointly" with no survivorship, pay Y together with X's legal heirs (or as per RBI's simplified claim procedure).
  4. ✅ ConclusionWith a survivorship mandate, pay the balance to Y; otherwise pay Y and X's legal heirs jointly. Either way, the bank should record the death and stop operations until the claim is settled.

4. X and Y hire a locker jointly, to be operated jointly. They have nominated S and W. X dies. Advise the bank.

  1. 🎯 IssueWho gets access to a jointly hired locker on the death of one hirer?
  2. 📜 LawS.45ZE(2) BR Act: where a locker is hired jointly and the hirers have made a nomination, on the death of a hirer the bank may give access and allow removal of contents to the nominee(s) jointly with the surviving hirer(s). S.45ZE(4): an inventory is prepared and signed; S.45ZE(5): the bank's liability then ends.
  3. 🔍 ApplyX has died. The bank should give access to Y together with the nominee(s), after verifying the death certificate and identities.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe bank should allow Y and the nominee(s) jointly to open the locker, prepare and get signed an inventory of the contents, and hand them over; it is then discharged. Without a nomination, Y and X's legal heirs would have to act jointly.
