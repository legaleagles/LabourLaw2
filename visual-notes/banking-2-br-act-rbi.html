🧠Mind map

1The big picture

A bank is a shop that deals in trust. People leave their savings with it because they trust they can take them back whenever they want; the bank lends that money to farmers, traders and home buyers. If even one big bank fails, panic spreads like fire. So the law places a strict referee over every bank: the Reserve Bank of India, armed with the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

India's story moves from the village sahukar and hundi, to the British-era Presidency Banks, to the Imperial Bank and the State Bank of India, to the nationalisation of 1969 and 1980, and now to UPI on every phone. At each step, the aim was the same: safe deposits, credit for all, and public confidence.

1

Meaning of banking

S.5(b)

"Banking" means accepting, for the purpose of lending or investment, deposits of money from the public, repayable on demand or otherwise, and withdrawable by cheque, draft, order or otherwise. Two tests: deposits from the public, and use of them for lending or investment.

🎨 Picture it

A bank is a shop that deals in trust.

🧠 Memory trick

Two tests: deposits from the public + used for lending or investment (S.5(b)).

2

Banker

S.3S.5(c)

S.3 NI Act: "banker" includes any person acting as a banker and any post office savings bank. A banking company is a company that transacts the business of banking in India (S.5(c) BR Act). Courts look at the real business, not the label.

🗣️ Jargon buster

Even a post office savings bank is a "banker" (S.3 NI Act).

🧠 Memory trick

Courts look at the real business, not the label.

3

Indigenous banking

Lending at interest is described in the Manusmriti and Kautilya's Arthashastra. Indigenous bankers (shroffs, sahukars, chettiars, multanis) accepted deposits and used hundis to send money across the country.

📖 Word origin

Sahukars, shroffs, chettiars, multanis: India's indigenous bankers, sending money across the country by hundi.

4

Presidency and Imperial

Bank of Hindustan (1770) was among the first European-style banks. The three Presidency Banks (Bengal 1806, Bombay 1840, Madras 1843) merged into the Imperial Bank of India in 1921. Indian-owned banks grew with the Swadeshi movement (e.g. Punjab National Bank, Bank of India, Canara Bank).

🔢 Number trick

Bengal 1806 · Bombay 1840 · Madras 1843 → merged into the Imperial Bank in 1921.

5

RBI and SBI

The RBI began work on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act, 1934, and was nationalised in 1949. The Banking Companies Act, 1949 (renamed the Banking Regulation Act in 1966) gave RBI control over banks. The Imperial Bank became the State Bank of India in 1955.

🔢 Number trick

1935 RBI starts work · 1949 RBI nationalised and the BR Act passed · 1955 Imperial Bank becomes SBI.

6

Nationalisation

On 19 July 1969, 14 major banks were nationalised to take credit to agriculture, small industry and villages; the first law was struck down R.C. Cooper and re-enacted in 1970. Six more banks followed on 15 April 1980. Regional Rural Banks (1975) and NABARD (1982) took credit to the countryside.

🔢 Number trick

14 banks on 19 July 1969; 6 more on 15 April 1980.

🎬 Case link

R.C. Cooper: the 1969 law fell (unfair compensation; banks barred from banking); a new Act followed in 1970.

7

Reforms and today

After 1991, the Narasimham Committees led to new private banks, prudential norms and recovery laws (DRT 1993, SARFAESI 2002). Payments banks and small finance banks came in 2015; public sector banks were merged (2019–2020). Technology (core banking, RTGS, NEFT, UPI) made banking available on the phone.

Real-world example: under the Jan Dhan scheme (2014), crores of zero-balance accounts were opened, so that subsidies now reach people directly through their bank accounts. This is "social banking" at work.
🔢 Number trick

1991 reforms · DRT 1993 · SARFAESI 2002 · payments and small finance banks 2015 · mergers 2019–20.

🌍 Real life

Jan Dhan (2014): crores of zero-balance accounts, so subsidies reach people directly. That is social banking.

8

Importance

Banks mobilise savings, create credit, finance trade and industry, help monetary policy reach the economy, enable payments, and support financial inclusion and government schemes.

🧠 Memory trick

Save · Credit · Finance · Policy · Payments · Inclusion: six things banks do for the economy.

🔁 Recall: Walk through time: sahukar's hundi → Presidency Banks → Imperial Bank → RBI 1935 → SBI 1955 → nationalisation 1969/1980 → UPI. Say one line per stop.

3Banking Regulation Act

1

Object and scope

S.56

A complete code to regulate banking companies, protect depositors and keep the banking system sound. It came into force in March 1949 as the Banking Companies Act, was renamed in 1966 and, through S.56, applies (with changes) to co-operative banks; the 2020 amendment brought them under closer RBI supervision.

🔢 Number trick

1949 Banking Companies Act → renamed 1966 → applies to co-operative banks through S.56.

2

Definitions

S.5(b)S.5(c)S.6

Banking (S.5(b)) and banking company (S.5(c)). Forms of business a bank may carry on (S.6): lending, discounting bills, guarantees, letters of credit, foreign exchange, safe deposit lockers, agency business and the like.

🧠 Memory trick

S.6 is the menu: lending, discounting bills, guarantees, letters of credit, foreign exchange, lockers, agency work.

3

Licensing and branches

S.22S.23S.7

No company can carry on banking without an RBI licence (S.22); RBI may cancel it. New places of business need RBI permission (S.23). Only banking companies may use the words "bank", "banker" or "banking" in their name (S.7).

🧠 Memory trick

No licence, no bank (S.22). No "bank" in the name unless it is one (S.7).

🌍 Real life

"Sunrise Bank Finance" takes deposits from villagers without a licence: it breaks S.7 and S.22.

4

Business and prohibitions

S.8S.9S.10S.10AS.20

A bank shall not trade in goods (S.8), except to realise a security; must dispose of immovable property not needed for its own use within seven years (S.9); cannot employ managing agents (S.10); at least a majority of directors must have special knowledge (S.10A); loans to its own directors are restricted (S.20).

🧠 Memory trick

No trading (S.8) · no surplus property beyond 7 years (S.9) · no managing agents (S.10).

🌍 Real life

A bank's board wants to buy and sell rice and pulses: S.8 forbids it, except to realise a security.

5

Capital and reserves

S.11S.12S.14S.15S.17

Minimum paid-up capital and reserves (S.11); rules on share capital and a cap on the voting rights of any single shareholder (S.12); no charge on unpaid capital (S.14); dividends only after capitalised expenses are written off (S.15); 20% of profits to the reserve fund before dividend (S.17).

🔢 Number trick

20% of profits to the reserve fund before any dividend (S.17).

6

Liquidity and advances

S.18S.42S.24S.21

Cash reserve for non-scheduled banks (S.18; scheduled banks under S.42 RBI Act); Statutory Liquidity Ratio in cash, gold or approved securities up to 40% of liabilities as RBI fixes (S.24); RBI may control advances: purpose, margins, interest (S.21).

📖 Word origin

SLR = Statutory Liquidity Ratio: cash, gold or approved securities, up to 40% of liabilities (S.24).

7

RBI control

S.35S.35AS.36AS.29S.46

Inspection (S.35); binding directions in the public interest or depositors' interest (S.35A); directions to start insolvency proceedings against defaulters (S.35AA); removal of managerial staff (S.36AA); supersession of the board (S.36ACA); accounts and balance sheet (S.29) and penalties (S.46, S.47A).

🧠 Memory trick

Inspect (S.35) · Direct (S.35A) · Remove managers (S.36AA) · Supersede the board (S.36ACA).

8

Failure and depositors

S.45S.45ZS.7S.22

RBI may seek a moratorium and a scheme of reconstruction or amalgamation (S.45), as in the rescue of Yes Bank (2020). Depositors may nominate up to four nominees for deposits, safe-custody articles and lockers (S.45ZA, S.45ZC, S.45ZE, as amended in 2025).

Example: a chit-fund style company called "Sunrise Bank Finance" takes deposits from villagers. It breaks S.7 (use of "bank") and S.22 (no licence); RBI can act and the promoters face penalties.
🌍 Real life

Yes Bank (2020): rescued through a moratorium and a reconstruction scheme (S.45).

🔢 Number trick

Up to 4 nominees for deposits, articles and lockers (2025 amendment).

🔁 Recall: Follow the life of a bank: born (licence), grows (capital), behaves (prohibitions, SLR, reserve), is watched (inspection, directions) and, if sick, is treated (moratorium, merger).

1

Commercial banks

S.42(6)

Accept deposits and give short and medium-term loans for profit. Kinds: public sector (SBI, nationalised banks), private sector, foreign banks and Regional Rural Banks. Scheduled banks are those in the Second Schedule to the RBI Act (S.42(6): adequate capital, affairs not harmful to depositors); they get RBI refinance and clearing facilities and keep CRR with RBI.

🧠 Memory trick

Public · Private · Foreign · RRBs.

🗣️ Jargon buster

A scheduled bank is one listed in the Second Schedule to the RBI Act.

2

Co-operative banks

S.56

Owned by members, working on "one member, one vote": urban co-operative banks, state and district central co-operative banks, and primary agricultural credit societies. Registered under co-operative laws but regulated as banks by RBI (S.56 BR Act).

🧠 Memory trick

One member, one vote. Registered under co-operative laws, regulated as banks by the RBI (S.56).

3

Development banks and NABARD

They give long-term finance for a sector: NABARD (set up 12 July 1982 under the NABARD Act, 1981) is the apex bank for agriculture and rural development: it refinances co-operative banks and RRBs, manages the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund and supervises RRBs and co-operative banks. Others: SIDBI (small industry), EXIM Bank (trade), NHB (housing).

🔢 Number trick

NABARD: set up 12 July 1982, the apex bank for agriculture and rural development.

🎨 Picture it

The bank that builds the farm road.

4

New banks

Payments banks take small deposits but cannot lend; small finance banks serve small borrowers.

🧠 Memory trick

Payments banks take deposits but cannot lend; small finance banks serve small borrowers.

5

Types of banking

Retail (individuals) and wholesale/corporate; branch v. unit banking; mixed banking (deposit banking plus industrial finance); universal banking (banking, insurance, investment under one group); investment/merchant banking; social banking (priority sector lending, financial inclusion). Together they serve India's different needs: farmers, MSMEs, large industry, and the unbanked poor.

📖 Word origin

Universal banking = banking, insurance and investment under one group. Social banking = priority-sector lending and inclusion.

6

Technology

S.6

Core banking (any-branch banking), ATMs, internet and mobile banking, RTGS and NEFT for fund transfers, cheque truncation, and UPI (2016) for instant payments. Security: two-factor authentication, RBI rules limiting customer liability for unauthorised electronic transactions, and the IT Act, 2000. The NI Act recognises a cheque in electronic form and the image of a truncated cheque (S.6).

Real-world example: a vegetable seller in a small town accepts payment by scanning a UPI QR code; the money moves from the buyer's bank to hers in seconds, without cash or cheques.
🌍 Real life

A vegetable seller takes payment by UPI QR code: money moves bank to bank in seconds.

🧠 Memory trick

Core · ATM · Net · Mobile · RTGS · NEFT · CTS · UPI.

🔁 Recall: Picture a banking street: big commercial banks in front, a co-operative bank for members, NABARD for the farm road, a small payments-bank kiosk, and everyone paying by UPI.

1

Status

S.22S.20S.21

The RBI is India's central bank, set up under the RBI Act, 1934, working since 1 April 1935, owned by the Government since 1949.

(1) Issue of notes: sole right to issue bank notes (S.22 RBI Act). (2) Banker to the Government (S.20, S.21). (3) Bankers' bank and lender of last resort: holds banks' cash reserves, lends to them in a crisis. (4) Monetary policy: an inflation target set by the Government, rates fixed by the Monetary Policy Committee. (5) Regulator and supervisor of banks and NBFCs (BR Act, RBI Act). (6) Manager of foreign exchange (FEMA, 1999) and payment systems (Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007).

🔢 Number trick

Act of 1934 · working from 1 April 1935 · Government-owned since 1949.

2

Issue of notes

The RBI has the sole right to issue bank notes (S.22 RBI Act).

🎨 Picture it

The note-printer.

3

Government's banker

The RBI is banker to the Government (S.20, S.21 RBI Act).

🎨 Picture it

The Government's banker.

4

Bankers' bank

The RBI is the bankers' bank and lender of last resort: it holds banks' cash reserves and lends to them in a crisis.

🎨 Picture it

The banks' banker: the lender of last resort in a crisis.

5

Monetary policy

Monetary policy: the Government sets an inflation target and the Monetary Policy Committee fixes the rates.

🎨 Picture it

The inflation guard.

6

Regulator

The RBI regulates and supervises banks and NBFCs (BR Act, RBI Act), and manages foreign exchange (FEMA, 1999) and payment systems (Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007).

🎨 Picture it

The referee of banks and NBFCs, and the keeper of forex and payments.

7

Development

Priority-sector lending to agriculture and small industry; setting up development institutions (IDBI, NABARD); financial inclusion; stable prices and a stable rupee, which encourage investment; and modern payment systems.

🧠 Memory trick

Priority sector · IDBI and NABARD · inclusion · stable prices · payments.

8

Customer protection

S.35A

(1) RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 (in force 1 July 2026; it replaced the 2021 Scheme, which had merged the Banking Ombudsman Scheme of 1995/2002/2006): complain first to the bank, which has 30 days to reply; then to the RBI Ombudsman within 90 days; the Ombudsman can award compensation for loss up to ₹30 lakh, plus up to ₹3 lakh for harassment; appeal lies within RBI. (2) Deposit insurance up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (DICGC). (3) Zero liability for a customer who reports an unauthorised electronic transaction within three working days (RBI, 2017). (4) KYC rules, fair practice codes, the Charter of Customer Rights, and controls on recovery agents.

The office was first created by RBI under S.35A BR Act through the Banking Ombudsman Scheme, 1995, revised in 2002 and 2006: a senior RBI officer for each area, receiving complaints of deficiency in banking service (delay in collecting cheques, refusal of deposits, charges without notice and the like), settling them by conciliation or passing an award. The courts too have pushed transparency: RBI must disclose inspection reports under the RTI Act, because it does not hold them in a fiduciary capacity (RBI v. Jayantilal N. Mistry, 2016).

Case story IAMAI v. RBI: RBI barred all banks from serving crypto-currency businesses. The Supreme Court accepted RBI's power to regulate, but set the circular aside as disproportionate, since no harm to banks had been shown and milder measures were not considered.
🔢 Number trick

Bank replies in 30 days → Ombudsman within 90 days → award up to ₹30 lakh (+ ₹3 lakh harassment). Deposits insured to ₹5 lakh. Report fraud within 3 working days for zero liability.

🎬 Case link

IAMAI v. RBI: the RBI may regulate crypto, but a total banking ban was disproportionate.

🔁 Recall: Count RBI's six hats on one hand plus one: note-printer, Government's banker, banks' banker, inflation guard, referee, forex keeper, and the customer's friend (Ombudsman).

8Golden rules

  • ★ 1Banking = accepting public deposits for lending or investment, repayable on demand or otherwise, withdrawable by cheque, draft or order (S.5(b)).
  • ★ 2No company can use the word "bank" in its name unless it is a banking company (S.7), and none can do banking without an RBI licence (S.22).
  • ★ 3A bank cannot trade in goods, except to realise its security (S.8).
  • ★ 4Nationalisation must still respect fundamental rights: the 1969 Act fell, the 1970 Act replaced it. R.C. Cooper
  • ★ 5RBI can regulate new technology, but its measures must be proportionate. IAMAI v. RBI

9Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. "Sunrise Bank Finance Pvt. Ltd." collects deposits from villagers promising high interest. It has no RBI licence. Decide its legality.

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a company without a licence use the word "bank" and accept public deposits?
  2. 📜 LawBR Act S.7: only a banking company may use "bank", "banker" or "banking" in its name. S.22: no company shall carry on banking business in India without an RBI licence. S.46: penalties. The Banning of Unregulated Deposit Schemes Act, 2019 also punishes unregulated deposit schemes.
  3. 🔍 ApplyAccepting public deposits for lending is "banking" under S.5(b). The company has no licence and misuses the word "bank".
  4. ✅ ConclusionIts business is illegal; RBI and the police can act, the directors face penalties, and depositors can claim their money back.

2. A bank's board decides to buy and sell rice and pulses to increase profits. Advise.

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a banking company trade in goods?
  2. 📜 LawBR Act S.8: no banking company shall directly or indirectly deal in the buying, selling or bartering of goods, except in connection with the realisation of a security given to it, or in bills of exchange received for collection or negotiation.
  3. 🔍 ApplyTrading in rice is ordinary trade, not realisation of a security.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe decision is illegal under S.8. Only if the bank sells goods pledged by a defaulting borrower (realisation of security) is it permitted.

3. ₹40,000 is debited from a customer's account through an unauthorised card transaction. He reports it to the bank the next day. The bank refuses a refund. Advise.

  1. 🎯 IssueWho bears the loss of an unauthorised electronic transaction and what is the remedy?
  2. 📜 LawRBI circular on limiting customer liability in unauthorised electronic banking transactions (2017): zero liability where the fraud is not the customer's fault and he reports within three working days. RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026.
  3. 🔍 ApplyHe reported within one day and did not share his PIN or OTP. The bank must credit (shadow-reverse) the amount within ten working days.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe bank must refund the ₹40,000. If it refuses, he can complain to the bank, and after 30 days, to the RBI Ombudsman, who can award the loss plus compensation for harassment.
