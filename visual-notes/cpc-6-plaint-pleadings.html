📝Plaint and Pleadings

A suit is a story told to the court. Tell facts, not law; material facts, not evidence; briefly. Tell the whole story once, and fix honest mistakes early.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

A suit is a story told to the court, and the plaint is its first chapter. The court will judge only the story that is written down, so the rules of pleading are the rules of good story-telling: tell facts, not law; tell material facts, not the evidence; tell them briefly, in numbered paragraphs.

Write the whole story once (O.II r.2): if Ravi sues Somu only for rent and forgets the damages, he cannot come back later for the damages. And if he made a genuine mistake in the story, the court lets him amend it, as long as he does not turn it into a different story or play tricks after the trial has begun.

2Plaint: return, rejection

Exam question: Plaint: S.26, essential contents (O.VII r.1–8) · Return (r.10) and Rejection (r.11) of plaint

1

Meaning and S.26

S.26S.26(2)

A plaint is the written statement of the plaintiff's claim, with which a suit begins. S.26: "Every suit shall be instituted by the presentation of a plaint or in such other manner as may be prescribed", and the facts in it must be verified by affidavit (S.26(2), added 2002). The date of presentation is the date of institution, which matters for limitation.

🎨 Picture it

The plaint is the first chapter of the story; the court judges only what is written.

🔢 Number trick

Since 2002, the facts in a plaint are verified by affidavit (S.26(2)).

2

Contents (r.1)

O.VII r.1

(a) name of the court; (b) name, description and residence of the plaintiff; (c) the same for the defendant, as far as known; (d) if either is a minor or of unsound mind, a statement to that effect; (e) facts constituting the cause of action and when it arose; (f) facts showing the court has jurisdiction; (g) the relief claimed; (h) any set-off or relinquishment allowed by the plaintiff; (i) a statement of the value of the subject matter for jurisdiction and court fee.

🧠 Memory trick

Nine particulars in order: Court · Plaintiff · Defendant · Minor or unsound mind · Cause of action · Jurisdiction facts · Relief · Set-off · Value.

3

Further rules

r.2: money suits state the precise amount. r.3: immovable property described sufficiently to identify it (boundaries, survey numbers). r.4: representative capacity shown. r.6: if time-barred on its face, the ground of exemption from limitation must be shown. r.7: relief stated specifically; general relief need not be asked. r.8: reliefs on separate grounds stated separately. r.14: documents relied on are listed and produced.

🌍 Real life

Land must be described so it can be identified: boundaries and survey numbers (r.3).

🧠 Memory trick

Time-barred on its face? Show the ground of exemption in the plaint (r.6).

4

Return (r.10)

At any stage, the plaint is returned to be presented in the court in which it should have been filed, when the court lacks territorial, pecuniary or subject-matter jurisdiction. The judge endorses on it the dates of presentation and return, the party and the reasons. r.10A: the court may fix a date for appearance in the proper court.

🧠 Memory trick

Right plaint, wrong court → Return.

🎨 Picture it

A rerouted parcel: the same plaint goes to the proper court.

5

Rejection (r.11)

O.VII r.11

(a) it does not disclose a cause of action; (b) the relief is undervalued and not corrected within the time given; (c) insufficiently stamped and not made good; (d) the suit appears from the plaint to be barred by any law (e.g. limitation, res judicata); (e) not filed in duplicate; (f) r.9 not complied with. Only the plaint (and documents filed with it) is read, not the defence. r.13: rejection does not bar a fresh plaint on the same cause.

Case story Arivandandam: a tenant who had lost an eviction case at every level got his minor son to file a suit calling the eviction "fraud", then a second near-copy suit before another judge, to stall eviction. Krishna Iyer J. said: if a plaint is manifestly vexatious and meritless, "nip it in the bud at the first hearing"; clever drafting that creates an illusion of a cause of action must be exposed, using O.VII r.11.
🧠 Memory trick

Six grounds, "Cats Under Sofas Bite Dogs Daily": no Cause of action · Undervalued · insufficient Stamp · Barred by law · not in Duplicate · Default with r.9.

🎬 Case link

Arivandandam: if a plaint is meaningless or vexatious, nip it in the bud at the first hearing.

6

Return v. rejection

S.14O.XLIII r.1S.2(2)S.96

A good plaint states a clear cause of action, the court's jurisdiction, the value and the relief; a bad one is returned or rejected at the threshold.

🎨 Picture it

Return = a parcel rerouted; rejection = a parcel sent back as undeliverable. A rejection is a decree; a fresh plaint is still allowed (r.13).

🔁 Recall: Arivandandam: nip a vexatious plaint in the bud.

3Pleadings and amendment

Exam question: Pleadings: meaning, fundamental rules (O.VI r.1–16) · Amendment of pleadings (O.VI r.17–18)

  1. Meaning
  2. Fundamental rule (r.2)
  3. Other rules
  4. Amendment (r.17)
  5. Principles
1

Meaning

"Pleading" means a plaint or a written statement. Object: to tell each side exactly what the other will prove, narrow the dispute to definite issues, and prevent surprise at the trial.

🎨 Picture it

Pleadings are each side's map of the battle: no surprises at the trial.

2

Fundamental rule (r.2)

Every pleading states (1) facts, not law; (2) material facts only, (3) not the evidence by which they will be proved; (4) concisely; in paragraphs numbered consecutively, with dates, sums and numbers in figures and words.

🧠 Memory trick

Facts, not law · material facts, not evidence · brief · numbered paragraphs.

3

Other rules

S.10

r.4: full particulars must be given of misrepresentation, fraud, breach of trust, wilful default or undue influence. r.6: performance of a condition precedent is implied. r.7: no new ground inconsistent with earlier pleadings, except by amendment. r.13: presumptions of law need not be pleaded. r.14: signed by the party and his pleader; r.15: verified (true to knowledge / information and belief). r.16: the court may strike out unnecessary, scandalous, frivolous or vexatious matter, or matter that abuses the process of the court.

Example: "The defendant, by fraud, got the plaintiff's signature" is not enough: r.4 needs particulars: when, where, what was said, what was false. "Under S.10 of the Contract Act the agreement is valid" is law, not fact: leave it out.
🧠 Memory trick

Fraud needs particulars: when, where, what was said, what was false (r.4).

🗣️ Jargon buster

No new ground inconsistent with earlier pleadings, except by amendment (r.7).

4

Amendment (r.17)

The court may at any stage allow either party to alter or amend his pleading as may be just, and all amendments necessary to decide the real questions in controversy shall be made. Proviso (2002): no amendment after the trial has commenced, unless the court concludes that, despite due diligence, the party could not have raised it before.

🧠 Memory trick

Amend freely before trial; after it begins, only with due diligence (2002 proviso).

🔢 Number trick

14 days to carry out the allowed amendment if no time is fixed (r.18).

5

Principles

(Revajeetu Builders, 2009): allowed if (1) necessary to decide the real controversy; (2) bona fide; (3) the other side can be compensated by costs; (4) refusing it would cause injustice or multiple suits. Refused if it (5) changes the fundamental nature of the case, or (6) introduces a claim already time-barred on the date of the application, taking away a right that has accrued to the other side. Amendments to a written statement are viewed more liberally. r.18: if the party does not amend within the time fixed (or 14 days), he cannot do so later without leave.

Pleadings are the foundation of the suit; amendment repairs the foundation, but cannot replace the building.

🧠 Memory trick

Allowed if necessary, bona fide, compensable by costs, and refusing it would cause injustice. Refused if it changes the nature of the case.

🔁 Recall: Amend freely before trial; after trial begins, only if you could not have raised it earlier.

4Cause of action, O.II, S.89

Exam question: Cause of action · Frame of suit (O.II) · Settlement of disputes outside court (S.89)

  1. Cause of action
  2. O.II r.2
  3. Other rules of O.II
  4. S.89 ADR
  5. How it works
1

Cause of action

S.20(c)

Not defined in the CPC. It is the bundle of facts which the plaintiff must prove, if traversed, to get a judgment. It includes the plaintiff's right and its infringement, but not the defence or the evidence. It decides jurisdiction (S.20(c)), limitation and the frame of the suit.

🎨 Picture it

A bundle of sticks: the facts the plaintiff must prove, his right plus its infringement. Not the defence, not the evidence.

2

O.II r.2

O.II r.2

(1) A suit must include the whole claim arising from the cause of action. (2) If the plaintiff omits or relinquishes part of the claim, he cannot sue for it later. (3) If he omits one of several reliefs without the court's leave, he cannot sue for it later. Object: no one should be vexed twice for the same cause.

Case story Gurbux Singh: Bhooralal first sued Gurbux Singh only for mesne profits; later he sued for possession. Gurbux pleaded O.II r.2. The Supreme Court rejected the plea because he had not filed the earlier plaint to prove that both suits rested on the same cause of action; the bar is technical and must be strictly proved.
🧠 Memory trick

Whole claim once.

🌍 Real life

Ravi sues Somu only for rent and forgets the damages: he cannot come back later for the damages.

🎬 Case link

Gurbux Singh: the bar works only if the defendant produces the earlier plaint to prove the same cause of action.

3

Other rules of O.II

O.II

r.3: several causes of action against the same defendant may be joined in one suit. r.4: in a suit for recovery of immovable property, only claims for mesne profits, arrears of rent, damages for breach of the contract under which the property was held, and claims for the same relief may be joined, unless the court allows others. r.6: the court may order separate trials if joinder embarrasses the trial. r.7: objections to misjoinder of causes of action must be taken early.

🧠 Memory trick

Several causes against the same defendant may be joined (r.3). With possession, only listed claims may be joined (r.4): mesne profits, rent arrears, damages under the tenancy.

4

S.89 ADR

S.89O.X r.1

Where it appears to the court that elements of a settlement acceptable to the parties exist, it refers the dispute to one of four routes: arbitration, conciliation, judicial settlement including Lok Adalat, or mediation. O.X r.1A–1C: after admissions and denials are recorded, the court directs the parties to opt for one; if the effort fails, they return to court.

🧠 Memory trick

A-C-L-M: Arbitration · Conciliation · Lok Adalat or judicial settlement · Mediation.

5

How it works

S.21

In Afcons Infrastructure v. Cherian Varkey Construction (2010), the Supreme Court said the court need only summarise the disputes, not draft settlement terms; arbitration and conciliation need the consent of both parties, while Lok Adalat and mediation do not; some cases (e.g. fraud, criminal offences, election disputes, representative suits) are unsuitable. A Lok Adalat award is deemed a decree and final (S.21, Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987), and the court fee is refunded on settlement.

State the whole cause of action once, claim every relief once, and try settlement before trial.

🧠 Memory trick

Consent needed for arbitration and conciliation; not for Lok Adalat and mediation.

🎬 Case link

Afcons: the court only summarises the dispute; it does not draft the settlement.

🔁 Recall: Gurbux Singh: prove the first plaint, or the O.II r.2 bar fails.

7Golden rules

  • ★ 1Plead facts, not law; material facts, not evidence; concisely (O.VI r.2).
  • ★ 2Return = right plaint, wrong court (r.10). Rejection = defective plaint (r.11); a rejection is a decree.
  • ★ 3A meaningless or vexatious plaint should be nipped in the bud at the first hearing. Arivandandam
  • ★ 4O.II r.2 bars an omitted claim only if the defendant proves the same cause of action by producing the earlier plaint. Gurbux Singh
  • ★ 5Amendments are liberally allowed before trial; after trial begins, only if the party could not have raised it earlier despite due diligence.

8Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. A wants to sue B for recovery of ₹10,000 due on a promissory note. Draft a plaint.

  1. 🎯 IssueDraft a plaint containing every particular required by O.VII r.1.
  2. 📜 LawS.26 and O.VII r.1–2, r.14: court, parties, cause of action, jurisdiction, limitation, valuation, relief; verification (O.VI r.15) and affidavit (S.26(2)). Art. 35 Lim. Act: 3 years.
  3. 🔍 ApplyIN THE COURT OF THE JUNIOR CIVIL JUDGE AT ______ · O.S. No. ___ of 20__. A, s/o ___, aged __, occupation, residing at ___ … Plaintiff; versus B, s/o ___, aged __, residing at ___ … Defendant. Suit for recovery of money on a promissory note. 1. Description of parties. 2. On __ (date) B borrowed ₹10,000 from A and executed a promissory note payable on demand with interest at __% p.a. 3. A demanded repayment; legal notice dated __; B has not paid. 4. Cause of action arose on the date of the note and on the date of demand, at ___, within this court's jurisdiction. 5. Jurisdiction: B resides / cause of action arose here (S.20). 6. Limitation: within 3 years (Art. 35). 7. Valuation: ₹10,000 + interest ₹___ = ₹___; court fee paid under the State Court-fees and Suits Valuation Act. 8. Relief: (a) decree for ₹___ with future interest; (b) costs; (c) other relief. Place, date; signatures of plaintiff and advocate. Verification: "I, A, verify that paras 1–8 are true to my knowledge and belief." List of documents: original pro-note, notice, postal receipt.
  4. ✅ ConclusionA plaint with all O.VII r.1 particulars, the original pro-note filed with it, verification and an affidavit under S.26(2).

2. A filed a declaration suit, but omitted some facts and made mistakes inadvertently. What remedy under the CPC?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a party correct his pleadings during the suit?
  2. 📜 LawO.VI r.17: amendment at any stage to decide the real controversy; proviso: after trial starts, only if he could not have raised it earlier with due diligence. O.VI r.18: amend within the time fixed or 14 days.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe mistakes were bona fide and do not change the nature of the suit; the defendant can be compensated by costs. If the trial has not started, the court should allow it; if it has, A must show why he could not discover it earlier.
  4. ✅ ConclusionA should apply under O.VI r.17 to amend the plaint; it will be allowed if bona fide and necessary, especially before trial, on costs.

3. A plaintiff whose cause of action is time-barred files a condone-delay petition along with his suit. Is it maintainable?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan delay in filing a suit be condoned?
  2. 📜 LawS.5 Limitation Act applies only to appeals and applications, not suits. S.3: a time-barred suit shall be dismissed. O.VII r.11(d): plaint rejected if barred by law on its face; O.VII r.6: grounds of exemption must be shown in the plaint.
  3. 🔍 ApplyUnless the plaint shows a legal ground of exemption (disability, acknowledgment, part payment, S.14 exclusion), the court cannot extend time for a suit.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo. A condone-delay petition is not maintainable for a suit; the plaint will be rejected (O.VII r.11(d)) or the suit dismissed (S.3).

4. C was not a party to a partnership suit. In final decree proceedings he files an impleading petition claiming to own the property; it is dismissed. What is his remedy?

  1. 🎯 IssueRemedy of a stranger whose property is affected by a decree between others.
  2. 📜 LawA decree binds only the parties (and those claiming under them). O.I r.10(2): a person may be added if necessary; refusal can be challenged by revision or under Article 227. O.XXI r.97–101: a stranger resisting delivery of possession can have his claim adjudicated in execution. He may also file a separate suit for declaration of his title.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe final decree between the partners cannot bind C's independent title.
  4. ✅ ConclusionC can challenge the dismissal in revision / Art. 227, file his own suit for declaration of title, or resist delivery under O.XXI r.97–101; the decree does not bind him.
