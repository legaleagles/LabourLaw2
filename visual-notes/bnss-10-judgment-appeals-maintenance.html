🧠Mind map

1The big picture

The trial ends, but the story does not. The judgment must give reasons; a death sentence needs special reasons and the High Court's confirmation. The victim, once a mere witness, now has rights: compensation, free treatment, protection, and even an appeal of her own. And the losing side has three doors: appeal (a full re-hearing), revision (a check by a superior court for illegality), and reference (a lower court asking the High Court about the law). A criminal court has no power to review its own judgment.

Two everyday chapters complete the picture: maintenance (S.144), a quick remedy so that no wife, child or parent is left destitute, and public order (S.148–163), the powers to disperse unlawful assemblies and remove public nuisances.

2Judgment and victims

Exam question: Judgment and sentence · Death sentence · Victims of crime and compensation (S.392–399)

🧠 Say it: “Judges Decide, Victims Collect Rights”

  1. JJudgesJudgment
  2. DDecideDeath sentence
  3. VVictimsWho is a victim
  4. CCollectCompensation
  5. RRightsOther victim rights
1

Judgment

S.392

Pronounced in open court; written in the court's language; contains the points for determination, the decision and the reasons; specifies the offence and the BNS section, and the sentence. On acquittal, it states the offence and directs release. After conviction, the accused is heard on sentence.

🧠 Memory trick

Three things in every judgment: reasons · sentence · compensation.

🎨 Picture it

A judgment without reasons is like a marksheet without marks: nobody can check it.

2

Death sentence

S.407

For an offence punishable with death or life, the judgment states the reasons for the sentence, and special reasons if death is awarded. A Court of Session's death sentence is not executed until confirmed by the High Court (S.407). It is given only in the "rarest of rare" cases Bachan Singh.

Case story Bachan Singh: a man convicted of murdering three people challenged the death penalty itself. A Constitution Bench (4:1) upheld it, but said it must be imposed only in the "rarest of rare" cases, after weighing aggravating and mitigating circumstances of both the crime and the criminal, when the alternative of life imprisonment is "unquestionably foreclosed".
🧠 Memory trick

Death needs three locks: special reasons + High Court confirmation (S.407) + "rarest of rare".

🎨 Picture it

A balance sheet: aggravating factors on one side, mitigating on the other; death only if life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed.

🎬 Case link

Bachan Singh: death penalty valid, but only in the rarest of rare cases.

3

Who is a victim

S.2

A person who has suffered any loss or injury because of the act or omission of the accused, and includes his guardian or legal heir.

🗣️ Jargon buster

A victim includes his guardian or legal heir.

🌍 Real life

When a man is murdered, his widow and children are victims too, and can claim compensation.

4

Compensation

S.395S.396S.399

(1) S.395: when passing judgment, the court may order the accused to pay compensation from the fine or otherwise, for the victim's loss or injury, or to the heirs in a death case. (2) S.396: every State has a victim compensation scheme; the Legal Services Authority pays even where the offender is not traced or identified, or is acquitted, and may give interim relief such as first aid and medical help. (3) S.399: compensation to persons groundlessly arrested.

🔢 Number trick

S.395: the court orders the accused to pay. S.396: the State scheme pays, even if the offender is never traced.

🌍 Real life

A hit-and-run driver is never found: the injured person can still get compensation under the State's victim compensation scheme (S.396).

🎬 Case link

Ankush Gaikwad: every court must apply its mind to compensation in every criminal case.

5

Other victim rights

S.397S.398S.193S.360S.18

Free first aid and medical treatment by all hospitals in cases of rape and acid attack (S.397); a Witness Protection Scheme (S.398); information on the progress of investigation within 90 days (S.193); a right to be heard before withdrawal of prosecution (S.360); an advocate to assist the prosecution (S.18); and a right of appeal (S.413 proviso) Mallikarjun Kodagali.

Case story Ankush Gaikwad: in a murder case, the courts below did not consider compensation for the deceased's family. The Supreme Court held that the compensation provision casts a duty on every court to apply its mind to compensation in every case, and to give reasons if it does not award it.
🧠 Memory trick

"To Protect Innocents, Hear And Appeal" → free Treatment (S.397) · Protection of witnesses (S.398) · Information on progress (S.193) · Heard before withdrawal (S.360) · Advocate to assist (S.18) · Appeal (S.413 proviso).

🔁 Recall: Three things in every judgment: reasons, sentence, compensation. For death: special reasons + HC confirmation + rarest of rare.

3Appeal, revision, review

Exam question: Appeals, reference, revision and review in criminal cases (S.413–442, S.403)

🧠 Say it: “All Kids Need Reading, Writing, Reasoning”

  1. AAllAppeal: general rule
  2. KKidsKinds of appeals
  3. NNeedNo appeal cases
  4. RReadingReference
  5. WWritingRevision
  6. RReasoningReview
1

Appeal: general rule

S.413

No appeal lies except as provided by the BNSS or another law. Proviso: the victim may appeal against acquittal, conviction for a lesser offence, or inadequate compensation, to the court that hears appeals from convictions.

🧠 Memory trick

No appeal unless a law gives one (S.413).

🎬 Case link

Mallikarjun Kodagali: the victim can appeal against acquittal as of right, without leave.

2

Kinds of appeals

S.415S.418S.419S.421S.430

(1) From conviction (S.415): from a Magistrate to the Sessions Court; from the Sessions Court (and from a sentence above 7 years) to the High Court; from the High Court's original trial to the Supreme Court. (2) By the State against an inadequate sentence (S.418). (3) Against acquittal (S.419): by the State through the Public Prosecutor, to the High Court with leave (to the Sessions Court in certain Magistrate cases); by a complainant with special leave of the High Court in complaint cases. (4) Special right where co-accused appeal (S.421). The appellate court can suspend the sentence and grant bail (S.430).

🎨 Picture it

Appeals climb one step up: Magistrate → Sessions Court → High Court → Supreme Court.

🔢 Number trick

S.415 from conviction · S.418 State against inadequate sentence · S.419 against acquittal.

3

No appeal cases

S.416S.417

When the accused pleads guilty and is convicted on that plea (except as to extent or legality of sentence); and in petty cases where only small sentences or fines are imposed (S.417). In such cases revision is still available.

🧠 Memory trick

Guilty plea or petty fine = no appeal, but revision is still open (S.416–417).

4

Reference

S.436

A court that is doubtful about the validity of a law (on which the case turns, not yet declared invalid by the High Court or Supreme Court) must state a case and refer it to the High Court; it may also refer other questions of law.

🎨 Picture it

Reference: the lower court raises its hand and asks the High Court a question of law (S.436).

5

Revision

S.438S.438(3)S.442

The High Court or Sessions Judge may call for the record of any inferior criminal court to check the correctness, legality or propriety of any finding, sentence or order, and the regularity of proceedings. Limits: not against interlocutory orders; a person who chose one forum cannot file a second revision (S.438(3)); the High Court cannot convert an acquittal into a conviction in revision (S.442), and cannot entertain revision by a party who could have appealed but did not.

🎨 Picture it

Revision is a supervisor checking the work for illegality or impropriety, not a fresh hearing.

🗣️ Jargon buster

Not against interlocutory orders · no second revision by the same person · the High Court cannot turn an acquittal into a conviction.

6

Review

S.403S.528Art. 137

There is no power of review in criminal courts: once a judgment is signed, no court can alter or review it except to correct a clerical or arithmetical error (S.403). The High Court's inherent power (S.528) cannot be used to review either. The Supreme Court can review its own judgments under Art. 137.

Case story Mallikarjun Kodagali: a man attacked in Karnataka saw the accused (one an MLA) acquitted; the State did not appeal. The High Court said his own appeal was not maintainable. The Supreme Court held that a victim can appeal against acquittal as of right, without seeking leave, and sent the appeal back to be heard.
🎨 Picture it

Review is a locked door, except to correct a typo: a clerical or arithmetical error (S.403).

🗣️ Jargon buster

Not even the High Court's inherent power (S.528) can review. Only the Supreme Court reviews its own judgments (Art. 137).

🔁 Recall: Four doors: Appeal (re-hear the whole case), Revision (check for illegality), Reference (ask about the law), Review (door is locked, except for typos).

4Maintenance and public order

Exam question: Maintenance of wives, children and parents (S.144–147) · Public order: unlawful assembly and public nuisance (S.148–163)

🧠 Say it: “Wise Children Protect Unhappy People”

  1. WWiseWho can claim
  2. CChildrenConditions
  3. PProtectProcedure and enforcement
  4. UUnhappyUnlawful assembly
  5. PPeoplePublic nuisance and urgent orders
1

Who can claim

S.144

If a person having sufficient means neglects or refuses to maintain: (a) his wife unable to maintain herself (including a divorced wife who has not remarried); (b) his minor child, legitimate or not; (c) a major child (not a married daughter) unable to maintain itself because of physical or mental abnormality; (d) his father or mother unable to maintain themselves, a Judicial Magistrate of the first class may order a monthly allowance. There is no upper limit; interim maintenance and expenses should be decided within 60 days of notice.

🧠 Memory trick

W-C-P (Wife, Children, Parents) + means + neglect.

🌍 Real life

Aged, infirm parents thrown out of the house by their son can claim maintenance from him under S.144.

🗣️ Jargon buster

A divorced wife who has not remarried is still a "wife" for maintenance.

2

Conditions

A wife is not entitled if she lives in adultery, refuses without sufficient reason to live with her husband, or they live separately by mutual consent. But if the husband marries another woman or keeps a mistress, that is a just ground for her to live separately and still claim maintenance.

🧠 Memory trick

Three things that bar a wife's claim: adultery · refusal without reason · separation by mutual consent.

🌍 Real life

If the husband marries again or keeps a mistress, the first wife has a just ground to live separately and still claim maintenance.

3

Procedure and enforcement

S.145S.146

The case may be filed where the respondent or the wife resides; evidence is taken in his presence (or ex parte if he avoids service). For non-payment, the Magistrate issues a warrant to levy the amount and may sentence him to imprisonment of up to 1 month for each month's default, on an application within 1 year. The order can be altered on change of circumstances (S.146).

Example (and case law): Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum (1985) held that this remedy is secular and available to wives of every religion. Rajnesh v. Neha (2021) directed both parties to file affidavits disclosing their assets and income, and made maintenance payable from the date of application.
🔢 Number trick

1 month's jail for each month unpaid; apply within 1 year of the default (S.147).

🗣️ Jargon buster

Shah Bano (1985): the remedy is secular, for wives of every religion. Rajnesh v. Neha (2021): both sides file affidavits of assets and income.

4

Unlawful assembly

S.148BNS 189S.149

An assembly of five or more persons with a common object to commit an offence, resist the law, etc. (BNS 189). An Executive Magistrate or police officer may command it to disperse; if it does not, use civil force (S.148); if necessary, armed forces may be called (S.149–150). Protection from prosecution for acts done in good faith.

🔢 Number trick

5 or more persons with a common unlawful object (BNS 189).

🧠 Memory trick

Command → civil force → armed forces (S.148–150).

5

Public nuisance and urgent orders

BNS 270S.152S.163S.144

Public nuisance is defined in BNS 270. A District / Sub-Divisional / Executive Magistrate may make a conditional order to remove a nuisance (obstruction of a public way, offensive trade, dangerous building) (S.152). In urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger, he may issue an order for up to 2 months (S.163, formerly S.144 CrPC), upheld as constitutional when used for public order (Madhu Limaye v. SDM Monghyr, 1970), but it must be proportionate (Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India, 2020).

🌍 Real life

The "Section 144 orders" you read about in the news are now orders under S.163 BNSS: urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger, for up to 2 months.

🔢 Number trick

S.152: conditional order to remove a public nuisance, such as an obstruction on a public way or a dangerous building.

🔁 Recall: Maintenance = "W-C-P" (Wife, Children, Parents) + "means and neglect"; Public order = disperse (S.148) → force (S.149) → remove nuisance (S.152) → urgent order (S.163).

7Golden rules

  • ★ 1Death penalty only in the "rarest of rare" cases, when life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed. Bachan Singh
  • ★ 2Every court must consider compensation for the victim in every criminal case and record reasons. Ankush Gaikwad
  • ★ 3The victim can appeal against acquittal as of right, without leave (S.413 proviso). Mallikarjun Kodagali
  • ★ 4A criminal court cannot review its judgment after signing it, except to correct clerical or arithmetical errors (S.403).
  • ★ 5Revision does not lie against interlocutory orders, and no second revision by the same person (S.438).

8Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. Satyam committed rape and is absconding. Can monetary relief be granted to the victim?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a victim get compensation when the accused is not caught or not convicted?
  2. 📜 LawS.396: victim compensation scheme; the State / District Legal Services Authority can award compensation even where the offender is not traced or identified, and interim relief. S.397: free medical treatment. S.184: medical examination of a rape victim within 24 hours, report within 7 days. S.84–85, S.356: proclamation, attachment, trial in absentia.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe victim (or her guardian) applies to the Legal Services Authority with the FIR; she gets interim and final compensation. Meanwhile the police proclaim Satyam and attach his property. (Where the investigation was botched and the case dismissed, the victim can appeal under S.413 proviso and seek compensation; the lapse is a ground for departmental action.)
  4. ✅ ConclusionYes: compensation under S.396 even though the accused is absconding, plus free treatment under S.397; the trial can later proceed, even in absentia (S.356).

2. An in-charge Sessions Judge awarded a death sentence. Can it be executed?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a death sentence by a Sessions Court be carried out directly?
  2. 📜 LawS.407: when a Court of Session passes a death sentence, the proceedings must be submitted to the High Court, and the sentence shall not be executed unless confirmed by the High Court. The in-charge judge exercising Sessions powers is a Court of Session.
  3. 🔍 ApplyWithout the High Court's confirmation the sentence cannot be executed; the convict also has appeals to the High Court and the Supreme Court, and mercy petitions.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo; it cannot be executed until the High Court confirms it (S.407).

3. H married W2 while married to W1. W1 wants maintenance from H. Advise her.

  1. 🎯 IssueIs a wife who lives separately because of her husband's second marriage entitled to maintenance?
  2. 📜 LawS.144: wife unable to maintain herself; husband with sufficient means neglecting her. Explanation: a husband's marriage with another woman is a just ground for the wife to refuse to live with him. Bigamy is also an offence (BNS 82).
  3. 🔍 ApplyW1 is the legally wedded wife; H's second marriage justifies her living separately.
  4. ✅ ConclusionW1 can file for maintenance (and interim maintenance) under S.144 before the JMFC; she may also prosecute H for bigamy.

4. C threw his aged, infirm parents A and B out of the house. What relief can they get?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat remedies do neglected parents have?
  2. 📜 LawS.144(1)(d): father or mother unable to maintain themselves. The Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007: a Maintenance Tribunal can order monthly maintenance; abandoning a senior citizen is an offence; a transfer of property by them can be declared void if the child fails to look after them.
  3. 🔍 ApplyA and B cannot maintain themselves, and C has means.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThey can claim maintenance under S.144 before the JMFC, or before the Maintenance Tribunal under the 2007 Act, and may seek to set aside any property gift made to C.

5. X, drunk, daily harasses his wife Y and one day tries to kill her. Under which provisions can she file a case, through whom, and what punishment?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat offences arise and how is the case started?
  2. 📜 LawAttempt to murder BNS 109 (up to 10 years and fine; if hurt is caused, up to life). Cruelty by husband BNS 85. FIR under S.173 (cognizable) by Y or a relative; Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 for protection and residence orders.
  3. 🔍 ApplyY (or her parents, or anyone who knows) can lodge an FIR. Attempt to murder is triable by the Court of Session; cruelty by a Magistrate, but both may be tried together.
  4. ✅ ConclusionFIR under S.173 for attempt to murder (BNS 109) and cruelty (BNS 85); trial by the Court of Session; punishment up to 10 years (life if hurt was caused), plus protection orders under the DV Act.

6. In a petty case the accused was fined a small amount. Can he appeal?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs an appeal available in petty cases?
  2. 📜 LawS.417: no appeal in petty cases where only a small sentence or fine (as specified) is imposed. S.416: no appeal on a plea of guilty except as to the sentence. S.438: revision lies.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe sentence falls within the petty-case limits.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo appeal lies, but he may file a revision before the Sessions Judge or High Court.

7. After conviction by the Sessions Court, the accused files a review petition before the same court. Maintainable?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a criminal court review its own judgment?
  2. 📜 LawS.403: once a judgment is signed, no court shall alter or review it, except to correct a clerical or arithmetical error. S.528: inherent power cannot be used to review. Remedy: appeal (S.415) or revision.
  3. 🔍 ApplyA review on merits is not allowed; only typographical or arithmetical slips can be corrected.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNot maintainable; the accused must appeal to the High Court under S.415.

8. The accused is convicted, but the court awards very low compensation to the victim. Can the victim challenge it?

  1. 🎯 IssueDoes the victim have a remedy against inadequate compensation?
  2. 📜 LawS.413 proviso: the victim may appeal against acquittal, conviction for a lesser offence, or inadequate compensation Mallikarjun Kodagali. S.396: the victim can also seek compensation under the State scheme.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe victim files an appeal before the court to which appeals from that conviction lie (the High Court for a Sessions trial).
  4. ✅ ConclusionYes: an appeal under the proviso to S.413, and an application under the victim compensation scheme (S.396).
