🧠Mind map

1The big picture

A client sends her lawyer ₹50,000: ₹10,000 for court fees and stamp papers, ₹40,000 as his fee. A year later the case is decreed and ₹3 lakh is deposited in court for her; the lawyer withdraws it. Whose money is in his hands, and how much? If he cannot answer with a clear account, he is not just careless: he may be guilty of professional misconduct.

That is why "accountancy for lawyers" is part of the ethics paper. An advocate handles other people's money, runs an office with expenses and taxes, and constantly reads accounts in cases (partnership, company, matrimonial, tax disputes). Accounting is the language of money, and a lawyer must speak it.

2Accounting basics

Exam question: Accounting: meaning, nature, functions and branches

1

Meaning

Accounting is the art of recording, classifying and summarising in money terms the transactions of a person or business, and interpreting and communicating the results to those who need them. Book-keeping is its first stage: the day-to-day recording.

🧠 Memory trick

R-C-S-I-C: Record · Classify · Summarise · Interpret · Communicate.

🗣️ Jargon buster

Book-keeping is only the first stage: the day-to-day recording.

2

Nature

(1) It is both an art (skill in presentation) and a science (systematic rules); (2) the "language of business"; (3) a service function that supports decision-making; (4) deals only with transactions measurable in money; (5) based on principles such as double entry.

🎨 Picture it

Accounting is the language of business: both an art and a science, measured only in money.

3

Functions

(1) Recording transactions systematically; (2) classifying them under accounts (ledger); (3) summarising (trial balance, profit and loss, balance sheet); (4) analysing and interpreting; (5) communicating to owners, clients, tax authorities and courts; (6) meeting legal requirements (tax, Bar Council rules); (7) protecting assets and preventing fraud.

🌍 Real life

A law firm records each fee in the cash book, posts it to the client's ledger, prepares the profit and loss account, and the partners use it to fix next year's fees.

4

Branches

(1) Financial accounting: records and final accounts (profit and loss, balance sheet); (2) cost accounting: cost of a product or service; (3) management accounting: information for decisions and budgets; (4) tax accounting: income-tax and GST; (5) auditing: independent checking of accounts; (6) forensic accounting: detecting fraud for litigation. For lawyers, financial, tax and forensic accounting matter most.

Example: a law firm's accountant records each fee receipt in the cash book (recording), posts it to the client's ledger account (classifying), prepares the year's profit and loss account (summarising), and the partners use it to decide fees and staff for next year (interpreting).
🧠 Memory trick

Six children: Financial · Cost · Management · Tax · Audit · Forensic.

5

Basic records

Journal (first entry of each transaction), ledger (accounts of each person, income and expense), cash book and bank book, trial balance (to check arithmetic), and final accounts. For an advocate: a client ledger for every client, a fee register, receipts, and a diary linking work to fees.

🧠 Memory trick

Journal → Ledger → Cash book → Trial balance → Final accounts; for a lawyer, a ledger for every client.

6

Concepts

Double entry (every debit has an equal credit), business entity (the lawyer's personal money is kept apart from the practice and from clients' money), accrual v. cash basis, going concern, consistency, and prudence.

🧠 Memory trick

Every debit has an equal credit.

🎨 Picture it

Three purses: the lawyer's own money, the practice's money and the clients' money never mix (business entity concept).

🔁 Recall: Accounting is "R-C-S-I-C": Record, Classify, Summarise, Interpret, Communicate. Its children are financial, cost, management, tax, audit and forensic accounting.

3Why lawyers need accounts

1

Client's money

An advocate receives fees, money for expenses (court fee, stamps, copies), and money paid to the client through him (decree amounts, compensation, settlements). This is money held in trust; accounts protect both the client and the advocate.

🎨 Picture it

Client's money is held in trust: court fees, stamps, decree amounts, compensation passing through the lawyer's hands.

2

BCI Rules 25–32

(Part VI, Chapter II, Duty to the Client). The advocate shall keep accounts of the client's money showing amounts received, expenses incurred and fees debited, with dates (r.25); entries must show whether money was for fees or expenses, and expenses cannot be diverted to fees without the client's written consent (r.26); he must tell the client at once of money received for him (r.27); at the end he may appropriate the settled fee from unspent money (r.28), or, if the fee was unsettled, deduct the fee payable under court rules and refund the balance (r.29); he must give a copy of the account on demand (r.30); he shall not convert client funds into loans (r.31), nor lend money to his client for the case (r.32).

Case story Harish Chandra Tiwari: an advocate withdrew his client's land-acquisition compensation from court and neither paid it nor told him, later producing a false "settlement" affidavit. The Supreme Court ordered his name removed from the roll: holding a client's money is a sacred trust.
🧠 Memory trick

Keep · Label · Tell · Settle · Show · Don't lend: Rules 25, 26, 27, 28–29, 30, 31–32.

🎬 Case link

Harish Chandra Tiwari: kept a client's compensation and faked a settlement: name removed from the roll.

3

Running the practice

Fees, salaries of clerks and juniors, rent, library, travel: proper accounts show the true income, help in fixing fees, budgeting and partnership sharing in law firms.

🧠 Memory trick

Fees · salaries · rent · library · travel: honest accounts show true income and help fix fees.

4

Tax compliance

Income-tax law requires professionals, including lawyers, whose receipts exceed a prescribed limit to maintain books of account and, above a higher limit, to have them audited. Under GST, legal services to business entities are taxed on reverse charge (the business client pays the tax).

🎨 Picture it

Above a limit, books of account; above a higher limit, an audit. GST applies to legal services for business entities.

5

Accounts in litigation

Lawyers must read balance sheets and accounts in partnership dissolution, company and insolvency matters, matrimonial maintenance (true income of a spouse), tax appeals, bank recovery and white-collar crime (forensic accounts, money trail).

🌍 Real life

A maintenance case turns on a spouse's true income; a partnership dissolution turns on the balance sheet. Lawyers must read accounts.

6

Misconduct risk

S.35

Without accounts, an honest advocate cannot prove he returned money, and a dishonest one is quickly exposed. Misappropriation of client's money is among the gravest professional misconduct (S.35 Advocates Act).

🧠 Memory trick

No accounts → an honest lawyer cannot prove he paid; a dishonest one is quickly exposed.

🔁 Recall: A lawyer's accounts answer three questions at any moment: whose money is this, what is it for (fee or expense), and has the client been told? Rules 25–32 are the answers.

4Sections and numbers

  • Rule 25keep accounts of client's money: receipts, expenses, fees, dates
  • Rule 26state whether money is for fees or expenses; no diversion without written consent
  • Rule 27tell the client at once of any amount received for him
  • Rules 28, 29at the end: appropriate settled fee; if unsettled, deduct court-scale fee; refund balance
  • Rule 30copy of the account to the client on demand
  • Rules 31, 32no converting client funds into loans; no lending to client for the case

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1An advocate must keep accounts of client's money showing receipts, expenses and fees, with dates (BCI Rule 25).
  • ★ 2Money received for expenses cannot be diverted to fees during the case without the client's written consent (Rule 26).
  • ★ 3Keeping or misusing a client's money is grave misconduct: it can end a career. Harish Chandra Tiwari
  • ★ 4An advocate shall not lend money to his client for the litigation, nor turn client funds into loans (Rules 31, 32).
  • ★ 5Accounting = recording, classifying, summarising, interpreting and communicating financial information.
