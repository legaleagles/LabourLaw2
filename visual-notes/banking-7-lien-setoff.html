✊Banker's Lien, Set-off and Appropriation

Three tools when a customer owes the bank: lien (hold his securities), set-off (adjust his money), appropriation (decide which debt a payment goes to).

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

Ramesh owes his bank ₹2 lakh on an overdraft. In the same bank he has left some company debentures, a fixed deposit of ₹1 lakh, and a locker full of jewellery. Can the bank hold on to any of these until he pays?

The law gives a bank three tools. Lien: the right to retain the customer's securities that came into its hands as a banker. Set-off: the right to adjust money it owes him against money he owes it. Appropriation: the rules on which debt a payment goes to. But each tool has limits: the jewellery in safe custody and the locker are not available to the bank at all.

2Banker's general lien

Exam question: Banker's general lien: meaning, features, when it is not available, and precautions

🧠 Say it: “Mummy Loves Idli, Chutney, Not Pizza”

  4. CChutneyConditions

1

Meaning

Lien is the right of a creditor in possession of the debtor's goods to retain them until the debt is paid. A particular lien covers only the debt connected with those goods; a general lien covers the general balance of all dealings.

📖 Word origin

Lien comes from French, from Latin ligamen, "a bond": a right to hold on.

🧠 Memory trick

Particular lien = only for the debt about that thing. General lien = for the whole balance.

2

Legal basis (S.171)

S.171

Bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys of a High Court and policy-brokers may, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, retain as security for a general balance of account any goods bailed to them. It is part of the law merchant Brandao v. Barnett.

🧠 Memory trick

Five who get a general lien (S.171): Bankers · Factors · Wharfingers · High Court attorneys · Policy-brokers.

3

Implied pledge

S.176

An ordinary lien only allows retention. A banker's lien is treated as an implied pledge, so the bank may sell the securities after reasonable notice to the customer (S.176), and it extends to fixed deposit receipts held by the bank Syndicate Bank v. Vijay Kumar.

🎨 Picture it

More than a grip: an implied pledge, so the bank may sell after reasonable notice (S.176).

🎬 Case link

Syndicate Bank v. Vijay Kumar: the lien covers fixed deposits and all securities received in the course of banking.

4

Conditions

(1) The property must come into the bank's possession as a banker, in the ordinary course of banking business; (2) it must belong to the customer (in the same capacity as the debt); (3) a debt must be due; (4) there must be no contrary agreement or inconsistent purpose.

🧠 Memory trick

Banking door · Customer's own · Debt due · No contrary deal.

5

Not available on

(1) articles left for safe custody (the bank is a bailee); (2) property given for a specific purpose (e.g. securities to be sold and the proceeds sent to someone); (3) money or securities held in trust; (4) securities left with the bank by mistake or after a loan is repaid; (5) a locker's contents; (6) third-party property.

Example: Ramesh leaves gold for safe custody and also hands the bank debentures to collect interest. His overdraft goes unpaid. The bank may retain (and after notice sell) the debentures, but must return the gold if he asks; for the gold, it can only sue and seek attachment through court.
🎨 Picture it

Lien works only on what came in through the banking door, never what came through the safe-custody door or the trust door.

🎬 Case link

Brandao v. Barnett: bills held only for custody: no lien.

6

Precautions

Take a written letter of lien/pledge; check the customer's title; make sure the debt is due; keep safe-custody items separate; give reasonable notice before selling.

🧠 Memory trick

Letter of lien · check title · debt due · keep custody items apart · notice before sale.

🔁 Recall: Lien is a bank's grip: it can grip what came in through the banking door (securities, FDs, bills), but not what came through the safe-custody door (jewels, lockers) or the trust door.

3Set-off and appropriation

Exam question: Right of set-off; set-off v. lien; appropriation of payments

🧠 Say it: “My Car Needs Little Air Conditioning”

  1. MMyMeaning
  2. CCarConditions
  3. NNeedsNot available when
  4. LLittleLien v. set-off
  5. AAirAppropriation (S.59–61)
  6. CConditioningClayton's rule
1

Meaning

O.VIII r.6

Set-off is the bank's right to combine accounts: adjust a credit balance in one account of the customer against a debit balance in another, so only the net is payable. It is a banker's own right, exercised without going to court (different from legal set-off in a suit, O.VIII r.6 CPC).

🎨 Picture it

Set-off is a calculator: plus here, minus there, pay only the net.

🗣️ Jargon buster

The banker's own right, used without going to court; different from legal set-off in a suit (O.VIII r.6 CPC).

2

Conditions

(1) Mutual debts between the bank and the customer; (2) in the same right/capacity (personal v. personal); (3) certain in amount and due (a fixed deposit can be set off only on maturity, unless agreed); (4) no agreement against it; (5) reasonable notice to the customer is good practice.

🧠 Memory trick

Mutual · Same right · Due · No contrary agreement · Notice.

3

Not available when

Trust accounts against personal debts; a joint account against one holder's personal debt; an account in a different capacity (executor, guardian); debts not yet due or contingent (an unenforced guarantee); money deposited for a specific purpose; after a garnishee order, only debts due before it can be set off.

Example: Ramesh has ₹50,000 in his savings account and owes ₹80,000 on an overdue personal loan. The bank may set off the ₹50,000 and claim the balance ₹30,000. But it cannot touch the ₹1 lakh in his joint account with his wife, nor money he holds as trustee for his nephew.
🌍 Real life

Ramesh owes ₹80,000 on a loan and has ₹50,000 in savings: the bank sets off ₹50,000 and claims ₹30,000. His joint account with his wife is untouchable.

4

Lien v. set-off

(1) Lien is over the customer's goods and securities; set-off is over money (debts). (2) Lien means retention (and sale as an implied pledge); set-off means adjustment of accounts. (3) Lien needs possession; set-off needs mutual debts due. (4) Lien can secure a general balance; set-off needs ascertained, matured amounts.

🧠 Memory trick

Lien = goods, hold. Set-off = money, adjust.

5

Appropriation (S.59–61)

S.59S.60S.61

When a debtor owing several debts pays money: (1) the debtor may say which debt it is for (S.59); (2) if he does not, the creditor may apply it to any lawful debt due, even a time-barred one (S.60); (3) if neither does, it goes to debts in order of time (S.61).

🧠 Memory trick

A queue: debtor chooses (S.59) → else creditor chooses, even a time-barred debt (S.60) → else order of time (S.61).

6

Clayton's rule

In a running account, the first payment in is set against the first debit out ("first in, first out") Clayton's case. So when a guarantor dies or a partner retires, the bank must stop (rule off) the account and open a new one; otherwise later deposits will wipe out the old debt for which the guarantor or the old partner was liable.

🎨 Picture it

First in, first out: the earliest payments wipe out the earliest debts.

🎬 Case link

Clayton's case (1816): so when a guarantor dies or a partner retires, the bank must rule off the account and open a new one.

🔁 Recall: Set-off is a calculator: plus here, minus there, same person, same pocket, amounts due. Lien is a hand holding securities. Appropriation is a queue: debtor chooses, else creditor, else first come first served.

5Case stories

Brandao v. Barnett

(1846) 12 Cl & F 787 · House of Lords

📖 What happenedExchequer bills belonging to a customer were kept by bankers in a locked box; the bankers took them out only to collect interest and renew them, then claimed a lien for another person's debt.
❓ The questionDid the bankers have a lien on the bills?
⚖️ HeldBankers have a general lien on securities deposited with them as bankers, as part of the law merchant; but there was no lien here, because the bills were held for a special purpose (custody).

Syndicate Bank v. Vijay Kumar

(1992) 2 SCC 330 · AIR 1992 SC 1066

📖 What happenedA customer's fixed deposits were attached by his creditor in execution of a decree; the bank said it could hold them for the customer's liabilities to it.
❓ The questionDoes the bank's lien over a customer's deposits prevail over a later attachment by another creditor?
⚖️ HeldA banker has a general lien over all forms of deposits and securities received in the course of banking; it is an implied pledge, and the bank may hold them against the customer's liabilities.

🪝 The bank holds first · Full story →

Devaynes v. Noble (Clayton's case)

(1816) 1 Mer 572 · Court of Chancery

📖 What happenedA partner in a banking firm died; a customer kept dealing with the surviving partners, and then the firm failed. He tried to claim his balance from the dead partner's estate.
❓ The questionHow are payments in and out of a running account appropriated?
⚖️ HeldIn a running account, payments are applied in order of time: the earliest withdrawals discharge the earliest balance. His later withdrawals had used up the balance owed at the death, so the estate was not liable.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1A banker's lien is a general lien (for the whole balance) and an implied pledge, so the bank may sell after reasonable notice. Brandao v. Barnett
  • ★ 2No lien on articles in safe custody or on property given for a specific purpose.
  • ★ 3A banker's lien extends to fixed deposits and securities that come to it in the course of banking. Syndicate Bank v. Vijay Kumar
  • ★ 4Set-off needs mutual debts, due, between the same parties in the same right.
  • ★ 5In a running account, first in, first out: early payments wipe out early debts. Clayton's case

7Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. X has deposited jewels worth ₹50,000 with the bank for safe custody. Later he becomes a debtor to the bank. Explain the bank's rights.

  1. 🎯 IssueCan the bank exercise its lien over articles kept for safe custody?
  2. 📜 LawS.171 Contract Act gives a general lien over goods bailed to a banker as banker; but articles deposited for safe custody are held as a bailee for a specific purpose, which is inconsistent with lien Brandao v. Barnett. S.151: bailee's duty of care. O.XXXVIII r.5 CPC: attachment before judgment.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe jewels were given only for safe custody, before any loan, and not as security. The purpose excludes lien.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe bank has no lien on the jewels and must return them on demand. Its remedy is to sue X for the debt and, if needed, seek attachment of the jewels through the court; it can also set off any credit balance X holds.

2. C instructs his banker to buy certain debentures for him, keep them and collect the interest. Later C's current account is overdrawn. Can the banker exercise a lien over the debentures?

  1. 🎯 IssueAre securities held for collection of interest subject to the banker's lien?
  2. 📜 LawS.171: lien over securities that come to the banker in the ordinary course of banking business. Securities held for collection of interest or dividends are held as banker, so lien attaches (In re United Service Co., Johnson's Claim, 1870). But where securities are kept only for safe custody, e.g. in a sealed box, there is no lien Brandao v. Barnett.
  3. 🔍 ApplyBuying debentures and collecting interest are banking services; the debentures are in the bank's hands as banker, not merely in safe custody, and no contrary agreement exists.
  4. ✅ ConclusionYes. The banker can retain the debentures for the overdraft and, after reasonable notice, sell them; it must account for any surplus. If the debentures had been lodged in a sealed cover purely for safe custody, there would be no lien.

3. Legal position of the banker: (a) B deposits gold ornaments worth ₹50,000 for safe custody; (b) D deposits ₹40,000 as a two-year fixed deposit.

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat relationship and rights arise in each case?
  2. 📜 Law(a) Bailment (S.148, S.151 Contract Act): bailee, no lien. (b) Debtor-creditor: the fixed deposit is a debt of the bank payable at maturity Foley v. Hill; general lien / set-off over fixed deposits Syndicate Bank v. Vijay Kumar.
  3. 🔍 Apply(a) The bank must keep the ornaments with reasonable care and return them on demand; if they are lost through its negligence, it must compensate B. (b) The bank owns the ₹40,000 and must repay it with interest on maturity (or earlier, as agreed, with a penalty); if D owes the bank money, it may set it off once the deposit matures or as agreed.
  4. ✅ Conclusion(a) Bailee: duty of care, no lien. (b) Debtor: repay with interest at maturity; it may grant a loan against the deposit and exercise lien/set-off for D's dues.

4. Ramesh has ₹50,000 in his savings account, an overdue personal loan of ₹80,000, and a joint account with his wife with ₹1,00,000. The bank debits both accounts to recover the loan. Advise Ramesh.

  1. 🎯 IssueCan the bank set off a joint account against one holder's personal debt?
  2. 📜 LawSet-off requires mutual debts in the same right. A joint account belongs to the holders together; it is not a debt owed to Ramesh alone.
  3. 🔍 ApplySetting off his own savings account (same right, loan overdue) is valid. Debiting the joint account is not.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe bank may adjust ₹50,000 from his savings account but must reverse the debit to the joint account; Ramesh can complain to the bank and then the RBI Ombudsman.
