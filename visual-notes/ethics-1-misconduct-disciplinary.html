🧠Mind map

1The big picture

An elderly couple hand their lawyer some promissory notes and money for court fees. "File the suits," they say. Months later he tells them the suits are filed and decrees obtained. Nothing was ever filed. When they complain to the State Bar Council, the lawyer faces the profession's own court: the disciplinary committee.

The Advocates Act, 1961 lets the profession police itself. A complaint goes to the State Bar Council, its disciplinary committee hears both sides like a court, and it can reprimand, suspend or remove the advocate from the roll. Appeals go to the Bar Council of India and then to the Supreme Court.

1

Meaning

"Misconduct" is not defined in the Advocates Act, 1961. Broadly, it is any act or omission by an advocate that is dishonourable, unbecoming of the profession, or breaks the standards of professional conduct laid down by the Bar Council of India (BCI Rules, Part VI, Chapter II). The Supreme Court has said misconduct includes conduct that, by the standards of the profession, is disgraceful or dishonourable.

🗣️ Jargon buster

"Misconduct" is not defined in the Act: any act that makes an advocate unworthy of the profession.

2

Professional and other misconduct

S.35

S.35 speaks of "professional or other misconduct": an advocate can be punished not only for acts in his work but also for serious misbehaviour outside it, if it shows he is unfit to be an advocate.

🧠 Memory trick

"Professional or other" misconduct (S.35): even serious misbehaviour outside work counts.

3

Client

Misappropriating client's money; taking fees and not doing the work; negligence causing loss; disclosing confidences; appearing for the other side; demanding a share of the result (contingency fee); misleading the client.

Case story V.C. Rangadurai: an advocate took promissory notes and money from an elderly couple to file suits, then falsely told them the suits were filed and decreed. The Supreme Court upheld the finding of gross professional misconduct, but shaped the punishment to reform him, including free legal-aid work.
🧠 Memory trick

Money · Neglect · Secrets · Switching sides · Contingency fee · Misleading.

🎬 Case link

V.C. Rangadurai: told an elderly couple their suits were filed and decreed when nothing was filed: gross misconduct.

4

Court

Misleading the court, fabricating evidence or affidavits, influencing judges, abusive or threatening behaviour, boycotting courts on strike (Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India, 2003), and interfering with witnesses (R.K. Anand v. Registrar, Delhi High Court, 2009).

🧠 Memory trick

Lies · Fake evidence · Influencing judges · Abuse · Strikes · Witness tampering.

🎬 Case link

Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal: lawyers have no right to strike or boycott courts.

5

Profession

Soliciting work through touts or advertising (BCI Rule 36), undercutting fees, carrying on a business while practising, full-time salaried employment while practising (Rule 49), accepting a brief where another advocate is engaged without his consent.

🧠 Memory trick

Touts and adverts (Rule 36) · business while practising · salaried job while practising (Rule 49) · stealing another advocate's brief.

6

Other misconduct

Criminal acts involving moral turpitude, cheating, forgery, violence, and similar conduct outside court that lowers the profession in public esteem.

🧠 Memory trick

Moral turpitude: cheating, forgery, violence outside court.

7

Consequences

S.35(3)

The disciplinary committee may dismiss the complaint, reprimand, suspend for a period, or remove the name from the roll (S.35(3)); it may also order costs. A forged or fraudulent act is treated very seriously: misappropriating a client's compensation money led to removal from the rolls (Harish Chandra Tiwari v. Baiju, 2002).

🧠 Memory trick

The penalty ladder: reprimand → suspend → remove (S.35(3)), plus costs.

🔁 Recall: Misconduct points in four directions: against the client (money, neglect), the court (lies, strikes), the profession (touting, business) and society (crimes). The penalty ladder: reprimand → suspend → remove.

1

Constitution (S.9)

S.9

Every State Bar Council and the Bar Council of India constitute one or more disciplinary committees of three members: two elected by the Council from its members and one co-opted from advocates with at least ten years' standing who are not members. The senior-most advocate among them is the chairman.

🔢 Number trick

2 + 1: two elected Council members and one co-opted advocate of at least 10 years' standing (S.9).

2

Who can complain (S.35)

S.35(1)S.35(1A)

On receipt of a complaint "or otherwise" (on its own motion, or on a court's reference), if the State Bar Council has reason to believe that an advocate on its roll is guilty of professional or other misconduct, it refers the case to its disciplinary committee. It may withdraw a case from one committee and give it to another (S.35(1A)).

🧠 Memory trick

Starts on a complaint "or otherwise": the Council's own motion or a court's reference (S.35(1)).

3

Procedure

S.35(2)S.42(2)

The committee fixes a date and gives notice to the advocate and to the Advocate-General of the State (S.35(2)); the advocate may file a reply and lead evidence; hearings follow natural justice and BCI Rules (Part VII). Proceedings are judicial proceedings for the purposes of the Penal Code provisions on false evidence (S.42(2)).

🧠 Memory trick

Notice to the advocate and the Advocate-General (S.35(2)); natural justice; judicial proceedings.

4

Powers (S.42)

S.42S.36S.48A

The powers of a civil court under the CPC for summoning and enforcing attendance and examining witnesses on oath, discovery and production of documents, receiving evidence on affidavits, requisitioning public records, and issuing commissions. The BCI can call for records of any proceeding and pass orders (S.36, S.48A revision).

🧠 Memory trick

A civil court's powers: summon · discover · affidavits · records · commissions (S.42).

5

Orders (S.35(3))

S.35(3)

After hearing, the committee may: (a) dismiss the complaint (or file the proceedings if started by the Council); (b) reprimand the advocate; (c) suspend him from practice for a period; (d) remove his name from the State roll. It may award costs.

Example: a client complains that her lawyer kept ₹2 lakh of decree money paid into court. The State Bar Council refers it to its disciplinary committee, which summons bank records, hears both, and orders the advocate's name removed from the roll and the money refunded.
🧠 Memory trick

Dismiss · Reprimand · Suspend · Remove (S.35(3)).

🌍 Real life

A lawyer keeps ₹2 lakh of decree money paid into court: the committee summons bank records and removes his name from the roll.

6

BCI and time limit (S.36, S.36B)

S.36S.36B

The BCI's disciplinary committee hears cases against advocates not on any State roll, and may withdraw any pending case from a State Bar Council (S.36). A State committee must decide a complaint within one year; otherwise the case is transferred to the BCI (S.36B).

🔢 Number trick

Decide within 1 year, or the BCI takes it over (S.36B).

7

Appeals and review (S.37, S.38, S.44)

S.37S.38S.44

A person aggrieved by a State committee's order (including the Advocate-General) may appeal to the BCI within 60 days (S.37); from the BCI's order, an appeal lies to the Supreme Court within 60 days (S.38). A committee may review its own order on its own motion or otherwise (S.44). An order of suspension or removal is communicated to the Supreme Court and all High Courts.

🔢 Number trick

60 days to the BCI (S.37); 60 days to the Supreme Court (S.38); review of its own order (S.44).

🎬 Case link

V.C. Rangadurai: punishment should reform as well as deter; suspension cut, free legal-aid work ordered.

🔁 Recall: Think of a three-judge panel of lawyers: complaint → notice → hearing with civil-court powers → four possible orders → 60 days to BCI → 60 days to the Supreme Court. Finish in a year.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1"Misconduct" is not defined in the Act: it covers any conduct that makes an advocate unworthy of the profession, in or outside his work.
  • ★ 2The Act punishes "professional or other misconduct" (S.35): even conduct outside court can count.
  • ★ 3Disciplinary proceedings aim to protect the public and reform the advocate, not merely to punish. V.C. Rangadurai
  • ★ 4A State Bar Council must decide a complaint within one year, or the BCI takes it over (S.36B).
  • ★ 5Only the Bar Councils can suspend or remove an advocate from the roll; courts punish contempt.
