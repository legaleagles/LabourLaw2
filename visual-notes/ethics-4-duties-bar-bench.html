⭐Duties of an Advocate and Bar-Bench Relations

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

An advocate stands at a crossroads of loyalties. He must fight fearlessly for his client, yet never mislead the court; he must be fair to his opponent and courteous to colleagues; and he owes society a duty to help those who cannot pay. When these pull in different directions, the duty to the court and to justice comes first.

The Bar Council of India has written these duties down as Standards of Professional Conduct and Etiquette (BCI Rules, Part VI, Chapter II, made under S.49(1)(c) of the Advocates Act). Breaking them is professional misconduct.

1

Source

S.7(1)(b)S.49(1)(c)S.35

The Advocates Act, 1961 empowers the BCI to lay down standards of professional conduct (S.7(1)(b), S.49(1)(c)); these are the BCI Rules, Part VI, Chapter II. Breach is misconduct under S.35.

🧠 Memory trick

S.7(1)(b) and S.49(1)(c) give the BCI the power; the rules are in Part VI, Chapter II; breaking them is misconduct under S.35.

2

Court

Act with dignity and self-respect; show respect to the court; not communicate privately with a judge about a pending case or try to influence him; not mislead the court or use illegal or improper means; refuse to act for a client who insists on unfair means; wear the prescribed dress in court; not appear before a judge who is a close relative; not appear in a matter in which he has a pecuniary interest; not stand surety for his client.

🎨 Picture it

An advocate is an officer of the court: the court is the top point of the star.

🧠 Memory trick

Dignity · Respect · No private word with the judge · No misleading · No improper means.

3

Client

S.132

Accept a brief in courts he practises in, at his usual fee (r.11); not withdraw without sufficient cause and reasonable notice; disclose any interest or connection; uphold the client's interest fearlessly by fair means (r.15); a prosecutor must not suppress evidence that may prove innocence; keep confidences (r.17; S.132 Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam); not foment litigation; no contingency fee (r.20); not buy any interest in the litigation; keep accounts of client's money (r.25–32); not act for the opposite party in the same matter (r.33).

Example: a client tells his lawyer in confidence that he signed the disputed document. The lawyer must not disclose it, but also must not tell the court that the signature is forged. He can test the other side's evidence and argue the law, but cannot assert what he knows to be false.
📖 Word origin

The cab-rank rule: like the first taxi in the rank, an advocate takes the brief in front of him, at his usual fee (r.11).

🌍 Real life

A client confides that he did sign the disputed document: the lawyer keeps the secret, but must not tell the court the signature is forged. He may still test the other side's evidence.

🧠 Memory trick

Fearless for the client (r.15), but by fair means; secrets kept (r.17); no share in the result (r.20).

4

Opponent

Not to negotiate directly with a party who is represented by an advocate, except through that advocate; to carry out legitimate promises made to the opposite side.

🧠 Memory trick

Talk to the opponent's lawyer, not the opponent (r.34); keep promises made to the other side (r.35).

5

Colleagues

No soliciting work or advertising, directly or through touts (r.36; a website with basic factual information is permitted); not let his name be used for unauthorised practice; not accept a fee below the minimum taxable fee; not appear in a case where another advocate has filed a vakalat without his consent.

🧠 Memory trick

No touts, no adverts (r.36; a factual website is allowed) · no undercutting · no lending your name for unauthorised practice.

6

Society

Every advocate must bear in mind that anyone genuinely in need of a lawyer is entitled to legal assistance even if he cannot pay; the advocate should render legal aid within his means. He should also support law reform and uphold the rule of law.

🧠 Memory trick

Can't pay? Still entitled to help (r.46).

7

Other restrictions

Not to carry on any business while practising (r.47); not to be a full-time salaried employee of any person or firm while practising (r.49); not to strike or boycott courts Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal.

🧠 Memory trick

No business (r.47) · no salaried job (r.49) · no strikes.

🎬 Case link

Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal: no right to strike or boycott courts, not even a token strike.

🔁 Recall: Draw a star with five points around the advocate: Court (top, first), Client, Opponent, Colleagues, Society. The rules are the lines joining them.

3Bar-Bench relations

Exam question: Bar-Bench relations

1

Meaning

The Bar (advocates) and the Bench (judges) are partners in the administration of justice, "two wheels of the same chariot". Judges come largely from the Bar; both need each other's respect for justice to be done quickly and fairly.

🎨 Picture it

Two wheels of one chariot: if one brakes (strike) or skids (rudeness), justice stops.

2

Duties of the Bar

Respect the court, accept its rulings (challenge them only by appeal), be punctual and prepared, not abuse or threaten judges, not use the Bar association to pressure courts, avoid strikes and boycotts Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal.

🧠 Memory trick

Respect · Accept rulings (appeal, don't abuse) · Punctual · Prepared · No pressure tactics.

3

Duties of the Bench

Be patient, courteous and impartial; hear lawyers fully; avoid harsh or humiliating remarks in open court; decide promptly; maintain the dignity of the court without arrogance. Judges are bound by the Restatement of Values of Judicial Life (1997).

🧠 Memory trick

Patient · Courteous · Impartial · Hear fully · Decide promptly; no humiliating remarks.

🗣️ Jargon buster

Judges follow the Restatement of Values of Judicial Life.

4

Causes of friction

Delays and adjournments, rude conduct on either side, strikes, contempt proceedings, perceived bias, and heavy workloads.

Example: when a judge refused an adjournment, an advocate shouted at him and the local Bar association called a strike. The right path was to seek the remedy in a higher court or through the Bar Council, not to stop the work of all courts.
🌍 Real life

A judge refuses an adjournment; an advocate shouts and the Bar association calls a strike. The right path: a higher court or the Bar Council.

5

Remedies

Joint Bar-Bench committees and grievance-redressal committees in courts; Bar Council discipline; judicial training; contempt power used sparingly; the Supreme Court's directions in Harish Uppal to resolve grievances without strikes.

🧠 Memory trick

Joint committees · Bar Council discipline · training · contempt sparingly.

🔁 Recall: Bar and Bench are two wheels of one cart: if one wheel brakes (strike) or skids (rudeness), the cart of justice stops.

5Case stories

Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India

(2003) 2 SCC 45 · decided 17 Dec 2002

📖 What happenedA former Army captain whose case was delayed by repeated lawyers' strikes asked the Supreme Court to declare such strikes illegal.
❓ The questionDo lawyers have a right to go on strike or call for a boycott of courts?
⚖️ HeldAdvocates have no right to go on strike or boycott courts, not even a token strike; at most they may protest peacefully without stopping court work. Abstaining from work can be professional misconduct.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1An advocate is an officer of the court: his first duty is to the administration of justice.
  • ★ 2Lawyers have no right to strike or boycott courts; abstaining from work harms clients and justice. Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal
  • ★ 3An advocate is bound to accept a brief in his field at his usual fee, unless there are special reasons (Rule 11).
  • ★ 4No contingency fees, no buying interest in litigation, no soliciting work (Rules 20, 22, 36).
  • ★ 5Bar and Bench are two wheels of the same chariot: mutual respect keeps justice moving.
