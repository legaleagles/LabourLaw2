🚨FIR, Complaint and Investigation

A crime is like a fire alarm: someone rings it (FIR or complaint), the police investigate, and the charge sheet reports what they found.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

A crime is like a fire alarm. Someone has to ring it (the FIR at the police station, or a complaint to the Magistrate), someone has to investigate it (the police), and someone has to write the report of what they found (the charge sheet). For serious crimes (cognizable) the police can act on their own; for minor ones (non-cognizable) they need the Magistrate's permission.

The BNSS has modernised the alarm: you can now ring it from any police station (zero FIR), or online (e-FIR, signed within 3 days), get a free copy at once, and the police must video-record searches and send forensic experts in serious cases.

2FIR

Exam question: First Information Report (FIR): meaning, procedure, zero FIR, refusal to register, evidentiary value (S.173)

1

Meaning

S.173

The FIR is the first information of a cognizable offence given to the officer in charge of a police station, recorded under S.173 BNSS. It sets the criminal law in motion: the police can then investigate without a Magistrate's order.

📖 Word origin

FIR = First Information Report: the first word the police hear about a cognizable crime. It sets the criminal law in motion.

🌍 Real life

Your phone is snatched (theft, BNS 303(2), cognizable). You go to the police station, the officer writes down what you say: that record is the FIR.

2

How it is recorded

S.173(1)

Oral information is reduced to writing, read over to the informant and signed by him; it is entered in the register; a copy is given free of cost to the informant or victim at once. Information by a woman victim of certain sexual offences is recorded by a woman officer.

🧠 Memory trick

Write → Read → Sign → Register → Copy: oral information is written down, read over, signed by the informant, entered in the register, and a free copy is given at once.

🎨 Picture it

Like a bank deposit slip: you always walk away with your counterfoil (the free copy).

3

New in BNSS

S.173(3)

(1) Zero FIR: information may be given irrespective of the area where the offence was committed; (2) e-FIR: by electronic communication, to be signed within 3 days; (3) preliminary enquiry for offences punishable with 3 to under 7 years, within 14 days, with a DSP's permission (S.173(3)).

🧠 Memory trick

"Zero, E, 14": Zero FIR at any station · e-FIR, signed within 3 days · preliminary enquiry within 14 days.

🌍 Real life

Robbed while travelling in another district? You need not hunt for the "right" station: a Zero FIR can be given irrespective of where the offence happened.

🔢 Number trick

3 and 14: the e-FIR is signed within 3 days; a preliminary enquiry (offences of 3 to under 7 years, with a DSP's permission) takes up to 14 days.

4

If police refuse

S.173(4)S.175(3)S.223

(1) Send the information in writing to the Superintendent of Police, who investigates or orders investigation (S.173(4)); (2) apply to the Magistrate under S.175(3) (with an affidavit) to order investigation; (3) file a complaint directly before the Magistrate (S.223); (4) writ petition.

🎨 Picture it

Climb the ladder when the SHO says no: SP (S.173(4)) → Magistrate (S.175(3)) → complaint (S.223) → writ in the High Court.

🎬 Case link

Lalita Kumari: if the information discloses a cognizable offence, registering the FIR is mandatory.

5

Evidentiary value

The FIR is not substantive evidence; it can be used only to corroborate or contradict its maker, or as a dying declaration if the informant dies. Delay in lodging the FIR is not fatal if explained, but an unexplained delay invites suspicion of embellishment.

For the same occurrence there can be only one FIR; later information is part of the same investigation T.T. Antony. A counter-FIR by the other side (a different version) is allowed.

🎨 Picture it

The FIR is a mirror held up to the informant: it can support (corroborate) or expose (contradict) him in court, but it is not proof by itself.

🧠 Memory trick

One incident, one FIR (T.T. Antony). But the other side's different version, a counter-FIR, is allowed.

6

Case law

S.173(3)
  • Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh: Registration of an FIR is mandatory when information discloses a cognizable offence; a preliminary inquiry is allowed only in limited categories.
  • T.T. Antony v. State of Kerala: The later FIR was quashed: there can be no second FIR for the same occurrence; the police should do further investigation instead.
Case story Lalita Kumari: a minor girl was kidnapped in Uttar Pradesh and the police did not register her father's complaint. A Constitution Bench held: if the information discloses a cognizable offence, registration of the FIR is mandatory; a preliminary inquiry is allowed only in limited kinds of cases (family, commercial, medical negligence, corruption, long delay), within a short time. BNSS S.173(3) now builds on this.
🧠 Memory trick

Register · One · Quash: Lalita Kumari says register; T.T. Antony says only one; Bhajan Lal says when courts may quash.

🔁 Recall: Imagine ringing a fire alarm: from any station (zero FIR), from your phone (e-FIR), get a receipt (free copy), and if the guard ignores you, ring his boss (SP) or the judge (Magistrate).

3Complaint

Exam question: Complaint (S.2, S.223–227) · Difference between FIR and complaint · Procedure for filing both

🧠 Say it: “Make Pickles, Don't Fry”

  1. MMakeMeaning
  2. PPicklesProcedure (S.223–227)
  3. DDon'tDismissal
  4. FFryFIR v. complaint
1

Meaning

S.2

A complaint is any allegation made orally or in writing to a Magistrate, with a view to his taking action, that some person has committed an offence; it does not include a police report. A police report on a non-cognizable case is deemed a complaint.

🧠 Memory trick

FIR goes to the Force; Complaint goes to the Court. A complaint is made to a Magistrate, never to the police.

🗣️ Jargon buster

A police report is not a complaint. But a police report on a non-cognizable case is deemed to be a complaint.

2

Procedure (S.223–227)

S.223S.225S.226S.227BNS 356

(1) The Magistrate examines the complainant and witnesses on oath and records it (S.223); under the BNSS proviso he shall not take cognizance without giving the accused an opportunity of being heard. (2) He may postpone process and inquire himself or order investigation (S.225); it is compulsory where the accused lives beyond his jurisdiction. (3) If there is no sufficient ground, he dismisses the complaint with brief reasons (S.226). (4) If there is sufficient ground, he issues summons or warrant (S.227).

Example: Ravi's neighbour defames him in a WhatsApp group. Defamation (BNS 356) is non-cognizable, so the police will not register an FIR. Ravi files a complaint before the Magistrate, is examined on oath, and the Magistrate issues summons to the neighbour.
🔢 Number trick

223 → 225 → 226 → 227: examine on oath → postpone and inquire → dismiss → issue process (summons or warrant).

🎨 Picture it

BNSS twist: the accused gets a hearing before cognizance (S.223 proviso), like being allowed to speak before the gate opens.

3

Dismissal

If there is no sufficient ground for proceeding, the Magistrate dismisses the complaint, recording brief reasons (S.226).

🎨 Picture it

No sufficient ground = the file goes back with a short note of reasons. The Magistrate cannot dismiss silently; he must record brief reasons.

4Cognizable v. non-cognizable

Exam question: Cognizable and non-cognizable offences: meaning, distinction, examples

🧠 Say it: “Mangoes Pick Fast, Don't Eat”

  1. MMangoesMeaning
  2. PPickPolice powers
  3. FFastFirst Schedule
  4. DDon'tDistinction

1

Meaning

S.2

A cognizable offence is one for which a police officer may arrest without warrant under the First Schedule or any other law. A non-cognizable offence is one for which he has no authority to arrest without warrant.

🧠 Memory trick

Cognizable = "Cop can" arrest without warrant. Non-cognizable = "Cop cannot".

📖 Word origin

Cognizance comes from Latin cognoscere, "to get to know": in a cognizable case the police may take notice and act on their own.

2

Police powers

S.173S.175(1)S.174(1)S.174(2)S.174(4)

Cognizable: the officer records the FIR (S.173) and investigates without a Magistrate's order (S.175(1)). Non-cognizable: he enters the substance in a register, refers the informant to the Magistrate (S.174(1)), and cannot investigate without the Magistrate's order (S.174(2)). If a case has both kinds of offences, it is treated as cognizable (S.174(4)).

🔢 Number trick

173 v. 174: S.173 = FIR and investigation (cognizable); S.174 = entry in a register and "go to the Magistrate" (non-cognizable).

🧠 Memory trick

Mixed case? If even one offence is cognizable, the whole case is treated as cognizable (S.174(4)).

3

First Schedule

Column-wise it tells whether each BNS offence is cognizable, bailable, and which court tries it. Generally, offences punishable with 3 years or more are cognizable; lesser ones are mostly non-cognizable.

🎨 Picture it

The First Schedule is a menu card with columns: cognizable? bailable? which court tries it?

🔢 Number trick

Rule of thumb from the notes: offences punishable with 3 years or more are generally cognizable.

4

Distinction

S.173S.174

(table):

Investigationwithout Magistrate's order · only with order

Seriousnessserious · less serious
Bailoften non-bailable · usually bailable

The label decides who moves first: the police alone (cognizable), or the police only after the Magistrate's permission (non-cognizable).

🎨 Picture it

Green light (cognizable): police move on their own. Red light (non-cognizable): police wait for the Magistrate's signal.

5

Examples

BNS 103BNS 64BNS 303(2)BNS 80BNS 85
Examples: cognizable: murder (BNS 103), rape (BNS 64), theft (BNS 303(2)), dowry death (BNS 80), cruelty by husband (BNS 85). Non-cognizable: simple hurt (BNS 115(2)), defamation (BNS 356), insult to provoke breach of peace (BNS 352).
🌍 Real life

Phone snatched (theft, BNS 303(2)) → cognizable. A neighbour's slap (simple hurt, BNS 115(2)) or a defamatory WhatsApp message (BNS 356) → non-cognizable.

🔁 Recall: Cognizable = "Cop can" arrest and investigate on his own; Non-cognizable = "Cop cannot" without the Magistrate.

5Investigation

Exam question: Investigation: duties of police, case diary, police report (charge sheet) and inquest (S.175–194)

🧠 Say it: “My Sister Studies Daily, Practising Inquiry”

  3. SStudiesStatements
  4. DDailyCase diary
  5. PPractisingPolice report

1

Meaning

S.2

Investigation includes all proceedings for the collection of evidence by a police officer or a person authorised by a Magistrate (not a Magistrate himself).

🗣️ Jargon buster

Investigation is done by the police (or a person a Magistrate authorises), never by the Magistrate himself.

📖 Word origin

Investigate, from Latin vestigare, "to track footprints": collecting the evidence.

2

Steps of investigation

S.176S.176(3)S.105S.185S.35

(1) Proceed to the spot and investigate (S.176); for offences punishable with 7 years or more, a forensic expert must visit the scene and the process is video-recorded (S.176(3)); (2) search and seizure, recorded by audio-video means (S.105, S.185); (3) arrest where necessary (S.35); (4) examine witnesses (S.180); (5) medical examination (S.51–53, S.184 for a rape victim); (6) police custody up to 15 days, in parts within the first 40 or 60 days (S.187); (7) police report (S.193).

🎨 Picture it

Follow the officer: scene → forensics → search → arrest → witnesses → medical → custody → report.

🔢 Number trick

7 years or more → forensic expert at the scene, video-recorded (S.176(3)). 15 days police custody, in parts, within the first 40 or 60 days (S.187).

3

Statements

S.181S.183

Statements to the police are not signed and cannot be used as evidence except to contradict the witness (S.181). Confessions and statements recorded by a Judicial Magistrate (S.183) can be used; confessions to the police are inadmissible.

🎨 Picture it

Two notebooks: what you tell the police is unsigned and can only be used to contradict you (S.181); what you tell a Judicial Magistrate (S.183) can be used.

🧠 Memory trick

Confession to the police = zero value in court.

4

Case diary

S.192

The investigating officer records day by day: when the information came, when he started and closed, places visited, and circumstances found. The court may use it to aid its inquiry, but not as evidence; the accused cannot demand it, except where the officer uses it to refresh memory or the court uses it to contradict him. (The general diary is the station's daily register of all events, kept under the police rules.)

🎨 Picture it

The case diary is the officer's school diary: day by day, what he did and found. The court may read it to understand the case, but it is not evidence, and the accused cannot demand it.

5

Police report

S.193S.193(9)

On completion, the officer sends the report to the Magistrate: names of parties, nature of information, witnesses, whether an offence appears, whether the accused was arrested or released. He must inform the victim of progress within 90 days, supply copies to the accused, and may make further investigation even after the report (S.193(9), to be completed within 90 days unless the court extends).

Case story Bhajan Lal: a complaint alleged that a former Chief Minister of Haryana had amassed disproportionate assets; the High Court quashed the FIR. The Supreme Court restored the investigation and laid down categories where an FIR may be quashed: e.g. no offence on its face, allegations absurd, legal bar, or proceedings malicious. Otherwise courts should not stop investigation at the start.
📖 Word origin

The charge sheet is the police report sent to the Magistrate when the investigation is complete (S.193).

🔢 Number trick

90 days: tell the victim the progress (S.193(3)). Further investigation is possible even after the report (S.193(9)).

🎬 Case link

Bhajan Lal: courts may quash an FIR only in the categories the Supreme Court laid down; otherwise the investigation goes on.

6

Inquest

S.194

On information of suicide, homicide, death by animal or machine, or a suspicious death (including a woman within 7 years of marriage), the officer informs the Executive Magistrate, examines the body in the presence of two or more respectable inhabitants, prepares a report on the apparent cause of death, and forwards it within 24 hours to the District or Sub-Divisional Magistrate.

📖 Word origin

Inquest means an inquiry into a death (from Old French enqueste, "inquiry").

🧠 Memory trick

Body · Two witnesses · 24 hours: examine the body before two or more respectable inhabitants, and send the report within 24 hours.

🔁 Recall: Follow the officer through one day: scene → search → statements → diary → report; and for a dead body, the inquest comes first.

8Golden rules

  • ★ 1If information discloses a cognizable offence, registering the FIR is mandatory; preliminary inquiry only in limited cases. Lalita Kumari
  • ★ 2No second FIR for the same incident; the police must do "further investigation" instead (S.193(9)). T.T. Antony
  • ★ 3An FIR is not substantive evidence; it can only corroborate or contradict its maker.
  • ★ 4Non-cognizable: no investigation without a Magistrate's order (S.174(2)).
  • ★ 5Courts can quash an FIR only in the clear categories laid down by the Supreme Court. Bhajan Lal

9Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. After receiving the FIR, the SHO decides not to investigate because the case "is not serious". Remedy of the aggrieved person?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat can be done when the police decline to investigate a cognizable offence?
  2. 📜 LawS.176: the officer may decline to investigate only if there is no sufficient ground, and must state his reasons and notify the informant. S.173(4): send the information to the SP. S.175(3): Magistrate may order investigation (on an application with affidavit). S.223: complaint to the Magistrate. Registration is mandatory Lalita Kumari.
  3. 🔍 ApplyIf the information discloses a cognizable offence, "not serious" is not a lawful ground. The informant first approaches the SP; if nothing happens, the Magistrate.
  4. ✅ ConclusionApproach the SP under S.173(4), then the Magistrate under S.175(3) or by complaint under S.223; in a fit case, a writ.

2. Ramu told the FIR a "fair and tall" man killed Krishna; at the trial he says a "black and short" man did it. What is the value of these statements?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat is the evidentiary value of the FIR when the informant changes his version?
  2. 📜 LawThe FIR is not substantive evidence; it may be used to corroborate or contradict its maker under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam. The court relies on sworn evidence in court, tested by cross-examination.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe contradiction will be put to Ramu in cross-examination. A material contradiction on identity seriously damages his credibility; the court will look for other evidence (other witnesses, forensics, identification parade).
  4. ✅ ConclusionHis court statement is the substantive evidence; the FIR only shows he contradicted himself, which weakens his testimony on identity.

3. A records a video of police misconduct during an investigation but will not hand it over, fearing the police. What are his duties and protections?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan A be required to produce the video, and how is he protected?
  2. 📜 LawS.94: a court or officer in charge may summon production of any document or electronic record. S.398: Witness Protection Scheme. S.105: searches are themselves to be video-recorded. Complaint to the SP or Magistrate (S.173(4), S.223).
  3. 🔍 ApplyA can be summoned to produce the video, but he need not hand it to the officers he fears: he can give it to the court or the SP, and seek protection.
  4. ✅ ConclusionA may be compelled to produce it, but he can produce it before the Magistrate or a senior officer and seek protection under the Witness Protection Scheme.

4. Police registered a second FIR for the same incident after an inquiry commission's report. Valid?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan there be a second FIR for the same occurrence?
  2. 📜 LawOne occurrence, one FIR; fresh material is handled by further investigation under S.193(9) T.T. Antony.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe second FIR concerns the same incident and the same accused.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe second FIR is invalid and will be quashed; the police should do further investigation in the first case.
