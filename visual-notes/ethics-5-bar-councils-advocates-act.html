🏛️Bar Councils and the Advocates Act, 1961

Lawyers govern themselves: advocates elect State Bar Councils, State Councils send members to the Bar Council of India, and one Act of 1961 ties it all together.

1The big picture

Doctors have the National Medical Commission, chartered accountants their Institute. Lawyers in India govern themselves through elected Bar Councils: one in each State (and one or two shared by small States) and the Bar Council of India at the top. Advocates elect the members; the Councils enrol new lawyers, keep the roll, frame the rules of conduct, and discipline those who break them.

All this comes from one law: the Advocates Act, 1961, which replaced a patchwork of colonial laws with an autonomous, unified All-India Bar.

1

Nature

S.5

Bar Councils are statutory, elected bodies corporate under the Advocates Act, 1961, with perpetual succession and a common seal (S.5). They make the legal profession self-regulating. Their forerunners were the Bar Councils under the Indian Bar Councils Act, 1926, which were only advisory.

💬 In simple words
  • Bar Council: the elected body that regulates advocates in a State or in India.
📖 Word origin

Body corporate: a legal person that never dies (perpetual succession) and signs with a common seal (S.5).

🧠 Memory trick

Statutory · elected · autonomous: the profession regulates itself. The 1926 Councils only advised.

2

State Bar Council: constitution

S.3S.8

For each State (some share one). Members: the Advocate-General ex officio (in Delhi, the Additional Solicitor-General), and 15, 20 or 25 elected members, depending on the number of advocates on its roll, elected by the advocates by single transferable vote. It elects its own Chairman and Vice-Chairman. Term: five years (S.8).

🔢 Number trick

15, 20 or 25 elected members, by the size of the roll; 5-year term (S.8); single transferable vote.

🧠 Memory trick

The Advocate-General sits ex officio (in Delhi, the Additional Solicitor-General).

3

State Bar Council: functions

S.6

Admit persons as advocates on its roll; prepare and maintain the roll; entertain and determine cases of misconduct; safeguard the rights, privileges and interests of advocates; promote Bar associations, law reform, seminars and legal aid; manage its funds and welfare schemes for advocates; conduct elections; inspect universities when the BCI directs.

💬 In simple words
  • Professional misconduct: behaviour unfit for an advocate, punishable by the Bar Council.
  • Legal aid: free legal help for those who cannot afford a lawyer.
🎨 Picture it

The State Bar Council is the gatekeeper and police: it lets you in (enrolment), keeps the register (roll) and punishes misconduct.

🧠 Memory trick

Admit · Roll · Discipline · Protect · Promote · Welfare · Elections.

4

BCI: constitution

S.4

The Attorney-General and the Solicitor-General of India ex officio, and one member elected by each State Bar Council from among its members. It elects its Chairman and Vice-Chairman.

🧠 Memory trick

AG + SG ex officio, plus one member from each State Bar Council (S.4).

🎨 Picture it

Like a parliament of States: every State Council sends one representative to Delhi.

5

BCI: functions

S.7S.37

Lay down standards of professional conduct and etiquette; lay down the procedure for disciplinary committees; safeguard advocates' rights; promote law reform; deal with matters referred by State Councils; exercise general supervision and control over State Bar Councils; promote legal education and lay down its standards, recognise universities whose law degree qualifies for enrolment and inspect them; organise legal aid; recognise foreign qualifications on reciprocity; hear disciplinary appeals (S.37). Under its rules it also conducts the All India Bar Examination.

Example: a new law college in Telangana wants its LL.B. recognised for enrolment. The BCI inspects it and grants approval; its graduates then apply to the Bar Council of Telangana for enrolment and, after enrolment, take the AIBE conducted by the BCI.
💬 In simple words
  • Disciplinary committee: the Bar Council body that hears complaints against advocates.
  • Enrolment: being entered on the State Bar Council's roll, which makes you an advocate.
🎨 Picture it

The BCI is the lawmaker and head teacher: it writes the conduct rules and sets the standards for law colleges.

🧠 Memory trick

Conduct · Procedure · Supervise · Educate · Recognise · Legal aid · Appeals (S.37); and the AIBE under its rules.

6

Committees and rules

S.9S.9AS.10S.15S.49

Disciplinary committees (S.9); legal aid committees (S.9A); executive and enrolment committees of State Councils, and executive and legal education committees of the BCI (S.10). State Councils make rules with BCI approval (S.15); the BCI makes rules (S.49), including the BCI Rules on conduct, enrolment and legal education. The BCI may give directions to State Councils (S.48B).

🔢 Number trick

S.9 disciplinary · S.9A legal aid · S.10 executive, enrolment, legal education.

🧠 Memory trick

Rules: State (S.15, with BCI approval) · BCI (S.49) · directions to State Councils (S.48B).

🔁 Recall: State Bar Council = "gatekeeper and police" (enrol, roll, discipline). BCI = "lawmaker and head teacher" (standards of conduct, legal education, supervision).

1

Background

Enacted on the recommendations of the All India Bar Committee (1951) and the 14th Report of the Law Commission (1958). It consolidated the law on legal practitioners and repealed the Indian Bar Councils Act, 1926 and the relevant parts of the Legal Practitioners Act, 1879.

🔢 Number trick

1951 All India Bar Committee + 1958 14th Law Commission Report → 1961 Act; repealed the 1926 Act and parts of the 1879 Act.

2

Unified Bar

S.29S.16

One class of legal practitioners: advocates (S.29), replacing vakils, pleaders, mukhtars and others; advocates are either senior advocates or other advocates (S.16).

💬 In simple words
  • Senior advocate: an advocate given special designation for ability and standing at the Bar.
🎨 Picture it

Many uniforms (vakil, pleader, mukhtar) → one black coat: the advocate (S.29).

🧠 Memory trick

Only two kinds: senior advocates and other advocates (S.16).

3

Bar Councils

S.6S.7

Autonomous, elected State Bar Councils and the Bar Council of India (Chapter II), with defined functions (S.6, S.7) and committees.

🧠 Memory trick

Chapter II: State Councils (S.6) and the BCI (S.7), each with committees.

4

Enrolment and roll

S.24S.24AS.17S.18S.26

Qualifications for enrolment (S.24: citizen, 21 years, recognised law degree, fee), disqualifications (S.24A), State rolls (S.17), transfer between rolls (S.18) and enrolment procedure (S.26).

🧠 Memory trick

S.24 entry ticket: Citizen · 21 years · Law degree · Fee.

🔢 Number trick

S.17 State roll · S.18 transfer · S.24A disqualification · S.26 procedure.

5

Right to practise

S.29S.33S.30S.45

Only advocates may practise (S.29, S.33); every advocate may practise throughout India in all courts, tribunals and before authorities (S.30, in force 2011); illegal practice punishable (S.45).

🎨 Picture it

One enrolment = a pass to every court in India (S.30, in force 2011).

🔢 Number trick

Illegal practice: punishable under S.45.

6

Discipline

S.35S.37S.38

Disciplinary committees for professional or other misconduct (S.35), with appeals to the BCI (S.37) and the Supreme Court (S.38).

Example: before 1961, a pleader in a district court could not argue in the High Court. After the Act, a young advocate enrolled in Hyderabad can argue the same week in a Munsif court in Warangal and, subject to court rules, in the Supreme Court.
🧠 Memory trick

Judged by your peers: State committee (S.35) → BCI (S.37) → Supreme Court (S.38).

7

Other features

S.15S.49S.9AS.45S.48A

Rule-making powers of Bar Councils (S.15, S.49); funds and welfare schemes; legal aid committees (S.9A); penalty for illegal practice (S.45); the BCI's powers of revision and directions (S.48A, S.48B); the Central Government's rule-making power (S.49A).

🧠 Memory trick

Rules (15, 49) · Funds · Legal aid (9A) · Penalty (45) · Revision and directions (48A, 48B) · Centre's rules (49A).

🔁 Recall: The Act in one line: "One Bar, self-governed, one roll per State, practise anywhere, punished by your peers."

5Golden rules

  • ★ 1A State Bar Council has the Advocate-General as an ex officio member; the BCI has the Attorney-General and Solicitor-General ex officio.
  • ★ 2State Bar Councils enrol and discipline; the BCI sets standards and supervises.
  • ★ 3The BCI lays down standards of legal education and recognises law degrees (S.7(1)(h), (i)).
  • ★ 4The Advocates Act created one class of advocates and one All-India Bar.
  • ★ 5Bar Councils are statutory, elected and autonomous: the profession regulates itself.
