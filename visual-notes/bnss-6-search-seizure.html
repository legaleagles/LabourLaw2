🔍Summons, Warrants, Search and Seizure

From gentle to forceful: a summons invites, a warrant escorts, a proclamation announces, attachment takes property. And a search must be witnessed, listed and recorded.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

The court has four ways of getting a person or a thing before it, from gentle to forceful: a summons (a polite written call), a warrant (an order to arrest and bring), a proclamation (a public notice for someone who is hiding) and attachment of the absconder's property. For things, it uses a summons to produce, and if that fails, a search.

A search enters a person's home and privacy, so the law surrounds it with witnesses, lists and records; the BNSS now adds video recording, preferably on a mobile phone. Yet even an illegal search does not make the things found inadmissible; it only makes the court look at that evidence with more care.

2Summons and warrants

Exam question: Summons and warrants: meaning, contents, service, when a warrant is issued; proclamation and attachment

🧠 Say it: “Simple Service, Wise Seniors Pray”

  1. SSimpleSummons
  2. SServiceService of summons

  5. PPrayProclamation and attachment
1

Summons

S.63

A written order of the court requiring a person to appear (or produce a document), in duplicate, signed by the presiding officer and bearing the seal of the court; the BNSS also allows it in encrypted electronic form with the court's digital signature.

📖 Word origin

Summon comes from Latin summonere, "to remind": a polite written call from the court.

🧠 Memory trick

Written · Duplicate · Signed · Sealed, or encrypted with the court's digital signature under the BNSS.

2

Service of summons

S.64

Served by a police officer or court officer, personally by delivering a copy (the person signs a receipt), or by electronic communication; if he cannot be found, on an adult member of his family residing with him; if that also fails, by affixing a copy on a conspicuous part of his house. Special modes for companies, government servants and persons outside the jurisdiction.

🧠 Memory trick

Hand → Home → House-door: to the person himself, else an adult family member living with him, else a copy fixed on a conspicuous part of his house.

🌍 Real life

A summons on your phone? The BNSS allows service by electronic communication.

3

Warrant

A written order, signed and sealed, directing a police officer to arrest and produce a named person. It remains in force until cancelled or executed. A court may issue a warrant instead of a summons (or in addition) if it believes the person has absconded or will not obey the summons, or he fails to appear without reasonable excuse after due service. A bailable warrant carries an endorsement allowing release on bail.

🎨 Picture it

A summons is an invitation; a warrant is an escort.

🗣️ Jargon buster

A bailable warrant carries an endorsement that the person be released on bail on giving security.

4

🧠 Memory trick

Invitation v. escort: the person himself gets the summons; a police officer gets the warrant.

5

Proclamation and attachment

S.84S.85S.356BNS 356

If a person against whom a warrant is issued has absconded or is hiding, the court may publish a written proclamation requiring him to appear within at least 30 days; for offences punishable with 10 years or more, the court may declare him a proclaimed offender. It may also attach his property (S.85), and, if he still does not appear, the property may be sold. The BNSS also allows trial in absentia of a proclaimed offender in serious cases (S.356).

Example (non-cognizable case): in a defamation complaint (BNS 356), the Magistrate first issues a summons; when the accused avoids it twice, the court issues a warrant; when he hides, a proclamation, then attachment of his property.
🎨 Picture it

A public "wanted" notice: appear within at least 30 days. Still hiding? His property is attached (S.85).

🔢 Number trick

10 years or more → the court may declare him a proclaimed offender (S.84(4)).

🔁 Recall: Climb the ladder of force: Summons → Warrant → Proclamation → Attachment (S.63 → S.84 → S.85).

3Search and seizure

Exam question: Search and seizure: search warrants, search without warrant, procedure, limitations, constitutional validity, effect of illegal search

🧠 Say it: “Mother Wants Water, Please Send Cold Ice”

  3. WWaterSearch without warrant
  4. PPleaseProcedure and limitations
  5. SSendSeizure

1

Meaning

A search is the examination of a person, place or thing to find evidence of an offence; seizure is taking possession of the thing found.

🧠 Memory trick

Search = look; Seizure = take.

2

Search warrant

S.96S.97S.94

A court may issue a search warrant where it believes a person will not produce a document or thing when summoned (S.94), or the thing is not known to be in anyone's possession, or a general search is needed for inquiry or trial. A District or Sub-Divisional Magistrate or JMFC may issue a warrant to search a place suspected to contain stolen property, forged documents or other objectionable articles (S.97).

🎨 Picture it

Ask politely first: a summons to produce (S.94). If he won't, or no one knows who has it, the court sends a search warrant (S.96).

🔢 Number trick

S.97: a warrant to search a place suspected to contain stolen property or forged documents.

3

Search without warrant

S.185

The officer in charge or investigating officer may search without warrant if he has reasonable grounds to believe a thing needed for investigation is in a place and cannot be obtained otherwise without undue delay. He must record his grounds in writing before the search, search in person where possible, and send copies of the record to the nearest Magistrate within 48 hours; the owner gets a free copy.

🧠 Memory trick

Write first, then search: grounds recorded in writing before the search (S.185).

🔢 Number trick

48 hours: copies of the record go to the nearest Magistrate (S.185(5)).

4

Procedure and limitations

S.103S.105

The occupant of a closed place must allow free entry; the officer may break open if refused. Search is made in the presence of two or more independent and respectable inhabitants of the locality; a list of things seized is prepared and signed by them; the occupant may attend, and gets a copy of the list; a woman is searched only by a woman, with decency. The BNSS adds audio-video recording of the search and seizure, preferably by mobile phone, forwarded to the Magistrate (S.105).

🧠 Memory trick

Three things every lawful search needs: Written grounds (S.185) · Witnesses (S.103) · Video (S.105).

🔢 Number trick

2 or more independent, respectable witnesses of the locality; they sign the list; the occupant gets a copy.

5

Seizure

S.106Art. 20

A police officer may seize any property alleged or suspected to have been stolen, or found in circumstances that create suspicion of an offence; he reports the seizure to the Magistrate at once.

Case story M.P. Sharma: during a fraud investigation against a business group, warrants were issued and 34 premises were searched at once. The companies said this forced them to be "witnesses against themselves". An eight-judge bench held that search and seizure is not testimonial compulsion under Art. 20(3); the power to search is a necessary part of the State's duty to protect society. (After K.S. Puttaswamy, 2017, privacy is a fundamental right, so a search must also be lawful, necessary and proportionate.)
🌍 Real life

A constable finds property in circumstances that suggest an offence, such as goods that look stolen: he may seize it, and must report the seizure to the Magistrate at once (S.106).

6

Constitutional validity

Search and seizure do not violate Art. 20(3), because they do not compel the accused to be a witness against himself M.P. Sharma.

🧠 Memory trick

Art. 20(3) protects your tongue, not your cupboard. A search takes things; it does not make you a witness against yourself.

🎬 Case link

M.P. Sharma: 34 premises searched at once; still valid, because search is not testimonial compulsion.

7

Illegal search

An irregular or illegal search does not make the seizure or the evidence inadmissible (Pooran Mal v. Director of Inspection, 1974; Radha Kishan v. State of U.P., 1963), but the court examines such evidence with great care, and the officer may face action. Under special laws such as the NDPS Act, non-compliance with mandatory safeguards can vitiate the case.

🎨 Picture it

An illegal search is like a dirty glass of water: the court still drinks it, but checks it very carefully. The officer may face action.

🗣️ Jargon buster

Pooran Mal (1974) and Radha Kishan (1963): the evidence stays admissible. Exception: under special laws like the NDPS Act, breaking mandatory safeguards can vitiate the case.

🔁 Recall: Remember the "3 W" of a lawful search: Written grounds (S.185), Witnesses (S.103), Video (S.105). Missing any? The search is irregular, but the evidence is still looked at, carefully.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1A search must have two independent respectable witnesses of the locality, a list of seized things, and a copy to the occupant (S.103).
  • ★ 2The BNSS requires audio-video recording of searches and seizures (S.105).
  • ★ 3A police officer searching without warrant (S.185) must record his grounds in writing first and send copies to the nearest Magistrate.
  • ★ 4Illegal search does not make the evidence inadmissible; the court only scrutinises it more carefully. M.P. Sharma
  • ★ 5Search and seizure do not violate Art. 20(3), because they do not compel the accused to be a witness. M.P. Sharma

7Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. Police searched A's house without a warrant. A objects that the search is illegal, and nothing was found. Is the search legal?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhen can the police search a house without a warrant, and what follows if the rules are broken?
  2. 📜 LawS.185: an investigating officer may search without warrant on reasonable grounds, after recording them in writing, and send copies to the Magistrate within 48 hours. S.103: two independent local witnesses; list of seized things. S.105: video recording. Art. 21 / privacy.
  3. 🔍 ApplyIf the officer had recorded grounds that a thing needed for investigation was in the house and could not be got without delay, the search was legal even though nothing was found. If he did not record grounds, call witnesses, or video-record, it was illegal.
  4. ✅ ConclusionLegal only if S.185, S.103 and S.105 were followed; otherwise illegal, and A may complain to the Magistrate and the SP. Finding nothing does not by itself make it illegal.

2. A policeman entered B's house without a warrant, searched it and seized some material illegally. What is B's remedy?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat remedies does a person have against an illegal search and seizure?
  2. 📜 LawS.185, S.103, S.105 BNSS. S.106: seizure must be reported to the Magistrate. The court may order custody or return of seized property pending inquiry or trial. Criminal trespass (BNS 329) and wrongful search by a public servant. Art. 21, writ jurisdiction. Illegally seized evidence remains admissible but is scrutinised M.P. Sharma.
  3. 🔍 ApplyB can ask the Magistrate to examine the search record and order return of the seized articles, complain against the officer, and seek a writ.
  4. ✅ ConclusionB can apply to the Magistrate for return of the property and action, file a complaint against the officer, and approach the High Court under Art. 226; the evidence is not automatically excluded.

3. The accused disappears after a warrant is issued and cannot be found for months. How can the court secure him?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat processes are available against an absconding accused?
  2. 📜 LawS.84: proclamation requiring him to appear within at least 30 days; proclaimed offender for offences of 10 years or more. S.85: attachment of his movable and immovable property, which may be sold if he does not appear. S.356: inquiry, trial and judgment in his absence in serious cases.
  3. 🔍 ApplyAfter the warrant fails, the court publishes the proclamation (in a newspaper, at his house, in court) and attaches his property; if he still hides, the trial can proceed in absentia.
  4. ✅ ConclusionProclamation (S.84) → attachment and sale of property (S.85) → trial in absentia (S.356).

4. Investigators need a shop's CCTV footage and a suspect's WhatsApp chats. Can they be compelled?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan documents and electronic records be compelled during investigation or trial?
  2. 📜 LawS.94: a court, or an officer in charge of a police station, may issue a summons or written order to produce any document, electronic communication or digital evidence. S.96: search warrant if it will not be produced. S.105: audio-video recording of seizure.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe shop owner can be asked to produce the footage; the suspect's device can be seized with a proper record if he will not cooperate.
  4. ✅ ConclusionYes: an order under S.94 to produce, and if refused, a search warrant (S.96) and seizure recorded on video (S.105).
