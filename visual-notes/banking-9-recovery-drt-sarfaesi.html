🏚️Recovery: NPAs, DRT and SARFAESI

Two doors for a bank whose loan has gone bad: the fast tribunal (DRT), or, if it holds security, SARFAESI: seize and sell without going to court.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

Before 1993, a bank that wanted its money back filed an ordinary civil suit and waited ten or fifteen years while the borrower's factory rusted. Two laws changed this. The Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 created special tribunals (DRTs) only for bank dues. The SARFAESI Act, 2002 went further: a secured bank can take possession of the security and sell it without going to court at all, after giving the borrower a 60-day notice.

Think of it as two doors. The DRT door: go to a fast tribunal for a recovery certificate. The SARFAESI door: if you hold a mortgage or hypothecation, act on the security yourself, and let the borrower go to the DRT if he feels wronged.

2Non-performing assets

Exam question: Non-performing assets: meaning and classification

🧠 Say it: “My Watch Can Easily Work”

  1. MMyMeaning
  2. WWatchWhen overdue
  3. CCanClassification
  4. EEasilyEarly warning (SMA)
  5. WWorkWhy it matters
1

Meaning

S.2(1)(o)

An asset (loan or advance) ceases to generate income for the bank when interest or principal is not paid. Under RBI's prudential norms, it becomes a non-performing asset when interest or instalment remains overdue for more than 90 days. SARFAESI S.2(1)(o) adopts RBI's classification.

🎨 Picture it

A loan is a cow that gives milk (interest). No milk for 90 days = non-performing.

🔢 Number trick

90 days overdue makes an NPA.

2

When overdue

Term loan: interest/instalment overdue over 90 days; overdraft/cash credit: account "out of order" for over 90 days; bills: overdue over 90 days; crop loans: two crop seasons (short-duration crops) or one season (long-duration crops).

🌍 Real life

Crop loans follow the farm calendar: two crop seasons for short-duration crops, one for long-duration crops.

3

Classification

(1) Standard asset (performing); (2) Sub-standard: NPA for up to 12 months; (3) Doubtful: sub-standard for 12 months; (4) Loss: identified as uncollectible but not yet written off. Higher categories need higher provisions out of profits.

🧠 Memory trick

Standard → Sub-standard → Doubtful → Loss: no milk for 90 days, a year as sub-standard, then the cow is dead.

4

Early warning (SMA)

SMA-0 (overdue up to 30 days), SMA-1 (31–60), SMA-2 (61–90): to flag stress before an account turns NPA.

🔢 Number trick

SMA-0 up to 30 days · SMA-1 31–60 · SMA-2 61–90: the warning lights before NPA.

5

Why it matters

NPAs cut a bank's income and capital, so it lends less; depositors and the economy suffer. Hence the recovery laws: DRTs, SARFAESI, ARCs and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.

🧠 Memory trick

Less income → less capital → less lending. Hence DRTs, SARFAESI, ARCs and the IBC.

🔁 Recall: A loan is like a cow: it gives milk (interest). If it gives no milk for 90 days, it is "non-performing"; after a year "doubtful"; and a dead cow is a "loss".

3Debts Recovery Tribunals

Exam question: Debts Recovery Tribunals: constitution, jurisdiction, powers, procedure and role

🧠 Say it: “Big Cats Jump Past Puppies, Running After Rats”

  1. BBigBackground
  2. CCatsConstitution
  3. JJumpJurisdiction
  4. PPastProcedure
  5. PPuppiesPowers
  6. RRunningRecovery
  7. AAfterAppeal
  8. RRatsRole
1

Background

On the Narasimham and Tiwari Committees' advice, Parliament passed the Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 1993 (renamed the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act in 2016) to create special tribunals for fast recovery of bank dues. Its validity was upheld Delhi High Court Bar Assn..

🔢 Number trick

RDDBFI Act 1993, renamed the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act in 2016.

2

Constitution

S.3S.8

The Central Government sets up DRTs (S.3), each with a Presiding Officer qualified to be a District Judge, and Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunals (S.8), each with a Chairperson qualified to be a High Court Judge.

🧠 Memory trick

DRT = a District Judge-level officer; DRAT = a High Court Judge-level chairperson.

3

Jurisdiction

S.17S.18

Applications by banks and financial institutions to recover debts of ₹20 lakh or more. Civil courts have no jurisdiction over such claims (S.18), except the High Courts and Supreme Court under Arts. 226 and 32. The DRT also hears borrowers' appeals under S.17 SARFAESI.

🔢 Number trick

₹20 lakh or more. Civil courts have no jurisdiction over such claims (S.18).

🎬 Case link

Delhi High Court Bar Assn.: Parliament could take bank recovery away from civil courts.

4

Procedure

S.19S.22

The bank files an application where the defendant resides or carries on business, or where the cause of action arose; the defendant files a written statement and may claim set-off or counter-claim. The DRT is not bound by the CPC, follows natural justice and regulates its own procedure (S.22).

🧠 Memory trick

No CPC speed-breakers: natural justice, written statements, set-off and counter-claims allowed.

5

Powers

S.22(2)S.19

A civil court's powers for summoning, discovery, affidavits, commissions, review and ex parte orders (S.22(2)); interim orders of attachment, injunction against transfer, and appointment of a receiver (S.19).

🧠 Memory trick

A civil court's powers, plus attach · injunct · receiver in the interim.

6

Recovery

S.25S.28

The DRT issues a recovery certificate; the Recovery Officer recovers it by attachment and sale of property, arrest and detention of the defendant, or appointment of a receiver (S.25, S.28).

🎨 Picture it

The Recovery Officer is the tow truck: attachment, sale, arrest, receiver.

7

Appeal

S.20S.21

To the DRAT within 45 days (S.20); the appellant must deposit 50% of the amount determined, which the DRAT may reduce to not less than 25% (S.21).

Real-world example: a bank that lent ₹5 crore to a steel unit files before the DRT; within months the DRT attaches the unit's property so it cannot be sold off, then issues a recovery certificate, and the Recovery Officer auctions the property.
🔢 Number trick

45 days to the DRAT; deposit 50%, reducible to 25%.

🌍 Real life

A bank's ₹5 crore loan to a steel unit: the DRT attaches the property, issues a recovery certificate, and the Recovery Officer auctions it.

8

Role

Faster and specialised recovery, keeping bank credit flowing and reducing NPAs. Problems remain: vacancies, backlogs and long delays, so SARFAESI and the IBC now carry much of the load.

🧠 Memory trick

Faster, specialised recovery, but vacancies and backlogs remain.

🔁 Recall: DRT is a fast-track court for banks: ₹20 lakh ticket to enter, no CPC speed-breakers, a Recovery Officer as the tow truck, and the DRAT as the toll booth (pay 50% to pass).

4SARFAESI Act

Exam question: SARFAESI Act, 2002: securitisation, asset reconstruction and enforcement of security interest

🧠 Say it: “Old Sailors Ate Every Sweet Easter Bun”

  1. OOldObject
  2. SSailorsSecuritisation
  3. AAteAsset reconstruction
  4. EEveryEnforcement (S.13)
  5. SSweetSafeguards
  6. EEasterExclusions (S.31)
  7. BBunBorrower's remedies
1

Object

The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 lets banks and financial institutions (1) sell bad loans to asset reconstruction companies, and (2) enforce their security without the intervention of a court. Its validity was upheld Mardia Chemicals.

🧠 Memory trick

Sell · Seize · Safeguard: sell bad loans to ARCs, seize the security, but with safeguards.

2

Securitisation

S.2(1)(z)

Acquisition of financial assets by an asset reconstruction company (ARC) from an originator (bank), funded by issuing security receipts to qualified buyers. The ARC holds the loans in a separate trust/scheme for each deal, which acts as the special purpose vehicle (SPV), so that the assets are ring-fenced and the receipt-holders are paid from recoveries.

🎨 Picture it

The bank packs bad loans into a parcel and sells it to an ARC, which issues security receipts to buyers.

3

Asset reconstruction

S.3S.5S.9

ARCs must be registered with RBI (S.3). After acquiring a bank's loan (S.5), the ARC steps into the bank's shoes and may change or take over the borrower's management, sell or lease the business, reschedule debts, settle dues, convert debt into shares, or enforce the security (S.9).

🧠 Memory trick

The ARC steps into the bank's shoes: take over management, sell the business, reschedule, settle.

4

Enforcement (S.13)

S.13S.13(2)S.13(4)S.14

Where a borrower in default has his account classified as an NPA, the secured creditor gives a notice under S.13(2) to pay the full dues within 60 days. If he does not, it may under S.13(4): take possession of the secured asset (and sell or lease it), take over the management of the business, appoint a manager, or require the borrower's debtors to pay it directly. The District Magistrate / Chief Metropolitan Magistrate helps take physical possession (S.14).

🔢 Number trick

60-day notice (S.13(2)) → possession, management or sale (S.13(4)).

🌍 Real life

A company stops paying interest; the account becomes an NPA; the bank serves a 60-day notice and then takes possession of the factory.

5

Safeguards

S.13(3A)S.13(8)S.13(9)

The bank must consider the borrower's objections and communicate reasons for rejecting them within 15 days (S.13(3A)); sale follows the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002 (valuation, 30 days' sale notice); the borrower can redeem the asset by paying the dues before the auction notice is published (S.13(8)); in a consortium, creditors with 60% in value must agree (S.13(9)).

🔢 Number trick

Reasons for rejecting objections within 15 days (S.13(3A)); 30 days' sale notice; redemption before sale.

🎬 Case link

Mardia Chemicals: Act valid, but a 75% pre-deposit to be heard was struck down; objections must get reasons.

6

Exclusions (S.31)

S.31

No SARFAESI for: liens and pledges of movables; aircraft and ships; agricultural land; security for a financial asset not exceeding ₹1 lakh; cases where the dues are less than 20% of the principal and interest.

🧠 Memory trick

Not for: pledges and liens · ships and aircraft · farm land · ₹1 lakh or less · dues under 20%.

7

Borrower's remedies

S.13(4)S.17S.18

Application to the DRT within 45 days of the S.13(4) measure (S.17), with no pre-deposit; appeal to the DRAT (S.18) on depositing 50% (reducible to 25%). A writ petition is not the normal remedy Satyawati Tondon.

🔢 Number trick

45 days to the DRT (S.17), no pre-deposit; then the DRAT (S.18).

🎬 Case link

Satyawati Tondon: no writ petition when the S.17 remedy before the DRT exists.

🔁 Recall: SARFAESI in three words: sell (bad loans to ARCs), seize (security after 60 days' notice), safeguard (reasons in 15 days, DRT in 45 days).

5Sections and numbers

  • 90 daysoverdue period for an NPA · SMA-0/1/2 = up to 30/60/90 days overdue
  • ₹20 lakhminimum debt for a DRT application (RDB Act S.1(4), notified 2018)
  • 60 · 15 daysS.13(2) notice period · bank's reply to objections (S.13(3A))
  • 45 daysappeal to DRT (S.17 SARFAESI; S.20 RDB Act to DRAT)
  • 50% (→ 25%)deposit for appeal to DRAT (S.18 SARFAESI; S.21 RDB Act)

6Case stories

Mardia Chemicals Ltd. v. Union of India

(2004) 4 SCC 311 · decided 8 Apr 2004

📖 What happenedBorrowers challenged SARFAESI, which let banks seize security without going to court and required a 75% deposit before a borrower could even be heard by the DRT.
❓ The questionIs SARFAESI constitutional, and is the 75% pre-deposit for a borrower's appeal valid?
⚖️ HeldThe Act is valid, but the 75% pre-deposit for an appeal to the DRT was struck down as oppressive; banks must consider objections and give reasons.

United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon

(2010) 8 SCC 110

📖 What happenedA guarantor facing SARFAESI action went straight to the High Court under Art. 226, which stayed the bank.
❓ The questionShould a High Court entertain a writ against SARFAESI action despite the remedy before the DRT?
⚖️ HeldHigh Courts must not entertain writ petitions against SARFAESI action when an effective remedy exists under S.17 before the DRT.

Union of India v. Delhi High Court Bar Association

(2002) 4 SCC 275

📖 What happenedLawyers challenged the law that moved bank recovery suits from civil courts to new Debts Recovery Tribunals; the Delhi High Court struck it down.
❓ The questionWas Parliament competent to set up tribunals to decide bank recovery claims?
⚖️ HeldThe Supreme Court upheld the Act: Parliament could create tribunals for bank recovery under its power over banking.

7Golden rules

  • ★ 1A loan becomes an NPA when interest or instalment is overdue for more than 90 days.
  • ★ 2SARFAESI lets a secured creditor enforce its security without the court, but it does not apply to pledges, liens, agricultural land, loans up to ₹1 lakh, or where dues are below 20% of principal and interest (S.31).
  • ★ 3SARFAESI is valid, but the borrower must get a fair hearing and reasons, and cannot be made to deposit 75% to be heard. Mardia Chemicals
  • ★ 4High Courts should not entertain writs that bypass the S.17 remedy before the DRT. Satyawati Tondon
  • ★ 5Parliament could take bank recovery away from civil courts and give it to DRTs. Delhi High Court Bar Assn.

8Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. Banban Bank is a creditor of CVC Pvt. Ltd. The company makes losses and stops paying interest. Can the bank use S.13 of SARFAESI? In what circumstances and on what procedural conditions?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhen and how can a bank enforce its security under S.13?
  2. 📜 LawS.13(1)–(4), (3A), (8), (9), S.14, S.17, S.31 SARFAESI; RBI NPA norms; Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002 Mardia Chemicals.
  3. 🔍 ApplyConditions: (1) the bank must be a secured creditor (mortgage, hypothecation over CVC's assets); (2) CVC's account must be an NPA (overdue over 90 days); (3) the dues must not be below 20% of principal and interest, and the security not agricultural land or a pledge (S.31); (4) in a consortium, 60% in value must agree. Procedure: S.13(2) notice → 60 days → consider objections and reply in 15 days → S.13(4) possession/management → DM's help (S.14) → valuation and sale with 30 days' notice. If an insolvency process under the IBC has begun, the moratorium bars it.
  4. ✅ ConclusionYes, if it is a secured creditor and the account is an NPA; it must follow the notice-objection-measure steps, and CVC can challenge the action before the DRT within 45 days. If the loan is unsecured, the bank must go to the DRT or file a suit instead.

2. Bank X has many NPAs and wants to transfer some of them to a third party. How can it do so under SARFAESI?

  1. 🎯 IssueHow are bad loans transferred under the Act?
  2. 📜 LawS.2(1)(z) securitisation; S.3 registered ARCs; S.5: acquisition of financial assets by an ARC, by agreement on terms; the ARC is then treated as the lender, with S.9 and S.13 powers; S.7: issue of security receipts to qualified buyers.
  3. 🔍 ApplyBank X can sell the NPAs to an RBI-registered ARC for cash or security receipts, usually by auction. The ARC places them in a trust (SPV) and issues security receipts to qualified buyers; it recovers by restructuring, settlement or enforcing the security. The borrowers are informed of the assignment.
  4. ✅ ConclusionBy assigning the NPAs to a registered asset reconstruction company under S.5, which securitises them through security receipts and recovers them using S.9 and S.13 powers.

3. A bank takes possession of a guarantor's house under S.13(4). She files a writ petition in the High Court, which stays the action. Is this proper?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs a writ the right remedy against SARFAESI action?
  2. 📜 LawS.17: an aggrieved person may apply to the DRT within 45 days; S.18: appeal to DRAT. High Courts should not entertain writs where this effective remedy exists, except in exceptional cases Satyawati Tondon.
  3. 🔍 ApplyShe has a full remedy before the DRT, which can set aside the measure and restore possession.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo. The High Court should decline to interfere; she must apply to the DRT under S.17 within 45 days.
