1The big picture

Two brothers quarrel over their late father's shop. A civil suit could take ten years, cost lakhs and end their relationship for ever. Instead, they can talk it out themselves (negotiation), call a neutral elder to help them talk (mediation), let that neutral suggest terms (conciliation), or let a chosen judge-like person decide for them (arbitration). These are the doors of Alternative Dispute Resolution.

The difference between the doors is who holds the pen. In negotiation, mediation and conciliation the parties write the final settlement; in arbitration the arbitrator writes a binding award. India's law supports all of them: the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, S.89 CPC, the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (Lok Adalats) and the Mediation Act, 2023.

1

Meaning

Alternative Dispute Resolution means methods of settling disputes outside the regular courts, with the help of a neutral third person or by the parties themselves: negotiation, mediation, conciliation, arbitration, Lok Adalat and their online forms. It is better called appropriate dispute resolution, since it supplements, not replaces, the courts.

💬 In simple words
  • ADR: ways of settling disputes outside the regular courtroom trial.
  • Arbitration: parties choosing a private judge (arbitrator) whose decision binds them.
🎨 Picture it

Doors outside the courtroom: negotiation, mediation, conciliation, arbitration, Lok Adalat, and their online forms.

📖 Word origin

ADR is often read as Appropriate Dispute Resolution: choose the method that suits the dispute.

2

Why ADR

Crores of cases are pending in Indian courts; trials are slow, costly and formal; relationships break. ADR offers speed, low cost, privacy and preserved relationships. It also helps business: investors want quick, expert and neutral dispute settlement.

🌍 Real life

Two brothers fight over their late father's shop: a suit could take ten years, cost lakhs and end the relationship. ADR offers speed, low cost and peace.

3

Constitutional and legal base

Art. 39AArt. 21Art. 51S.89O.X r.1

Art. 39A (equal justice and free legal aid), Art. 21 (speedy justice: Hussainara Khatoon), Art. 51(d) (settlement of international disputes by arbitration). Statutes: Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996; S.89 and O.X r.1A–1C CPC; Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987; Family Courts Act, 1984 (S.9 settlement); Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (conciliation); Commercial Courts Act, 2015 (S.12A pre-institution mediation); Mediation Act, 2023.

💬 In simple words
  • Conciliation: a neutral person helping the parties and suggesting terms to settle.
  • Mediation: a neutral person helping the parties talk and reach their own settlement.
🧠 Memory trick

39A · 21 · 51(d): equal justice and legal aid, speedy justice, international arbitration.

🎬 Case link

Hussainara Khatoon: speedy justice is part of Art. 21.

4

Scope

Commercial and contractual disputes, family disputes (other than status), property and partition, consumer, labour, motor accident claims, banking dues (Lok Adalat), and compoundable offences. Not suitable: criminal prosecution of non-compoundable offences, matrimonial status and guardianship decisions, insolvency, matters involving the public at large or fraud needing a trial.

💬 In simple words
  • Lok Adalat: a people's court that settles cases by compromise; its award is final.
🧠 Memory trick

Yes: commercial, family (not status), property, consumer, labour, accident claims, bank dues. No: serious crimes, matrimonial status, guardianship, insolvency.

5

Advantages

S.19S.66S.75

(1) Speed; (2) low cost; (3) informal and flexible procedure (not bound by CPC or the Evidence Act: S.19, S.66); (4) confidential (S.75); (5) party autonomy: parties choose the neutral, the place, the language; (6) expert neutral (an engineer for a construction dispute); (7) relationships preserved (win-win, not win-lose); (8) finality: limited appeals; (9) reduces the burden on courts.

Example: a builder and a flat-owners' association disagree about delayed amenities. Instead of five years in court, a retired engineer-arbitrator visits the site, hears both sides in three months, and makes an award the builder must carry out.
💬 In simple words
  • Arbitral award: the final decision of the arbitrator.
🧠 Memory trick

S-C-I-C-E-R-F: Speed · Cost · Informality · Confidentiality · Expertise · Relationships · Finality.

🌍 Real life

A builder and a flat-owners' association: a retired engineer-arbitrator visits the site and decides in three months instead of five years.

6

Disadvantages

S.9S.34S.36

No binding precedent; limited appeal even if the award is wrong; arbitration can become costly (arbitrators' fees, institutional charges); a stronger party can dominate a weak one; courts still intervene through S.9, S.34 and S.36, causing delay; enforcement of a mediated settlement may need the court.

🧠 Memory trick

No precedent · limited appeal · can get costly · stronger party may dominate · court intervention still delays.

7

Conclusion

S.89

ADR is not a rival to courts but a partner of the justice system: courts decide what needs adjudication, ADR settles the rest. As the Supreme Court said in Afcons (2010), S.89 makes ADR a mandatory consideration for every civil court.

🎬 Case link

Afcons: S.89 makes ADR a mandatory consideration for every civil court. ADR is the court's partner, not its rival.

🔁 Recall: Remember the word "S-C-I-C-E-R-F" for advantages: Speed, Cost, Informality, Confidentiality, Expertise, Relationships, Finality. Count them on your fingers.

1

Negotiation

The parties (or their representatives) talk directly to reach an agreement. No third party. Most informal and private. Example: a tenant and landlord agree on a rent increase.

🎨 Picture it

The top step: the parties alone, no third person.

🌍 Real life

A tenant and landlord agree on a rent increase over tea.

2

Mediation

A neutral mediator facilitates communication so the parties find their own solution; he does not impose or usually propose terms. Used in family, community and commercial disputes (court-annexed mediation, Mediation Act, 2023).

🎨 Picture it

The mediator holds the torch, not the pen: he lights the way; the parties write their own solution.

3

Conciliation

S.67(4)S.73S.74

A neutral conciliator assists the parties and may make proposals for settlement (S.67(4), A&C Act). If they agree, they sign a settlement agreement which has the status of an arbitral award (S.73, S.74).

🎨 Picture it

The conciliator may suggest terms (S.67(4)).

🔢 Number trick

A signed settlement has the status of an arbitral award (S.73, S.74).

4

Arbitration

S.7S.35S.36

The parties, by an arbitration agreement (S.7), refer their dispute to an arbitral tribunal, which hears both sides and makes a binding award, enforceable like a court decree (S.35, S.36). It is adjudication by a private judge chosen by the parties.

💬 In simple words
  • Tribunal: a specialised body that decides disputes in one field, like tax or service matters.
🎨 Picture it

A private judge the parties chose, whose award binds like a decree (S.35, S.36).

5

Table of differences

S.62S.18

(1) Third party: none (negotiation); facilitator (mediation); facilitator who can propose (conciliation); decision-maker (arbitration). (2) Outcome: agreement in the first three; award in arbitration. (3) Binding: only if the parties sign the settlement; an award binds even the unwilling party. (4) Prior agreement: arbitration needs a written arbitration agreement; conciliation can start by invitation (S.62). (5) Procedure: arbitration follows natural justice (S.18) and is more formal. (6) Party control: highest in negotiation, lowest in arbitration. (7) Statute: arbitration and conciliation in the 1996 Act; mediation in the Mediation Act, 2023; negotiation is unregulated.

One dispute, four doors: a supplier says ₹10 lakh is due; the buyer says the goods were faulty. They may negotiate a ₹7 lakh deal; a mediator may help them reach it; a conciliator may suggest "₹7 lakh in two instalments"; or an arbitrator may hear evidence and award ₹8 lakh, which the buyer must pay.
🌍 Real life

A supplier claims ₹10 lakh; the buyer says the goods were faulty. They negotiate ₹7 lakh, a mediator helps, a conciliator suggests two instalments, or an arbitrator decides.

6

ADR v. court

S.8S.9S.36S.34

Courts: public, formal, bound by CPC and Evidence Act, judge imposed by the State, appeals, precedent, delay. ADR: private, flexible, neutral chosen by parties, limited or no appeal, faster. But courts support ADR (S.8 reference, S.9 interim measures, S.36 enforcement) and control it (S.34 setting aside).

💬 In simple words
  • Setting aside: a court cancelling an award on limited grounds.
🧠 Memory trick

Court: public · formal · State judge · appeals · precedent. ADR: private · flexible · chosen neutral · limited appeal · faster.

🔁 Recall: Picture a staircase of control: on the top step the parties alone (negotiation); one step down a helper (mediation); then a helper who suggests (conciliation); at the bottom a judge they chose (arbitration).

1

Meaning

Negotiation is a voluntary, direct discussion between the parties (or their lawyers or agents) to reach a mutually acceptable agreement, without any third party. It is the oldest and most common form of dispute resolution.

🧠 Memory trick

No third person: the oldest and commonest way to settle.

2

Features

Voluntary; informal; private and confidential; parties keep full control; flexible outcome; no statute governs it; the agreement binds only as a contract once concluded.

🧠 Memory trick

Voluntary · Informal · Private · Parties in control · Flexible; binds as a contract once agreed.

3

Stages

(1) Preparation: know your interests, your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement) and limits; (2) opening and exchange of information; (3) bargaining: proposals and concessions; (4) closing: agreement put in writing.

Example: an employee and employer negotiate a settlement of unpaid dues: the employee knows that if talks fail he can go to the Labour Court (his BATNA), so he does not accept less than what the court would likely give.
📖 Word origin

BATNA = Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement: what you get if talks fail.

🌍 Real life

Buying a saree: know your budget and the shop next door (your BATNA), then open, bargain, close.

4

Styles and skills

Positional (haggling over positions) v. principled/interest-based negotiation (Fisher and Ury, Getting to Yes): separate people from the problem, focus on interests not positions, invent options for mutual gain, insist on objective criteria. Skills: active listening, patience, clear communication, creativity and honesty.

🧠 Memory trick

Getting to Yes, in four words: People · Interests · Options · Criteria.

🔁 Recall: Think of buying a saree: prepare (know your budget and the shop next door = BATNA), open, bargain, close.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1ADR is "appropriate" dispute resolution: choose the method that suits the dispute; courts remain for what needs adjudication.
  • ★ 2From negotiation to arbitration, party control falls and third-party power rises.
  • ★ 3Only arbitration ends in a decision imposed on the parties; the others end in an agreement.
  • ★ 4Article 39A (equal justice) and Article 21 (speedy justice) give ADR its constitutional base.
  • ★ 5Not every dispute can go to ADR: crimes, matrimonial status, guardianship, insolvency and similar matters stay with courts.
