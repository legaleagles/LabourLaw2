🪪Enrolment, Admission and Disqualification of Advocates

Enrolment is the profession's front gate: a checklist to pass (S.24), a gate that stays shut for two years after moral turpitude (S.24A), and a fee capped at ₹750.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

Priya finishes her LL.B. and wants to argue her first case. A law degree alone does not make her an advocate. She must apply to the State Bar Council, show that she is an Indian citizen of at least 21 with a recognised law degree, pay the enrolment fee fixed by the Act (₹750), and have no disqualifying conviction. Her name then goes on the State roll, and she gets a certificate of enrolment. To keep practising, she must also clear the All India Bar Examination.

Enrolment is the profession's front gate: it keeps out the unqualified and the unworthy, and it gives the enrolled advocate the right to practise across India.

1

Who is an advocate

S.2(1)(a)S.29S.33

An advocate is a person entered in any roll under the Advocates Act, 1961 (S.2(1)(a)). Only advocates may practise law (S.29, S.33).

🧠 Memory trick

An advocate = a name on a roll (S.2(1)(a)). A law degree alone is not enough.

2

Qualifications (S.24)

S.24

A person may be admitted if he: (a) is a citizen of India (a foreign national may be admitted on reciprocity); (b) has completed 21 years of age; (c) holds a degree in law from a university recognised by the BCI (three-year course after graduation, or five-year integrated course), or a foreign degree recognised by the BCI; (d) fulfils the other conditions in the State Bar Council's rules; and (e) pays the stamp duty and the enrolment fee.

🧠 Memory trick

C-A-D-F: Citizen · Age 21 · Degree recognised by the BCI · Fee (and the State Council's other conditions).

🗣️ Jargon buster

A foreign national may be admitted on reciprocity: if his country lets Indians practise there.

3

Procedure (S.25, S.26)

S.25S.26S.10S.26(1)S.26A

Apply in the prescribed form to the State Bar Council within whose jurisdiction one proposes to practise (S.25). The enrolment committee (S.10) examines the application; if it proposes to refuse, it must refer the application to the BCI for its opinion, with reasons (S.26(1)). Names can later be removed if wrongly entered (S.26A).

🧠 Memory trick

Apply (S.25) → enrolment committee (S.10) → refuse only after the BCI's opinion (S.26) → remove if wrongly entered (S.26A).

🎨 Picture it

The State Council holds the key, but cannot slam the door alone: a refusal must go to the BCI first.

4

Roll and certificate

S.17S.22S.18

Each State Bar Council keeps a roll in two parts, senior advocates and other advocates (S.17). An enrolled advocate gets a certificate of enrolment (S.22) and may get his name transferred to another State roll (S.18).

🎨 Picture it

The roll has two pages: senior advocates and other advocates (S.17).

🔢 Number trick

S.22 certificate of enrolment · S.18 transfer to another State roll.

5

Fee

S.24(1)(f)

S.24(1)(f) fixes it at ₹600 to the State Bar Council and ₹150 to the BCI (₹100 and ₹25 for SC/ST candidates). Bar Councils cannot collect more under any other name Gaurav Kumar.

🔢 Number trick

₹600 to the State Council + ₹150 to the BCI = ₹750 (SC/ST: ₹100 + ₹25 = ₹125).

🎬 Case link

Gaurav Kumar: no extra charges under any other name; ₹15,000–₹40,000 enrolment bills struck down (prospectively).

6

All India Bar Examination

Since 2010, the BCI requires new advocates to pass the AIBE to get a certificate of practice. A Constitution Bench upheld the BCI's power to hold it, and allowed final-year students to take it Bonnie Foi Law College.

Example: Priya, 23, an Indian citizen with an LL.B. from Osmania University, applies to the Bar Council of Telangana, pays ₹750, is enrolled, and later clears the AIBE. She can now practise in any court in India.
🔢 Number trick

AIBE since 2010, for a certificate of practice.

🎬 Case link

Bonnie Foi Law College: the BCI may hold the AIBE; final-year students may take it.

7

Significance

S.30

Enrolment ensures that only qualified and fit persons enter the profession, brings them under the Bar Council's discipline, protects the public from unqualified practitioners, and gives the advocate the right to practise throughout India (S.30).

🧠 Memory trick

Enrolment: only the fit get in · they come under discipline · the public is protected · the advocate may practise anywhere (S.30).

🔁 Recall: Enrolment checklist: "C-A-D-F": Citizen, Age 21, Degree recognised, Fee ₹750. Then the roll, the certificate, and the AIBE.

3Disqualification (S.24A)

Exam question: Disqualification of advocates (S.24A) and other bars

🧠 Say it: “Good People Eat Oranges”

  1. GGoodGrounds (S.24A)
  2. PPeoplePeriod
  3. EEatExceptions

1

Grounds (S.24A)

S.24A

No person shall be admitted as an advocate if he: (a) is convicted of an offence involving moral turpitude; (b) is convicted of an offence under the Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955 (now the Protection of Civil Rights Act); or (c) is dismissed or removed from employment or office under the State on a charge involving moral turpitude.

🧠 Memory trick

Three Ms: Moral-turpitude conviction · untouchability Misdeed (PCR Act) · Moral-turpitude dismissal from State service.

2

Period

The disqualification ceases two years after the release from imprisonment, or after the dismissal or removal, as the case may be.

🔢 Number trick

The bar lasts 2 years from release or dismissal.

🌍 Real life

A clerk dismissed for bribery in 2022 finishes his LL.B. in 2023: he cannot be enrolled before 2024.

3

Exceptions

It does not apply to a person who, though found guilty, has been dealt with under the Probation of Offenders Act, 1958. "Moral turpitude" means conduct that is base, vile or contrary to accepted morality, such as cheating, forgery, bribery or sexual offences.

Example: a government clerk dismissed in 2022 for taking bribes completes his LL.B. in 2023. He cannot be enrolled before 2024 (two years after dismissal); after that, he may apply, subject to the State Bar Council's scrutiny.
🧠 Memory trick

Released on probation (Probation of Offenders Act, 1958)? No disqualification.

📖 Word origin

Moral turpitude: base, vile conduct, such as cheating, forgery, bribery or sexual offences.

4

Other bars

S.35S.26A

An advocate cannot be a full-time salaried employee of any person, firm or company while practising (BCI Rule 49), nor carry on business; one who takes up such employment must suspend practice and inform the Bar Council. An advocate whose name is removed for misconduct cannot practise (S.35). Enrolment obtained by false statements can be cancelled (S.26A).

🧠 Memory trick

No salaried job (Rule 49) · no business · removed for misconduct (S.35) · false enrolment cancelled (S.26A).

🔁 Recall: S.24A in three Ms and a 2: Moral turpitude conviction, untouchability Misdeed, Moral-turpitude dismissal; bar lasts 2 years.

5Case stories

Gaurav Kumar v. Union of India

2024 INSC 558 · decided 30 Jul 2024

📖 What happenedLaw graduates complained that State Bar Councils charged ₹15,000 to over ₹40,000 for enrolment under various heads, though the Act fixes ₹750.
❓ The questionCan Bar Councils charge enrolment fees and other charges beyond the amount fixed in S.24(1)(f) of the Advocates Act?
⚖️ HeldState Bar Councils and the BCI cannot charge enrolment fees or other charges beyond those fixed by S.24(1)(f) (₹750; ₹125 for SC/ST). Excessive fees violate equality and the right to practise. The ruling applies prospectively.

Bar Council of India v. Bonnie Foi Law College

Supreme Court, 2023 · decided 10 Feb 2023

📖 What happenedThe power of the BCI to require new advocates to pass a bar examination was questioned, since an earlier judgment had said the BCI cannot add pre-enrolment conditions.
❓ The questionDoes the BCI have the power to conduct a bar examination, and can it be taken before enrolment?
⚖️ HeldThe BCI can conduct the All India Bar Examination as a condition for practice; the earlier contrary view was overruled; final-year law students may take the AIBE.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1Qualifications (S.24): Indian citizen, 21 years, recognised law degree, enrolment fee and the State Council's other conditions.
  • ★ 2Bar Councils cannot charge more than the enrolment fee fixed by S.24(1)(f): ₹750 (₹125 for SC/ST). Gaurav Kumar
  • ★ 3The BCI may hold the All India Bar Examination, and final-year students may take it. Bonnie Foi Law College
  • ★ 4Disqualification (S.24A): conviction for moral turpitude or untouchability offences, or dismissal from State service for moral turpitude; it ends two years after release or dismissal.
  • ★ 5A State Bar Council cannot refuse enrolment on its own: it must refer the case to the BCI (S.26).
