1The big picture

Before Ravi can win, he must knock on the right door. Three questions decide which door: what is the dispute (civil or not, barred or not: S.9), how much is it worth (pecuniary: S.15), and where did it happen or where does the defendant live (territorial: S.16–20).

Once he is inside the right court, the court must tell Somu he has been sued. That message is the summons: delivered by hand, by post, by courier or email, pasted on the door, or published in a newspaper if Somu keeps running away. No one can be condemned unheard (audi alteram partem).

1

Meaning

Jurisdiction is the authority of a court to hear and decide a case. It depends on the subject matter, the value and the place. A decision without jurisdiction is a nullity Kiran Singh.

💬 In simple words
  • Jurisdiction: the power of a court to hear a particular case.
📖 Word origin

Jurisdiction: Latin juris (law) + dictio (saying): the power to "speak the law" in a case.

🎬 Case link

Kiran Singh: a wrong valuation does not make a decree void; only a lack of inherent jurisdiction does.

🏘️Real-life exampleThe decree from the wrong court
  1. 📍 What happened

    A Small Causes Court, which cannot try partition suits, goes ahead and passes a partition decree. Years later the losing side challenges it.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    Jurisdiction is the authority to hear and decide. A decree passed without subject-matter jurisdiction is a nullity (Kiran Singh).

  3. ✅ Result

    The decree can be challenged even in execution: consent or delay cannot give a court power it never had.

2

S.9

S.9

Civil courts have jurisdiction to try all suits of a civil nature, except those expressly or impliedly barred. "Civil nature" means a dispute about private rights (property, office, contract, status). Explanation I: a suit about a right to property or to an office is civil even if it turns on religious rites or ceremonies. Explanation II: it does not matter whether fees attach to the office or whether it is tied to a place.

🎨 Picture it

The civil court's door is open to every dispute about private rights, unless a law locks it.

🌍 Real life

A dispute over the right to a temple office is civil, even if deciding it means looking at religious rites (Explanation I).

🏘️Real-life exampleThe temple priest's post
  1. 📍 What happened

    Two families fight over who holds the hereditary post of priest at a village temple, which carries a share of offerings and land.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.9: civil courts try all suits of a civil nature. Explanation I: a suit about a right to an office is civil even if it turns on religious rites.

  3. ✅ Result

    The civil court can decide who holds the post. A purely religious question (e.g. whose prayer is correct) would not be a civil suit.

3

Bars

Express bar: a statute says civil courts shall not entertain the matter (e.g. matters given to Rent Controllers, Revenue Courts, Debts Recovery Tribunals). Implied bar: the statute creates a new right with its own complete remedy, or public policy forbids suits (e.g. a suit over political or sovereign acts).

"There is an inherent right in every person to bring a suit of a civil nature and, unless the suit is barred by statute, one may, at one's peril, bring a suit of one's choice." Civil courts can also decide incidental questions needed to decide the suit, unless a statute reserves that question for another authority.

🧠 Memory trick

Express bar: the statute says "no civil court". Implied bar: a new right with its own complete remedy, or public policy.

🌍 Real life

Express bars send matters to Rent Controllers, Revenue Courts or Debts Recovery Tribunals.

🏘️Real-life exampleThe tenant and the Rent Controller
  1. 📍 What happened

    A landlord in a city where the Rent Control Act applies files an eviction suit in the civil court against a protected tenant.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.9 bars suits that are expressly or impliedly barred; an express bar sends the matter to bodies like Rent Controllers, Revenue Courts and DRTs.

  3. ✅ Result

    Where the Rent Act gives the Rent Controller exclusive power, the civil court must return the plaint.

4

Case story

  • Kiran Singh v. Chaman Paswan: Not a nullity: valuation errors need proof of prejudice; only a lack of inherent jurisdiction makes a decree void.
  • Dhulabhai v. State of Madhya Pradesh: Suits maintainable. Exclusion of civil courts is not readily inferred; an unconstitutional levy or no adequate remedy keeps the suit alive.
Case story Dhulabhai: tobacco dealers in Ujjain were charged sales tax on tobacco brought from outside the State, while local tobacco was not taxed. After the levy was struck down, they sued for a refund. The State said the Sales Tax Act barred civil suits. The Supreme Court allowed the suits and laid down principles: exclusion of civil courts is not readily inferred, and where the tax itself is unconstitutional, or the Act gives no adequate remedy, a civil suit lies.
💬 In simple words
  • Decree: the court's final, formal decision on who wins the suit.
🎬 Case link

Dhulabhai: the civil court's door is hard to lock; exclusion is never lightly inferred.

🏘️Real-life exampleTax refund from the civil court
  1. 📍 What happened

    Traders paid a tax that the courts later struck down as unconstitutional. The tax law says, "No civil court shall question any assessment."

  2. ⚖️ The law

    Dhulabhai: exclusion of civil courts is not readily inferred; where the tax itself is unconstitutional or the Act gives no adequate remedy, a civil suit lies.

  3. ✅ Result

    The traders can sue for a refund in the civil court despite the bar clause.

5

Kinds of jurisdiction

S.6S.15S.16

(1) Subject-matter (e.g. a Small Causes Court cannot try a partition suit); (2) pecuniary (value: S.6, S.15); (3) territorial (place: S.16–20); (4) original and appellate; (5) exclusive and concurrent; (6) general and special (e.g. Family Court, Commercial Court).

🧠 Memory trick

What · How much · Where: subject-matter, pecuniary, territorial; plus original and appellate, exclusive and concurrent, general and special.

🏘️Real-life exampleThree questions before filing
  1. 📍 What happened

    A client wants to sue for ₹15 lakh over a house in Warangal, and lives in Hyderabad.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    Jurisdiction has kinds: subject matter (what), pecuniary (how much), territorial (where), plus original or appellate, exclusive or concurrent, general or special.

  3. ✅ Result

    The advocate checks what kind of suit, which court can try ₹15 lakh, and which place (here, where the house is).

6

Pecuniary jurisdiction

S.6S.15S.3

S.6: no court tries a suit above its pecuniary limits. S.15: every suit is filed in the court of the lowest grade competent to try it, to reduce the load on higher courts. Value is fixed by the plaintiff in the plaint, subject to the Court-fees and Suits Valuation laws; the limits of the Junior and Senior Civil Judges are fixed by the State Civil Courts Act. Subordination: District Court above Senior Civil Judge above Junior Civil Judge; all subordinate to the High Court (S.3).

The door of the civil court is open to every civil claim; it closes only where the law clearly says so.

💬 In simple words
  • Plaint: the written complaint with which a civil suit begins.
🧠 Memory trick

Start at the bottom: file in the lowest court competent to try it (S.15); no court goes beyond its limits (S.6).

🏘️Real-life exampleStart at the lowest court
  1. 📍 What happened

    A plaintiff with a small money claim wants to file straight in the District Court because "it is a bigger court".

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.15: every suit is filed in the court of the lowest grade competent to try it; S.6: no court tries a suit above its pecuniary limits.

  3. ✅ Result

    He must file in the lowest competent court, usually the Junior Civil Judge for a small claim.

🔁 Recall: Dhulabhai: the civil court door is hard to lock; Kiran Singh: wrong value is not void.

1

S.16 property

S.16

Suits for recovery of immovable property, partition, mortgage (foreclosure, sale, redemption), any right in it, compensation for wrong to it, or recovery of movables under attachment: filed where the property is situated. Proviso: if the relief can be obtained through the defendant's personal obedience, also where he resides or works.

🧠 Memory trick

Land where it lies.

🌍 Real life

A dispute over a house in Hyderabad goes to the court where the house is.

🏘️Real-life exampleThe flat in Visakhapatnam
  1. 📍 What happened

    A Hyderabad resident wants to recover possession of his flat in Visakhapatnam from a squatter who also lives in Hyderabad.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.16: suits for immovable property are filed where the property is situated.

  3. ✅ Result

    He must sue in Visakhapatnam. (Only where the relief can be got through the defendant's personal obedience can he also sue where the defendant lives.)

2

S.17 several courts

S.17S.18

Suit may be filed in any court within whose limits any portion of the property lies, if the whole claim is within its pecuniary limits. S.18: where the local limits are uncertain, a court may record that and proceed.

🌍 Real life

Houses in Hyderabad and lands in Khammam: sue in either, if the whole claim is within that court's pecuniary limits.

🏘️Real-life exampleLand on both sides of a border
  1. 📍 What happened

    A farm runs across two districts. The owner wants one suit for the whole farm.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.17: where property lies in several courts' limits, sue in any one of them, if the whole claim is within its pecuniary limits.

  3. ✅ Result

    One suit in either district covers the whole farm.

3

S.19 wrongs

S.19

The plaintiff may choose: where the wrong was done, or where the defendant resides, carries on business or works for gain. Illustration: A of Delhi beats B in Calcutta, or publishes in Calcutta statements defamatory of B: B may sue in Calcutta or in Delhi.

🧠 Memory trick

Wrong where it happened, or where he lives.

🌍 Real life

A of Delhi beats B in Calcutta, or publishes defamatory statements about B in Calcutta: B may sue in Calcutta or in Delhi.

🏘️Real-life exampleDefamed on a visit
  1. 📍 What happened

    A Hyderabad businessman is publicly defamed in Vijayawada by a man who lives in Chennai.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.19: for wrongs to the person or movables, sue where the wrong was done or where the defendant resides or works.

  3. ✅ Result

    He can choose Vijayawada or Chennai, but not Hyderabad (unless something else ties the case there).

4

S.20 other suits

S.20

Where the defendant (or each of several defendants) resides, carries on business or works for gain; or any of them, with leave of the court or the others' acquiescence; or where the cause of action wholly or partly arises. Explanation: a corporation carries on business at its principal office, or, for a cause of action arising where it has a subordinate office, at that place.

Case story Patel Roadways: cardamom booked at Patel Roadways' Bodinayakanur branch was destroyed in a Delhi godown fire. The company said only Bombay courts (its head office, and the contract clause) could hear the suit. The Supreme Court disagreed: the branch where the cause of action arose is the place, "an alternative locus, not an additional one"; and a contract cannot give jurisdiction to a court that has none.
💬 In simple words
  • Cause of action: the set of facts that gives you the right to sue.
🧠 Memory trick

Others where he lives (or works), or where the cause of action arose.

🎬 Case link

Patel Roadways: sue where the branch dealt with you, not at the head office.

🗣️ Jargon buster

An agreement can only choose between courts that have jurisdiction; it cannot create jurisdiction.

🏘️Real-life exampleGoods lost by a transport company
  1. 📍 What happened

    A trader in Karimnagar books goods with a transport company whose head office is in Mumbai; the booking is at its Karimnagar branch, and the goods never arrive.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.20: sue where the defendant resides or carries on business, or where the cause of action arises. A company is taken to carry on business at a subordinate office where the cause arose (Patel Roadways).

  3. ✅ Result

    He can sue in Karimnagar: the booking happened at that branch.

5

S.21 objections

S.21S.21A

An objection to the place of suing or to pecuniary limits is allowed in appeal or revision only if it was taken in the trial court at the earliest opportunity and there was a consequent failure of justice. S.21A: no separate suit to set aside a decree on such an objection. (Lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is different: it makes the decree a nullity.)

💬 In simple words
  • Revision: the High Court checking whether a lower court acted beyond or without its power.
🧠 Memory trick

Object early, show prejudice: late objections to place or value die (S.21).

🏘️Real-life exampleRaising place of suing too late
  1. 📍 What happened

    A defendant loses at trial in a court of the wrong place, and only in appeal says the suit should have been filed elsewhere.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.21: objections to place or pecuniary limits succeed in appeal only if taken at the earliest opportunity at trial and there was a failure of justice.

  3. ✅ Result

    Raised for the first time in appeal, the objection fails. (A lack of subject-matter jurisdiction would be different: that is a nullity.)

6

Transfer S.22–25

S.22S.23S.24S.25

S.22: where a suit may be filed in two or more courts and is filed in one, the defendant may apply, at the earliest, to transfer it to another. S.23: to which court the application goes. S.24: the High Court or District Court may, on application or on its own, transfer or withdraw any suit, appeal or proceeding pending in a subordinate court. S.25: the Supreme Court may transfer a suit from a court in one State to a court in another, if expedient for the ends of justice.

Property cases go where the property is; wrongs, where they happened or where the wrongdoer lives; everything else, where the defendant lives or the cause of action arose.

🔢 Number trick

S.22 defendant asks · S.24 High Court or District Court transfers · S.25 Supreme Court moves it to another State.

🏘️Real-life exampleThe biased atmosphere
  1. 📍 What happened

    A widow's suit is pending in a town where the defendant is a powerful local leader, and she fears she will not get a fair hearing.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.24: the High Court or District Court may transfer a suit from a subordinate court, on application or on its own. S.25: the Supreme Court can move it to another State.

  3. ✅ Result

    She applies to the District Court, which can transfer the suit to another court in the district.

🔁 Recall: Patel Roadways: sue where the branch dealt with you.

1

Meaning

S.27O.V r.1

A summons is the court's written order calling the defendant to appear and answer the claim (S.27, O.V r.1), signed by the judge and sealed. It may require him to appear for settlement of issues or for final disposal (r.5), and to bring the documents he relies on (r.7). It must be served within 30 days of the institution of the suit.

📖 Word origin

A summons is the court's written call: appear and answer.

🔢 Number trick

Served within 30 days of filing (S.27).

🏘️Real-life exampleThe court calls you
  1. 📍 What happened

    A shop owner receives a sealed paper from the court telling him to appear on 15 July and answer a claim for ₹3 lakh, bringing his account books.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    A summons (S.27, O.V) is the court's signed and sealed order to appear and answer; it may ask him to bring documents (r.7). Served within 30 days of the suit.

  3. ✅ Result

    He must appear (or send an advocate) with the account books, or risk an ex parte decree.

2

Modes of service

(1) Personal service by the court's officer: on the defendant himself or his authorised agent (r.10–12), or an adult family member residing with him if he is absent and has no agent (r.15). (2) By the court through registered post, speed post, courier, fax or email (r.9), or by the plaintiff himself (r.9A). (3) Affixing on the outer door of his house if he refuses or cannot be found (r.17), with the serving officer's report verified (r.19). (4) Substituted service (r.20): where he keeps out of the way, the court orders a copy affixed in the court-house and at his house, or publication in a local daily newspaper; it is as good as personal service.

🧠 Memory trick

Hand · Post · Door · Paper: personal service · post, courier or email · affixing · newspaper.

🌍 Real life

Courier, speed post, fax and email are all allowed now (r.9).

🏘️Real-life exampleAway at work
  1. 📍 What happened

    The court process server visits a defendant's home at noon. He is at work, and his adult son takes the summons.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    Service on an adult family member residing with him is valid when he is absent and has no agent (r.15). Service by registered post, courier or email is also allowed (r.9).

  3. ✅ Result

    Service on the son is good service.

3

Special cases

O.XXIX r.2O.XXX r.3

r.21: defendant in another court's area: sent to that court. r.24: prisoner: through the jail officer. r.25: outside India with no agent: by post or courier to him abroad. r.27: public officer: sent to the head of his office. r.28: soldiers and sailors: through the commanding officer. Companies: on the secretary, director or principal officer (O.XXIX r.2). Firms: on any partner or the manager (O.XXX r.3).

🧠 Memory trick

Prisoner → the jail officer · soldier → the commanding officer · public officer → the head of his office.

🏘️Real-life exampleThe soldier and the company
  1. 📍 What happened

    One defendant is a soldier posted in Kashmir; another is a private limited company.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    Special cases: soldiers through their commanding officer (r.28); companies on the secretary, director or principal officer (O.XXIX r.2).

  3. ✅ Result

    Both are validly served through the right channel, not at random.

4

Exemptions (S.133)

S.133S.87BS.133(2)O.XXVIArt. 31

Exempt from personal appearance: the President, Vice-President, Speaker of the Lok Sabha, Union Ministers, Supreme Court and High Court judges, Governors and Administrators, Speakers and Chairmen of State legislatures, State Ministers, and persons under S.87B. If needed as witnesses they are examined on commission (S.133(2), O.XXVI). Diplomatic agents are not obliged to give evidence as witnesses at all (Art. 31(2), Vienna Convention, given force by the Diplomatic Relations (Vienna Convention) Act, 1972).

Service is about fair notice: the Code keeps adding modes so that no defendant can defeat a suit by hiding.

🗣️ Jargon buster

The President, Vice-President, Governors, Ministers, and Supreme Court and High Court judges need not appear personally; they can be examined on commission (S.133).

🏘️Real-life exampleA Minister as a witness
  1. 📍 What happened

    A party wants a State Minister to come to court to testify about a meeting.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.133: State Ministers, judges, Governors and others are exempt from personal appearance; if needed as witnesses they are examined on commission.

  3. ✅ Result

    The Minister's evidence is taken on commission, not in the witness box.

5

Example

O.IX r.6
Example: the bailiff goes to Somu's house thrice; Somu locks it each time. The bailiff affixes the summons on the door and reports (r.17). The court, satisfied that Somu is avoiding service, orders publication in a Telugu daily (r.20). If Somu still stays away, the court proceeds ex parte (O.IX r.6).
💬 In simple words
  • Ex parte: decided in the absence of one side, who did not appear.
🎨 Picture it

A defendant who hides cannot escape: affix (r.17) → publish (r.20) → ex parte.

🔁 Recall: A defendant who hides cannot escape: affix (r.17), then substitute (r.20), then ex parte.

7Golden rules

  • ★ 1Civil courts can try all suits of a civil nature unless barred; exclusion is never lightly inferred. Dhulabhai
  • ★ 2A decree without inherent jurisdiction is a nullity; but objections to place or value must be taken early and need prejudice (S.21, S.11 Suits Valuation Act). Kiran Singh
  • ★ 3For a company, the branch where the cause of action arose is the place to sue, not the head office. Patel Roadways
  • ★ 4Parties cannot confer jurisdiction by agreement on a court that has none; they can only choose between courts that have it. Patel Roadways
  • ★ 5The President, Governors, Ministers, judges and others are exempt from personal appearance (S.133); they can be examined on commission.

8Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. A transport company has its head office in Delhi and branches in Chennai, Jaipur and Mumbai. Sam's dispute arose from a transaction at the Chennai office, but he sues at Jaipur. How will the court decide?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhere can a corporation be sued for a cause of action that arose at a branch?
  2. 📜 LawS.20(c): where the cause of action arises. S.20 Explanation: a corporation carries on business at its principal office, or, for a cause of action arising at a place with a subordinate office, at that place Patel Roadways.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe cause of action arose at Chennai, where the company has a branch. So Chennai is the place; Jaipur has neither the cause of action nor the deemed place of business for this claim.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe Jaipur court has no territorial jurisdiction; it will return the plaint under O.VII r.10 for presentation at Chennai.

2. Raju wants a partition of joint family property: houses in Hyderabad and lands in Khammam. Where can he sue?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhich court has jurisdiction when immovable property lies in two districts?
  2. 📜 LawS.16(b): partition suits where the property is situated. S.17: if the property is in the limits of different courts, in any court within whose limits any portion lies, provided the whole claim is within its pecuniary limits.
  3. 🔍 ApplyPortions of the property lie in Hyderabad and in Khammam.
  4. ✅ ConclusionRaju may sue either in Hyderabad or in Khammam (S.17), in the court of the lowest grade competent for the whole value (S.15).

3. X, living in Warangal, publishes in Hyderabad a defamatory statement about Y of Nizamabad. Where can Y sue for damages?

  1. 🎯 IssuePlace of suing for compensation for a wrong to the person.
  2. 📜 LawS.19: where the wrong was done, or where the defendant resides, carries on business or works for gain; the plaintiff may choose. Illustration (b) to S.19.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe wrong (publication) was done in Hyderabad; X resides in Warangal. Y's own residence (Nizamabad) is not a ground.
  4. ✅ ConclusionY may sue at Hyderabad or at Warangal, not at Nizamabad.

4. A sues B for ₹5,00,000 on a negotiable instrument in the Junior Civil Judge's court. Maintainable?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs the suit within the pecuniary jurisdiction of the Junior Civil Judge?
  2. 📜 LawS.6 and S.15: lowest court competent; pecuniary limits are fixed by the State Civil Courts Act. O.VII r.10: plaint returned if filed in the wrong court.
  3. 🔍 ApplyUnder the Telangana Civil Courts Act, 1972 as amended, the Junior Civil Judge's pecuniary limit has been raised over the years and is now well above ₹5 lakh. So the suit is within limits. If a claim exceeds the limit on the date of filing, the plaint is returned.
  4. ✅ ConclusionMaintainable, as ₹5 lakh is within the Junior Civil Judge's present pecuniary limit; always state the rule (S.15 + State Act) and the consequence (return of plaint) for full marks.

5. X wants the President of India (or a diplomatic agent) as a witness in a civil suit. Can the court summon him?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan high constitutional functionaries and diplomats be compelled to appear?
  2. 📜 LawS.133(1): the President and others are exempt from personal appearance; S.133(2): if they claim the exemption, they are examined on commission (O.XXVI r.1). Diplomatic agents: Art. 31(2) Vienna Convention (Diplomatic Relations (Vienna Convention) Act, 1972): not obliged to give evidence.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe President cannot be compelled to come to court; his evidence, if relevant, is taken by commission (costs on the person claiming the exemption, unless the party seeking the evidence pays). A diplomat may testify only voluntarily.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo personal summons: the President is examined on commission under S.133; a diplomatic agent cannot be compelled at all.

6. A party keeps locking his house whenever the bailiff comes with summons. How can it be served?

  1. 🎯 IssueHow to serve a defendant who evades service.
  2. 📜 LawO.V r.17: affixing on the outer door when he cannot be found or refuses. r.19: court examines the serving officer and declares service. r.20: substituted service by affixing in the court-house and at his house, or newspaper publication; as effective as personal service.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe defendant is deliberately avoiding service, so ordinary service fails.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe summons can be affixed on his door (r.17) and the court can order substituted service (r.20); then, if he stays away, it proceeds ex parte.

7. In a suit for specific performance of an agreement to sell agricultural land, can the civil court decide the incidental issue "whether B is a farmer"?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a civil court decide an incidental question that a special law may govern?
  2. 📜 LawS.9: civil courts try all civil suits unless expressly or impliedly barred; they may decide incidental questions needed to decide the suit Dhulabhai. If a statute gives that specific question exclusively to another authority, the civil court must leave it to that authority.
  3. 🔍 ApplyWhether B is a farmer is incidental to whether the sale can be enforced. Unless the land law makes that question exclusively triable by a revenue authority, the civil court decides it. (Similarly, a maintenance suit goes to the Family Court where one is set up for the area: Family Courts Act, S.7–8; elsewhere, to the civil court within its limits.)
  4. ✅ ConclusionYes, the civil court can decide it as an incidental issue under S.9, unless a special law expressly reserves that question for another forum.

8. A suit was filed in a court without territorial jurisdiction. The defendant took no objection at trial, lost, and now objects in appeal. Will he succeed?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan an objection to place of suing be raised for the first time in appeal?
  2. 📜 LawS.21(1): no objection as to the place of suing is allowed by an appellate or revisional court unless it was taken in the court of first instance at the earliest possible opportunity, and there has been a consequent failure of justice Kiran Singh.
  3. 🔍 ApplyHe did not object at trial and shows no failure of justice; territorial defects are not "inherent".
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo. The objection is barred under S.21; the decree stands.

9. A suit is pending in Hyderabad, but the defendant says he cannot get a fair trial there and wants it moved to a court in another State. Who can transfer it?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhich authority can transfer a civil suit to another State?
  2. 📜 LawS.24: the High Court or District Court can transfer within its own jurisdiction. S.25: only the Supreme Court can transfer a suit from a court in one State to a court in another State, if expedient for the ends of justice, after hearing the parties.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe transfer sought is inter-State.
  4. ✅ ConclusionOnly the Supreme Court, on an application under S.25; within Telangana, the High Court or District Court under S.24.
