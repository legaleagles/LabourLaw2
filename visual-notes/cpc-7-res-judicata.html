🔁Res Judicata and Res Sub Judice

Decided once, decided forever (S.11). Still pending? Wait, so two courts don't give two answers (S.10).

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

Two brothers fight over a field and the court decides it for the elder. Can the younger file the same case again next year, maybe before a different judge? If he could, no judgment would ever be final and the elder would never sleep in peace. Res judicata (S.11) says: a matter once decided is decided forever between the same parties.

And if the younger files a second case while the first is still pending? Res sub judice (S.10) says: wait; the second court stays its trial so that two courts do not give two different answers. Three Latin maxims carry the idea: nemo debet bis vexari pro una et eadem causa (no one should be vexed twice for the same cause), interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium, and res judicata pro veritate accipitur (a decided matter is accepted as true).

1

Meaning

S.11

Res judicata means "a matter already judged". S.11: no court shall try a suit or issue in which the matter directly and substantially in issue was directly and substantially in issue in a former suit between the same parties (or parties under whom they claim), litigating under the same title, in a court competent to try the later suit, and has been heard and finally decided by that court.

📖 Word origin

Res judicata = "a thing already judged".

🎨 Picture it

Two brothers fight over a field; the court decides for the elder. The younger cannot file the same case next year before another judge.

2

Basis

Three maxims: nemo debet bis vexari pro una et eadem causa; interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium; res judicata pro veritate accipitur. In short: finality, no double harassment, and respect for judgments.

📖 Word origin

Nemo debet bis vexari: no one twice troubled for one cause · Interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium: litigation must end · Res judicata pro veritate accipitur: a judgment is taken as the truth.

🧠 Memory trick

Finality · No double harassment · Respect for judgments.

3

Essentials

(1) The matter is directly and substantially in issue in both suits (not collaterally or incidentally). (2) Same parties or their privies. (3) Same title (same capacity). (4) The first court was competent to try the later suit. (5) It was heard and finally decided on merits (not dismissed for default or withdrawn).

🧠 Memory trick

Same Matter · Same Parties · Same Title · Competent Court · Finally decided on merits.

🗣️ Jargon buster

"On merits": a suit dismissed for default or withdrawn was not finally decided.

4

Constructive res judicata

Any matter which might and ought to have been made a ground of attack or defence in the former suit is deemed to have been directly and substantially in issue. It stops a party from splitting his grounds and fighting in instalments.

Case story Nawab Hussain: a sub-inspector dismissed by the DIG challenged his dismissal in a writ, saying he was not heard, and lost. He then filed a suit saying the DIG could not dismiss him because he was appointed by the IGP. The Supreme Court threw out the suit: he knew this ground and should have raised it in the writ, so it was barred by constructive res judicata.
🎨 Picture it

All your arrows in one quiver: a ground that might and ought to have been raised is treated as decided.

🎬 Case link

Nawab Hussain: lost a writ on "no hearing", then sued on "wrong authority": barred.

5

Other Explanations

S.11

I: "former suit" means one decided earlier, whatever its filing date. II: competence is judged without regard to appeal provisions. III: the matter must have been alleged by one party and denied or admitted by the other. V: relief claimed but not expressly granted is deemed refused. VI: in representative suits (public or common rights), all persons interested are bound. VII: S.11 applies to execution proceedings. VIII: a decision of a court of limited jurisdiction, competent to decide that issue, binds even though it could not try the later suit.

🔢 Number trick

IV constructive · V relief not granted = refused · VI representative suits · VII execution · VIII courts of limited jurisdiction.

6

Where it applies

To writ petitions Daryao; between different stages of the same suit (an earlier order binds later stages: Satyadhyan Ghosal v. Deorajin Debi, 1960); and to co-defendants if there was a conflict between them which had to be decided. Where it does not: a decision without jurisdiction; a pure question of law about jurisdiction wrongly decided; a suit dismissed for default or withdrawn; a different capacity.

One fair fight, one final answer: res judicata gives judgments their finality.

🎬 Case link

Daryao: even writs end once: a High Court decision on merits bars an Art. 32 petition on the same grounds.

🗣️ Jargon buster

It also binds different stages of the same suit, and co-defendants where a conflict between them had to be decided.

7

Illustrations

Illustrations: A sues B for possession, claiming as owner, and loses. A later sues B for possession as mortgagee: barred (Expl. IV: he ought to have raised it as an alternative). C loses a suit as heir in his personal capacity; he later sues as trustee: not barred (different title). A loses a declaration suit when his adverse possession was not yet 12 years; after 12 years he sues on his newly perfected title: not barred (a fresh title that did not exist then).
🧠 Memory trick

Owner first, mortgagee later? Barred (should have been raised). Heir first, trustee later? Not barred (a different title). Adverse possession perfected later? Not barred (a fresh title).

🔁 Recall: Nawab Hussain: all your arrows in one quiver; Daryao: even writs end once.

3Res sub judice (S.10)

Exam question: Res sub judice (S.10) · Distinction between res sub judice and res judicata

🧠 Say it: “Sub judice = wait; judicata = stop”

  1. SSubMeaning
  2. jjudiceConditions
  3. wwaitObject
  4. jjudicataPoints to add
  5. sstopDifferences
1

Meaning

S.10

Sub judice = "under judicial consideration". S.10: no court shall proceed with the trial of a suit in which the matter in issue is also directly and substantially in issue in a previously instituted suit between the same parties (or their privies), litigating under the same title, where the earlier suit is pending in the same or another court in India competent to grant the relief claimed (or in the Supreme Court).

📖 Word origin

Sub judice = "under judicial consideration".

🧠 Memory trick

Sub judice = wait; judicata = stop.

2

Conditions

(1) Two suits, the earlier one pending; (2) the matter in issue directly and substantially the same (courts look for the whole subject matter being identical: Aspi Jal v. Khushroo Dadyburjor, 2013); (3) same parties or privies, same title; (4) the earlier court is competent to grant the relief. Then the later suit is stayed, not dismissed. Pendency of a suit in a foreign court does not bar an Indian court (Explanation).

🧠 Memory trick

Four conditions: two suits, the earlier pending · same matter · same parties and title · earlier court competent.

3

Object

To prevent two courts of concurrent jurisdiction trying the same matter at the same time and giving conflicting decisions, and to avoid multiplicity of suits.

🎨 Picture it

Two courts, one question: stop them giving two different answers.

4

Points to add

S.10S.24S.151

S.10 bars the trial, not the institution of the later suit, so the later court can still pass interim orders such as an injunction or a receiver (and a summary suit at the leave-to-defend stage is not a "trial": Indian Bank v. Maharashtra State Co-op. Marketing Federation, 1998). The court may also consolidate the suits, or the High Court / District Court may transfer one to the other (S.24). S.151 (inherent power) can stay a suit in cases outside S.10.

Example: A sues B in Hyderabad for eviction from a house. While it is pending, he files a second suit for the same eviction in Secunderabad. The Secunderabad court must stay the trial under S.10; it may still pass urgent interim orders.
🗣️ Jargon buster

S.10 bars the trial, not the filing: the later court can still grant an injunction or appoint a receiver.

🌍 Real life

An eviction suit pending in Hyderabad and a second one filed in Secunderabad: the Secunderabad court stays its trial.

5

Differences

S.10S.11

Res sub judice (S.10)Res judicata (S.11)
Matterpending in an earlier suitalready decided

S.10 says "wait", S.11 says "stop": together they keep one dispute in one court at a time.

🧠 Memory trick

Pending → stay. Decided → bar.

🔁 Recall: S.10 stays the trial of the later suit; interim orders are still possible.

4Foreign judgments (S.13)

Exam question: Foreign judgments (S.13, S.14) and their effect in India

  1. Meaning and S.13
  2. S.14 and S.44A
  3. Case story
1

Meaning and S.13

S.2(6)S.13

A judgment of a foreign court. S.13: a foreign judgment is conclusive as to any matter directly adjudicated between the same parties (or their privies), except where: (a) it was not pronounced by a court of competent jurisdiction; (b) it was not given on the merits; (c) it is founded on an incorrect view of international law or a refusal to recognise Indian law where applicable; (d) the proceedings were opposed to natural justice; (e) it was obtained by fraud; (f) it sustains a claim founded on a breach of any law in force in India.

🧠 Memory trick

Six exceptions: Competence · Merits · Law (international or Indian) · Natural justice · Fraud · Breach of Indian law.

🌍 Real life

A foreign divorce on a ground unknown to the law under which the couple married is not recognised in India.

2

S.14 and S.44A

S.14S.44A

On production of a certified copy, the court presumes the foreign court was competent, unless the contrary appears on the record or is proved. S.44A: a decree of a superior court of a "reciprocating territory" notified by the Central Government can be executed in India directly; others need a fresh suit on the foreign judgment.

India respects foreign judgments, but only those given fairly, on merits, by a competent court, under a law India recognises.

🧠 Memory trick

Certified copy → presumption of competence (S.14). Reciprocating territory → direct execution (S.44A). Any other country → a fresh suit on the judgment.

3

Case story

S.13(a)
  • Y. Narasimha Rao v. Y. Venkata Lakshmi: The US decree is not recognised in India: wrong forum, a ground unknown to Hindu law, no real contest (S.13).
Case story Y. Narasimha Rao: a couple married at Tirupati under Hindu law. The husband stayed 90 days in Missouri, USA, and got a divorce there for "irretrievable breakdown", while the wife protested the court's jurisdiction. He remarried; she charged him with bigamy. The Supreme Court refused to recognise the US decree: wrong forum, a ground unknown to the Hindu Marriage Act, no real contest, so it fell within S.13(a), (b), (c), (d) and (e).
🎬 Case link

Y. Narasimha Rao: married here, divorced by our law; a Missouri divorce was not recognised.

🔁 Recall: Y. Narasimha Rao: married here, divorced by our law.

7Golden rules

  • ★ 1S.10 stays the later suit; S.11 bars it. Sub judice = pending; judicata = decided.
  • ★ 2Constructive res judicata (Expl. IV): a ground that might and ought to have been raised is treated as decided. Nawab Hussain
  • ★ 3Res judicata applies to writ petitions too: a High Court decision on merits bars an Art. 32 petition on the same grounds. Daryao
  • ★ 4"Same title" means the same capacity: losing as an individual does not bar a suit as a trustee.
  • ★ 5A foreign judgment is conclusive except in the six cases of S.13; a foreign decree on a ground unknown to our law is not recognised. Y. Narasimha Rao

8Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. Ashok sues Bhaskar for redemption, alleging a usufructuary mortgage. The mortgage is not proved; suit dismissed. Ashok now sues for possession as owner. Barred?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs the second suit barred by res judicata, actual or constructive?
  2. 📜 LawS.11 Explanation IV: any matter which might and ought to have been made a ground of attack in the former suit is deemed directly and substantially in issue. Same parties, same property, competent court, decided on merits.
  3. 🔍 ApplyAshok's claim to possession as owner was available to him when he filed the redemption suit; he could and ought to have claimed it in the alternative. Textbook illustration: a plaintiff who loses claiming in one character (owner) cannot sue again for the same property in another available character (mortgagee), and vice versa.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe second suit is barred by constructive res judicata (S.11, Explanation IV). (It would not be barred only if the ownership claim arose after the first suit, or the first court expressly left it open.)

2. A was a trustee. After his death B takes the trust property. C, A's son, sues B as A's heir in his individual capacity and loses. C, now appointed trustee, sues again as trustee. Barred?

  1. 🎯 IssueDoes the earlier decision against C as an individual bar a suit by C as trustee?
  2. 📜 LawS.11 requires the parties to be litigating "under the same title", i.e. in the same capacity.
  3. 🔍 ApplyIn the first suit C sued in his personal capacity as heir; in the second he sues in a different, representative capacity as trustee, on behalf of the trust.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNot barred: the title (capacity) is different, so neither res judicata nor constructive res judicata applies.

3. A's suit for declaration of ownership is dismissed. At that time he was in adverse possession, but not for 12 years. After the period is complete, he sues on title by adverse possession. Barred?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan a title perfected after the first suit be the basis of a second suit?
  2. 📜 LawS.11 and Explanation IV apply only to matters that existed and might and ought to have been raised in the former suit. S.27 and Art. 65 Lim. Act: title by adverse possession arises on completion of 12 years.
  3. 🔍 ApplyWhen the first suit was decided, A had no title by adverse possession; it came into existence later. It could not have been raised earlier.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNot barred: it is a new title and a new cause of action that did not exist at the time of the first suit.

4. A sues B for eviction. While it is pending, A files another suit for the same relief in another court. Is the second suit maintainable?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan two suits on the same matter between the same parties proceed together?
  2. 📜 LawS.10: the court shall not proceed with the trial of a later suit where the same matter is directly and substantially in issue in an earlier pending suit between the same parties in a competent court.
  3. 🔍 ApplySame parties, same title, same relief, earlier suit pending in a competent court.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe trial of the second suit must be stayed under S.10 (it is not dismissed); the court may also impose costs for abuse of process.

5. A company sues a rival company for passing off its medicine (same name, carton and colours). The rival later sues the first company in a different court with similar allegations. Advise the first company.

  1. 🎯 IssueHow do courts handle two parallel suits on the same dispute?
  2. 📜 LawS.10: the later suit's trial is stayed where the matter in issue is directly and substantially the same. S.10 bars only the trial, so interim orders can still be passed. S.24: the High Court or District Court can transfer and consolidate the suits.
  3. 🔍 ApplyBoth suits turn on the same question: whose trade dress is it and who is copying whom. The first suit was filed earlier.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe first company should apply in the later suit for a stay of trial under S.10 and, if useful, for transfer and joint trial under S.24; interim injunctions remain available in its own suit.

6. A sues B in a USA court; the suit is dismissed. Can A file a fresh suit on the same cause of action in India?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs a foreign judgment conclusive in India?
  2. 📜 LawS.13: a foreign judgment is conclusive between the same parties on any matter directly adjudicated, except in the six cases in S.13(a)–(f). S.14: presumption of competence from a certified copy. S.11 itself applies to Indian courts, but S.13 gives the foreign decision the same finality.
  3. 🔍 ApplyIf the US court was competent, decided on the merits, followed natural justice, and was not misled by fraud, its dismissal is conclusive. If any S.13 exception applies (e.g. dismissed on a technical ground, not on merits), A can sue afresh Y. Narasimha Rao.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo fresh suit if the US judgment was on the merits and falls in none of the S.13 exceptions; yes, if one of those exceptions applies.

7. A suit is dismissed for the plaintiff's default (absence). He files a fresh suit on the same cause. Is it barred by res judicata?

  1. 🎯 IssueDoes a dismissal for default operate as res judicata?
  2. 📜 LawS.11 needs the matter to have been "heard and finally decided". A dismissal for default is not a decision on merits. But O.IX r.9 CPC separately bars a fresh suit on the same cause after a dismissal under O.IX r.8; the remedy is restoration.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThere was no hearing on merits, so S.11 does not apply; yet the fresh suit is barred by O.IX r.9.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNot res judicata, but the fresh suit is barred by O.IX r.9; he must apply to restore the first suit.

8. X's writ petition challenging his dismissal from service was dismissed by the High Court on merits. He now files a civil suit on the same grounds. Maintainable?

  1. 🎯 IssueDoes a writ decision on merits bar a later suit?
  2. 📜 LawThe general principles of res judicata apply to writ proceedings Daryao; a ground he could have raised is also barred (constructive res judicata) Nawab Hussain.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe same grounds were decided on merits in the writ.
  4. ✅ ConclusionThe suit is barred by res judicata; any new ground he could have raised earlier is barred too.
