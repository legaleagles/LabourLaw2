🔨Decree and Execution

Winning a suit is like getting a cheque the other side won't cash. Execution turns paper into money, land or obedience.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

Winning a suit is like getting a cheque that the other side will not cash. More than 150 years ago the Privy Council observed that a litigant's real troubles in India begin after he gets his decree. Execution is the part of the CPC that turns paper into money, land or obedience.

Think of the decree-holder knocking on four doors in turn: give me the thing (delivery), sell his property (attachment and sale), put him in civil prison (arrest, only for a dishonest debtor), or let a receiver collect (receiver). The judgment-debtor, in turn, keeps a small bundle that no creditor may touch: his clothes, his tools, his plough and his bullocks (S.60).

2Judgment, decree, order

Exam question: Judgment, decree and order · Preliminary and final decree · Mesne profits

1

Judgment

S.2(9)O.XX r.4

The statement given by the judge of the grounds of a decree or order. It contains the facts, issues, findings and reasons (O.XX r.4–5).

🧠 Memory trick

Judgment = reasons. Decree = result. Order = everything else.

2

Decree

S.2(2)S.144

The formal expression of an adjudication which conclusively determines the rights of the parties on all or any matters in controversy in the suit. Essentials: (1) an adjudication; (2) in a suit; (3) on rights in controversy; (4) conclusive; (5) formally expressed. It includes rejection of a plaint and questions under S.144 (restitution). It excludes an appealable order and a dismissal for default.

🧠 Memory trick

Five essentials: Adjudication · in a Suit · on Rights in controversy · Conclusive · Formally expressed.

🗣️ Jargon buster

Rejection of a plaint is a decree; dismissal for default is not.

3

Preliminary and final

O.XX r.18O.XXXIV

A decree is preliminary when further proceedings are needed before the suit is completely disposed of; final when it completely disposes of the suit. It can be partly preliminary and partly final. Preliminary decrees are passed in suits for partition (O.XX r.18), mortgage (O.XXXIV), accounts (r.16), dissolution of partnership (r.15), administration (r.13), pre-emption (r.14), and possession with mesne profits (r.12).

Example: in a partition suit the court first declares "A gets 1/3, B 1/3, C 1/3" (preliminary decree). Then a commissioner divides the land by metes and bounds, and the court passes the final decree allotting actual plots.
🎨 Picture it

Partition: shares first ("A, B and C get 1/3 each": preliminary), plots later (actual land allotted: final).

4

Order

S.2(14)S.2(3)S.2(10)

The formal expression of any decision of a civil court which is not a decree, e.g. an order on an application for temporary injunction. Decree-holder (S.2(3)): the person in whose favour the decree is passed; judgment-debtor (S.2(10)): the person against whom it is passed.

📖 Word origin

Decree-holder wins; judgment-debtor loses (S.2(3), S.2(10)).

5

Mesne profits

S.2(12)O.XX r.12

The profits which a person in wrongful possession actually received or might with ordinary diligence have received, with interest, but not profits due to improvements made by that person. The court may order an inquiry into them (O.XX r.12).

📖 Word origin

Mesne (pronounced "meen") is old French for "middle": profits from the in-between period of wrongful possession.

🌍 Real life

Someone wrongly occupies your shop for 3 years: the rent he earned, or could have earned with ordinary diligence, plus interest = mesne profits. Not profits from his own improvements.

6

Decree v. order

S.104O.XLIIIS.100

A decree arises only in a suit; an order may arise in a suit, application or proceeding. A decree may be preliminary or final; an order is never preliminary. Every decree is appealable unless barred; only orders listed in S.104 and O.XLIII are appealable. A second appeal lies against a decree (S.100), never against an order.

The judgment gives reasons, the decree gives the result, and only a decree conclusively settles rights.

🧠 Memory trick

Decree: only in a suit, can be preliminary, appealable unless barred, second appeal lies. Order: anywhere, never preliminary, appealable only if listed (S.104, O.XLIII), no second appeal.

🔁 Recall: Judgment = reasons; decree = result; order = everything else. Partition: shares first (preliminary), plots later (final).

3Execution

Exam question: Execution of decrees: modes (S.51), arrest and detention, attachment and sale, garnishee

  1. Meaning and S.47
  2. Modes (S.51)
  3. Arrest and detention
  4. Attachment (S.60)
  5. Garnishee
  6. Sale
  7. Speedy execution
1

Meaning and S.47

S.36O.XXIS.38S.39S.47

Execution is the enforcement of a decree by the process of the court, so the decree-holder actually gets what was decreed (S.36–74, O.XXI). The court that passed the decree, or the court to which it is sent, executes it (S.38, S.39). Questions between the parties relating to execution are decided by the executing court under S.47, not by a fresh suit.

🎨 Picture it

All disputes about execution go to one window: the executing court (S.47), never a fresh suit.

🎬 Case link

Vasudev Modi: the executing court cannot go behind the decree; only a nullity can be refused.

2

Modes (S.51)

S.51O.XXI r.11

On the decree-holder's application the court may order execution: (a) by delivery of property specifically decreed; (b) by attachment and sale, or sale without attachment; (c) by arrest and detention in civil prison; (d) by appointing a receiver; or (e) in such other manner as the nature of the relief requires. Money decrees can be executed at once on an oral application (O.XXI r.11).

🧠 Memory trick

D-A-A-R-O: Delivery · Attachment and sale · Arrest · Receiver · Other (S.51).

🎨 Picture it

Four doors, knocked in turn: give me the thing → sell his property → civil prison → let a receiver collect.

3

Arrest and detention

S.51O.XXI r.37S.55S.56S.58

For a money decree, arrest is a last resort. Proviso to S.51: the court must first give the judgment-debtor a chance to show cause (O.XXI r.37) and record reasons that he (a) is likely to abscond or has dishonestly transferred or concealed property, or (b) has or had the means to pay and refuses or neglects to pay, or (c) holds the money as a fiduciary.

S.55: arrest may be made at any hour, but no dwelling house is entered between sunset and sunrise, no outer door is broken open unless the debtor himself occupies it and refuses access, and a woman's room is not entered without notice to her. S.56: no arrest or detention of a woman in execution of a money decree. S.58: maximum detention 3 months (decree above ₹5,000), 6 weeks (above ₹2,000 up to ₹5,000); none for ₹2,000 or less. Release does not wipe out the debt, but he cannot be re-arrested for the same decree. S.135, 135A: judicial officers going to court and legislators during sessions are exempt.

Case story Jolly George Varghese: the Bank of Cochin got arrest warrants against two judgment-debtors who owed over ₹7 lakh, without asking whether they could pay now. Krishna Iyer J. stopped it: "there must be some element of bad faith beyond mere indifference to pay". Jailing a man for being poor offends Article 21 and the spirit of Article 11 of the ICCPR.
🧠 Memory trick

No jail for honest poverty. Arrest needs bad faith, or means to pay plus refusal, or money held as a fiduciary.

🎬 Case link

Jolly George Varghese: "some element of bad faith beyond mere indifference to pay" is needed (Art. 21, Art. 11 ICCPR).

4

Attachment (S.60)

S.60O.XXIS.64

All saleable property of the judgment-debtor can be attached, except: necessary clothes, cooking vessels, bedding, and ornaments a woman cannot part with by religious custom; tools of artisans; and an agriculturist's implements of husbandry, cattle and seed-grain needed to earn his livelihood; an agriculturist's or labourer's house; account books; a mere right to sue for damages; pensions; wages of labourers; part of salary (first ₹1,000 and two-thirds of the rest); provident fund; and others in S.60.

Movables in the debtor's possession: actual seizure (r.43). Debts and shares: prohibitory order (r.46). Negotiable instruments: actual seizure (r.51). Immovable property: an order prohibiting transfer, proclaimed by beat of drum and affixed (r.54). Any private transfer after attachment is void against the decree-holder (S.64).

🎨 Picture it

The debtor keeps a small bundle no creditor can touch: clothes, cooking vessels, bedding, tools of his trade, a farmer's cattle and seed, his house, pension, wages.

🔢 Number trick

Salary: the first ₹1,000 and two-thirds of the rest are protected.

5

Garnishee

O.XXI r.46

When a debt owed to the judgment-debtor by a third person (the garnishee) is attached, the court can call the garnishee to pay it into court or show cause. If he pays, he is discharged; if he disputes, the court tries the issue; the order is executable like a decree.

Example: Ravi has a decree for ₹2 lakh against Somu. Somu's customer Gopal owes Somu ₹1.5 lakh. The court attaches that debt and orders Gopal (garnishee) to pay ₹1.5 lakh into court instead of to Somu.
📖 Word origin

The garnishee is the third person who owes the debtor money; he is "warned" (old French garnir) to pay the court instead.

🌍 Real life

Somu owes Ravi ₹2 lakh under a decree; Gopal owes Somu ₹1.5 lakh. The court orders Gopal to pay that ₹1.5 lakh into court.

6

Sale

O.XXI r.64Art. 127S.65

Proclamation of sale (r.66); not before 15 days (immovable) or 7 days (movable) from the proclamation (r.68); purchaser pays 25% at once and the balance within 15 days (r.84, 85). Setting aside: by depositing the amount due plus 5% for the purchaser (r.89), or for material irregularity or fraud causing substantial injury (r.90), within 60 days (Art. 127). Then confirmation (r.92) and a sale certificate (r.94); title passes from the date of sale (S.65).

Execution balances a winner's right to the fruits of the decree with the debtor's right to live with dignity.

Case story Rahul S. Shah: land in Bengaluru, a suit since 1987, a decree in 2006 and an execution petition pending from 2007 to 2021. The Supreme Court ordered that execution must normally be completed within 6 months of filing, discouraged frivolous objections, and allowed police help. And Vasudev Modi: the executing court must take the decree as it is.
🔢 Number trick

15 days (immovable) or 7 days (movable) after proclamation · buyer pays 25% at once, balance within 15 days · set aside within 60 days.

🧠 Memory trick

To undo a sale: deposit + 5% (r.89), or irregularity or fraud causing substantial injury (r.90).

7

Speedy execution

Execution must normally be completed within 6 months of filing; frivolous objections, third-party claims and delays are to be curbed Rahul S. Shah.

🔢 Number trick

6 months from filing to the end of execution.

🎨 Picture it

A suit from 1987, a decree in 2006, execution pending from 2007 to 2021: that is why the 6-month rule came.

🔁 Recall: Jolly George: no jail for being poor; Vasudev Modi: execute, don't re-judge; Rahul Shah: done in 6 months.

5Case stories

Jolly George Varghese v. Bank of Cochin

(1980) 2 SCC 360 · AIR 1980 SC 470 · decided 4 Feb 1980

📖 What happenedThe Bank of Cochin got arrest warrants against two judgment-debtors without any inquiry into whether they could pay now.
❓ The questionCan a judgment-debtor be sent to civil prison merely because he has not paid a money decree, without any finding that he can pay and is refusing?
⚖️ HeldSet aside. Arrest needs bad faith or present means plus refusal; poverty alone is no ground (Art. 21, Art. 11 ICCPR).

Vasudev Dhanjibhai Modi v. Rajabhai Abdul Rehman

AIR 1970 SC 1475 · decided 18 Mar 1970

📖 What happenedA tenant facing an eviction decree argued in execution that the Small Causes Court never had jurisdiction, so the decree was a nullity.
❓ The questionCan an executing court refuse to execute a decree on the ground that the court which passed it lacked jurisdiction?
⚖️ HeldObjection rejected. The executing court takes the decree as it is; only a nullity apparent on the record can be refused.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1A decree conclusively determines rights; an order does not. Rejection of a plaint is a decree; dismissal for default is not.
  • ★ 2The executing court cannot go behind the decree, unless it is a nullity. Vasudev Modi
  • ★ 3No civil prison for honest poverty. Arrest needs bad faith or means to pay plus refusal. Jolly George Varghese
  • ★ 4All questions between the parties about execution are decided by the executing court under S.47, not by a separate suit.
  • ★ 5Execution should normally end within 6 months of filing. Rahul S. Shah

7Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. P is executing a money decree against Q. Q deposits the decretal amount in a bank. Is Q discharged?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs a deposit in a bank a valid mode of paying a decree?
  2. 📜 LawO.XXI r.1: payment under a money decree is made (a) by deposit into the executing court (or sent to it by postal money order or through a bank), or (b) out of court to the decree-holder by postal money order, through a bank, or any other mode evidenced in writing, or (c) as the court directs. r.1(2): notice to the decree-holder. r.1(4): interest stops from payment or notice. r.2: payment out of court must be certified.
  3. 🔍 ApplyDepositing money in Q's own bank account is not payment to P or to the court. Only if Q deposited it to P's account through the bank, or into court, with written proof and notice, is it a valid payment.
  4. ✅ ConclusionA deposit in his own bank account does not discharge Q; he is discharged only on payment into court or to P through a recognised mode under O.XXI r.1, duly certified.

2. A wants to attach the agricultural produce and implements of the judgment-debtor X (an agriculturist). Maintainable?

  1. 🎯 IssueAre an agriculturist's implements and produce exempt from attachment?
  2. 📜 LawS.60(1) proviso (b): an agriculturist's implements of husbandry, cattle and seed-grain, as in the court's opinion are necessary to earn his livelihood as such, are exempt. S.61: the State may exempt the portion of agricultural produce needed for cultivation and support till the next harvest.
  3. 🔍 ApplyCourts have treated a tractor (Allahabad High Court, 1958) and a water pump engine as implements of husbandry. If they are needed for X's livelihood as an agriculturist, they cannot be attached. Produce beyond the exempted portion can be attached.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNot maintainable as to the implements (and the pump set and tractor) needed for X's cultivation; the produce can be attached only beyond the portion exempted under S.61.

3. A filed an execution petition asking for attachment and sale of B's property and also for B's arrest. Can he do so?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan arrest and attachment be sought together, and is arrest automatic?
  2. 📜 LawO.XXI r.11(2)(j): the application states the mode of execution sought. O.XXI r.21: the court may, in its discretion, refuse simultaneous execution against the person and property. S.51 proviso and O.XXI r.37: show-cause notice and recorded reasons before arrest.
  3. 🔍 ApplyA may pray for both, but the court will usually proceed against property first. Arrest needs proof that B has the means and refuses, or is acting in bad faith Jolly George Varghese.
  4. ✅ ConclusionHe can ask for both, but the court may refuse simultaneous execution (O.XXI r.21), and arrest will be ordered only after notice and a finding under the S.51 proviso.

4. The judgment-debtor objects in execution that the decree is wrong. Can the executing court go into it?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan an executing court question the decree it is executing?
  2. 📜 LawS.47: execution questions are decided by the executing court. But the executing court must take the decree as it stands Vasudev Modi.
  3. 🔍 ApplyOnly if the decree is a nullity (passed without inherent jurisdiction, apparent on the face of the record, or against a dead person) can the objection be heard. A plea that the decree is wrong must go to appeal or revision.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo; the executing court cannot go behind the decree, unless it is a nullity.

5. The judgment-debtor is a woman who owes money under a decree. Can she be arrested?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs civil arrest available against a woman judgment-debtor?
  2. 📜 LawS.56: notwithstanding anything in this Part, the court shall not order the arrest or detention in civil prison of a woman in execution of a decree for payment of money.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe decree is for money, so arrest is barred. Her property can still be attached and sold.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo; S.56 bars arrest of a woman in execution of a money decree, but her property can be attached.
