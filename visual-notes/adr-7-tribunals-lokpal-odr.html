🧠Mind map

1The big picture

Not every dispute needs a regular court. A government employee denied promotion goes to the Central Administrative Tribunal; a company in insolvency goes to the NCLT; an environmental harm goes to the National Green Tribunal. These specialised tribunals bring expertise and speed. And when the complaint is against a corrupt public servant, the citizen can go to an ombudsman: the Lokpal at the Centre or the Lokayukta in the State.

Today, disputes can even be settled online: a buyer and an e-commerce seller can negotiate, mediate or arbitrate through a portal, with video hearings and e-signed settlements. That is Online Dispute Resolution (ODR).

2Tribunals

Exam question: Tribunals: meaning, constitutional basis, kinds, merits and judicial review

🧠 Say it: “Many Children Eat Mangoes Joyfully”

  1. MManyMeaning
  2. CChildrenConstitutional basis
  3. EEatExamples
  4. MMangoesMerits
  5. JJoyfullyJudicial review
1

Meaning

A tribunal is a specialised adjudicating body, outside the regular hierarchy of courts, set up by statute to decide disputes in a particular field, usually with judicial and expert (technical/administrative) members.

📖 Word origin

Tribunal, from Latin tribunus: the seat of a magistrate. Today, a specialist court outside the regular hierarchy.

2

Constitutional basis

Art. 323AArt. 323B

The 42nd Amendment (1976), on the Swaran Singh Committee's advice, added Art. 323A (Parliament may set up administrative tribunals for service matters of public servants) and Art. 323B (Parliament or State Legislatures may set up tribunals for taxation, foreign exchange, industrial and labour disputes, land reforms, ceiling on property, elections, essential supplies, rent and tenancy and similar matters).

🔢 Number trick

42nd Amendment, 1976 (Swaran Singh Committee): 323A = service matters; 323B = other matters (tax, foreign exchange, labour, land reforms, elections, essential supplies, rent).

3

Examples

Central Administrative Tribunal (Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985); Income Tax Appellate Tribunal; NCLT and NCLAT; National Green Tribunal (2010); Debts Recovery Tribunals; Armed Forces Tribunal; Securities Appellate Tribunal; Consumer Commissions (quasi-judicial).

🧠 Memory trick

CAT · ITAT · NCLT/NCLAT · NGT · DRT · AFT · SAT · Consumer Commissions.

4

Merits

Expertise; speed; simpler procedure (not bound by CPC, follow natural justice); lower cost; reduce the burden on courts. Concerns: executive control over appointments and service conditions, vacancies, short tenures; the Supreme Court has repeatedly struck down rules that weakened their independence (e.g. the Madras Bar Association cases).

🧠 Memory trick

Expert · Quick · Simple · Cheap; but watch executive control over appointments and vacancies.

5

Judicial review

Art. 226Art. 32

A Constitution Bench of seven judges held that the clauses excluding the jurisdiction of High Courts were invalid: judicial review under Art. 226/227 and Art. 32 is part of the basic structure; tribunals act as courts of first instance, and their decisions can be challenged before a Division Bench of the High Court L. Chandra Kumar.

Example: a railway employee who is denied promotion files an application before the Central Administrative Tribunal, not the civil court. If he loses, he can go to the High Court under Art. 226 and then to the Supreme Court.
🎨 Picture it

Tribunals are specialist doctors; the High Court is the senior consultant who can always review them.

🎬 Case link

L. Chandra Kumar: judicial review under Art. 226/227 and 32 is basic structure; tribunals are only courts of first instance.

🌍 Real life

A railway employee denied promotion goes to the CAT, then to the High Court, then the Supreme Court.

🔁 Recall: Tribunals are specialist doctors of the justice system (expert, quick), but the High Court remains the senior consultant who can always review them.

3Lokpal and Lokayukta

Exam question: Lokpal and Lokayukta: origin, composition, jurisdiction, powers and role

🧠 Say it: “Old Lions Jump Past Little Rabbits”

  1. OOldOrigin
  2. LLionsLokpal: composition and selection
  3. JJumpJurisdiction
  4. PPastProcedure and powers
  5. LLittleLokayukta
  6. RRabbitsRole and criticism
1

Origin

Based on the Scandinavian ombudsman (Sweden, 1809): an independent authority that inquires into citizens' complaints against public officials. The Administrative Reforms Commission (1966) recommended a Lokpal for the Centre and Lokayuktas for the States. After many failed Bills and a nationwide anti-corruption movement (2011), Parliament passed the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 (in force 16 January 2014).

📖 Word origin

Ombudsman is Swedish for "representative": Sweden created the first one in 1809.

🔢 Number trick

Recommended for India by the Administrative Reforms Commission, 1966.

2

Lokpal: composition and selection

A Chairperson (a former Chief Justice of India, Supreme Court judge, or eminent person) and up to eight members, of whom half are judicial and at least half are from SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women. Selection committee: the Prime Minister (chair), the Speaker, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, the Chief Justice of India (or a judge nominated by him) and an eminent jurist. The first Lokpal was appointed in 2019.

🔢 Number trick

1 + up to 8: half the members judicial; at least half from SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women.

3

Jurisdiction

Corruption complaints (under the Prevention of Corruption Act) against the Prime Minister (except on international relations, security, public order, atomic energy and space; an inquiry needs approval of a full bench by two-thirds, held in camera), Union Ministers, MPs, Central Government officers of all groups, and bodies funded or controlled by the Centre.

🎨 Picture it

Even the Prime Minister is on the lead, loosely: some subjects excluded, and an inquiry needs a full bench and two-thirds approval.

4

Procedure and powers

An Inquiry Wing and a Prosecution Wing; preliminary inquiry, then investigation (it may refer to the CBI and superintend it in such cases); powers of a civil court; search and seizure; provisional attachment of property acquired by corruption; recommendation of transfer or suspension; trial by Special Courts, to be completed within a fixed time.

🧠 Memory trick

Inquiry Wing + Prosecution Wing; civil-court powers, search and seizure, provisional attachment of corrupt property.

5

Lokayukta

S.63

S.63 requires every State to set up a Lokayukta by its own law within one year. Maharashtra created one of the first (1971). In Telangana, the Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayukta function under the State's Lokayukta Act. The Lokayukta inquires into complaints against State Ministers, MLAs and officials; its powers differ from State to State.

Example: a citizen has proof that a Central Government officer demanded a bribe to clear a licence. He can complain to the Lokpal, which can order a preliminary inquiry, have it investigated, attach the officer's ill-gotten property and send him for trial in a Special Court.
🔢 Number trick

Every State must set up a Lokayukta within one year (S.63). Maharashtra was among the first (1971).

🌍 Real life

A Central officer demands a bribe to clear a licence: complain to the Lokpal, which can inquire, investigate, attach property and prosecute.

6

Role and criticism

They give citizens an independent forum against corruption, outside the government hierarchy. Criticisms: delays in appointments, few complaints acted upon, dependence on investigating agencies, and uneven Lokayukta laws across States.

🧠 Memory trick

Watchdogs that bark at corruption and bite through inquiry and prosecution; criticised for delays and uneven State laws.

🔁 Recall: Lokpal is the national watchdog (even the PM is on its lead, loosely), Lokayukta the State watchdog; both bark at corruption, bite through inquiry and prosecution wings.

4Online dispute resolution

Exam question: Online dispute resolution (ODR)

🧠 Say it: “Mobile Friendly Legal Methods”

  1. MMobileMeaning
  2. FFriendlyForms
  3. LLegalLegal support
  4. MMethodsMerits and challenges
1

Meaning

ODR is the resolution of disputes, wholly or partly online, using technology for filing, communication, hearings and settlement, through negotiation, mediation or arbitration.

🎨 Picture it

ODR = ADR + a smartphone.

2

Forms

Automated/assisted negotiation (e.g. blind bidding on amounts); online mediation and conciliation by video; online arbitration with e-filing, virtual hearings and e-signed awards; e-Lok Adalats; platforms set up by regulators (e.g. SEBI's ODR portal for securities disputes) and by e-commerce firms.

🧠 Memory trick

Blind bidding · video mediation · online arbitration · e-Lok Adalat · regulator portals.

📖 Word origin

Blind bidding: each side secretly enters a figure; if they are close enough, the software settles it.

3

Legal support

S.4S.5S.10AS.7(4)(b)

The IT Act, 2000 recognises electronic records and signatures (S.4, S.5) and contracts made electronically (S.10A); an arbitration agreement may be made by electronic communication (S.7(4)(b), A&C Act); the Mediation Act, 2023 provides for online mediation; electronic evidence is admissible (Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023). NITI Aayog's ODR Policy Plan (2021) recommends wider use.

Example: a customer in Warangal who received a damaged phone from an online seller files a complaint on an ODR platform; a mediator holds a 20-minute video call and the seller agrees to a refund the same week.
🔢 Number trick

IT Act S.4, S.5, S.10A (e-records, e-signatures, e-contracts) · A&C Act S.7(4)(b) (e-mail arbitration agreements) · Mediation Act 2023 (online mediation).

4

Merits and challenges

Merits: no travel, low cost, speed, suits small and cross-border disputes. Challenges: digital divide, data security and confidentiality, identity verification, enforcement of online settlements, and lack of awareness.

🌍 Real life

A Warangal customer gets a damaged phone; a 20-minute video mediation produces a refund the same week.

🧠 Memory trick

Challenges: digital divide · data security · identity · enforcement · awareness.

🔁 Recall: ODR = ADR + a smartphone: same doors (negotiate, mediate, arbitrate), opened by video, e-mail and e-signature.

6Case stories

L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India

(1997) 3 SCC 261 · decided 18 Mar 1997

📖 What happenedLaws creating administrative tribunals shut out the High Courts from service disputes, leaving only an appeal to the Supreme Court.
❓ The questionCan a law creating tribunals exclude the High Courts' power of judicial review?
⚖️ HeldThe power of judicial review of the High Courts (Art. 226/227) and the Supreme Court (Art. 32) is part of the basic structure; clauses excluding it are invalid. Tribunal decisions can be challenged before a High Court Division Bench.

7Golden rules

  • ★ 1Tribunals were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976): Art. 323A (service matters) and Art. 323B (other matters).
  • ★ 2Tribunals cannot shut out the High Court and Supreme Court: judicial review is a basic feature. L. Chandra Kumar
  • ★ 3Lokpal and Lokayukta are ombudsmen against corruption, inspired by the Scandinavian ombudsman.
  • ★ 4The Lokpal can inquire even into the Prime Minister, with strict safeguards.
  • ★ 5ODR rests on the IT Act, 2000 and electronic arbitration agreements (S.7) and online mediation (Mediation Act, 2023).
