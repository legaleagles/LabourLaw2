🛣️Trials, Plea Bargaining and Compounding

Four roads of decreasing length (Sessions, warrant, summons, summary), and two exits with no trial at all: the victim forgives (compounding), or the accused admits and settles (plea bargaining).

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

Not every case needs the full ceremony of a Sessions trial. The BNSS gives four roads of decreasing length: the Sessions trial (gravest), the warrant-case trial (offences above 2 years), the summons-case trial (up to 2 years, no formal charge), and the summary trial (petty cases, short record, at most 3 months' jail).

And some cases need no trial at all. In compounding, the victim forgives and the case ends in acquittal. In plea bargaining, the accused admits guilt and, after a settlement with the victim, gets a lighter sentence. Both save time, but neither is allowed for grave offences or crimes against women and children.

2Warrant and summons cases

1

Meaning

S.2

A warrant-case relates to an offence punishable with death, life imprisonment or imprisonment above 2 years; a summons-case is any case that is not a warrant-case (up to 2 years). The classification decides the procedure, not whether a summons or a warrant is issued.

🧠 Memory trick

The two-year line: above it, a warrant case; up to it, a summons case.

🗣️ Jargon buster

The names decide the procedure, not whether a summons or a warrant is actually issued.

🏘️Real-life exampleWarrant case or summons case?
  1. 📍 What happened

    One man is accused of cheating (up to 7 years); another of simple hurt (up to 1 year).

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.2: a warrant-case is an offence punishable with death, life or above 2 years; a summons-case is everything else. The label decides the procedure.

  3. ✅ Result

    Cheating is tried as a warrant case; simple hurt as a summons case.

2

Warrant case on police report

S.261S.262S.263S.264S.265

Copies of documents supplied (S.261); accused may seek discharge within 60 days of supply; Magistrate discharges if the charge is groundless (S.262); otherwise frames the charge in writing within 60 days of the first hearing on charge (S.263); plea (S.264); prosecution evidence (S.265); defence evidence (S.266); judgment of acquittal or conviction (S.271).

🔢 Number trick

261 → 262 → 263 → 264 → 265 → 266: documents → discharge → charge → plea → prosecution → defence.

🔢 Number trick

60 + 60: discharge plea within 60 days of getting the documents; charge within 60 days of the first hearing on charge.

🏘️Real-life exampleDischarged for want of material
  1. 📍 What happened

    In a police-report warrant case for cheating, the accused finds the charge sheet has no document showing he received any money.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.261–262: copies are supplied; the accused may seek discharge within 60 days; the Magistrate discharges if the charge is groundless.

  3. ✅ Result

    He applies in time and is discharged.

3

Warrant case on complaint

S.267S.268S.269S.270S.273

Prosecution evidence is taken first (S.267); discharge if no case (S.268); charge framed afterwards and the accused can cross-examine the prosecution witnesses again (S.269); defence (S.270). If the complainant is absent, the Magistrate may discharge the accused in compoundable or non-cognizable offences; for a false and frivolous accusation, compensation can be ordered (S.273).

🎨 Picture it

On a complaint, the prosecution shows its cards first (S.267), before any charge is framed.

🧠 Memory trick

Witnesses face a second cross-examination after the charge (S.269).

🏘️Real-life exampleThe complainant who stopped coming
  1. 📍 What happened

    In a complaint-based warrant case for a non-cognizable offence, the complainant stops attending before the charge is framed.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.267–270: prosecution evidence comes first, then the charge; the accused can cross-examine again after the charge. If the complainant is absent, the accused may be discharged in compoundable or non-cognizable cases.

  3. ✅ Result

    The Magistrate may discharge the accused.

4

Summons case

S.274S.275S.277S.279S.280

No formal charge: the particulars of the offence are stated to the accused (S.274); plea of guilty and conviction, even by post for petty offences (S.275–276); else evidence and judgment (S.277–278); absence of the complainant may lead to acquittal (S.279); the complainant may withdraw (S.280); the Magistrate may convert a summons case into a warrant case if the interests of justice require (S.282).

🎨 Picture it

No formal charge: just "this is what you are accused of" (S.274).

🌍 Real life

For petty offences the accused can even plead guilty by post (S.275–276).

🏘️Real-life examplePleading guilty by post
  1. 📍 What happened

    A man is summoned for a petty offence and lives far away.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.274–276: in a summons case no formal charge is framed; the particulars are stated, and for petty offences the accused may plead guilty by post and pay the fine.

  3. ✅ Result

    He pleads guilty by post and pays the fine without travelling.

  2. ⚖️ The law

  3. ✅ Result

1

Meaning

A summary trial is a short, quick trial of petty offences with an abridged record, to clear simple cases fast.

🎨 Picture it

The express counter of criminal courts: small cases, short record, quick result.

🏘️Real-life exampleClearing small cases fast
  1. 📍 What happened

    A Magistrate has 300 pending petty theft and intimidation cases.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    A summary trial is a short trial of petty offences with an abridged record.

  3. ✅ Result

    Many can be disposed of quickly through summary trials.

2

Who and which offences

S.283S.283(2)S.284

A CJM or JMFC shall try summarily: theft, theft in a dwelling house, theft by a servant, receiving or helping hide stolen property, where the value does not exceed ₹20,000; criminal intimidation, insult to provoke breach of peace, certain house-trespass offences, abetment and attempts of these. Under S.283(2), a Magistrate may also try summarily any offence up to 3 years, after hearing the accused and recording reasons (no appeal against that decision). A second-class Magistrate may be specially empowered (S.284).

🔢 Number trick

₹20,000: theft-type cases up to this value; any offence up to 3 years by choice, after hearing the accused and recording reasons (S.283(2)).

🌍 Real life

A ₹15,000 phone is stolen: the Magistrate can try the case summarily.

🏘️Real-life exampleA ₹15,000 phone theft
  1. 📍 What happened

    A man is accused of stealing a phone worth ₹15,000.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.283: a CJM or JMFC shall try summarily theft where the value does not exceed ₹20,000, plus intimidation, insult and some trespass offences.

  3. ✅ Result

    The case is tried summarily.

3

Procedure

S.285

The procedure of a summons case is followed (even for a warrant case), with modifications.

🧠 Memory trick

Summons-case procedure, even if it is a warrant case.

🏘️Real-life exampleSummons-case steps
  1. 📍 What happened

    In a summary trial for theft (a warrant-case offence), the accused asks for a formal written charge.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.285: the summons-case procedure is followed in summary trials, even for a warrant case.

  3. ✅ Result

    No formal charge: the particulars are stated to him.

4

Limits

S.285

No sentence of imprisonment above 3 months (S.285); if the Magistrate feels a summary trial is undesirable, he must re-hear the case in the regular manner.

🧠 Memory trick

Short case, short record, short jail: never more than 3 months (S.285).

🗣️ Jargon buster

If a summary trial looks unsuitable midway, the Magistrate must re-hear the case in the regular way.

🏘️Real-life exampleOnly 3 months
  1. 📍 What happened

    In a summary trial the Magistrate feels the accused deserves 1 year in jail.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.285: no imprisonment above 3 months in a summary trial; if summary trial is undesirable, the Magistrate must re-hear the case in the regular way.

  3. ✅ Result

    He must re-hear it as a regular trial if he wants to give more than 3 months.

5

Judgment

S.286

The record contains particulars: serial number, date of offence, complainant, accused, offence and value of property, plea, finding, sentence, date of termination. Where the accused does not plead guilty, the Magistrate records the substance of the evidence and a judgment containing a brief statement of reasons.

🎨 Picture it

The record is a printed form: serial number, date, complainant, accused, offence, property value, plea, finding, sentence, end date.

🏘️Real-life exampleA one-page record
  1. 📍 What happened

    A summary trial ends in conviction after the accused pleads not guilty.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.286–287: a short record of particulars, and where the accused does not plead guilty, the substance of evidence and a brief judgment with reasons.

  3. ✅ Result

    The judgment is short but reasoned.

6

Differences

S.283

: offences listed (S.283) v. any up to 2 years; maximum sentence 3 months v. the full punishment for the offence; abridged record v. full record; only CJM / JMFC (or empowered JMSC) v. any Magistrate.

🧠 Memory trick

List · 3 months · Abridged · CJM/JMFC for a summary trial, against any offence up to 2 years · full punishment · full record · any Magistrate for a summons case.

🏘️Real-life exampleSummary or summons?
  1. 📍 What happened

    Two Magistrates hear two small cases: one a ₹10,000 theft, the other a defamation complaint.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    Summary trial: listed offences, max 3 months, abridged record, CJM/JMFC only. Summons case: any offence up to 2 years, full punishment, full record.

  3. ✅ Result

    The theft can go summary; the defamation case follows the summons-case procedure.

🔁 Recall: Summary trial = "small case, short record, short jail (3 months)".

1

Meaning

Plea bargaining is a pre-trial negotiation in which the accused pleads guilty in return for a lesser sentence, after a mutually satisfactory settlement with the victim. It was introduced in 2005 (Chapter XXIA CrPC) and continues as Chapter XXIII of the BNSS.

🎨 Picture it

A bargain at the counter: plead guilty, settle with the victim, get a lighter sentence.

🔢 Number trick

Born in 2005 as Chapter XXIA CrPC; now Chapter XXIII BNSS.

🏘️Real-life exampleA deal with the victim
  1. 📍 What happened

    A man accused of cheating a neighbour of ₹2 lakh wants to admit it, repay, and get a lighter sentence.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    Plea bargaining: the accused pleads guilty for a lesser sentence after a mutually satisfactory settlement with the victim (Chapter XXIII BNSS).

  3. ✅ Result

    He can apply for plea bargaining.

2

When available

S.289S.300

For offences punishable with imprisonment up to 7 years. Not for: offences punishable with death, life or above 7 years; offences affecting the socio-economic condition of the country; offences against a woman or a child; and not for a person previously convicted of the same offence. Juveniles are excluded (S.300).

🧠 Memory trick

Never for W · C · E: offences against Women, Children, or the Economy (socio-economic offences).

🔢 Number trick

Only up to 7 years. Not for repeat offenders of the same offence; not for juveniles (S.300).

🏘️Real-life exampleNot for everyone
  1. 📍 What happened

    Three accused want plea bargaining: one for cheating (7 years), one for an offence against a child, one for a 10-year offence.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.289: only for offences up to 7 years; not for offences against women or children, socio-economic offences, or repeat offenders of the same offence.

  3. ✅ Result

    Only the cheating accused qualifies.

3

Procedure

S.290S.291S.292

(1) The accused files an application within 30 days of framing of charge (S.290), with an affidavit that he is doing so voluntarily; (2) the court examines him in camera to confirm voluntariness; (3) the court gives time to work out a mutually satisfactory disposition with the victim and prosecutor (in a police case also the investigating officer) (S.291), which may include compensation to the victim; (4) report to the court (S.292).

🔢 Number trick

30 days from framing of the charge (S.290), with an affidavit.

🎨 Picture it

An in-camera check: the judge talks to him alone to make sure the plea is voluntary.

🏘️Real-life exampleVoluntary, in camera
  1. 📍 What happened

    The cheating accused files his application 20 days after the charge is framed.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.290: application within 30 days of the charge, with an affidavit; the court examines him in camera to confirm it is voluntary, then gives time for a settlement.

  3. ✅ Result

    The court confirms he is not pressured, and the parties work out the repayment.

4

Disposal and sentence

S.293

The court awards compensation as agreed, hears parties on sentence, and may: release on probation or admonition; or, where a minimum is prescribed, sentence to half the minimum (one-fourth for a first-time offender); otherwise one-fourth of the maximum (one-sixth for a first-time offender).

🧠 Memory trick

½ of the minimum (¼ first-timer), else ¼ of the maximum (⅙ first-timer).

🌍 Real life

Cheating, up to 7 years, no minimum, first offender: about one-sixth of 7 years, roughly 14 months, or even probation.

🏘️Real-life exampleThe reduced sentence
  1. 📍 What happened

    The cheating accused is a first-time offender, and cheating carries up to 7 years with no minimum.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.293: where no minimum is prescribed, sentence of one-fourth of the maximum, or one-sixth for a first-time offender; or probation or admonition; plus agreed compensation.

  3. ✅ Result

    He faces at most about 14 months (one-sixth of 7 years), or probation.

5

Effect

S.295S.299S.297

The judgment is final; no appeal lies except a special leave petition or writ (S.295). Statements made in the application cannot be used for any other purpose (S.299). Time already in custody is set off (S.297).

🧠 Memory trick

Final: no appeal, only an SLP or a writ (S.295). Statements can't be used for anything else (S.299). Custody already served is set off (S.297).

🏘️Real-life exampleNo appeal afterwards
  1. 📍 What happened

    After plea bargaining, the accused changes his mind and wants to appeal.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.295: the judgment is final; no appeal except special leave or a writ. S.299: his statements cannot be used elsewhere.

  3. ✅ Result

    He cannot appeal.

6

Compounding

S.359S.528BNS 318(4)

Settling a criminal case by the victim forgiving the accused. A table lists offences compoundable by the victim without the court's permission (e.g. simple hurt, wrongful restraint, defamation) and offences compoundable only with the court's permission. Composition has the effect of an acquittal. Offences not listed cannot be compounded; but the High Court may quash such cases on settlement in suitable private disputes (S.528).

Example: Ravi is charged with cheating (BNS 318(4), up to 7 years, no minimum) for a ₹1 lakh fraud. Within 30 days of the charge he applies; he repays the victim ₹1 lakh plus compensation; being a first offender, he can get one-sixth of 7 years, about 14 months, or even probation.
📖 Word origin

Compound, from Latin componere, "to settle": the victim forgives and the case ends.

🎨 Picture it

The victim forgives; the law treats it as an acquittal.

🗣️ Jargon buster

Not on the list? It cannot be compounded, but the High Court may quash suitable private disputes on settlement (S.528).

🏘️Real-life exampleThe neighbours who made up
  1. 📍 What happened

    Two neighbours fought and one caused the other simple hurt. They later reconcile.

  2. ⚖️ The law

    S.359: compounding settles the case by the victim forgiving the accused; simple hurt is compoundable without the court's permission. It has the effect of an acquittal.

  3. ✅ Result

    They file a compromise and the accused is acquitted.

🔁 Recall: "7 · 30 · ½ · ¼ · ⅙": up to 7 years, apply within 30 days, half of minimum (¼ first-timer), or ¼ of maximum (⅙ first-timer). Never for women, children, or the economy.

6Golden rules

  • ★ 1Warrant case = more than 2 years; summons case = up to 2 years.
  • ★ 2In a summons case no formal charge is framed; the particulars are stated (S.274).
  • ★ 3A summary trial can never end in imprisonment of more than 3 months (S.285).
  • ★ 4Plea bargaining is not available for offences punishable with death, life or more than 7 years, socio-economic offences, or offences against a woman or a child.
  • ★ 5Compounding has the effect of an acquittal; a plea-bargain judgment is final (no appeal, only SLP or writ).

7Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. An accused charged with cruelty to his wife (dowry harassment) admits guilt and asks for plea bargaining. Discuss.

  1. 🎯 IssueIs plea bargaining available for an offence against a woman?
  2. 📜 LawCruelty by husband: BNS 85 (up to 3 years and fine). S.289: plea bargaining does not apply to offences committed against a woman or a child.
  3. 🔍 ApplyAlthough the punishment is below 7 years, the offence is committed against a woman, which is expressly excluded.
  4. ✅ ConclusionPlea bargaining is not available; the trial must proceed (he may plead guilty in the ordinary way, but without the plea-bargain concessions).

2. Z burnt his wife to death for dowry. Before the court he admits guilt and asks for a reduced punishment. Will he succeed?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan an accused of murder / dowry death get a reduced sentence by pleading guilty?
  2. 📜 LawMurder BNS 103 (death or life); dowry death BNS 80 (minimum 7 years, up to life). S.289: no plea bargaining for offences punishable with death or life, or against a woman. S.252: on a plea of guilty, the Sessions Court may convict, but in grave cases courts usually still take evidence.
  3. 🔍 ApplyBoth exclusions apply. A plea of guilty does not lower the punishment fixed by law; the court cannot go below life for murder.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNo. He cannot get plea-bargain benefits; the Court of Session will convict on evidence and sentence him under BNS 103 (or BNS 80).

3. Can a Magistrate try a theft of property worth ₹15,000 summarily? What is the maximum sentence?

  1. 🎯 IssueIs the case triable summarily, and what are the limits?
  2. 📜 LawS.283(1): theft where the value of property does not exceed ₹20,000 shall be tried summarily by a CJM or JMFC. S.285: no imprisonment above 3 months in a summary conviction.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe value is ₹15,000, within the limit.
  4. ✅ ConclusionYes, it must be tried summarily; maximum imprisonment 3 months (plus fine as provided).
