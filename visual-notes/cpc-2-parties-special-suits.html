👥Parties and Special Suits

A suit is a photograph of a dispute: the right people must be in the frame. Some need a special door: minors, the poor, crowds, the Government, and the honest stakeholder.

🧠Mind map

1The big picture

A suit is like a photograph of a dispute. If someone who must be in the picture is left out, the photo is useless; if a stranger walks in, the photo is confusing. Order I decides who stands in the frame.

Some people cannot stand in the frame on their own, so the Code gives them a special door: a minor comes with a next friend, a poor person comes without paying court fee, a crowd with one interest sends a representative, the Government must first get a 2-month notice, and a stakeholder holding someone else's property can ask the claimants to fight it out between themselves (interpleader).

2Parties (O.I)

Exam question: Parties to a suit: joinder, necessary and proper parties, non-joinder and misjoinder (O.I)

1

Who sues together

A decree binds only those who are parties. So the right people must be joined: all who must be bound, and no strangers. Order I (r.1–13) deals with this.

Persons may join as plaintiffs if (1) the right to relief arises from the same act or transaction, or series of acts or transactions, and (2) if they sued separately, a common question of law or fact would arise. Joinder of defendants (r.3): the same two tests apply to persons sued together.

🎨 Picture it

A suit is a photograph of a dispute: leave out someone who must be in it and the photo is useless; let a stranger in and it is confusing.

🧠 Memory trick

Two tests to join people together: same transaction + common question of law or fact (r.1 for plaintiffs, r.3 for defendants).

2

Necessary v. proper

A necessary party is one (1) against whom some relief is claimed, and (2) without whom no effective decree can be passed. A proper party is one whose presence enables the court to decide all questions completely and effectively, though no relief is claimed against him.

Case story Kasturi: Kasturi sued two sellers for specific performance of a sale agreement. Strangers who claimed the land by their own independent title asked to be added. The Supreme Court said no: they were neither necessary nor proper parties, because adding them would turn a contract suit into a title suit.
🧠 Memory trick

Necessary = no effective decree without him. Proper = helps the court decide everything fully.

🌍 Real life

In a partition suit every co-sharer is necessary: leave out one heir and the decree cannot work.

🎬 Case link

Kasturi: "contract suit, contract parties"; strangers claiming independent title are not added.

3

Non-joinder and misjoinder

No suit shall be defeated by misjoinder or non-joinder; the court decides the rights of those before it. Proviso: this does not apply to non-joinder of a necessary party; such a suit fails.

🧠 Memory trick

Only a missing necessary party kills the suit (r.9 proviso). Misjoinder never does.

4

Court's power (r.10)

S.21

At any stage, on application or on its own, the court may strike out a party wrongly joined or add anyone who ought to have been joined, or whose presence is necessary to decide all questions. Objection (r.13): must be taken at the earliest opportunity (before settlement of issues), else it is waived, unless the ground arose later.

r.4: judgment may be given for one or more plaintiffs, or against one or more defendants. r.6: persons liable on the same contract may be joined. r.7: if the plaintiff is in doubt from whom to get relief, he may join both. r.10(1): suit filed in the name of the wrong plaintiff by bona fide mistake can be corrected. r.10(4): an added defendant must be served afresh; for limitation the suit against him begins on the date he is added (S.21 Limitation Act).

🎨 Picture it

The court can add or remove people from the photo at any stage, on application or on its own (r.10(2)).

5

Objection (r.13)

Objection to misjoinder or non-joinder must be taken at the earliest opportunity, before the settlement of issues; otherwise it is waived, unless the ground arose later (r.13).

🧠 Memory trick

Object early or lose it: before the issues are settled.

6

Death of a party

O.XXIIArt. 120Art. 121

If the right to sue survives, the legal representatives are brought on record. If they are not brought within 90 days (Art. 120 Lim. Act), the suit abates as against that party; the abatement can be set aside for sufficient cause within 60 days (Art. 121). No abatement after the hearing has concluded and before judgment (r.6).

Join everyone needed for an effective decree, object early, and remember that only the absence of a necessary party is fatal.

Example: in a partition suit every co-sharer is a necessary party: each one's share must be fixed, so leaving out even one heir means the decree cannot work. In a suit for recovery of rent, the landlord's neighbour is neither necessary nor proper.
🔢 Number trick

90 days to bring the legal representatives on record (Art. 120); 60 days to set aside the abatement (Art. 121).

📖 Word origin

Abate, from Old French abatre, "to beat down": the suit comes to an end against the dead party.

🔁 Recall: Kasturi: "contract suit, contract parties". Only the absence of a necessary party kills a suit.

3Suits against Government

Exam question: Suits by or against Government or public officers: S.79–82 and O.XXVII (notice under S.80)

  1. S.79 naming
  2. S.80 notice
  3. Object
  4. Urgent relief S.80(2)
  5. S.81–82
  6. O.XXVII
1

S.79 naming

S.79

A suit by or against the Central Government is in the name of the "Union of India"; by or against a State, in the name of the "State of …" (e.g. State of Telangana).

🧠 Memory trick

Sue the "Union of India" or the "State of Telangana", never just "the Government".

2

S.80 notice

S.80(1)

No suit against the Government, or against a public officer for an act done in his official capacity, until 2 months after written notice is delivered: for the Centre to the Secretary (for railways, the General Manager); for a State to the Secretary or the District Collector; for a public officer, to him personally or at his office. The notice must state the name, description and residence of the plaintiff, the cause of action and the relief claimed, and the plaint must say it was served.

🎨 Picture it

Knock before you enter: 2 months' written notice gives the Government a chance to settle.

🌍 Real life

The railway lost your parcel? The notice goes to the General Manager of that railway.

3

Object

S.80

To give the Government a chance to reconsider and settle without litigation. So the notice is read sensibly, not as a trap Raghunath Dass.

Case story Raghunath Dass: a trader's copper goods sent by railway in 1947 never arrived. His S.80 notice went in his firm's name, signed "Raghunath Dass, Proprietor". The High Court threw out the suit because the notice-giver and plaintiff looked different. The Supreme Court restored his decree: S.80 is "not intended to be used as booby traps against ignorant and illiterate persons".
🎬 Case link

Raghunath Dass: a notice in the firm's name signed by its proprietor was good enough. S.80 is not a booby trap.

4

Urgent relief S.80(2)

S.80(2)S.80(3)

A suit for urgent or immediate relief may be filed without notice, with leave of the court; but no relief (interim or final) is granted without giving the Government a chance to show cause. If no urgency is shown, the plaint is returned for filing after notice. S.80(3): a suit is not dismissed for a mere defect in the notice if the plaintiff is identifiable and the cause of action and relief are substantially indicated.

🧠 Memory trick

Urgent? Sue with the court's leave, but no relief until the Government gets a chance to show cause.

5

S.81–82

S.81S.82

A public officer sued for an official act is not arrested and his property is not attached before judgment (except in execution); the court may exempt him from appearing in person. S.82. A decree against Government must specify a time for satisfaction; no execution unless it stays unsatisfied for 3 months from the decree.

🔢 Number trick

3 months: no execution until the decree stays unsatisfied that long (S.82).

🗣️ Jargon buster

A public officer sued for an official act is not arrested, and his property is not attached before judgment (S.81).

6

O.XXVII

O.XXVIIS.15(2)S.685

r.1–2: pleadings are signed and verified by an officer appointed by the Government; r.4: the Government Pleader is its agent; r.5: the court gives the Government reasonable time to file its written statement; r.5A: when a public officer is sued for an official act, the Government must be joined as a party; r.5B: the court must first try to help the parties settle; r.8A: no security is demanded from the Government.

The 2-month notice period is excluded when computing limitation (S.15(2) Limitation Act). Local bodies: a municipal corporation is not "Government", but its own Act may demand notice: e.g. GHMC needs 1 month's notice, and the suit must be filed within 6 months (S.685 GHMC Act, 1955).

Notice first, name the State correctly, join the Government, and expect a 3-month grace before execution.

🗣️ Jargon buster

The Government Pleader is the Government's agent (r.4); no security is demanded from the Government (r.8A); when an officer is sued for an official act, the Government is joined (r.5A).

🔁 Recall: Raghunath Dass: the notice is a chance to settle, not a booby trap.

4Representative and public suits

Exam question: Representative suits (O.I r.8) and suits relating to public matters: public nuisance (S.91) and public charities (S.92)

  1. O.I r.8 representative suit
  2. Safeguards
  3. S.91 public nuisance
  4. S.92 public trusts
  5. Reliefs and cy-près
1

O.I r.8 representative suit

O.I r.8

Where numerous persons have the same interest, one or more of them may sue or be sued, or defend, on behalf of all, with the permission of the court. They need not have the same cause of action; a common interest is enough.

🎨 Picture it

Many people, one plaintiff: a few sue for all, with the court's permission.

🌍 Real life

300 flat owners object to a builder blocking their common road; three of them sue for all 300, and the decree binds everyone.

🗣️ Jargon buster

They need the same interest, not the same cause of action.

2

Safeguards

S.11

(1) The court gives notice to all interested persons at the plaintiff's expense, by personal service or public advertisement (r.8(2)); (2) any of them may apply to be made a party (r.8(3)); (3) no withdrawal, abandonment or compromise without notice to all (r.8(4)); (4) a representative who is not proceeding with due diligence can be replaced (r.8(5)); (5) the decree binds all on whose behalf the suit was brought (r.8(6); also S.11 Explanation VI).

Example: 300 flat owners of a colony object to a builder blocking their common road. Three of them, with the court's permission, sue on behalf of all 300. After notice in a newspaper, the decree binds every owner, so the builder does not face 300 separate suits.
🧠 Memory trick

Notice · Join · No secret compromise · Replace a slow representative.

3

S.91 public nuisance

S.91

For a public nuisance or other wrongful act affecting the public, a suit for declaration, injunction or other relief may be filed by the Advocate General, or by two or more persons with leave of the court, even if they suffered no special damage. Their own private rights of suit are not affected.

🧠 Memory trick

Two or more with leave, or the Advocate General; no special damage needed (S.91).

🌍 Real life

A road blocked for everyone, or a factory polluting a whole street: a public nuisance.

4

S.92 public trusts

S.92

For an alleged breach of an express or constructive trust created for public purposes of a charitable or religious nature, or where directions are needed for its administration, the Advocate General, or two or more persons having an interest in the trust, with leave of the court, may sue in the principal civil court of original jurisdiction (or a court empowered by the State).

🔢 Number trick

Again two or more persons interested in the trust, with leave, or the Advocate General (S.92).

5

Reliefs and cy-près

S.92S.92(3)

removing a trustee, appointing a new one, vesting property, directing accounts and inquiries, declaring shares of the property for particular objects, authorising sale or lease, settling a scheme, or any other relief the case needs. S.92(3) (cy-près): the court may alter the original purposes where they cannot be carried out, e.g. the purpose is fulfilled or impossible.

These provisions avoid multiplicity of suits and let the public protect public rights, while the leave and notice rules stop busybodies and secret compromises.

📖 Word origin

Cy-près is old French for "as near as possible": if the original purpose fails, the court keeps the trust as close to it as it can.

🔁 Recall: Picture 300 flat owners and three of them suing for all: notice to everyone, decree binds everyone.

5Interpleader

Exam question: Interpleader suits (S.88, O.XXXV)

  1. Meaning
  2. Conditions
  3. Procedure
  4. Agents and tenants (r.5)
  5. Illustrations
1

Meaning

S.88

Where two or more persons claim adversely to one another the same debt, money or property from a third person, who claims no interest in it other than charges or costs and is ready to pay or deliver it to the rightful claimant, he may file an interpleader suit against all the claimants, so that the court decides to whom it should go, and he is protected.

🎨 Picture it

An honest holder between two claimants says: "You two fight it out; I'll hand it to the winner."

📖 Word origin

Interplead = make the claimants plead against each other.

2

Conditions

(1) Same debt or property; (2) claimed adversely by two or more persons; (3) the plaintiff claims no interest except costs or charges; (4) no collusion with any claimant; (5) no other suit pending in which all the rights can be properly decided.

🧠 Memory trick

Same thing · two claimants · honest holder (no interest, no collusion) · no other suit pending.

3

Procedure

O.XXXV

r.1: the plaint states that the plaintiff claims no interest, the separate claims made, and no collusion. r.2: the court may order the thing claimed to be paid or placed in court. r.3: any suit pending against the plaintiff about the same thing is stayed. r.4: at the first hearing the court may discharge the plaintiff with costs and let the claimants fight it out, or keep all parties and decide. r.6: the plaintiff's costs can be made a charge on the thing claimed.

🧠 Memory trick

Deposit · Stay · Discharge: the thing goes into court, other suits are stayed, the holder may be discharged with costs.

4

Agents and tenants (r.5)

Agents and tenants cannot sue their principals or landlords to make them interplead with others, except with persons claiming through those principals or landlords.

Interpleader protects an honest stakeholder from paying twice or being sued twice.

🧠 Memory trick

An agent cannot fight his principal; a tenant cannot fight his landlord through interpleader (r.5).

5

Illustrations

Illustrations to r.5 (learn both): (a) A gives a box of jewels to his agent B. C says the jewels were stolen from him and claims them from B. B cannot interplead A and C, as B is A's agent. (b) A gives the box to B, then writes to C making the jewels security for a debt to C. Later A says the debt is paid; C says it is not. Both claim the jewels: B can interplead, because C claims through A.
🎨 Picture it

The jewel box: if the claim comes through the principal, the agent can interplead; if it comes against the principal, he cannot.

🔁 Recall: The jewel box: if the claimant comes through the principal, the agent can interplead; if he comes against the principal, he cannot.

6Indigent persons

Exam question: Suits by indigent persons (O.XXXIII)

  1. Meaning
  2. Case story
  3. Procedure
  4. Rejection grounds
  5. Afterwards
1

Meaning

A person is indigent if he does not have sufficient means (other than property exempt from attachment and the subject matter of the suit) to pay the court fee, or, where no fee is prescribed, is not entitled to property worth ₹1,000 (excluding the same). Such a person may sue without paying court fee first. Access to justice should not depend on money.

🧠 Memory trick

Poor today, pay later: the court fee is deferred, not waived.

🔢 Number trick

Where no fee is prescribed, the test is property worth ₹1,000.

2

Case story

  • Union Bank of India v. Khader International Construction: "Person" in O.XXXIII includes a juristic person, so a company can sue as an indigent person.
Case story Union Bank v. Khader: a construction company in financial trouble applied at Kochi to sue a bank as an indigent person. The bank said only a human being can be "indigent". The Supreme Court disagreed: "person" includes a company, so a juristic person can also sue without paying court fee upfront.
🎬 Case link

Union Bank v. Khader: "person" includes a company, so even a company can be poor.

3

Procedure

r.2: the application gives the plaint's particulars plus a schedule of the applicant's movable and immovable property with values, signed and verified like a pleading. r.3: presented in person by the applicant (unless exempted). r.4: the court may examine him. r.6: notice to the opposite party and the Government Pleader, who may lead evidence on his means. r.7: hearing. r.8: if allowed, the application is numbered and registered as the plaint.

🧠 Memory trick

Presented in person, with a schedule of all his property and its value.

4

Rejection grounds

Not properly framed or presented; applicant not indigent; he fraudulently disposed of property within 2 months before applying; no cause of action shown; he made an agreement giving someone else an interest in the subject matter; the claim is barred by law; or a third party has agreed to finance the suit.

🔢 Number trick

Disposed of property fraudulently within 2 months before applying? Rejected.

🧠 Memory trick

Rejection grounds: bad form · not poor · fraud · no cause · sold interest · barred · financier.

5

Afterwards

S.13O.XXXIII

r.9: permission can be withdrawn (vexatious conduct, means appear, financing deal). r.9A: the court may assign a pleader. r.10: if he wins, the court fee is calculated and is a first charge on the subject matter of the suit, recoverable by the State. r.11: if he loses or the permission is withdrawn, the court orders him to pay the fee. r.15: a refused applicant cannot apply again for the same right, but may sue normally on paying the fee (r.15A allows time). Limitation: the time spent on the application is excluded (S.13 Lim. Act).

O.XXXIII is deferred payment of court fee, not a waiver: the State collects it from whoever loses.

🎨 Picture it

If he wins, the State collects the fee from what he wins: a first charge on the subject matter (r.10).

🔁 Recall: Union Bank v. Khader: even a company can be poor.

7Minors (O.XXXII)

Exam question: Suits by or against minors and persons of unsound mind (O.XXXII)

  1. Suit by minor
  2. Suit against minor
  3. Who may act
  4. Protection
  5. On majority
  6. Unsound mind
1

Suit by minor

Filed in the minor's name by a "next friend". If filed without one, the defendant may have the plaint taken off the file, with costs against the pleader or person who filed it (r.2).

🧠 Memory trick

Next friend for a plaintiff; guardian for a defendant.

2

Suit against minor

The court appoints a "guardian for the suit" (guardian ad litem) on the plaintiff's application, after notice to the minor and his natural guardian. The order continues through appeals and execution.

📖 Word origin

A guardian ad litem is a guardian "for the suit" only.

3

Who may act

Any person of sound mind and full age whose interest is not adverse to the minor, and who is not a defendant (for next friend) or the plaintiff (for guardian). A guardian may not be appointed without his written consent.

🧠 Memory trick

Adult · sane · not adverse · consents in writing.

4

Protection

r.5: every application is made through the next friend or guardian. r.6: he cannot receive money or movables for the minor without leave of the court. r.7: no compromise or agreement without leave of the court; otherwise it is voidable against everyone except the minor. r.9–11: removal or retirement of a next friend or guardian.

🧠 Memory trick

No money, no compromise without the court's leave (r.6, r.7). A compromise without leave is voidable against everyone except the minor.

5

On majority

r.12: as plaintiff he must elect to proceed (the suit continues in his own name, next friend discharged) or abandon it (as sole plaintiff, he applies for dismissal on paying costs). r.13: as a co-plaintiff he may repudiate and be struck off. r.14: he may apply to dismiss the suit as unreasonable or improper.

🧠 Memory trick

On majority, elect: continue in his own name, or abandon (r.12).

6

Unsound mind

Rules 1–14 apply to persons adjudged of unsound mind, and to those found by the court to be incapable of protecting their interests because of mental infirmity.

The minor is always the real party; the next friend or guardian is only his voice, watched by the court.

🗣️ Jargon buster

The same rules apply to persons of unsound mind (r.15).

🔁 Recall: On majority the minor elects: continue in his own name or abandon (r.12).

10Golden rules

  • ★ 1Necessary party = without him no effective decree can be passed. Proper party = his presence helps decide everything completely. Kasturi
  • ★ 2Non-joinder of a necessary party is fatal (r.9 proviso); misjoinder or non-joinder of a proper party never is.
  • ★ 3No suit against Government without 2 months' notice (S.80), unless the court gives leave for urgent relief. Raghunath Dass
  • ★ 4"Person" in O.XXXIII includes a company; a company too can sue as an indigent person. Union Bank v. Khader
  • ★ 5An agent cannot interplead against his principal, nor a tenant against his landlord (O.XXXV r.5).

11Part C: problem routes

Every problem takes the same four stops: issue → law → apply → conclusion.

1. X failed to include a necessary party in the suit. What is the effect of non-joinder of a necessary party?

  1. 🎯 IssueDoes the suit fail when a necessary party is left out, and can it be cured?
  2. 📜 LawO.I r.9: no suit is defeated by misjoinder or non-joinder; proviso: except non-joinder of a necessary party. O.I r.10(2): the court may add a party at any stage. O.I r.13: objection to be taken early.
  3. 🔍 ApplyWithout a necessary party no effective decree can be passed Kasturi. But X can apply under r.10(2) (with an amendment of the plaint) to add him; the court may also add him on its own.
  4. ✅ ConclusionIf the necessary party is not added, the suit must fail; if he is added under O.I r.10(2), the defect is cured. For limitation, the suit against the added party starts from the date he is added (S.21 Lim. Act).

2. In a partition suit the plaintiff left out some legal heirs. Can he add them now? How?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan omitted heirs be added as defendants mid-suit?
  2. 📜 LawO.I r.10(2): add any person who ought to have been joined, at any stage. O.VI r.17: amend the plaint accordingly. Every co-sharer is a necessary party in partition.
  3. 🔍 ApplyThe heirs' shares must be fixed for the decree to work, so they are necessary parties. He files an impleading application under O.I r.10(2) and an amendment application; the court orders fresh summons to them (r.10(4)).
  4. ✅ ConclusionYes. He can add them at any stage under O.I r.10(2), with a consequent amendment of the plaint. Without them, the partition decree would be ineffective.

3. A deposits a box of jewels with B. A pledges the jewels as security for a debt to C. A says the debt is paid; C says not. Both claim the jewels from B. Remedy for B?

  1. 🎯 IssueCan B, holding the jewels, make A and C fight it out?
  2. 📜 LawS.88 and O.XXXV: interpleader where two persons claim the same property adversely from a stakeholder who claims no interest. O.XXXV r.5: an agent cannot interplead against his principal, except with persons claiming through the principal (Illustration (b)).
  3. 🔍 ApplyB claims nothing for himself. C claims through A (A made the jewels security for C), so the r.5 bar does not apply. This is exactly Illustration (b) to O.XXXV r.5.
  4. ✅ ConclusionB may file an interpleader suit against A and C, deposit the jewels in court and seek discharge with costs (O.XXXV r.2, r.4).

4. Smt. Roja wants to sue the State Government for damages. What must she do before filing?

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat procedural steps are compulsory before suing the State?
  2. 📜 LawS.79: sue the "State of Telangana". S.80(1): 2 months' written notice to the Secretary to the Government or the District Collector, stating name, description, residence, cause of action and relief. S.80(2): urgent relief with leave. S.15(2) Lim. Act: notice period excluded.
  3. 🔍 ApplyShe serves the notice, waits 2 months, then files, pleading that notice was served. For a local body such as GHMC, S.80 does not strictly apply, but S.685 GHMC Act requires 1 month's notice and a suit within 6 months. For a sudden demolition notice of 2 days, she may also rely on the Supreme Court's 2024 directions that demolitions need a prior show-cause notice of at least 15 days.
  4. ✅ ConclusionNotice under S.80 (2 months), suit against the "State of Telangana", plaint stating the notice was served; if the matter is urgent, apply for leave under S.80(2).

5. X is an indigent person who wants to sue for ₹1 lakh on a promissory note. What procedure should he follow?

  1. 🎯 IssueHow does a poor plaintiff sue without paying court fee?
  2. 📜 LawO.XXXIII r.1–8: application with property schedule, presented in person, inquiry, notice to opposite party and Government Pleader; if allowed, registered as the plaint. Art. 35 Lim. Act: 3 years; S.13 Lim. Act excludes time on the application.
  3. 🔍 ApplyX files the application (plaint particulars + list of his property), appears personally, is examined, and proves he cannot pay the court fee. If he later wins, the fee becomes a first charge on the decree amount (r.10).
  4. ✅ ConclusionHe should apply under O.XXXIII to sue as an indigent person; once permitted, the application stands as his plaint and the suit proceeds without court fee being paid upfront.

6. A wants to sue B, who is a minor. What procedure must he adopt?

  1. 🎯 IssueHow is a minor defendant represented?
  2. 📜 LawO.XXXII r.3: the court appoints a guardian for the suit on the plaintiff's application, after notice to the minor and his guardian. r.4: the guardian must be of full age, sound mind, without adverse interest, and consent in writing.
  3. 🔍 ApplyA must file the suit and an application to appoint a guardian ad litem (usually the natural guardian). A decree against an unrepresented minor is not binding on him.
  4. ✅ ConclusionA must apply under O.XXXII r.3 for appointment of a guardian for the suit; the suit then proceeds against the minor through that guardian.

7. A files a suit for his minor son B against C. B attains majority while the suit is pending. Advise B and C.

  1. 🎯 IssueWhat happens when a minor plaintiff becomes major mid-suit?
  2. 📜 LawO.XXXII r.12: he must elect to proceed or abandon; r.12(2)–(3): if he proceeds, the next friend is discharged and the suit continues in his name; r.12(4): if he abandons, as sole plaintiff he applies for dismissal on paying costs. r.14: he may apply to dismiss it as unreasonable or improper.
  3. 🔍 ApplyB decides. C cannot force B to continue, but if B abandons, C can ask for his costs.
  4. ✅ ConclusionB should elect: continue in his own name (A is discharged as next friend) or have the suit dismissed; C is entitled to costs if B abandons.

8. The plaintiff (or defendant) dies while the suit is pending. What happens?

  1. 🎯 IssueDoes the suit end on the death of a party?
  2. 📜 LawO.XXII r.1: no abatement if the right to sue survives. r.3/r.4: legal representatives are brought on record on application within 90 days (Art. 120 Lim. Act); else the suit abates. r.9: abatement may be set aside for sufficient cause (Art. 121: 60 days). r.6: no abatement after hearing and before judgment.
  3. 🔍 ApplyFor a property or money claim the right survives, so the heirs step in. For a purely personal claim (e.g. defamation), it dies with the person.
  4. ✅ ConclusionIf the right to sue survives, the suit continues with the legal representatives brought on record within 90 days; otherwise it abates.
