Monsanto Technology v. Nuziveedu Seeds
2019 · Supreme Court of India
STORYMonsanto held patents on Bt cotton gene (Cry2Ab). Indian companies paid "trait fees" (royalties). Andhra Pradesh capped fees → Monsanto terminated licences → sued Indian companies. HELDSection 3(j) of Patents Act bars plant patents. GM plant varieties = PPV&FR Act governs. Monsanto's patent monopoly cannot override farmers' rights.
WHY LANDMARKProtected 8 million cotton farmers in India. Challenged Monsanto's global business model of controlling seeds through patents.

Diamond v. Chakrabarty
1980 · US Supreme Court
STORYDr. Ananda Chakrabarty (Indian-American) at General Electric spent years engineering a Pseudomonas bacterium that could break down crude oil (4 different oil-degrading genes combined). No naturally occurring bacterium could do this. USPTO rejected the patent — "living things can't be patented."
HELD5-4 decision: The distinction is NOT living vs non-living — it is NATURAL vs HUMAN-MADE. "Anything under the sun that is made by man" can be patented if it has markedly different characteristics from anything in nature.
EXAM ANGLEIndia is more cautious: Section 3(c) bars naturally occurring substances. Non-naturally occurring micro-organisms may be patentable. Section 3(j) bars plants/animals.

Novartis AG v. Union of India
2013 · Supreme Court of India · THE MOST IMPORTANT CASE IN IPL
STORYNovartis (Swiss pharma) held patents globally for Gleevec/Glivec (imatinib mesylate) — a leukaemia drug. In India it applied to patent the BETA CRYSTALLINE FORM (a polymorph — same molecule, different solid arrangement). Gleevec = Rs.1,20,000/month. Indian generics (Veenat by Natco, Hyderabad) = Rs.8,000/month. 12,000+ Indian patients and millions globally depended on affordable generics. Novartis fought for 7 years. MSF, patient groups from Africa and South America filed amicus briefs.
HELDSection 3(d) is TRIPS-COMPLIANT. TRIPS Art.27 requires patents for all fields but does NOT define "invention" — India has the right to define patentability. The beta crystalline form showed better BIOAVAILABILITY (absorption) but NOT better THERAPEUTIC EFFICACY (patient outcomes). Bioavailability ≠ Therapeutic Efficacy. Patent REJECTED.
WHY LANDMARKIndia supplies 80% of ARV medicines to developing nations. Preserved pharmacy of the world. Exposed "evergreening" — filing incremental patents to extend monopoly beyond 20 years. Brazil, Thailand, South Africa modelled their anti-evergreening laws on India's Section 3(d).
EXAM ANGLEWorth 30-40% of any TRIPS essay. Write: (1) Facts + Rs.1.2L vs Rs.8K (2) Therapeutic efficacy ≠ bioavailability (3) TRIPS compliance (India defines "invention") (4) Global impact.
🚨 ABSOLUTE RULE
Section 3(d) requires THERAPEUTIC EFFICACY — not just improved bioavailability. Bioavailability = how much drug is absorbed. Therapeutic efficacy = whether the PATIENT GETS BETTER. These are different things. This distinction = the Novartis case holding.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
TRIPS = WTO Annex 1C, 1 Jan 1995, 164 members. 4 principles: National Treatment(Art.3), MFN(Art.4), Minimum Standards(Art.1), Exhaustion(Art.6). 7 areas: Copyright/TM/GI/Designs/Patents(20y)/IC Layouts/Trade Secrets. India: 1994 sign → 1999 mailbox → 2002 term → 2005 product patents+Sec.3(d)+Bolar. NOVARTIS 2013: beta crystalline form; bioavailability≠therapeutic efficacy; Sec.3(d) upheld; Rs.1.2L vs Rs.8K; evergreening exposed.

Paris Convention — 3 Core Principles
🤝
National Treatment — Art.2
Give foreigners the SAME IP protection as your own nationals. Samsung filing in India = treated like Indian company.
⏱️

Unit II · Topic 5 · ★★★★☆
Berne Convention (1886) & PCT (1970)
Memory Hook
"Victor Hugo's books were being pirated in Belgium. He fought for 10 years. In 1886, aged 84, he got the Berne Convention. Its ONE key principle: COPYRIGHT IS AUTOMATIC — no registration needed."
Berne Convention — 6 Principles
⚡
1. Automatic Protection ★
Copyright EXISTS on creation. No registration, no ©, no formality of any kind.
🌐
2. National Treatment
Foreign authors = same as domestic authors in every member country
⏰
3. Minimum Term
Life + 50 years minimum. India gives Life + 60 years (exceeds — permitted).
🔗
4. Independence
Each country's protection is independent — doesn't depend on protection in country of origin
🔄
5. Retroactivity
Works already in copyright when country joins Berne → continue to be protected
📝
6. No Formalities
No registration, no ©, no deposit needed — ever

⚡ QUICK RECALL

Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak
2008 · Supreme Court of India
STORYEBC publishes "Supreme Court Cases" (SCC) — they don't just print bare judgments. Their editors add headnotes (summaries of each case's ratio), cross-references, paragraph numbering, and footnotes. D.B. Modak (Lexis Nexis) copied EBC's editorial additions into their competing database. EBC sued for copyright infringement. The key question: do editorial additions to government documents (public domain) get copyright?
HELDSupreme Court judgments = public domain (Sec.52(1)(q)). BUT editorial additions enjoy copyright — only where they show MINIMUM CREATIVITY. Well-crafted headnotes showing legal judgment = copyrightable. Simple paragraph numbering = mechanical = NOT copyrightable. India adopts "MODICUM OF CREATIVITY" standard — NOT "sweat of brow."
EXAM ANGLE"What is the standard of originality in Indian copyright?" → Modicum of creativity (Eastern Book Co. 2008). Mere labour/effort = NOT enough. Creative judgment + effort = copyrightable.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
Copyright = exclusive rights in original expression; automatic; no registration. Sec.13: 6 subject matters (literary/dramatic/musical/artistic/film/sound recording). ORIGINALITY = modicum of creativity, NOT sweat of brow (Eastern Book Co. 2008 SC). Sec.17 OWNERSHIP: author (general) / employer (employment) / creator (freelance, unless assigned) / commissioner who paid (portrait/photo) / producer (film+recording) / government (govt work). Sec.14 RIGHTS: reproduce/communicate/translate/adapt. Sec.19: Assignment MUST be in WRITING. DURATION: literary/artistic = Life+60y; films/recordings = 60y from publication.
Unit III · Topic 7 · ★★★★★ — Heavy Part C use
Copyright Infringement, Exceptions & Remedies
Memory Hook
"3 tests to prove infringement: SUBSTANTIAL (quality not quantity) + ACCESS (they had it) + ORDINARY OBSERVER (would a normal person say 'this was copied?')"
3 Tests for Infringement
📏
1. Substantial Copying
QUALITY not quantity. Copying the "heart" of the work = infringement. 50 boring pages copied ≠ infringement. 2 key paragraphs with the plot twist = infringement.
👁️
2. Causal Connection
Defendant must have had ACCESS + actually copied. Independent creation of a similar work = NOT infringement, even if identical.
🧑‍⚖️
3. Ordinary Observer Test
Would an ordinary reasonable person say "this was copied from that"? NOT an expert test.
🎭
R.G. Anand v. M/s Delux Films
1978 · Supreme Court of India — The Foundational Indian Copyright Case
STORYR.G. Anand wrote a Hindi play "Hum Hindustani" (We Indians) about inter-state provincialism — a man from one state loves a woman from another, families oppose on regional grounds. He showed it to filmmaker Mohan Sehgal. Sehgal later made the film "New Delhi" — also about inter-state regionalism and prejudice. Anand claimed the film copied his play.
HELDNO infringement. The THEME of inter-state regionalism = an IDEA, not expression. Copyright protects specific expression (characters, dialogues, scenes) — not general themes or social subjects. TEST: Would an ordinary person say the film was copied from the play? No — different characters, dialogues, scenes.
EXAM ANGLEUse for BOTH infringement (T7) AND idea-expression (T8). "Similar themes ≠ infringement. The ordinary observer test."
Section 52 — Fair Dealing Exceptions (What is NOT Infringement)
🔬
Private/Research [52(1)(a)]
Fair dealing for private study, research, criticism, review — with acknowledgement. NOT commercial.
📺
News Reporting [52(1)(b)]
Reporting current events in newspaper or broadcast. DD News showing clips of PM's speech.
🏛️
Judicial Proceedings [52(1)(iv)]
Reproduction for judicial, legislative, or administrative proceedings.
🏫
Educational Use [52(1)(j)]
Performance in educational institution. Teacher performing a play on annual day.
📚
Libraries [52(1)(o)]
Preservation copies; replacing damaged copies in libraries and archives.
💻
Computer Programs [52(1)(aa)]
Backup copies; studying functionality; decompilation for interoperability.
Remedies — Civil vs Criminal
⚖️

⛓️

🔍
Anton Pillar KG v. Manufacturing Processes Ltd.
1976 · UK Court of Appeal (Lord Denning MR)
STORYAnton Pillar KG (German electronics company) discovered their UK agent was secretly giving their confidential circuit diagrams to competitors. Problem: if they filed a standard court notice, the agent would immediately DESTROY all evidence — circuit diagrams, correspondence, customer lists. They needed to search the premises WITHOUT giving advance warning.
HELD3 conditions for ex parte search order WITHOUT NOTICE: (1) Extremely strong prima facie case; (2) Very serious damage potential; (3) Real, concrete possibility defendant WILL DESTROY evidence if given notice.
WHY LANDMARKCreated the "Anton Pillar Order" — the nuclear weapon of IP enforcement. India: granted under Order 39 Rules 1-2 CPC + Section 151 CPC. Bollywood studios use these routinely against DVD pirates.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
Infringement (Sec.51) = doing what only owner can do, without licence. 3 TESTS: substantial copying (quality≠quantity) + causal connection (access+copying) + ordinary observer test. RG ANAND 1978 SC: theme of regionalism = IDEA not expression; ordinary observer test established. Sec.52 fair dealing: private/research · news reporting · judicial proceedings · educational · library · computer backup. CIVIL (Sec.55): injunction+damages+account of profits+delivery up. CRIMINAL (Sec.63): 6 months–3 years+fine. ANTON PILLAR 1976: ex parte search order; 3 conditions; India = Order 39 CPC + Sec.151 CPC.
Unit III · Topic 8 · ★★★★☆ — 2025 Standalone Essay
Idea-Expression Dichotomy
Memory Hook
"The THEME of revenge is free for everyone — Shakespeare, Bollywood, Hollywood. What is protected is Shakespeare's specific words, characters, and scenes. Ideas belong to humanity. Only your unique expression belongs to you."
The Spectrum — From Idea to Protected Expression
ABSTRACTION SPECTRUM (Left = not protected → Right = fully protected)
Abstract IdeaYoung lovers, families oppose
General ThemeStar-crossed lovers
Elaborated PlotSpecific setting, subplot
Specific ScenesThe balcony scene
Exact Dialogue"What light through yonder window breaks"
Two Related Doctrines
🔀
Merger Doctrine
When there's only ONE way (or very few ways) to express an idea → the expression MERGES with the idea → NO copyright. Example: "Please enter your PIN" on an ATM. E=mc².
🎭
Scenes à Faire
"Scenes that must be done" — stock genre elements can't be copyrighted. Bollywood courtroom drama MUST have cross-examination. Horror MUST have creaking doors. No one owns genre conventions.
📊
Baker v. Selden
1879 · US Supreme Court — Directly Influential on Indian Law
STORYSelden wrote a book explaining a new bookkeeping system with specifically designed blank accounting forms (columns, headers, ruled lines) needed to use the system. He got copyright in the book. Baker started selling similar accounting forms — because any double-entry bookkeeping system NATURALLY leads to similar forms. Selden's estate sued Baker for copying the forms.
HELDCopyright in the BOOK is protected. But the blank FORMS needed to PRACTISE the accounting system are NOT protectable — that would give Selden a monopoly over the bookkeeping METHOD (which is an idea/system). Copyright in description of a system ≠ copyright in the system itself.
EXAM ANGLEDigital age relevance: Microsoft can copyright its specific Word code — but cannot copyright the IDEA of a word processor. Adobe can copyright its specific Photoshop code — not the CONCEPT of photo editing. Same principle.
🚨 ABSOLUTE RULE — TRIPS Art.9(2)
"Copyright protection shall extend to expressions and not to ideas, procedures, methods of operation or mathematical concepts as such." — TRIPS Article 9(2). MUST cite this in every idea-expression answer.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
GOLDEN RULE: copyright = EXPRESSION not IDEAS. TRIPS Art.9(2) codifies this. THE SPECTRUM: abstract idea (free) → general theme (free) → specific plot (protected) → exact dialogue (fully protected). MERGER DOCTRINE: only one way to express idea → no copyright (ATM PIN instruction, E=mc²). SCENES À FAIRE: stock genre elements not protected. BAKER v. SELDEN 1879: copyright in bookkeeping book ≠ copyright in the forms needed to use the system. R.G. ANAND 1978: theme of regionalism = idea, not protectable.
Unit III · Topic 9 · ★★★★☆ — Part A EVERY PAPER
Moral Rights — Section 57
Memory Hook
"You sold your novel to a publisher for ₹1 crore. They can publish it, adapt it, sell millions. But if they change the ending to the opposite of what you wrote, YOU CAN STILL SUE. Because moral rights survive even after you sell copyright."
The Two Rights
✍️
Paternity Right — Section 57(1)(a)
Right to CLAIM AUTHORSHIP. Right to have your name on the work. Right to prevent FALSE attribution — stop others claiming they wrote YOUR work.
🛡️
Integrity Right — Section 57(1)(b)
Right to OBJECT to any distortion, mutilation, or modification — BEFORE OR AFTER assignment of copyright — if it is PREJUDICIAL TO YOUR HONOUR OR REPUTATION.
Three Features That Make Moral Rights Special
💫
Survive Assignment
Even after selling ALL copyright → moral rights REMAIN with the author. They follow the person, not the property.
🔒
Inalienable
Cannot be contractually waived. Any clause saying "I waive all moral rights" = VOID. Law protects artists from being pressured.
♾️
Perpetual
No expiry date. Last FOREVER. After death → exercised by legal heirs. Unlike economic rights (Life+60y).
🗿
Amar Nath Sehgal v. Union of India
2005 · Delhi High Court (Justice Pradeep Nandrajog)
STORYAmar Nath Sehgal (Padma Shri sculptor) was commissioned by the Government of India in the 1950s to create a monumental bronze mural (~140×40 feet) for Vigyan Bhawan — India's premier international conference centre. He spent years creating it depicting Indian mythology and culture. The Government owned both the physical mural and its economic copyright. Then in the 1970s-80s, during renovation, Government officials — WITHOUT TELLING SEHGAL — demolished the mural and DUMPED IT IN A WAREHOUSE where it sat, broken and deteriorating, for years. Sehgal, in his 80s, discovered this through a magazine article. He sued the Union of India itself.
HELDSection 57 moral rights are INDEPENDENT of economic rights. Government ownership does NOT give it the right to demolish and mutilate the work prejudicially. Demolition + warehouse storage = violation of integrity right. Court awarded ₹5,00,000 damages AND directed the Government to RETURN THE MURAL to Sehgal for restoration.
WHY LANDMARKMost important Indian case on moral rights. Physical destruction of artwork = integrity right violation. Remedy can include PHYSICAL RETURN of the artwork — not just damages. India's Sec.57 goes beyond TRIPS (which excludes moral rights under Art.9(1)).
⚡ QUICK RECALL
Moral rights = Section 57, Copyright Act 1957. TWO RIGHTS: Paternity (claim authorship, prevent false attribution) + Integrity (object to distortion/mutilation prejudicial to honour — BEFORE OR AFTER assignment). THREE FEATURES: survive assignment + inalienable (waiver clause void) + perpetual (no expiry, heirs exercise). AMAR NATH SEHGAL 2005 Delhi HC: commissioned mural → Government demolished in warehouse → ₹5L damages + MURAL RETURNED. TRIPS Art.9(1) EXCLUDES moral rights. India's Sec.57 goes beyond TRIPS. Berne Art.6bis = international source.
Unit IV · Topic 10 · ★★★★★ — OVERDUE for 2026
Trademark Infringement vs Passing Off
Memory Hook
"INFRINGEMENT needs a REGISTERED mark. PASSING OFF needs GOODWILL. Always advise BOTH — they are independent, and if registration fails, passing off survives."
Classical Trinity of Passing Off — ALL THREE REQUIRED
🌟
1. GOODWILL
Established business reputation. New business with zero reputation = no goodwill = no passing off. Must exist in the relevant territory.
🎭
2. MISREPRESENTATION
False suggestion that defendant's goods ARE the plaintiff's — or associated with/endorsed by plaintiff. Intent irrelevant — innocent misrepresentation still counts.
💸
3. DAMAGE
Actual or likely damage to goodwill. Indian courts accept likelihood of future damage — proof of actual loss not always required.
Infringement vs Passing Off — The Key Differences
FeatureTM Infringement (Sec.29)Passing Off (Common Law)

ProofSimilarity + likelihood of confusionGoodwill + Misrepresentation + Damage
TerritoryWhere TM is registeredWhere goodwill exists
Transborder reputationGenerally irrelevantRELEVANT — Dongre v. Whirlpool
Can they overlap?YES — same act can be BOTH. Always plead both.
💊
Cadila Healthcare v. Cadila Pharmaceuticals
2001 · Supreme Court of India
STORYAfter a family split, two Cadila companies co-existed. Cadila Healthcare registered FALCIGO for an anti-malarial injection. Cadila Pharmaceuticals launched FALCITAB for the same purpose (falciparum malaria). FALCIGO vs FALCITAB — visually different endings but both start with FALCI-. Cadila Healthcare sued for passing off. Stakes: mix-up between anti-malarial drugs can be fatal.
HELDHIGHER STANDARD of confusion for pharmaceutical trademarks. Medicines are dispensed under time pressure, to semi-literate patients, by busy pharmacists. A mix-up can be life-threatening. The FALCI- prefix similarity + both being anti-malarials = sufficient confusion risk. Injunction granted.
EXAM ANGLEFor any pharma trademark Part C: apply the Cadila stricter standard — phonetic + visual + nature of goods (drugs = higher risk) + class of purchasers (literate/semi-literate).
🌊
N.R. Dongre v. Whirlpool Corporation
1996 · Supreme Court of India
STORYWhirlpool (American appliances) was globally famous but had not started selling in India or registered its TM here. An Indian trader N.R. Dongre registered "WHIRLPOOL" in India and began selling appliances. Whirlpool sued for passing off — arguing that Indians knew their brand from international magazines like Time circulated in India, and from Indians who had used Whirlpool products abroad.
HELDTRANSBORDER REPUTATION DOCTRINE: A mark can have GOODWILL in India through international advertising and exposure — even WITHOUT any Indian sales or registration. Dongre's registration cancelled.
WHY LANDMARKClosed the door on "trademark squatting" — registering foreign brands before they enter India. Applied to protect McDonald's, Yahoo!, Dominos in India before they launched officially.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
TM = Sec.2(1)(zb): badge of origin. INFRINGEMENT = Sec.29: (1)identical+identical [29(1)] (2)identical+similar→confusion [29(2)(a)] (3)similar+identical→confusion [29(2)(b)] (4)well-known mark+any goods→dilution [29(4)]. PASSING OFF = common law; NO registration needed. CLASSICAL TRINITY = Goodwill+Misrepresentation+Damage. PERRY v.TRUEFITT 1842: origin of passing off. CADILA 2001 SC: FALCIGO/FALCITAB; higher pharma standard. DONGRE v.WHIRLPOOL 1996: transborder reputation. ALWAYS ADVISE BOTH infringement AND passing off.

Unit V · Topic 13 · ★★★★★ — Highest Part C frequency
Patentability — Conditions & Section 3 Exclusions
Memory Hook
"To get a patent: NEW + CLEVER + USEFUL. Then check Section 3 — the list of things India absolutely refuses to patent (plants, drugs that aren't really new, software code, etc.)."
3 Conditions — ALL Must Be Satisfied
🆕
1. NOVELTY — Sec.2(1)(l)
Not in prior art ANYWHERE in the world before filing date. Even inventor's own prior publication destroys novelty (except Sec.31 grace period).
🧠
2. INVENTIVE STEP — Sec.2(1)(ja)
Not obvious to a "person skilled in the art" — a hypothetical expert with ALL current knowledge but only ORDINARY creative ability.
🏭
3. INDUSTRIAL APPLICATION — Sec.2(1)(ac)
Can be made or used in some kind of industry. Pure theoretical concept with no practical use = not patentable.
Section 3 — Absolute Exclusions (Override All 3 Conditions)
🚫
3(a)
Frivolous / violates natural laws (perpetual motion machine)
🚫
3(b)
Contrary to law, morality, public health (bio-weapons)
🚫
3(c)
Discovery of naturally occurring substance (mineral, bacterium found in soil)
💊
3(d) ★
New form of known substance WITHOUT enhanced THERAPEUTIC EFFICACY (anti-evergreening)
🚫
3(e)
Mere admixture of known compounds (no synergy)
🚫
3(i)
Methods of treatment — surgical, therapeutic, diagnostic
🌱
3(j) ★
Plants, animals, essentially biological processes (PPV&FR Act governs)
💻
3(k) ★
Mathematical methods · business methods · computer programs PER SE · algorithms
🚫
3(m)
Literary/dramatic/musical/artistic/aesthetic creations (Copyright Act covers these)
🌿
3(p) ★
Traditional knowledge — turmeric, neem, basmati (belongs to communities, prior art)
⚗️
Novartis AG v. Union of India
2013 · Supreme Court of India · Section 3(d) Case
STORYNovartis wanted to patent the BETA CRYSTALLINE FORM (polymorph) of imatinib mesylate for leukaemia. Gleevec = Rs.1,20,000/month. Indian generics = Rs.8,000/month. The beta crystalline form had better BIOAVAILABILITY (30% better absorbed into bloodstream). But Section 3(d) asks: is there enhanced THERAPEUTIC EFFICACY (better patient treatment outcomes)?
HELDSection 3(d) is TRIPS-compliant. Bioavailability (how much drug is absorbed) ≠ Therapeutic efficacy (whether patient gets better). Novartis proved bioavailability — NOT therapeutic efficacy. Patent REJECTED. India's pharmacy of the world status preserved.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
Patent = 20-year exclusive right from FILING DATE. Sec.2(1)(j): new product/process + inventive step + capable of industrial application. 3 CONDITIONS: Novelty(Sec.2(1)(l)) + Inventive Step(not obvious to person skilled in art, Sec.2(1)(ja)) + Industrial Application(Sec.2(1)(ac)). SEC.3 KEY EXCLUSIONS: 3(c) naturally occurring · 3(d) new form without enhanced THERAPEUTIC EFFICACY (not just bioavailability — Novartis 2013) · 3(j) plants/animals (PPV&FR Act) · 3(k) software/algorithms/business methods PER SE · 3(p) traditional knowledge. Product patent ≠ process patent: product = any process blocked; process = only that process blocked.
Unit V · Topic 14 · ★★★★★
Rights & Obligations of the Patentee
Memory Hook
"Patent = monopoly for 20 years in exchange for FULL DISCLOSURE. Four ceilings on that monopoly: CL (Section 84), Government use (100), Bolar (107A(a)), Parallel imports (107A(b))."
Rights Under Section 48
📦
Product Patent
Prevent others from: MAKING · USING · OFFERING FOR SALE · SELLING · IMPORTING the product — by ANY method
⚙️
Process Patent
Prevent others from: USING the process + selling/importing product DIRECTLY OBTAINED by that process. Others can make same product by a DIFFERENT process.
4 Limitations on Patent Rights
📋
Compulsory Licence — Sec.84
After 3 years from GRANT. Any ONE of 3 grounds. Controller can override patentee's monopoly.
🏛️
Government Use — Sec.100
Central Govt can use any patent for govt purposes without consent. Compensation must be paid.
🔬
Bolar Exemption — Sec.107A(a) ★
Generic companies can MANUFACTURE a patented drug for regulatory testing BEFORE patent expiry. Day 1 generic entry. NOT infringement.
🌍
Parallel Imports — Sec.107A(b)
Importing from country where patentee sells at lower price = NOT infringement. International exhaustion.
🫁
F. Hoffmann-La Roche v. Cipla Ltd.
2008 · Delhi High Court (Justice S. Ravindra Bhat)
STORYRoche held Indian patent for erlotinib (Tarceva) — targeted lung cancer therapy. Tarceva = Rs.4,800/tablet (Rs.1,44,000/month). Cipla launched generic Erlocip at Rs.1,600/tablet — before Roche's patent expired. Roche applied for URGENT interim injunction to stop Cipla. Cancer patients' groups intervened.
HELDInterim injunction REFUSED. Balance of convenience: if injunction granted → cancer patients who can't afford Rs.4,800/tablet are denied life-saving treatment (irreversible harm = death). If refused → Roche loses some sales but can be compensated in damages later (reversible harm). PUBLIC INTEREST in affordable cancer medicine outweighs Roche's commercial interest.
EXAM ANGLEFor any pharma patent injunction Part C: apply Roche v. Cipla — don't automatically grant injunction for registered patent. Balance of convenience + public interest in affordable medicines must be weighed.
🚨 ABSOLUTE RULE — Most Tested Patent Fact
PATENT INFRINGEMENT = NO CRIMINAL PENALTY in India. CIVIL REMEDIES ONLY: injunction + damages/account of profits + delivery up. This is DIFFERENT from Copyright (Sec.63: up to 3 years imprisonment) and Trademark (Sec.103 TMA: up to 3 years). Writing "file a criminal complaint" for patent infringement = wrong answer = lose marks.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
Sec.48 RIGHTS: product patent = prevent making/using/selling/importing by ANY process; process patent = prevent using process + selling/importing product directly obtained. Duration: 20 years from FILING DATE (Sec.53). Annual renewal fees required. OBLIGATIONS: Sec.10 full disclosure (complete spec: describe+best method+claims+abstract); Sec.83+Form 27 annual working statement. 4 LIMITATIONS: CL(Sec.84) + Govt use(Sec.100) + BOLAR(Sec.107A(a) — regulatory testing not infringement) + Parallel imports(Sec.107A(b)). NO CRIMINAL PENALTY for patent infringement. ROCHE v.CIPLA 2008: public interest in affordable cancer medicine → interim injunction REFUSED.
Unit V · Topic 15 · ★★★★☆
Compulsory Licensing
Memory Hook
"Natco's story: Bayer's cancer drug costs Rs.2.84 lakhs/month. Natco asked for permission — Bayer said no. Natco went to court. India's first compulsory licence. Rs.8,880/month. 97% cheaper."
Section 84 — 3 Grounds (Any ONE suffices)
👥
Ground (a)
Reasonable requirements of the PUBLIC not satisfied. Bayer supplied 200 patients out of 8,842 needed = 2% of need.
💰
Ground (b)
Not available at REASONABLY AFFORDABLE PRICE. Rs.2.84L/month for a cancer drug = unaffordable for 99% of Indians.
🏭
Ground (c)
Not WORKED in India. Bayer was IMPORTING Nexavar, not manufacturing it. Importing ≠ working the patent in India.
Section 90 — Conditions on Every CL
🔄
Non-Exclusive
Patentee keeps the patent and can grant other licences too
⛔
Non-Assignable
Compulsory licensee CANNOT sub-license or transfer the CL
💵
Royalty Must Be Paid
6% of net sales = Natco benchmark for all future CLs
🇮🇳
Predominantly Domestic
For supply within India primarily. Exception: Section 92A (export to LDCs)
🔬
Natco Pharma v. Bayer Corporation — India's First CL
2012 (Controller) / 2014 (upheld by Bombay HC & SC)
STORYBayer held Indian Patent IN215758 for sorafenib tosylate (Nexavar) — used for kidney and liver cancer. Price: Rs.2,84,428/month (Rs.9,500/tablet). Bayer was IMPORTING Nexavar from Germany — not manufacturing in India. Need: 8,842 patients/year. Bayer supplied: ~200 patients (2%). Natco Pharma (Hyderabad) applied for CL November 2011, arguing all three Section 84 grounds.
HELDIndia's first EVER compulsory licence granted. (1) All 3 Section 84 grounds satisfied independently; (2) Royalty = 6% of Natco's net sales (BENCHMARK); (3) Natco must sell at Rs.8,880/month (97% reduction); (4) Natco must supply 600 patients FREE/year; (5) IMPORTING ≠ working the patent in India.
WHY LANDMARKFirst Indian CL. 6% royalty = benchmark for all future CLs. Importing ≠ working = landmark holding. Showed TRIPS flexibilities actually work in court. Encouraged other countries to pursue CLs for essential medicines.
Section 92 & 92A — Emergency Routes
🚨
Section 92 — National Emergency
Government grants CL directly WITHOUT 3-year wait. For national emergency, extreme urgency, or public health crises. Used as threat during COVID-19 negotiations.
🌍
Section 92A — Export to LDCs
CL for export to countries that can't manufacture pharmaceuticals (LDCs). Implements TRIPS Art.31bis. India as pharmacy of the world exports affordable generics.
🚨 TWO COMMON EXAM ERRORS
(1) "3-year period runs from FILING" — WRONG. It runs from DATE OF GRANT. If patent was granted 1 Jan 2022, CL available from 1 Jan 2025. (2) "All 3 grounds must be proved" — WRONG. ANY ONE of the three Section 84 grounds is sufficient.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
CL = authorisation to use patent without owner's consent in public interest. Sec.84: apply after 3 years from GRANT (not filing); any ONE of 3 grounds: (a)public need unmet (b)unaffordable price (c)not worked in India (importing≠working). Sec.90 conditions: non-exclusive+non-assignable+royalty(6%=benchmark)+predominantly domestic. Sec.92: national emergency, NO 3-year wait. Sec.92A: export to LDCs (TRIPS Art.31bis). NATCO v.BAYER 2012: first Indian CL; Nexavar Rs.2.84L→Rs.8,880; 6% royalty; 600 free patients; importing≠working.

Patent Infringement & Remedies
Memory Hook
"Patent infringement = civil case only. No handcuffs. No jail. Just injunction, damages, and delivery up. If you write 'file a criminal complaint' for patent infringement — you lose marks."
Acts Constituting Infringement (Section 48)
📦
Product Patent Infringement
Without authorisation: MAKING · USING · OFFERING FOR SALE · SELLING · IMPORTING the patented product in India
⚙️
Process Patent Infringement
Without authorisation: USING the patented process + selling/importing the product DIRECTLY OBTAINED by that process
What is NOT Infringement — Defences
DefenceSectionWhat It Covers
Bolar Exemption107A(a)Manufacturing for regulatory approval testing BEFORE patent expiry. Cipla making Roche's drug to get DCGI approval = NOT infringement.
Parallel Imports107A(b)Importing from country where patentee authorised sale at lower price = NOT infringement.
Prior Use64(1)(k)Person commercially using the invention in India BEFORE patent filing date can continue.
Experimental/Research Use47(3)Using patented invention for scientific research or experimentation = NOT infringement.
Government Use100Central/State Govt authorised use for government purposes = NOT infringement (with compensation).
Remedies — CIVIL ONLY
🛑
Injunction
Interim (during trial) or permanent (after judgment). Primary remedy. For pharma: Roche v. Cipla public interest test applies.
💰
Damages OR Account of Profits
Plaintiff ELECTS one or the other — cannot claim both simultaneously. Account of profits = defendant pays ALL profits from infringement.
🗑️
Delivery Up & Destruction
All infringing goods delivered to plaintiff and destroyed. Prevents back-door re-entry to market.
🚨 THE SINGLE MOST TESTED PATENT FACT
PATENT INFRINGEMENT → NO CRIMINAL PENALTY. Civil remedies only: injunction + damages OR account of profits + delivery up. Compare: Copyright infringement (Sec.63) = up to 3 years imprisonment. TM infringement (Sec.103 TMA) = up to 3 years imprisonment. Patents = NO imprisonment. NO fine. NO police. CIVIL COURT ONLY.
Section 64 & 106 — Two Additional Tools
⚖️
Sec.64 — Revocation Counter-Claim
Defendant in infringement suit can COUNTER-CLAIM for revocation of the patent (novelty/inventive step/Sec.3 grounds). This is Cipla's and Natco's standard defence strategy.
⚠️
Sec.106 — Groundless Threats
Patentee makes unjustified infringement threats without basis? Threatened party can sue for: declaration + injunction against threats + damages caused by threats.
⚡ QUICK RECALL
INFRINGEMENT = Sec.48 acts without authorisation: making/using/selling/importing (product); using process or selling product directly from process (process patent). DEFENCES: Bolar(Sec.107A(a)—regulatory testing) · Parallel imports(Sec.107A(b)) · Prior use(Sec.64(1)(k)) · Experimental use(Sec.47(3)) · Govt use(Sec.100). REMEDIES (CIVIL ONLY, Sec.108): injunction(primary—apply Roche v.Cipla public interest test for pharma) + damages OR account of profits + delivery up. NO CRIMINAL PENALTY. Sec.64 revocation counter-claim. Sec.106 groundless threats.
