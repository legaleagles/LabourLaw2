🔍 How to Judge Saree Quality — Even If the Tag Was Changed
The honest truth about price tags
There is no hidden mark woven into a saree that reveals its "original" price — unlike a sealed packaged product, a saree isn't legally required to carry a printed MRP on the fabric itself. The tag is genuinely the only price carrier, which is exactly why tag-swapping works. What you CAN do instead is verify what the saree actually is using the marks below — then judge whether the price makes sense for that verified category, rather than trusting the tag blindly.

🏷️
The Silk Mark Hologram
▾
A genuine Silk Mark tag is a cream-coloured hologram with a serial number, issued by the Silk Mark Organisation of India (SMOI) under the Central Silk Board. It guarantees 100% pure natural silk — no synthetic blend. It's tamper-proof: if someone peels it off to reuse on another piece, it visibly damages.
How to check: Look for the hologram tag physically, note the serial number, and verify it at silkmarkindia.com. No tag on a "pure silk" claim is itself a red flag worth asking about.
🧾
HSN Code 5007 on the Bill
▾
Pure silk fabric has its own tax classification code, HSN 5007. Some sellers avoid printing the correct code on the invoice to bypass tax scrutiny or quietly cover for a synthetic blend.
How to check: Ask for a proper bill and check it mentions HSN 5007 for a "pure silk" purchase. A vague or missing HSN code on an expensive silk claim is worth questioning.
📍
GI Tag for Named Regional Weaves
▾
Pochampally Ikat, Gadwal, Uppada Jamdani, Venkatagiri, Kanchipuram, Banarasi and other named weaves have Geographical Indication (GI) status — only genuine products from that specific region/technique can legally use that name and tag. A "Pochampally-style" saree without the GI tag may be a copy made elsewhere using power looms.
How to check: For any named regional weave sold at a premium, ask specifically for the GI tag or certificate, not just the name being used verbally.
🪢
Cooperative Loom Number or QR Code
▾
Sarees sold through official state handloom cooperatives (like APCO in Andhra Pradesh or TSCO in Telangana) often carry a sewn-in loom number, cooperative stamp, or QR code that traces back to the specific weaver or society — a genuine traceability mechanism that mass-produced copies don't have.
How to check: If buying from a cooperative-linked outlet or exhibition, ask to see this number/QR and what it traces to — genuine sellers will know immediately.
✋
Small Irregularities = Handmade, Not Flawed
▾
Genuine handloom weaving has tiny natural variations in the weave — slightly uneven spacing, small texture shifts. This is a sign of real hand-weaving, not a defect. Power-loom or machine-made copies are unnaturally perfect and uniform throughout.
How to check: Run your hand across the fabric and look closely at the weave. Perfect uniformity on a "handloom" claim is a sign it may not be hand-woven at all.
👂
The Rustle Test
▾
Genuine silk makes a distinctive soft rustling sound (called "scroop") when rubbed gently between your fingers, and has a particular dry, slightly rough feel. Synthetic "art silk" tends to feel slippery or plasticky and doesn't rustle the same way.
How to check: Gently rub a corner of the fabric between your fingers and listen. This is free, quick, and doesn't damage anything — do it before deciding on any expensive silk piece.
✨
The Zari Rub Test
▾
Genuine metal-coated zari (real silver or gold-coated thread) holds its colour under light friction. Cheap synthetic "zari finish" can show a different coloured thread underneath quickly when rubbed, since the metallic coating is just a thin surface layer.
How to check: Gently rub a small, inconspicuous part of the zari border with your thumbnail. Colour changing fast underneath is a sign it's a finish, not genuine zari.
💰
Now Sanity-Check the Price
▾
Once you've verified what the saree actually is — Silk Mark certified, GI-tagged, genuinely handloom — that specific, verified category has a typical market price range. A verified premium silk saree being sold far below that range is unusual; a plain power-loom piece priced like a certified handloom one is overpriced.
How to check: Search the specific weave name plus "price range" before a big purchase, or ask two other shops what a similarly certified piece costs. Verification tells you what it is; comparison tells you if the price is fair.

GST Confusion on an Expensive Saree
Confession: Some billing staff — usually not deliberately — charge 18% on a pricey silk saree, assuming "costly item means higher tax." Sarees are classified as fabric, not stitched apparel: flat 5% GST no matter how expensive.
Beat it: If a saree bill shows more than 5% GST, question it on the spot. Stitched pieces like blouses and lehengas do scale by price — 5% up to ₹2,500/piece, 18% above — so this rule is saree-specific.

Kavitha's Diwali Saree Hunt
6 tricks in one shopping trip — 5 min read
▾
Kavitha stepped out of the auto at 10 a.m., Diwali bonus in her purse and a mental list of three sarees — one for herself, two for her daughters-in-law. The shop's entrance was impossible to miss: a life-size cutout of a film actress draped in gold-zari silk, and beside it, a single saree on a velvet stand with a tag reading ₹48,000.
🎯 Real Trick — Anchoring: That ₹48,000 showpiece isn't there to sell. It's there so the ₹9,000 saree Kavitha actually wants feels like a steal by comparison. Her sense of "reasonable" just got reset before she'd even said a word.
A young saleswoman appeared instantly. "Randi randi, akka! Meeku entha bagundo choodandi" — come, come sister, see how lovely this looks on you — and within two minutes Kavitha had a cup of filter coffee in hand and her name remembered from a visit eight months ago.
🎯 Real Trick — Warmth Before the Price Tag: The "akka," the coffee, the remembered name — all genuine-feeling, and all designed so that saying "too expensive, I'll pass" starts to feel rude, not financial.
"Amma, only for today — first 100 customers get an extra 10% off the whole bill," the saleswoman added, glancing at a queue that didn't actually seem to have any counter or token system at all.
🎯 Real Trick — Fake Urgency: No token, no counter, no list — just a line repeated to every customer that walks in, all day. Kavitha decided to ask for a token number. There wasn't one. She relaxed and kept browsing at her own pace.
Near the back, a "Kg Sale" table was piled high with sarees, a hand-written sign reading "₹1,200/kg." A heavier piece with dense zari border caught her eye — it felt like more saree for the same weight-based price. She almost grabbed it before checking the actual fabric quality underneath the border.
🎯 Real Trick — Kg Sale: Weight isn't quality. A heavier zari border can just mean more embroidery, not better fabric. Kavitha picked pieces by what she genuinely liked first, then asked for them to be weighed separately — not bundled into the general pile.
At the counter, the bill for her chosen saree read: MRP ₹9,500, Flat 60% off, Final: ₹3,800. It sounded incredible — until Kavitha remembered she'd seen an almost identical saree at a shop two streets away, no "discount" banner at all, priced at ₹3,600.
🎯 Real Trick — Inflated MRP: The ₹9,500 "original price" likely never existed as a real selling price. The 60% off is theatre. Kavitha's habit of remembering one other shop's plain price was the only real protection she needed.
As she paid by card, the billing staff added, "Would akka like matching bangles? Only ₹299, goes so well with this piece" — offered right as her card was already in the machine.
🎯 Real Trick — Billing Counter Add-On: Timed for the exact moment you're least likely to say no. Kavitha smiled, said "not today," and walked out with exactly the three sarees she came in for — nothing more, nothing less.
What Kavitha actually used:
✓ Didn't let the ₹48,000 showpiece reset her sense of a fair price
✓ Enjoyed the warmth, kept her decision separate from it
✓ Asked for a token number — none existed, so she took her time
✓ Picked pieces by preference before letting them be weighed
✓ Compared the final price to a shop with no "discount" theatre
✓ Said no to the counter-side add-on without a second thought

Meena's Wedding Shopping Trip
6 more tricks, wedding-season edition — 5 min read
▾
Meena's daughter was getting married in ten weeks, and the "Bridal Exclusive Collection" section of the showroom glowed under warm gold lighting, every saree looking richer and more festive than anything on the regular racks.
🎯 Real Trick — Shop Lighting: That warm, golden light is chosen deliberately. Colours and zari look far more brilliant under it than they will at home under a normal tube light. Meena asked to step toward the entrance, where daylight actually reached, before deciding on anything.
The same weave, she noticed once outside in the daylight, looked almost identical to a saree she'd seen in the regular section for 35% less — just without the word "Bridal" on its tag.
🎯 Real Trick — Bridal Markup: Wedding shopping runs on emotion, not comparison. Meena asked the staff directly to show her similar craftsmanship outside the bridal section — and found nearly the same piece for meaningfully less.
A large poster nearby featured a well-known Telugu actress draped in the season's "signature" saree. "This is the exact one madam wore at the function," the salesman said proudly.
🎯 Real Trick — Celebrity Endorsement: The actress's fee is baked into that saree's price somewhere. Meena reminded herself to judge the fabric and price exactly as she would with no famous face anywhere near it.
While trying on a blouse-piece bundle, the salesman removed the price tag from a saree "just to show how it falls" when draped. Meena, remembering something she'd read once, asked him to pin it back on before she decided anything.
🎯 Real Trick — Tag Removed, No Return: A missing tag is exactly what gets cited later if a return doesn't go smoothly. Meena kept the tag in sight the entire time she was deciding.
The "matching blouse piece" quoted for the bridal saree came to ₹2,200 — nearly four times what her regular tailor charged for a similarly well-matched plain fabric cut.
🎯 Real Trick — Matching Blouse Upsell: Framed as essential, priced at a steep premium. Meena asked the blouse-piece price separately before agreeing to any bundle — and chose to get a plain piece stitched by her regular tailor instead.
One saree didn't quite work for the muhurtham colour scheme, so Meena asked to return it. "Only store credit, valid 3 months," the counter said, printed in small text on the back of the bill she'd only just received.
🎯 Real Trick — Expiring Store Credit: Meena asked the expiry date out loud on the spot and set a phone reminder for two weeks before it lapsed — rather than risk forgetting and losing the value entirely.
What Meena actually used:
✓ Checked colour and zari in daylight, not shop lighting
✓ Compared the "Bridal" piece against the regular section
✓ Judged the deal without letting the celebrity face influence her
✓ Kept the price tag in sight until she'd decided
✓ Priced the blouse piece separately, used her own tailor instead
✓ Got the store credit expiry in writing and set a reminder
