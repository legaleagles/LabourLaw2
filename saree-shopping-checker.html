Saree & Dress Shopping Reality Check

Big "Flat 70% Off" banners, combo offers, and GST confusion cost families real money every festival season. 90 seconds before you pay.

1
What are you buying?
This decides the correct GST rate — sarees and stitched garments follow different rules
Sarees are classified as fabric by material (silk, cotton, etc.), not as a stitched garment — so GST is a flat 5%, no matter how expensive the saree is. A shop charging 18% on a costly silk saree is charging the wrong rate.
Stitched garments (lehenga, blouse, salwar suit) follow price-based GST: 5% if the piece is ₹2,500 or below, 18% if above ₹2,500 — this is the post-Sep-2025 rule, replacing the older ₹1,000/12% slab.

Inflated MRP is the classic trick: a shop marks the "original price" up first, so the advertised 50–70% off still lands at a normal or even above-market final price. Compare the final ₹ price to other shops — never trust the % alone.
4
Combo / "Buy 2 Get 1" offer?
Work out the real per-piece price
Enter the combo details to see your real per-piece price.
Combo deals often bundle one popular design with two slow-moving ones. Once you know the true per-piece price, ask to swap a filler piece for one you actually want at the same rate — many shops agree.
5
Hidden stitching charges
Fall, pico, and blouse stitching are often billed separately, unannounced
Not illegal, but should be quoted upfront, not sprung on you at the counter. Ask for the rate before the item goes for stitching, and get it itemised on the bill.
6
Silk or handloom claim?
Kanjivaram, Pochampally, Gadwal, Uppada, Mangalagiri, Banarasi, etc.
Genuine pure silk and authentic handloom sarees should carry a Silk Mark (Central Silk Board) or Handloom Mark tag. For high-value silk purchases, ask to see this tag physically and request it be mentioned on the bill — "pure silk" claimed verbally with no tag is unverifiable after you leave the shop.
7
Exchange / return policy
Check before paying, not after
Sale-season purchases are very commonly marked "exchange only, no refund" or "store credit only" — sometimes only in fine print on the bill. Ask this out loud before paying: "If this doesn't suit, can I get a refund or only exchange?" Get the answer while you still have a choice.

"Kg Sale" — Sarees Sold by Weight
A pile of mixed sarees priced per kilogram instead of per piece — often clearing old or mixed-quality stock, weight includes any zari/embellishment which makes some pieces "cost" more just by being heavier.
Leverage it: Go early, before the pile is picked over. Separate pieces you actually like, weigh them individually if allowed, and don't let staff bundle-weigh your pile with random extra pieces "to help you." A heavier saree isn't automatically better quality.

"Direct from Weaver / Wholesale Price"
Implies you're skipping the middleman markup — sometimes true (especially at genuine handloom exhibitions), sometimes just a phrase printed on every banner regardless of actual sourcing.
Leverage it: Ask directly which weaving cluster/cooperative it's from (Pochampally, Gadwal, Uppada, etc.) — a genuine direct-sourcing seller can usually answer specifically. Compare the quoted price against 2 other "wholesale" claims before trusting any single one.

The "Closing Sale" That Never Closes
Some stores run a "stock clearance / closing down" banner for months or years — it's a permanent discount framing, not an actual closure.
Leverage it: This isn't necessarily bad for you — a permanently discounted price is still a discount. Just don't let the fake urgency stop you from comparing prices elsewhere first; there's no real deadline pushing you.

A separate, often heavily marked-up section for wedding shopping, priced on emotion and occasion rather than the fabric/craft itself.
Leverage it: Ask to see similar craftsmanship/fabric outside the "bridal" section — often the same weave at a lower price without the wedding-collection label. Shop 2–3 months before the wedding season peak, when the same pieces are cheaper.

Celebrity / Film Star in the Ad
A well-known actor's face builds instant trust and warmth — but it tells you nothing about the actual price, quality, or fairness of the offer. The endorsement fee is baked into what you pay, one way or another.
Leverage it: Enjoy the ad, but evaluate the deal exactly as you would without the celebrity — same GST check, same MRP-math check, same per-piece combo math. A famous face has never once lowered your bill.

Group Buying / Kitty-Party Bulk Deals
Shops sometimes offer better per-piece rates for group purchases (5+ women buying together) — this one's a genuine leverage point, not a trick.
Leverage it: Actively use this — organise the trip with friends or a kitty-party group and ask upfront for a group rate before anyone picks pieces. Shops are far more likely to negotiate on a confirmed group of 5–10 buyers than one person alone.

Your Legal Backing
GST Council, effective 22 Sep 2025
Sarees taxed flat 5% by material classification (silk/cotton/synthetic fabric), regardless of price. Stitched apparel: 5% up to ₹2,500/piece, 18% above.
Legal Metrology Act
MRP shown must be genuine — inflating it to fabricate a bigger "discount" is a deceptive practice you can report.
Consumer Protection Act, 2019
Misleading discount claims and undisclosed mandatory charges are unfair trade practices — you can complain and claim redress.
