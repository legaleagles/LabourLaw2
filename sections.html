🧠 Why Section Numbers Feel Impossible — And How to Fix It
Section numbers feel arbitrary because they ARE arbitrary — the legislature chose them randomly. Rote repetition doesn't work because there's no logic to latch onto.

The solution: attach each section number to something that already lives in your memory — a story, a word sound, a pattern, or a visual image. Once attached, it never leaves.
📖
Story Method
Section number embedded in a story. Your brain remembers stories, not numbers.
🔢
Number Patterns
Find logic in the sequence. 436-437-438-439 = bail progression.
🔤
Word Sounds
154 = "one-five-four" = "ONE FIR" . The sound triggers the section.
🎨
Visual Anchor
Picture the number doing something related to the law. Makes it unforgettable.
CrPC 1973 / BNSS 2023 — Section Memory
Sec 154
→ 173
FIR — First Information Report
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word Sound
154 = "One Five Four" = "ONE FIR". The word "FIR" is hiding inside "one-five-four" if you say it fast: ONE-FI[R]-four. Whenever anyone says "154" you hear "FIR".
Story
Imagine a police station with the address House No. 154. That's where you go to file an FIR. "154 = FIR address." BNSS moved the FIR to a new address: 173 — "One-Seven-Three = ONE SEVEN FIR Three." The FIR moved to a bigger building (173 > 154).
Sec 41
→ 35
Arrest Without Warrant
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Visual
Police officer has 41 kilograms of handcuffs — heavy enough to arrest anyone without warrant. 41 = heavy arrest power. BNSS changed it to 35 — police got lighter cuffs (35 kg) because there are now more restrictions on arrest.
Pattern
Section 41A = 41 + A = Arrest with a WARNING LETTER first (A = Advance notice). BNSS 35A follows same logic. The "A" always means "give notice first."
Sec 50
→ 47
Inform Grounds of Arrest + Family
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Story
You are arrested. You have 50 seconds to know why — Sec 50 says police must tell you the grounds immediately. Then you get 50A = 50 seconds + A (Additional right) to inform your family. "50 = 50 seconds to know why, 50A = also tell your A-dam (family)."
Pattern
50 → 50A is always a pair. Never forget one without the other. BNSS: 47 (inform grounds) and 48 (inform family). They're still neighbours — 47 and 48, side by side, just like your rights go side by side.
Sec 57
→ 58
Produce Before Magistrate — 24 Hours
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word Sound
57 = "Five-Seven" = "FIVE-SEVEN hours?" No — 24 hours! The number 57 sounds like it could be a time (5:7 on a clock face). Every time you see 57, think: "That's when the clock runs out for police — produce before Magistrate in 24 hours!"
Story
A person is arrested. They sit in jail for exactly 57 minutes before the panic sets in — "57! 57! Time is running out!" — but they actually have 24 hours (Sec 57). BNSS moved this to 58 — one more minute! Same principle, just renumbered.
Sec 436
437 · 438 · 439
Bail Sections — The Staircase
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pattern — The Bail Staircase
These four sections climb like stairs — each step gives MORE freedom:

436 Bailable = Right to bail. You walk in, you walk out. 436 = "four-three-six = For Three Steps forward" (easiest)

437 Non-bailable = Discretion of Magistrate. One step harder.

438 Anticipatory Bail = Before arrest. One step ahead (anticipate!). 438 = "ante" (before) = anticipate

439 HC/Sessions = Highest court bail. Top of the staircase.

Memory: 436-437-438-439 = Easy → Hard → Before → Supreme. The numbers climb like your desperation to get out of jail!
Word Trick for 438
438 = "ANTE-cipatory" Bail. "ANTE" in poker means you put money in BEFORE the cards are dealt — similarly, anticipatory bail is applied for BEFORE arrest. "Ante = before = 438."
Sec 167(2)
→ 187
Chargesheet Filing — Default Bail
⭐⭐⭐⭐
Story
Police have 60 days (most cases) or 90 days (serious cases) to file chargesheet after arrest. Section 167 = "One-Six-Seven = 1, 6, 7 = One after 6 is 7 = after 60 comes 90 days." The numbers 6 and 7 inside 167 remind you of the 60/90 day rule!

If police miss the deadline → accused gets Default Bail (also called Statutory Bail). This is the most important practical right under Sec 167(2).
CPC 1908 — Section Memory
Sec 9
Courts to Try All Civil Suits
⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word Sound
9 = NAYA (new) in Hindi — every new civil dispute goes to court under Sec 9. "9 = Naya case filed." Also: 9 = a complete circle (look at the digit) = complete jurisdiction of civil courts.
Sec 10
Sub Judice — Stay of Suit
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word Trick
10 = TEN = "THEN wait" — when the same matter is already being heard (Sub Judice), the court says "THEN wait — stay the suit." Ten fingers up = STOP = Stay. Sec 10 = STOP the second suit.
Sec 11
Res Judicata
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Story
A thief tries to steal the same house ELEVEN times. The judge says: "ELEVEN = ENOUGH! Sec 11 = it is DONE, no more suits!" 11 = eleven = E-LEVEN = "enough, leave it." Res Judicata = matter already judged = leave it at 11.
Pattern
10 and 11 are consecutive — remember them as a PAIR:
10 = Same matter PENDING (Sub Judice) → STOP the suit
11 = Same matter DECIDED (Res Judicata) → BAR the suit
10 = it's still going on. 11 = it's over.
Sec 80
Notice Before Suing Government
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Visual
Picture the number 80 as a traffic speed limit sign on the road to the Government office. You MUST slow down (give 2 months notice) before entering (suing). "80 = speed limit = slow down before suing Government."
Word Trick
80 = "EIGHT-OH = WAIT-OH!" — Sec 80 says: "WAIT! Oh! Give notice first before suing Government." 80 days? No — 60 days (2 months). But 8+0 = 8 weeks = 2 months. The math works!
Sec 96
Sec 100
First Appeal / Second Appeal
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pattern — Appeal Numbers
96 First Appeal = 96 = "nine-six" = NINESIX = NICE trick (first try again). You get to re-argue everything — facts AND law.

100 Second Appeal = 100 = HUNDRED = ONLY ONE chance — one issue (Substantial Question of Law). 100% focus on ONE legal question only — no re-arguing facts. "100 = 100% legal, 0% facts."
Story
Round 1 of a boxing match (First Appeal, Sec 96): You can use ALL your moves — hooks, jabs, uppercuts (facts and law).
Round 100 (Second Appeal, Sec 100): The referee only allows ONE type of punch — the legal question punch. Miss it = you lose. "96 = freestyle, 100 = only one punch."
Order VII
Rule 11
Rejection of Plaint
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word + Number
Order VII = 7 = SEVEN = SE-VEN = "SIEVE it" (filter it) — Order VII is about the Plaint. Rule 11 is where the court uses a SIEVE to filter out bad plaints. "If it doesn't pass the SEVEN-SIEVE, Rule 11 REJECTS it."

Rejection grounds memory: 4 BASES — No cause of Action, Barred by law, Insufficient fee, wrong Valuation = NABIV? Easy: "NAB IV" — NAB the bad plaint under rule IV... (No COA, Abridged fee, Barred, Improper Valuation).
Negotiable Instruments Act 1881 — Section Memory
Sec 4 · 5 · 6
Promissory Note · Bill · Cheque
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pattern — Perfect Sequence
4 → 5 → 6 in ORDER of complexity and parties:

4 Promissory Note = 2 parties (Maker + Payee). Simplest. 4 = square = 2 sides each with one party.

5 Bill of Exchange = 3 parties (Drawer + Drawee + Payee). One more. 5 = five fingers = third party added.

6 Cheque = Bill + BANK. Special. 6 = 5+1 = Bill + bank added.

4-5-6 = PN-BoE-Cheque = 2 parties → 3 parties → 3 parties + bank. The numbers grow as the parties grow!
Sec 9
Holder in Due Course
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word Sound
9 = NINE = "IN TIME" anagram. Holder in Due Course must take the instrument IN TIME (before maturity). "Section NINE = IN TIME = Holder IN Due Course." Also: 9 is the highest single digit = highest protection (HDC gets the best title).
Sec 138
Cheque Bounce — Criminal Offence
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Story — The 138 Story
A man writes a cheque. The cheque BOUNCES. Think of a bouncing ball:

1 = ONE bounce (dishonour)
3 = THREE: send notice within 30 days (3 tens = 30)
8 = EIGHT: wait 8+7=15 days for payment... actually: 8 = "ate" = the deadline ate up 15 days

Better: 138 = "ONE THREE EIGHT = ONE THRICE WAIT" — you wait THREE times: wait for dishonour, wait for notice response, wait before filing. "138 = bounce, notice, wait = criminal if ignored."
Pattern — The 30-15-30 Rhythm
The timelines inside Sec 138 have a rhythm:
Notice within 30 days of dishonour
Pay within 15 days of notice
File complaint within 30 days after 15-day period

30 → 15 → 30. The middle (15) is half of 30. "30 down, half in the middle, 30 back up." Once you know this rhythm, you'll never forget Sec 138's requirements.
Sec 139
Presumption of Debt
⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pattern
139 = 138 + 1. The offence is 138. The PRESUMPTION supporting it is 139 — one step after. "138 creates the crime, 139 creates the proof." Always cite them together: "Section 138 read with Section 139."

Sec 3 · 4
State Bar Councils · Bar Council of India
⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pattern — Ground Floor vs Top Floor
3 State Bar Council = 3 = STREET level. States are on the ground — foundation of the profession.

4 Bar Council of India = 4 = FOUR floors up. National body at the top. "3 is state, 4 is nation — like a 4-storey building where floors 1-3 are states and floor 4 is BCI."
Sec 16
Senior Advocate
⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word Trick
16 = SIXTEEN = "SEEN-IOR" — close to "SENIOR." Sec 16 = Senior Advocate. "SixTEEN = senIOR." The number 16 sounds like it begins with "Sen" if you squint. Also: you need to be SEEN (visible, known) as a great lawyer to be designated Senior — Sec 16.
Sec 24
Sec 24A

Story
You want to become an advocate. You go to the enrollment office at Door Number 24. The guard checks if you QUALIFY (Sec 24). But there's a sign on the door: "24A = Those who are DISQUALIFIED, enter not." The "A" in 24A = "Avoid" — those who are disqualified must avoid this door. 24 = Enter (qualify), 24A = Avoid (disqualify).
Sec 30
Right to Practice in All Courts
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Visual
30 = THIRTY = THIRTY courts. Sec 30 gives you the right to practice in ALL courts — imagine 30 courts all opening their doors to you. "Sec 30 = 30 courts = ALL courts." Also: 30 = a round number = complete, full, all-encompassing right.
Sec 35
Disciplinary Proceedings & Punishment
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word Trick
35 = "THIR-FIVE = THRIVE... until misconduct!" An advocate THRIVEs in their career until Sec 35 catches them. "35 = the misconduct section that ends the thriving."

Punishments (3 levels):
1️⃣ Reprimand (slap on wrist)
2️⃣ Suspension (time out)
3️⃣ Removal from roll (permanent exit)

3-5 = 3 punishments, in 5 (Sec 35)

35 SBC decision (guilty or not)

35-37-38 = Crime → BCI → SC. Each number goes up, each forum goes up. Easy!
Contempt of Courts Act 1971 — Section Memory
Sec 2(b)
Sec 2(c)
Civil Contempt · Criminal Contempt
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pattern — Alphabet = Type
2(b) = B = Breaking court ORDER (disobeying decree/order/undertaking) = CIVIL contempt.

2(c) = C = Criticising the court (scandalising, prejudicing, interfering) = CRIMINAL contempt.

"B = Break an order = Civil. C = Criticise the court = Criminal." The NEXT letter in alphabet = the STRONGER contempt. B comes before C = Civil is milder, Criminal is worse.
Sec 12
Punishment for Contempt
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Word + Number
12 = TWELVE = "TWELVE months in jail?" No — only 6 months! But 12 ÷ 2 = 6. "Section TWELVE = half a year (6 months) jail OR ₹2000 fine." Also: 12 = a dozen = a complete punishment package (jail + fine).
Sec 13
Truth as Defence
⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pattern
12 = Punishment. 13 = Get out! Section 13 comes RIGHT AFTER 12 — it's the ESCAPE from punishment. "If you can prove TRUTH (Sec 13), you escape the punishment of Sec 12." In Western superstition, 13 is unlucky for the prosecution but lucky for the accused.
Art 129
Art 215
SC Contempt Power · HC Contempt Power
⭐⭐⭐⭐
Pattern
129 Supreme Court contempt power. 129 = 1 court at top = Supreme Court. Also: 1+2+9 = 12 = contempt punishment (Sec 12). The digits add up to contempt!

215 High Court contempt power. 215 = 2+1+5 = 8 = eight High Courts originally = HC. "215 = multiple courts (HCs) = HC contempt power."

Simpler: 129 = SC (one at top). 215 = HC (many below SC). 129 < 215 = SC comes first (top) even though number is smaller.
