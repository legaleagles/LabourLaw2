Social Media Scams — Stay Safe, Stay Private

A small, personal purchase gone wrong feels too embarrassing to talk about. It shouldn't stop you from getting help — reporting it doesn't mean anyone else finds out.

Why nobody talks about the ₹500 fraud
A lot of what gets sold through Instagram DMs and WhatsApp is genuinely personal — innerwear, beauty products, small accessories — and often under ₹1,000. When it turns out to be fake or never arrives, most women say nothing at all, not because the amount doesn't matter, but because reporting it feels like it means explaining what you bought, to someone you don't know, possibly in public. It doesn't have to be that way. Reporting to the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or calling 1930 is completely confidential — nothing you report is ever made public, and you're not required to explain the purchase to anyone beyond what's needed to process it. The amount doesn't have to be large for it to be worth reporting.

🔍
Verify Before You Ever Pay
▾
A page with many followers and pretty photos still isn't verified as a real, accountable seller. Followers can be bought, photos can be taken from anywhere, and a handle can be deleted and recreated in minutes.
Do this: Look for a real name, city, and — ideally — a phone number you actually call and get an answer from, before paying anything. If none of that exists, treat it as a stranger, not a shop.
🔢
Never Share an OTP, Ever
▾
No legitimate seller, delivery agent, or bank employee ever needs your OTP read out to them over a call or message. An OTP request during a "delivery confirmation" or "refund process" is one of the most common ways money actually leaves your account.
Do this: Treat any OTP request from anyone other than an app you personally initiated an action in as an automatic red flag — no exceptions, no matter how convincing the story.
📷
A QR Code Is for Sending Money, Not Receiving It
▾
Scanning a QR code and entering your UPI PIN always sends money out, never brings money in — a common scam has someone claiming you'll "receive a refund" by scanning a code and entering your PIN, which actually debits your account instead.
Do this: You never need to scan anything or enter a UPI PIN to receive money. If someone asks you to do either "to get a refund," stop immediately.
🖥️
Never Install a Screen-Sharing App on Request
▾
Apps like AnyDesk or TeamViewer, installed at a stranger's instruction to "help fix an issue" or "process a refund," give that person live access to everything on your screen, including banking apps.
Do this: No genuine customer support process for a small purchase ever requires remote access to your phone. Refuse, uninstall immediately if you already did it, and check your bank app for any unfamiliar activity.
📞
Google-Searched "Customer Care" Numbers Are Often Fake
▾
Scammers post fake customer care numbers that show up in search results for real brands and delivery companies — calling them connects you to the scammer directly, who then walks you through "verifying" your card or bank details.
Do this: Get support contact numbers only from the official app or the company's verified website, never from a general search result.
📸
Save Evidence Before You Need It
▾
Sellers who intend to disappear often block you or delete their account the moment you complain — taking your only record of the chat, product listing, and payment confirmation with them.
Do this: Screenshot the product listing, full conversation, and payment proof as you go, saved outside the app itself — not after a problem starts, before.
⏱️
The "Golden Hour" for Financial Fraud
▾
If money was actually taken from your account — not just a bad product, but genuine unauthorised payment — reporting within the first hour genuinely improves the chance your bank can freeze the transfer before it's withdrawn.
Do this: For any unauthorised transaction, call 1930 immediately and inform your bank's fraud helpline in parallel — speed matters more here than almost anything else you can do.
🤐
Reporting Is Confidential, Not Public
▾
Filing on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or calling 1930 is a private process between you and trained officials — nothing you submit is published, and the portal also has a specifically anonymous reporting option for certain sensitive categories.
Do this: Report even a small loss. Amount is not a filter for whether it's "worth" reporting — patterns from many small reports also help authorities catch repeat scammers faster.

Your Remedies — Step by Step
Step 1 — Save Everything Immediately
Screenshot the chat, product listing, payment proof, and seller profile before you complain or the seller might block you first.
Step 2 — Report Confidentially
File at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 — this is private, not public. For financial fraud, do this within the first hour if possible.
Step 3 — Tell Your Bank Too
If any payment was involved, inform your bank's fraud/dispute helpline in parallel — they can sometimes reverse or freeze a transaction independently of the cybercrime process.
Step 4 — Report on the Platform Itself
Report the seller's account/post to Instagram or WhatsApp directly — this doesn't recover your money but helps stop the same seller reaching others.
📞 Cyber Crime Helpline: 1930 (24/7, confidential) · cybercrime.gov.in · National Consumer Helpline: 1915
