Judgment, Victims, Appeals and Maintenance

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

The trial ends, but the story does not. The judgment must give reasons; a death sentence needs special reasons and the High Court's confirmation. The victim, once a mere witness, now has rights: compensation, free treatment, protection, and even an appeal of her own. And the losing side has three doors: appeal (a full re-hearing), revision (a check by a superior court for illegality), and reference (a lower court asking the High Court about the law). A criminal court has no power to review its own judgment.

Two everyday chapters complete the picture: maintenance (S.144), a quick remedy so that no wife, child or parent is left destitute, and public order (S.148–163), the powers to disperse unlawful assemblies and remove public nuisances.

Chapter 1 · asked 10× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Judgment and sentence · Death sentence · Victims of crime and compensation (S.392–399)

📖 The story: Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab

(1980) 2 SCC 684 · decided 9 May 1980

Bachan Singh was convicted of murder and sentenced to death. His case, along with others, raised the question whether the death penalty for murder was constitutional at all.

A Constitution Bench of five judges heard the challenge.

❓ Is the death penalty for murder constitutional, and when may it be imposed?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Judgment (S.392–393). Pronounced in open court; written in the court's language; contains the points for determination, the decision and the reasons; specifies the offence and the BNS section, and the sentence. On acquittal, it states the offence and directs release. After conviction, the accused is heard on sentence.

Death sentence. For an offence punishable with death or life, the judgment states the reasons for the sentence, and special reasons if death is awarded. A Court of Session's death sentence is not executed until confirmed by the High Court (S.407). It is given only in the "rarest of rare" cases Bachan Singh.

Who is a victim (S.2). A person who has suffered any loss or injury because of the act or omission of the accused, and includes his guardian or legal heir.

Compensation. (1) S.395: when passing judgment, the court may order the accused to pay compensation from the fine or otherwise, for the victim's loss or injury, or to the heirs in a death case. (2) S.396: every State has a victim compensation scheme; the Legal Services Authority pays even where the offender is not traced or identified, or is acquitted, and may give interim relief such as first aid and medical help. (3) S.399: compensation to persons groundlessly arrested.

Other victim rights. Free first aid and medical treatment by all hospitals in cases of rape and acid attack (S.397); a Witness Protection Scheme (S.398); information on the progress of investigation within 90 days (S.193); a right to be heard before withdrawal of prosecution (S.360); an advocate to assist the prosecution (S.18); and a right of appeal (S.413 proviso) Mallikarjun Kodagali.

🏛️ How the story ended

By a majority of 4:1, the death penalty was held constitutional.

Life imprisonment is the rule and death the exception; death may be imposed only in the "rarest of rare" cases, when the alternative option is unquestionably foreclosed, after weighing aggravating and mitigating circumstances, and with special reasons recorded.

Why it matters:

  • Consider both the crime and the criminal: age, mental condition, possibility of reform, circumstances.
  • Special reasons must be recorded (now S.393 BNSS); death needs High Court confirmation (S.407).
  • Justice Bhagwati, dissenting, held the death penalty unconstitutional.

📚 More stories on this topic

Mallikarjun Kodagali v. State of Karnataka: A man attacked in Karnataka saw the accused (one of them an MLA) acquitted; the State did not appeal, and the High Court held his own appeal not maintainable. Held: A victim can appeal against acquittal as of right, without seeking leave; the appeal was sent back to be heard. 🪝 The victim has a voice in appeal
Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra: In a murder case, the courts below convicted the accused but never considered compensation for the victim's family. Held: The compensation provision casts a mandatory duty on every court to apply its mind to compensation in every criminal case. 🪝 Don't forget the victim

Appeals, reference, revision and review in criminal cases (S.413–442, S.403)

🔁 Flashback: Mallikarjun Kodagali v. State of Karnataka (told earlier on this page). A man attacked in Karnataka saw the accused (one of them an MLA) acquitted; the State did not appeal, and the High Court held his own appeal not maintainable. Held: A victim can appeal against acquittal as of right, without seeking leave; the appeal was sent back to be heard. 🪝 The victim has a voice in appeal

📖 The story: The victim appeals

An accused is acquitted of attempted murder. The State decides not to appeal, but the injured victim wants to.

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

General rule (S.413). No appeal lies except as provided by the BNSS or another law. Proviso: the victim may appeal against acquittal, conviction for a lesser offence, or inadequate compensation, to the court that hears appeals from convictions.

Kinds of appeals. (1) From conviction (S.415): from a Magistrate to the Sessions Court; from the Sessions Court (and from a sentence above 7 years) to the High Court; from the High Court's original trial to the Supreme Court. (2) By the State against an inadequate sentence (S.418). (3) Against acquittal (S.419): by the State through the Public Prosecutor, to the High Court with leave (to the Sessions Court in certain Magistrate cases); by a complainant with special leave of the High Court in complaint cases. (4) Special right where co-accused appeal (S.421). The appellate court can suspend the sentence and grant bail (S.430).

No appeal (S.416–417). When the accused pleads guilty and is convicted on that plea (except as to extent or legality of sentence); and in petty cases where only small sentences or fines are imposed (S.417). In such cases revision is still available.

Reference (S.436). A court that is doubtful about the validity of a law (on which the case turns, not yet declared invalid by the High Court or Supreme Court) must state a case and refer it to the High Court; it may also refer other questions of law.

Revision (S.438–442). The High Court or Sessions Judge may call for the record of any inferior criminal court to check the correctness, legality or propriety of any finding, sentence or order, and the regularity of proceedings. Limits: not against interlocutory orders; a person who chose one forum cannot file a second revision (S.438(3)); the High Court cannot convert an acquittal into a conviction in revision (S.442), and cannot entertain revision by a party who could have appealed but did not.

Review. There is no power of review in criminal courts: once a judgment is signed, no court can alter or review it except to correct a clerical or arithmetical error (S.403). The High Court's inherent power (S.528) cannot be used to review either. The Supreme Court can review its own judgments under Art. 137.

🏛️ How the story ends

S.413 proviso: the victim may appeal against acquittal, conviction for a lesser offence, or inadequate compensation.

The victim can file his own appeal, without waiting for the State.

Chapter 3 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Maintenance of wives, children and parents (S.144–147) · Public order: unlawful assembly and public nuisance (S.148–163)

📖 The story: Neglected parents

An elderly couple's well-earning son refuses to support them.

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

Who can claim (S.144). If a person having sufficient means neglects or refuses to maintain: (a) his wife unable to maintain herself (including a divorced wife who has not remarried); (b) his minor child, legitimate or not; (c) a major child (not a married daughter) unable to maintain itself because of physical or mental abnormality; (d) his father or mother unable to maintain themselves, a Judicial Magistrate of the first class may order a monthly allowance. There is no upper limit; interim maintenance and expenses should be decided within 60 days of notice.

Conditions. A wife is not entitled if she lives in adultery, refuses without sufficient reason to live with her husband, or they live separately by mutual consent. But if the husband marries another woman or keeps a mistress, that is a just ground for her to live separately and still claim maintenance.

Procedure and enforcement (S.145–147). The case may be filed where the respondent or the wife resides; evidence is taken in his presence (or ex parte if he avoids service). For non-payment, the Magistrate issues a warrant to levy the amount and may sentence him to imprisonment of up to 1 month for each month's default, on an application within 1 year. The order can be altered on change of circumstances (S.146).

💡 Example (and case law): Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum (1985) held that this remedy is secular and available to wives of every religion. Rajnesh v. Neha (2021) directed both parties to file affidavits disclosing their assets and income, and made maintenance payable from the date of application.

Unlawful assembly (S.148–150). An assembly of five or more persons with a common object to commit an offence, resist the law, etc. (BNS 189). An Executive Magistrate or police officer may command it to disperse; if it does not, use civil force (S.148); if necessary, armed forces may be called (S.149–150). Protection from prosecution for acts done in good faith.

Public nuisance and urgent orders. Public nuisance is defined in BNS 270. A District / Sub-Divisional / Executive Magistrate may make a conditional order to remove a nuisance (obstruction of a public way, offensive trade, dangerous building) (S.152). In urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger, he may issue an order for up to 2 months (S.163, formerly S.144 CrPC), upheld as constitutional when used for public order (Madhu Limaye v. SDM Monghyr, 1970), but it must be proportionate (Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India, 2020).

🏛️ How the story ends

S.144: a person with sufficient means who neglects his wife, minor child, disabled major child, or father or mother unable to maintain themselves can be ordered to pay maintenance.

The parents petition the Magistrate and get a monthly maintenance order.

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. Satyam committed rape and is absconding. Can monetary relief be granted to the victim?

Asked: 2013 (also 2017: rape victim and accused not medically examined, case dismissed)

Issue
Can a victim get compensation when the accused is not caught or not convicted?
Law
S.396: victim compensation scheme; the State / District Legal Services Authority can award compensation even where the offender is not traced or identified, and interim relief. S.397: free medical treatment. S.184: medical examination of a rape victim within 24 hours, report within 7 days. S.84–85, S.356: proclamation, attachment, trial in absentia.
Apply
The victim (or her guardian) applies to the Legal Services Authority with the FIR; she gets interim and final compensation. Meanwhile the police proclaim Satyam and attach his property. (Where the investigation was botched and the case dismissed, the victim can appeal under S.413 proviso and seek compensation; the lapse is a ground for departmental action.)
Conclusion
Yes: compensation under S.396 even though the accused is absconding, plus free treatment under S.397; the trial can later proceed, even in absentia (S.356).

Problem 2. An in-charge Sessions Judge awarded a death sentence. Can it be executed?

Asked: 2014

Issue
Can a death sentence by a Sessions Court be carried out directly?
Law
S.407: when a Court of Session passes a death sentence, the proceedings must be submitted to the High Court, and the sentence shall not be executed unless confirmed by the High Court. The in-charge judge exercising Sessions powers is a Court of Session.
Apply
Without the High Court's confirmation the sentence cannot be executed; the convict also has appeals to the High Court and the Supreme Court, and mercy petitions.
Conclusion
No; it cannot be executed until the High Court confirms it (S.407).

Problem 3. H married W2 while married to W1. W1 wants maintenance from H. Advise her.

Asked: May 2022

Issue
Is a wife who lives separately because of her husband's second marriage entitled to maintenance?
Law
S.144: wife unable to maintain herself; husband with sufficient means neglecting her. Explanation: a husband's marriage with another woman is a just ground for the wife to refuse to live with him. Bigamy is also an offence (BNS 82).
Apply
W1 is the legally wedded wife; H's second marriage justifies her living separately.
Conclusion
W1 can file for maintenance (and interim maintenance) under S.144 before the JMFC; she may also prosecute H for bigamy.

Problem 4. C threw his aged, infirm parents A and B out of the house. What relief can they get?

Asked: Nov 2022

Issue
What remedies do neglected parents have?
Law
S.144(1)(d): father or mother unable to maintain themselves. The Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007: a Maintenance Tribunal can order monthly maintenance; abandoning a senior citizen is an offence; a transfer of property by them can be declared void if the child fails to look after them.
Apply
A and B cannot maintain themselves, and C has means.
Conclusion
They can claim maintenance under S.144 before the JMFC, or before the Maintenance Tribunal under the 2007 Act, and may seek to set aside any property gift made to C.

Problem 5. X, drunk, daily harasses his wife Y and one day tries to kill her. Under which provisions can she file a case, through whom, and what punishment?

Asked: Aug 2023

Issue
What offences arise and how is the case started?
Law
Attempt to murder BNS 109 (up to 10 years and fine; if hurt is caused, up to life). Cruelty by husband BNS 85. FIR under S.173 (cognizable) by Y or a relative; Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 for protection and residence orders.
Apply
Y (or her parents, or anyone who knows) can lodge an FIR. Attempt to murder is triable by the Court of Session; cruelty by a Magistrate, but both may be tried together.
Conclusion
FIR under S.173 for attempt to murder (BNS 109) and cruelty (BNS 85); trial by the Court of Session; punishment up to 10 years (life if hurt was caused), plus protection orders under the DV Act.

Problem 6. In a petty case the accused was fined a small amount. Can he appeal?

Asked: Sep 2021

Issue
Is an appeal available in petty cases?
Law
S.417: no appeal in petty cases where only a small sentence or fine (as specified) is imposed. S.416: no appeal on a plea of guilty except as to the sentence. S.438: revision lies.
Apply
The sentence falls within the petty-case limits.
Conclusion
No appeal lies, but he may file a revision before the Sessions Judge or High Court.

Problem 7. After conviction by the Sessions Court, the accused files a review petition before the same court. Maintainable?

Asked: (Part A "Review": 2015 · 2016 · Sep 2021 · Feb 2024 · Aug 2025)

Issue
Can a criminal court review its own judgment?
Law
S.403: once a judgment is signed, no court shall alter or review it, except to correct a clerical or arithmetical error. S.528: inherent power cannot be used to review. Remedy: appeal (S.415) or revision.
Apply
A review on merits is not allowed; only typographical or arithmetical slips can be corrected.
Conclusion
Not maintainable; the accused must appeal to the High Court under S.415.

Problem 8. The accused is convicted, but the court awards very low compensation to the victim. Can the victim challenge it?

Asked: (Part A "Victim": 2017 · 2019 · Sep 2021)

Issue
Does the victim have a remedy against inadequate compensation?
Law
S.413 proviso: the victim may appeal against acquittal, conviction for a lesser offence, or inadequate compensation Mallikarjun Kodagali. S.396: the victim can also seek compensation under the State scheme.
Apply
The victim files an appeal before the court to which appeals from that conviction lie (the High Court for a Sessions trial).
Conclusion
Yes: an appeal under the proviso to S.413, and an application under the victim compensation scheme (S.396).
