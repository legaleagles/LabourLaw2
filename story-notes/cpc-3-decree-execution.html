Decree and Execution

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Winning a suit is like getting a cheque that the other side will not cash. More than 150 years ago the Privy Council observed that a litigant's real troubles in India begin after he gets his decree. Execution is the part of the CPC that turns paper into money, land or obedience.

Think of the decree-holder knocking on four doors in turn: give me the thing (delivery), sell his property (attachment and sale), put him in civil prison (arrest, only for a dishonest debtor), or let a receiver collect (receiver). The judgment-debtor, in turn, keeps a small bundle that no creditor may touch: his clothes, his tools, his plough and his bullocks (S.60).

Chapter 1 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Judgment, decree and order · Preliminary and final decree · Mesne profits

⚡ 30-second version · Reasons, then result

📍After a two-year trial over a shop, the judge writes 20 pages: the facts, the issues, the evidence on each, and why he believes the plaintiff.
⚖️A judgment (S.2(9)) is the judge's statement of the grounds of the decree: facts, issues, findings and reasons.
✅Those 20 pages are the judgment. The one-page order "Defendant to vacate the shop" that follows is the decree.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Judgment (S.2(9))

The statement given by the judge of the grounds of a decree or order. It contains the facts, issues, findings and reasons (O.XX r.4–5).

Decree (S.2(2))

The formal expression of an adjudication which conclusively determines the rights of the parties on all or any matters in controversy in the suit. Essentials:

  1. an adjudication;
  2. in a suit;
  3. on rights in controversy;
  4. conclusive;
  5. formally expressed. It includes rejection of a plaint and questions under S.144 (restitution). It excludes an appealable order and a dismissal for default.
Preliminary and final

A decree is preliminary when further proceedings are needed before the suit is completely disposed of; final when it completely disposes of the suit. It can be partly preliminary and partly final. Preliminary decrees are passed in suits for partition (O.XX r.18), mortgage (O.XXXIV), accounts (r.16), dissolution of partnership (r.15), administration (r.13), pre-emption (r.14), and possession with mesne profits (r.12).

Order (S.2(14))

The formal expression of any decision of a civil court which is not a decree, e.g. an order on an application for temporary injunction. Decree-holder (S.2(3)): the person in whose favour the decree is passed; judgment-debtor (S.2(10)): the person against whom it is passed.

Mesne profits (S.2(12))

The profits which a person in wrongful possession actually received or might with ordinary diligence have received, with interest, but not profits due to improvements made by that person. The court may order an inquiry into them (O.XX r.12).

Conclusion

The judgment gives reasons, the decree gives the result, and only a decree conclusively settles rights.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
💡 ExampleExample: in a partition suit the court first declares "A gets 1/3, B 1/3, C 1/3" (preliminary decree). Then a commissioner divides the land by metes and bounds, and the court passes the final decree allotting actual plots.
Decree v. order (table points)

A decree arises only in a suit; an order may arise in a suit, application or proceeding. A decree may be preliminary or final; an order is never preliminary. Every decree is appealable unless barred; only orders listed in S.104 and O.XLIII are appealable. A second appeal lies against a decree (S.100), never against an order.

Chapter 2 · asked 7× 🔥🔥 Important

Execution of decrees: modes (S.51), arrest and detention, attachment and sale, garnishee

⚡ 30-second version · Jolly George Varghese

📍The Bank of Cochin got arrest warrants against two judgment-debtors without any inquiry into whether they could pay now.
❓Can a judgment-debtor be sent to civil prison merely because he has not paid a money decree, without any finding that he can pay and is refusing?
🏛️Set aside. Arrest needs bad faith or present means plus refusal; poverty alone is no ground (Art. 21, Art. 11 ICCPR).

🪝 No jail for being poor

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Jolly George Varghese v. Bank of Cochin · (1980) 2 SCC 360 · AIR 1980 SC 470 · decided 4 Feb 1980

The Bank of Cochin had a decree for ₹2.5 lakh against Jolly George Varghese and another; with two other money decrees, they owed the bank over ₹7 lakh.

The bank had already attached their immovable properties, and a receiver had been appointed to manage them. Still, on 22 June 1979 the executing court issued a warrant to arrest them and put them in civil prison under S.51 and O.XXI r.37.

The court did not inquire whether they had the means to pay at that time or were refusing in bad faith. The High Court dismissed their revision summarily, and they went to the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

Execution is the enforcement of a decree by the process of the court, so the decree-holder actually gets what was decreed (S.36–74, O.XXI). The court that passed the decree, or the court to which it is sent, executes it (S.38, S.39). Questions between the parties relating to execution are decided by the executing court under S.47, not by a fresh suit.

Modes (S.51)

On the decree-holder's application the court may order execution: (a) by delivery of property specifically decreed; (b) by attachment and sale, or sale without attachment; (c) by arrest and detention in civil prison; (d) by appointing a receiver; or (e) in such other manner as the nature of the relief requires. Money decrees can be executed at once on an oral application (O.XXI r.11).

Arrest and detention

For a money decree, arrest is a last resort. Proviso to S.51: the court must first give the judgment-debtor a chance to show cause (O.XXI r.37) and record reasons that he (a) is likely to abscond or has dishonestly transferred or concealed property, or (b) has or had the means to pay and refuses or neglects to pay, or (c) holds the money as a fiduciary.

Attachment (S.60)

All saleable property of the judgment-debtor can be attached, except: necessary clothes, cooking vessels, bedding, and ornaments a woman cannot part with by religious custom; tools of artisans; and an agriculturist's implements of husbandry, cattle and seed-grain needed to earn his livelihood; an agriculturist's or labourer's house; account books; a mere right to sue for damages; pensions; wages of labourers; part of salary (first ₹1,000 and two-thirds of the rest); provident fund; and others in S.60.

Garnishee order (O.XXI r.46A–46I)

When a debt owed to the judgment-debtor by a third person (the garnishee) is attached, the court can call the garnishee to pay it into court or show cause. If he pays, he is discharged; if he disputes, the court tries the issue; the order is executable like a decree.

Conclusion

Execution balances a winner's right to the fruits of the decree with the debtor's right to live with dignity.

➕ More for Part B and examples 4 more points
Limits on arrest

S.55: arrest may be made at any hour, but no dwelling house is entered between sunset and sunrise, no outer door is broken open unless the debtor himself occupies it and refuses access, and a woman's room is not entered without notice to her. S.56: no arrest or detention of a woman in execution of a money decree. S.58: maximum detention 3 months (decree above ₹5,000), 6 weeks (above ₹2,000 up to ₹5,000); none for ₹2,000 or less. Release does not wipe out the debt, but he cannot be re-arrested for the same decree. S.135, 135A: judicial officers going to court and legislators during sessions are exempt.

How attachment is made (O.XXI)

Movables in the debtor's possession: actual seizure (r.43). Debts and shares: prohibitory order (r.46). Negotiable instruments: actual seizure (r.51). Immovable property: an order prohibiting transfer, proclaimed by beat of drum and affixed (r.54). Any private transfer after attachment is void against the decree-holder (S.64).

💡 ExampleExample: Ravi has a decree for ₹2 lakh against Somu. Somu's customer Gopal owes Somu ₹1.5 lakh. The court attaches that debt and orders Gopal (garnishee) to pay ₹1.5 lakh into court instead of to Somu.
Sale (O.XXI r.64–94)

Proclamation of sale (r.66); not before 15 days (immovable) or 7 days (movable) from the proclamation (r.68); purchaser pays 25% at once and the balance within 15 days (r.84, 85). Setting aside: by depositing the amount due plus 5% for the purchaser (r.89), or for material irregularity or fraud causing substantial injury (r.90), within 60 days (Art. 127). Then confirmation (r.92) and a sale certificate (r.94); title passes from the date of sale (S.65).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. The Supreme Court set aside the orders and sent the matter back to decide afresh whether the debtors had the means to pay.

Detention under S.51 is not automatic: there must be a finding, with reasons, under the proviso, such as present means to pay combined with refusal or neglect.

  • "There must be some element of bad faith beyond mere indifference to pay."
  • A debtor who once had means but is now penniless cannot be jailed on that ground; his present means and other pressing needs must be weighed.
  • Article 11 of the ICCPR (no imprisonment merely for inability to fulfil a contract) guides how S.51 is read, though it is not directly enforceable.
  • Imprisoning a person for poverty would be an unfair procedure under Article 21.

📚 More stories on this topic

Rahul S. Shah v. Jinendra Kumar Gandhi: A 1986 land sale in Bengaluru led to a decree in 2006, but the execution petition filed in 2007 was still being fought in 2021. Held: Execution must normally end within 6 months of filing; frivolous objections, third-party claims and delays were curbed. 🪝 Decree in hand, money in 6 months
Vasudev Dhanjibhai Modi v. Rajabhai Abdul Rehman: A tenant facing an eviction decree argued in execution that the Small Causes Court never had jurisdiction, so the decree was a nullity. Held: Objection rejected. The executing court takes the decree as it is; only a nullity apparent on the record can be refused. 🪝 Execute it, don't re-judge it

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. P is executing a money decree against Q. Q deposits the decretal amount in a bank. Is Q discharged?

Asked: Nov 2022

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is a deposit in a bank a valid mode of paying a decree?
Law
O.XXI r.1: payment under a money decree is made (a) by deposit into the executing court (or sent to it by postal money order or through a bank), or (b) out of court to the decree-holder by postal money order, through a bank, or any other mode evidenced in writing, or (c) as the court directs. r.1(2): notice to the decree-holder. r.1(4): interest stops from payment or notice. r.2: payment out of court must be certified.
Apply
Depositing money in Q's own bank account is not payment to P or to the court. Only if Q deposited it to P's account through the bank, or into court, with written proof and notice, is it a valid payment.
Conclusion
A deposit in his own bank account does not discharge Q; he is discharged only on payment into court or to P through a recognised mode under O.XXI r.1, duly certified.

Problem 2. A wants to attach the agricultural produce and implements of the judgment-debtor X (an agriculturist). Maintainable?

Asked: 2018 · Nov 2020 (also Aug 2021: pump set and tractor)

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Are an agriculturist's implements and produce exempt from attachment?
Law
S.60(1) proviso (b): an agriculturist's implements of husbandry, cattle and seed-grain, as in the court's opinion are necessary to earn his livelihood as such, are exempt. S.61: the State may exempt the portion of agricultural produce needed for cultivation and support till the next harvest.
Apply
Courts have treated a tractor (Allahabad High Court, 1958) and a water pump engine as implements of husbandry. If they are needed for X's livelihood as an agriculturist, they cannot be attached. Produce beyond the exempted portion can be attached.
Conclusion
Not maintainable as to the implements (and the pump set and tractor) needed for X's cultivation; the produce can be attached only beyond the portion exempted under S.61.

Problem 3. A filed an execution petition asking for attachment and sale of B's property and also for B's arrest. Can he do so?

Asked: 2020

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can arrest and attachment be sought together, and is arrest automatic?
Law
O.XXI r.11(2)(j): the application states the mode of execution sought. O.XXI r.21: the court may, in its discretion, refuse simultaneous execution against the person and property. S.51 proviso and O.XXI r.37: show-cause notice and recorded reasons before arrest.
Apply
A may pray for both, but the court will usually proceed against property first. Arrest needs proof that B has the means and refuses, or is acting in bad faith Jolly George Varghese.
Conclusion
He can ask for both, but the court may refuse simultaneous execution (O.XXI r.21), and arrest will be ordered only after notice and a finding under the S.51 proviso.

📖 Story to quote: Jolly George Varghese

Problem 4. The judgment-debtor objects in execution that the decree is wrong. Can the executing court go into it?

Asked: (standard problem; Part A "Execution")

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can an executing court question the decree it is executing?
Law
S.47: execution questions are decided by the executing court. But the executing court must take the decree as it stands Vasudev Modi.
Apply
Only if the decree is a nullity (passed without inherent jurisdiction, apparent on the face of the record, or against a dead person) can the objection be heard. A plea that the decree is wrong must go to appeal or revision.
Conclusion
No; the executing court cannot go behind the decree, unless it is a nullity.

📖 Story to quote: Vasudev Modi

Problem 5. The judgment-debtor is a woman who owes money under a decree. Can she be arrested?

Asked: (Part A "Arrest and detention": 2016 · 2018 · Sep 2024 · Aug 2025)

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is civil arrest available against a woman judgment-debtor?
Law
S.56: notwithstanding anything in this Part, the court shall not order the arrest or detention in civil prison of a woman in execution of a decree for payment of money.
Apply
The decree is for money, so arrest is barred. Her property can still be attached and sold.
Conclusion
No; S.56 bars arrest of a woman in execution of a money decree, but her property can be attached.
