Law of Limitation

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Every right to go to court comes with a clock. Ravi lends Somu ₹50,000 on a promissory note payable on demand, dated 1 June 2022. The Limitation Act, 1963 gives Ravi 3 years to sue (Art. 35). If he sleeps till July 2025, the court must send him away. Not because Somu is honest, but because Ravi was late.

Two old maxims explain why: Vigilantibus non dormientibus jura subveniunt (the law helps those who are awake, not those who sleep on their rights) and Interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium (it is in the public interest that litigation must end). Evidence fades, witnesses die, and people need certainty.

Chapter 1 · asked 9× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Limitation: meaning, object, general principles · S.3 · "Limitation bars the remedy, not the right"

⚡ 30-second version · Katiji

📍The State appealed just 4 days late. The High Court threw the appeal out as time-barred.
❓Should a short delay, without any bad intention, stop a court from hearing a serious appeal on its merits? And should the State be judged more harshly than a private person?
🏛️Condone it. "Sufficient cause" must be read liberally; substantial justice beats technicality, and the State is treated like any other litigant.

🪝 4 days late, 6 golden rules

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag v. Mst. Katiji · (1987) 2 SCC 107 · AIR 1987 SC 1353 · decided 19 Feb 1987

The Jammu & Kashmir government acquired land at Anantnag for a public purpose. A lower court then raised the compensation from ₹1,000 to ₹8,000 per kanal, an increase of about 800%, adding nearly ₹14 lakh to the bill.

The State wanted to challenge this in the High Court, but its appeal was filed 4 days after the time limit. It asked the High Court to excuse the delay under Section 5. The High Court refused and dismissed the appeal as time-barred, without looking at whether the huge increase was right.

The State went to the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

Limitation is the fixed time within which a suit, appeal or application must be filed. The Limitation Act, 1963 (in force from 1 January 1964) lists these periods in its Schedule of 137 Articles. It is a statute of repose: after a time, disputes must rest.

Object
  1. Certainty and peace: interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium.
  2. Diligence: vigilantibus non dormientibus jura subveniunt.
  3. Fair trial: old claims are hard to prove or disprove.
  4. Stale claims should not be used to harass.
S.3: the bar

Every suit, appeal or application filed after the prescribed period shall be dismissed, although limitation has not been set up as a defence. So the court must check limitation on its own. S.3 is subject to S.4 to S.24.

"Bars the remedy, not the right."

After the period ends, the debt is not dead; only the court route is closed. So: if the debtor pays, he cannot ask for it back; the creditor can adjust money of the debtor he lawfully holds; and a written promise to pay a time-barred debt is a valid contract (S.25(3) Contract Act).

Conclusion

The Act balances two values: it shuts stale claims for certainty, but it saves honest litigants through disability, exclusion, acknowledgment and condonation.

General principles

(write at least 6):

  1. the court applies limitation on its own (S.3);
  2. it bars the remedy, not the right;
  3. exception: for property the right itself is extinguished (S.27);
  4. once time begins to run, it does not stop (S.9);
  5. courts cannot extend time on sympathy, only through the Act (S.5 is for appeals and applications, never suits);
  6. sufficient cause is read liberally Katiji;
  7. legal disability postpones the start, capped at 3 years after it ends (S.6, S.8);
  8. acknowledgment and part payment before expiry restart the clock (S.18, S.19).
🏛️ Why the court decided so

The Supreme Court condoned the delay, set aside the High Court's order and sent the appeal back to be decided on merits after hearing both sides.

It laid down six principles that every court in India still follows when it reads "sufficient cause" in Section 5.

It added one more point: the State must be given the same treatment as every other litigant, no better and no worse. Government files move slowly through many hands, so courts may take a practical view of official delay.

  • Ordinarily, a litigant gains nothing by filing an appeal late.
  • Refusing to condone delay can throw out a deserving case at the very threshold; condoning it only means the case is heard on merits.
  • "Every day's delay must be explained" does not mean a pedantic, hair-splitting approach. Apply it in a rational, common-sense, practical way.
  • When substantial justice and technical considerations clash, substantial justice wins. The other side has no vested right in an injustice caused by a non-deliberate delay.
  • There is no presumption that delay is deliberate, due to culpable negligence or mala fides. A litigant actually runs a serious risk by delaying.
  • Courts earn respect by removing injustice, not by legalising it on technical grounds.

📚 More stories on this topic

Punjab National Bank v. Surendra Prasad Sinha: The loan became time-barred. The bank still adjusted the guarantor's fixed deposit against it, and the guarantor filed a criminal case against the bank officers. Held: Complaint quashed. Limitation bars only the remedy (the court route); the debt itself survives, so the bank could adjust money it already held. 🪝 The door closes, the debt stays

Chapter 2 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Legal disability and its effect on the computation of limitation (S.6–9)

⚡ 30-second version · The 15-year-old landlord

📍A boy of 15 inherits a small shop in Secunderabad. The tenant stops paying rent. His elder brother, who could have helped, was in jail that whole time.
⚖️Only minority, insanity and idiocy are legal disabilities. Jail, illness, poverty and ignorance are not.
✅The boy's clock is postponed until he turns 18. The brother gets no extension for being in jail.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

The law does not expect a child or a person of unsound mind to protect his own rights, so it postpones the clock. Only three legal disabilities: minor (including a child in the womb), insane, idiot. Illness, imprisonment, poverty or ignorance are not legal disabilities.

S.6

If the person is under disability when the clock should start, he may sue within the same period counted from when the disability ends.

S.7: joint claimants

If one of several persons is disabled but the others can give a valid discharge without him, time runs against all; if not, it runs against none until one can.

S.8: the cap

S.6 and S.7 can never extend the period beyond 3 years after the disability ends (or death); no extension at all for pre-emption suits.

S.9

Once the clock has started, a later disability does not stop it. (Exception: if the debtor becomes administrator of the creditor's estate, the clock pauses during that administration.)

Conclusion

Disability postpones the start, never pauses a running clock, and is always capped at 3 years after it ends.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points

S.6 in detail:

  1. two disabilities at once, or a new one before the first ends: counted from when both end;
  2. disability lasts till death: his legal representative gets the same period from the death;
  3. if the legal representative is himself disabled, the same rules apply to him;
  4. if he dies after the disability ends but within his extended time, his legal representative gets the remaining benefit.

💡 ExampleWorked example: a right to possession (12 years, Art. 65) accrues when Arun is 10. The ordinary period ends at 22. S.6 would give 12 years from 18, but S.8 caps the extension at 3 years after majority (age 21). Since the ordinary period (22) is later, Arun can sue up to 22. Had the right accrued at age 5, the ordinary period would end at 17; then the cap gives him till 21.

Condonation of delay and "sufficient cause" (S.5)

🔁 Flashback: Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag v. Mst. Katiji. The State appealed just 4 days late. The High Court threw the appeal out as time-barred. Held: Condone it. "Sufficient cause" must be read liberally; substantial justice beats technicality, and the State is treated like any other litigant. 🪝 4 days late, 6 golden rules

⚡ 30-second version · N. Balakrishnan

📍883 days late, because his advocate took ₹2,000, did nothing and left practice without telling him.
❓Is a very long delay (883 days) automatically fatal, even when the reason is genuine and the fault was mainly the lawyer's?
🏛️Condoned. "Length of delay is no matter, acceptability of the explanation is the only criterion." The other side was paid ₹10,000.

🪝 883 days, but an honest reason wins

📖 Read the full story 4 short paragraphs

N. Balakrishnan v. M. Krishnamurthy · (1998) 7 SCC 123 · decided 3 Sep 1998

A suit for declaration of title was decided against the defendant ex parte (in his absence) on 28 October 1991. He applied to set aside that ex parte decree, but his application was itself dismissed for default on 17 February 1993.

His advocate never told him. In July 1995, when execution papers reached him, the same advocate took ₹2,000 and his signatures, and again did nothing. Only when an execution warrant was issued did he check the court records and learn the truth. The advocate had left practice to join a company.

He filed for restoration on 19 August 1995, a delay of 883 days. (He also won a consumer case against the advocate, who was ordered to pay ₹50,000.) The trial court accepted his explanation and condoned the delay. The Madras High Court reversed it in revision.

He went to the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

Under S.5 the court may admit an appeal or application after the time limit if the person shows sufficient cause for not filing in time.

Scope

Not for suits, and not for applications under Order XXI CPC (execution). Explanation: being misled by an order, practice or judgment of the High Court can be sufficient cause.

Sufficient cause

= a genuine reason beyond the person's control, without negligence or bad faith: illness, death in the family, the advocate's fault, being misled by the court. Courts read it liberally, so that substantial justice beats technicality Katiji.

Conclusion

Count the honesty of the reason, not the number of days.

➕ More for Part B and examples 1 more point
Limits

Liberal does not mean automatic. Delay caused by deliberate inaction, a casual approach or a tactic to harass is not condoned. Courts may put the applicant on costs to compensate the other side. Once the first court accepts the explanation, a higher court interferes only if that discretion was perverse.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

The Supreme Court set aside the High Court's order and restored the trial court's order condoning the delay, on condition that the appellant pay ₹10,000 to the other side within one month.

"Length of delay is no matter, acceptability of the explanation is the only criterion."

  • Condoning delay is a discretion of the court; what matters is whether the explanation is acceptable, not how many days have passed.
  • Rules of limitation are not meant to destroy the rights of parties. They exist so that people do not use dilatory tactics and seek remedies promptly.
  • "Sufficient cause" must be read liberally to advance substantial justice, when there is no mala fides or deliberate delay.
  • Once the first court accepts an explanation, a higher court should not interfere unless that discretion was arbitrary or perverse.
  • The court must also be fair to the other side. Where the delay is partly the applicant's own fault, he can be made to compensate the other side (here, ₹10,000).

Chapter 4 · asked 7× 🔥🔥 Important

Acknowledgment (S.18) and part payment (S.19): "a fresh period of limitation"

⚡ 30-second version · Shapoor Mazda

📍A borrower's letter said "a payment of 10,000 will stop the sale" and never said "I promise to pay".
❓Does a letter count as an "acknowledgment of liability" even if it never says "I owe you" or "I promise to pay"?
🏛️Still a valid acknowledgment. It must admit a present, subsisting liability; a promise to pay is not needed. The clock restarts from the date it is signed.

🪝 Admit the debt, restart the clock

📖 Read the full story 4 short paragraphs

Shapoor Fredoom Mazda v. Durga Prosad Chamaria · AIR 1961 SC 1236 · decided 1 Mar 1961

Durga Prosad Chamaria held a second (puisne) mortgage over property owned by J.C. Galstaun. A prior mortgagee had advertised the Chandni Bazar property for sale.

On 5 March 1932, Galstaun wrote and signed a letter to Chamaria saying that "a payment of 10,000 will stop the sale" and asking him to "take up the whole".

Chamaria sued Galstaun's heirs much later, on 18 May 1944. The suit could survive only if that 1932 letter counted as an acknowledgment of the mortgage debt, which would restart the limitation clock.

The trial judge said the letter was not an acknowledgment. The Calcutta High Court on appeal said it was. The auction purchaser of the property appealed to the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

S.18: acknowledgment

A written admission of liability, made before the period expires, in writing and signed by the party (or his agent), starts a fresh period from the date it was signed.

No promise to pay is needed, and the exact amount need not be stated (Explanation (a)); but it must admit a present, subsisting liability. Oral admissions do not count, and oral evidence of what the writing "meant" is not allowed.

S.19: part payment

Payment of part of a debt (or of interest on it) by the person liable or his agent, before the period expires, starts a fresh period from the date of payment, provided the payment is shown in the payer's handwriting or in a writing signed by him. A cash payment with no written trace does not help.

Conclusion

Both give a completely new period, not just extra days, but only if they come before the clock runs out.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
💡 ExampleExample: pro-note on demand dated 1 Jun 2022 (3 years, Art. 35). On 1 Jan 2025 Somu signs a letter admitting the debt: Ravi can now sue till 1 Jan 2028. If Somu instead pays ₹5,000 by cheque on 1 Mar 2025, the new period runs till 1 Mar 2028.
After expiry

Neither S.18 nor S.19 can revive a debt once the period has ended. Only a fresh written, signed promise to pay under S.25(3) of the Contract Act can, and then the suit is on that new promise.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

Yes. The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal: read in its context, the letter admitted the existing mortgagor–mortgagee relationship, so it was a valid acknowledgment and the suit was within time.

The case was decided under S.19 of the Limitation Act, 1908. The same rule is now S.18 of the Limitation Act, 1963.

  • The statement must relate to a present, subsisting liability.
  • It must show a legal relationship such as debtor and creditor, and an intention, express or implied, to admit it.
  • No promise to pay is needed. An acknowledgment only renews the debt; it does not create a new cause of action.
  • Read the words in context, with the surrounding circumstances, but oral evidence of what the writer "meant" is not allowed.
  • Courts should not stretch words to find an admission where none was made.

⚡ 30-second version · The Dussehra holiday

📍The 30-day appeal period ends on a day the court is closed for the Dussehra holidays.
⚖️S.4: if the period ends when the court is closed, file on the day it reopens.
✅Filed on the reopening day, the appeal is in time.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Intro

The period is strict, but the Act itself gives fixed ways to exclude, extend or restart time. Courts cannot add others.

S.4: court closed

If the period ends on a day the court is closed, file on the day it reopens. A court closed for part of its normal working hours counts as closed.

S.12

Exclude the first day. For an appeal, revision or review, also exclude the day of judgment and the time taken to get the certified copy.

S.13

Exclude time spent on an application to sue or appeal as an indigent person. S.14. Exclude time spent in good faith, with due diligence, in a court without jurisdiction. S.15. Exclude time under a stay or injunction, and the period of a required notice (e.g. 2 months under S.80 CPC).

Conclusion

The clock is strict, but it bends for honest causes the Act itself recognises.

➕ More for Part B and examples 3 more points
S.9: continuous running

"When time begins to run, it will continue to run": no later disability or inability stops it. The clock pauses only where the Act itself says so (S.4, S.12–15).

The full list of ways to save time

(for "When can the period be extended?"):

  1. court closed (S.4);
  2. sufficient cause for appeals and applications (S.5) Katiji N. Balakrishnan;
  3. legal disability (S.6–8);
  4. exclusions (S.12–15);
  5. fresh start by acknowledgment (S.18) Shapoor Mazda or part payment (S.19).
💡 ExampleExample: a District Court decree is passed on 1 May. The 90-day appeal period to the High Court (Art. 116) is counted from the next day, plus the days taken to get the certified copy. If the last day falls during summer vacation, the appeal filed on reopening day is in time (S.4).

Chapter 6 · asked 5× 🔥🔥 Important

How and when a person's right to property is extinguished · Adverse possession · Easement by prescription (S.25, S.27, Art. 64–65)

⚡ 30-second version · Ravinder Kaur Grewal

📍Can a person who has completed 12 years of adverse possession go to court as plaintiff, or only use it as a defence?
❓Can a person who has perfected title by adverse possession file a suit as plaintiff under Article 65 to protect that title?
🏛️Both. After 12 years the owner's right is extinguished (S.27) and the possessor gets title, usable as a sword (to sue) and a shield (to defend).

🪝 12 years: sword and shield

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Ravinder Kaur Grewal v. Manjit Kaur · (2019) 8 SCC 729 · decided 7 Aug 2019

Earlier, in Gurdwara Sahib v. Gram Panchayat, Village Sirthala (2014), the Supreme Court had said in passing that adverse possession could be used only as a shield, as a defence when the owner sues, and never as a sword to file a suit.

That left a strange gap. Suppose a person has openly and continuously occupied land against the owner for more than 12 years, and the owner's title is gone under Section 27. If the old owner (or someone he sells to) now throws him out, could he not even go to court?

This question was referred to a larger, three-judge bench of the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Intro

Usually limitation only bars the remedy. Part IV is the exception: time can transfer ownership itself.

S.27

When the period for a suit for possession of any property ends, the owner's right to that property is extinguished, not just his remedy.

Art. 65

(possession based on title): 12 years from when the defendant's possession becomes adverse to the plaintiff. Art. 64 (possession based on earlier possession, after being dispossessed): 12 years from dispossession.

S.25: easement by prescription

Light and air to a building, a way, a watercourse or any other easement, enjoyed peaceably, openly, as of right and without interruption for 20 years, becomes absolute and indefeasible. Against Government property the period is 30 years. The 20 years must end within 2 years before the suit in which it is claimed.

Conclusion

S.27 and S.25 protect long, open, settled enjoyment, and penalise an owner who sleeps for 12 years.

➕ More for Part B and examples 1 more point
When is possession "adverse"?

It must be nec vi, nec clam, nec precario: not by force, not secret, not by permission. It must also be continuous, peaceful, open and hostile to the true owner's title for 12 years. A tenant, licensee or relative allowed to stay is never in adverse possession. A trespasser's long possession alone is not enough.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

Yes. A person who has perfected title by adverse possession can sue as plaintiff, for example for declaration of title, injunction or recovery of possession. Adverse possession is both a sword and a shield.

Gurdwara Sahib (2014) and the decisions that followed it were overruled.

  • Article 65: a suit for possession of immovable property based on title can be filed within 12 years from when the defendant's possession becomes adverse to the plaintiff.
  • Once those 12 years end, Section 27 extinguishes the owner's right, and the possessor acquires the title that was extinguished.
  • Adverse possession must be open, continuous, peaceful and hostile to the owner (nec vi, nec clam, nec precario: not by force, not in secret, not by permission). A trespasser's long possession alone is not enough.
  • Article 64 still protects anyone dispossessed by force, even before title ripens (12 years from dispossession).

Problem 1. The plaintiff was in jail for the whole limitation period. Can he get the delay condoned after release?

Asked: 2015 · Feb 2024

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is imprisonment a legal disability, and can delay in filing a suit be condoned?
Law
S.6 lists only minor, insane, idiot. S.5 applies to appeals and applications only, not suits. S.3: a late suit must be dismissed.
Apply
Imprisonment is not among the three disabilities, so S.6 does not postpone the clock. And S.5 cannot rescue a suit at all.
Conclusion
No. The suit is barred and will be dismissed under S.3. (S.14 would help only if he had meanwhile pursued the claim in good faith in a wrong court.)

Problem 2. A owes a debt to a firm of B, C and D. B is insane, C is a minor, D can give a discharge alone. When does time run?

Asked: Feb 2024

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Does the disability of B and C stop time running against the firm's claim?
Law
S.7: if one of several persons jointly entitled is disabled, but a discharge can be given without his concurrence, time runs against all.
Apply
D can validly receive payment and give a discharge without B and C.
Conclusion
Time runs against all three (B, C and D) from the date the debt became due. B's insanity and C's minority give no extension.

Problem 3. A right to sue for immovable property accrued to a minor. After majority he stayed silent for 3 years. Can he sue now?

Asked: Aug 2021

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
How long does a minor get after attaining majority?
Law
S.6(1): same period counted from cessation of disability. S.8: but never more than 3 years after it ends. Art. 65: 12 years for possession based on title.
Apply
He gets whichever is later: (a) the ordinary 12 years counted from when the right accrued, or (b) 3 years after majority. He has used up the 3 years after majority.
Conclusion
He can sue only if the ordinary 12 years from accrual have not yet run out. If they have, the suit is barred: S.8 allows no more than 3 years after majority. Show both outcomes with ages for full marks.

Problem 4. The limitation period ends on a day when the court is closed (summer holidays / a holiday). When can he file?

Asked: 2019 · Aug 2023

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is the appeal or suit barred if filed after the holiday?
Law
S.4: if the period expires on a day the court is closed, it may be filed on the day the court reopens. S.12(2): for an appeal, exclude the day of judgment and the time for the certified copy.
Apply
The last day fell on a closed day, so the law moves the last day to the reopening day.
Conclusion
Filing on the first day the court reopens is within time. No condonation is needed.

Problem 5. Mr. Z lent money to Mr. G on a promissory note in 2020. Unpaid. Can Z file a recovery suit in 2025?

Asked: Aug 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is a 2025 suit on a 2020 pro-note within limitation?
Law
Art. 35: pro-note payable on demand, 3 years from the date of the note. S.18 / S.19: acknowledgment or part payment before expiry gives a fresh period. S.3: a late suit is dismissed.
Apply
3 years from 2020 would normally end in 2023. Bonus point most students miss: because of Covid, the Supreme Court excluded 15 Mar 2020 to 28 Feb 2022 from all limitation periods (In Re: Cognizance for Extension of Limitation, order of 10 Jan 2022). So the 3 years effectively ran from 1 Mar 2022 and ended by about the end of February 2025 at the latest. After that the suit is late, unless G acknowledged the debt in writing (S.18) or paid part, shown in his writing (S.19), before the period ended.
Conclusion
A suit filed in early 2025 may just be in time because of the Covid exclusion; one filed later in 2025 is time-barred and must be dismissed under S.3. If G acknowledged in writing or paid part before expiry, a fresh 3 years runs from that date. The debt itself survives PNB v. Surendra Prasad Sinha.

📖 Story to quote: PNB v. Surendra Prasad Sinha

Problem 6. X owned land in Y's possession since 1998. X sues Y in 2012 for recovery on the basis of title. Maintainable?

Asked: Feb 2025 (also 2017 · 2018 · Sep 2024: "how are rights to property extinguished?")

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Has X's title been extinguished by Y's possession for 14 years?
Law
Art. 65: 12 years from when the defendant's possession becomes adverse. S.27: on expiry, the owner's right is extinguished. Adverse possession must be open, continuous, peaceful and hostile (nec vi, nec clam, nec precario).
Apply
Possession alone is not enough; time runs only from when Y's possession became adverse to X. If Y was a tenant, licensee or permitted occupant, it was never adverse. If Y openly claimed the land as his own from 1998, 12 years ended in 2010.
Conclusion
If Y proves hostile possession from 1998, the 2012 suit is barred and X's title is extinguished (S.27); Y becomes owner Ravinder Kaur Grewal. If Y's possession was permissive or not proved adverse, the suit is maintainable.

📖 Story to quote: Ravinder Kaur Grewal

Problem 7. X paid a time-barred debt to Y and now wants the money back. Can he recover it?

Asked: 2020

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is payment of a time-barred debt recoverable?
Law
S.3 bars only the remedy, not the right. The debt continues to exist; S.25(3) Contract Act even makes a written promise to pay such a debt valid.
Apply
When X paid, he was paying a debt that legally still existed. Y received what was actually owed.
Conclusion
No; the suit is not maintainable. Limitation bars the remedy, not the right, so the payment was a valid discharge PNB v. Surendra Prasad Sinha.

📖 Story to quote: PNB v. Surendra Prasad Sinha

Problem 8. An ex parte decree was passed against Balu. Because of illness he could not apply to set it aside in time. What can he do?

Asked: Nov 2022

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can the delay in applying under O.IX r.13 CPC be condoned?
Law
Art. 123: 30 days from the decree (or from knowledge of it, if summons was not duly served). S.5 applies to applications, so delay can be condoned on sufficient cause.
Apply
Genuine illness, proved by medical records, is a recognised sufficient cause. The court looks at the honesty of the reason, not just the number of days.
Conclusion
He should file the O.IX r.13 application with an S.5 application and a medical certificate; the delay can be condoned Katiji N. Balakrishnan, possibly on payment of costs to the other side.

📖 Story to quote: N. Balakrishnan, Katiji
