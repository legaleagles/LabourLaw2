Trials, Plea Bargaining and Compounding

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Not every case needs the full ceremony of a Sessions trial. The BNSS gives four roads of decreasing length: the Sessions trial (gravest), the warrant-case trial (offences above 2 years), the summons-case trial (up to 2 years, no formal charge), and the summary trial (petty cases, short record, at most 3 months' jail).

And some cases need no trial at all. In compounding, the victim forgives and the case ends in acquittal. In plea bargaining, the accused admits guilt and, after a settlement with the victim, gets a lighter sentence. Both save time, but neither is allowed for grave offences or crimes against women and children.

Chapter 1 · asked 5× 🔥🔥 Important

Trial of warrant cases and summons cases (S.261–282) · Difference

📖 The story: Warrant case or summons case?

One man is accused of cheating (up to 7 years); another of simple hurt (up to 1 year).

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

Meaning (S.2). A warrant-case relates to an offence punishable with death, life imprisonment or imprisonment above 2 years; a summons-case is any case that is not a warrant-case (up to 2 years). The classification decides the procedure, not whether a summons or a warrant is issued.

Warrant case on a police report (S.261–266). Copies of documents supplied (S.261); accused may seek discharge within 60 days of supply; Magistrate discharges if the charge is groundless (S.262); otherwise frames the charge in writing within 60 days of the first hearing on charge (S.263); plea (S.264); prosecution evidence (S.265); defence evidence (S.266); judgment of acquittal or conviction (S.271).

Warrant case on a complaint (S.267–270). Prosecution evidence is taken first (S.267); discharge if no case (S.268); charge framed afterwards and the accused can cross-examine the prosecution witnesses again (S.269); defence (S.270). If the complainant is absent, the Magistrate may discharge the accused in compoundable or non-cognizable offences; for a false and frivolous accusation, compensation can be ordered (S.273).

Summons case (S.274–282). No formal charge: the particulars of the offence are stated to the accused (S.274); plea of guilty and conviction, even by post for petty offences (S.275–276); else evidence and judgment (S.277–278); absence of the complainant may lead to acquittal (S.279); the complainant may withdraw (S.280); the Magistrate may convert a summons case into a warrant case if the interests of justice require (S.282).

Differences (table):

Offenceabove 2 years · up to 2 years
Chargeformal written charge · particulars stated
Dischargepossible before charge · no discharge stage; acquittal
Absence of complainantdischarge only in some cases · may acquit
Recall of witnessesafter charge (complaint cases) · not provided
💡 Example: cheating (BNS 318(4), up to 7 years) is a warrant case, so a charge is framed; simple hurt (BNS 115(2), up to 1 year) is a summons case, so the Magistrate just states the accusation.

S.2: a warrant-case is an offence punishable with death, life or above 2 years; a summons-case is everything else. The label decides the procedure.

Cheating is tried as a warrant case; simple hurt as a summons case.

Chapter 2 · asked 7× 🔥🔥 Important

Summary trial (S.283–288) · Summary trial v. summons case · Judgment in a summary case

📖 The story: Clearing small cases fast

A Magistrate has 300 pending petty theft and intimidation cases.

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

Meaning. A summary trial is a short, quick trial of petty offences with an abridged record, to clear simple cases fast.

Who and which offences (S.283). A CJM or JMFC shall try summarily: theft, theft in a dwelling house, theft by a servant, receiving or helping hide stolen property, where the value does not exceed ₹20,000; criminal intimidation, insult to provoke breach of peace, certain house-trespass offences, abetment and attempts of these. Under S.283(2), a Magistrate may also try summarily any offence up to 3 years, after hearing the accused and recording reasons (no appeal against that decision). A second-class Magistrate may be specially empowered (S.284).

Procedure (S.285). The procedure of a summons case is followed (even for a warrant case), with modifications.

Limits. No sentence of imprisonment above 3 months (S.285); if the Magistrate feels a summary trial is undesirable, he must re-hear the case in the regular manner.

Record and judgment (S.286–287). The record contains particulars: serial number, date of offence, complainant, accused, offence and value of property, plea, finding, sentence, date of termination. Where the accused does not plead guilty, the Magistrate records the substance of the evidence and a judgment containing a brief statement of reasons.

Summary trial v. summons case: offences listed (S.283) v. any up to 2 years; maximum sentence 3 months v. the full punishment for the offence; abridged record v. full record; only CJM / JMFC (or empowered JMSC) v. any Magistrate.

🏛️ How the story ends

A summary trial is a short trial of petty offences with an abridged record.

Many can be disposed of quickly through summary trials.

Chapter 3 · asked 9× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Plea bargaining (S.289–300) and compounding of offences (S.359)

📖 The story: A deal with the victim

A man accused of cheating a neighbour of ₹2 lakh wants to admit it, repay, and get a lighter sentence.

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

Meaning. Plea bargaining is a pre-trial negotiation in which the accused pleads guilty in return for a lesser sentence, after a mutually satisfactory settlement with the victim. It was introduced in 2005 (Chapter XXIA CrPC) and continues as Chapter XXIII of the BNSS.

When available (S.289). For offences punishable with imprisonment up to 7 years. Not for: offences punishable with death, life or above 7 years; offences affecting the socio-economic condition of the country; offences against a woman or a child; and not for a person previously convicted of the same offence. Juveniles are excluded (S.300).

Procedure. (1) The accused files an application within 30 days of framing of charge (S.290), with an affidavit that he is doing so voluntarily; (2) the court examines him in camera to confirm voluntariness; (3) the court gives time to work out a mutually satisfactory disposition with the victim and prosecutor (in a police case also the investigating officer) (S.291), which may include compensation to the victim; (4) report to the court (S.292).

Disposal (S.293). The court awards compensation as agreed, hears parties on sentence, and may: release on probation or admonition; or, where a minimum is prescribed, sentence to half the minimum (one-fourth for a first-time offender); otherwise one-fourth of the maximum (one-sixth for a first-time offender).

Effect. The judgment is final; no appeal lies except a special leave petition or writ (S.295). Statements made in the application cannot be used for any other purpose (S.299). Time already in custody is set off (S.297).

Compounding (S.359). Settling a criminal case by the victim forgiving the accused. A table lists offences compoundable by the victim without the court's permission (e.g. simple hurt, wrongful restraint, defamation) and offences compoundable only with the court's permission. Composition has the effect of an acquittal. Offences not listed cannot be compounded; but the High Court may quash such cases on settlement in suitable private disputes (S.528).

💡 Example: Ravi is charged with cheating (BNS 318(4), up to 7 years, no minimum) for a ₹1 lakh fraud. Within 30 days of the charge he applies; he repays the victim ₹1 lakh plus compensation; being a first offender, he can get one-sixth of 7 years, about 14 months, or even probation.

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. An accused charged with cruelty to his wife (dowry harassment) admits guilt and asks for plea bargaining. Discuss.

Asked: 2016

Issue
Is plea bargaining available for an offence against a woman?
Law
Cruelty by husband: BNS 85 (up to 3 years and fine). S.289: plea bargaining does not apply to offences committed against a woman or a child.
Apply
Although the punishment is below 7 years, the offence is committed against a woman, which is expressly excluded.
Conclusion
Plea bargaining is not available; the trial must proceed (he may plead guilty in the ordinary way, but without the plea-bargain concessions).

Problem 2. Z burnt his wife to death for dowry. Before the court he admits guilt and asks for a reduced punishment. Will he succeed?

Asked: 2017

Issue
Can an accused of murder / dowry death get a reduced sentence by pleading guilty?
Law
Murder BNS 103 (death or life); dowry death BNS 80 (minimum 7 years, up to life). S.289: no plea bargaining for offences punishable with death or life, or against a woman. S.252: on a plea of guilty, the Sessions Court may convict, but in grave cases courts usually still take evidence.
Apply
Both exclusions apply. A plea of guilty does not lower the punishment fixed by law; the court cannot go below life for murder.
Conclusion
No. He cannot get plea-bargain benefits; the Court of Session will convict on evidence and sentence him under BNS 103 (or BNS 80).

Problem 3. Can a Magistrate try a theft of property worth ₹15,000 summarily? What is the maximum sentence?

Asked: (Part A "Summary trial": 2018 · Nov 2020 · Nov 2022 · Aug 2023 · Feb 2024)

Issue
Is the case triable summarily, and what are the limits?
Law
S.283(1): theft where the value of property does not exceed ₹20,000 shall be tried summarily by a CJM or JMFC. S.285: no imprisonment above 3 months in a summary conviction.
Apply
The value is ₹15,000, within the limit.
Conclusion
Yes, it must be tried summarily; maximum imprisonment 3 months (plus fine as provided).
