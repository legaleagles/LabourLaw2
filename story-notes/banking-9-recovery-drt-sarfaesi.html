Recovery: NPAs, DRT and SARFAESI

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Before 1993, a bank that wanted its money back filed an ordinary civil suit and waited ten or fifteen years while the borrower's factory rusted. Two laws changed this. The Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 created special tribunals (DRTs) only for bank dues. The SARFAESI Act, 2002 went further: a secured bank can take possession of the security and sell it without going to court at all, after giving the borrower a 60-day notice.

Think of it as two doors. The DRT door: go to a fast tribunal for a recovery certificate. The SARFAESI door: if you hold a mortgage or hypothecation, act on the security yourself, and let the borrower go to the DRT if he feels wronged.

Chapter 1 · asked 2× 🔥 Good to know

Non-performing assets: meaning and classification

⚡ 30-second version · The EMI that stopped

📍A small manufacturer stops paying the EMI on his term loan from 1 January. By early April nothing has been paid.
⚖️A loan becomes an NPA when interest or instalment is overdue for more than 90 days (RBI prudential norms; SARFAESI S.2(1)(o)).
✅After 90 days the bank classifies the loan as an NPA.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

An asset (loan or advance) ceases to generate income for the bank when interest or principal is not paid. Under RBI's prudential norms, it becomes a non-performing asset when interest or instalment remains overdue for more than 90 days. SARFAESI S.2(1)(o) adopts RBI's classification.

When overdue

Term loan: interest/instalment overdue over 90 days; overdraft/cash credit: account "out of order" for over 90 days; bills: overdue over 90 days; crop loans: two crop seasons (short-duration crops) or one season (long-duration crops).

Classification
  1. Standard asset (performing);
  2. Sub-standard: NPA for up to 12 months;
  3. Doubtful: sub-standard for 12 months;
  4. Loss: identified as uncollectible but not yet written off. Higher categories need higher provisions out of profits.
➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Early warning (Special Mention Accounts)

SMA-0 (overdue up to 30 days), SMA-1 (31–60), SMA-2 (61–90): to flag stress before an account turns NPA.

Why it matters

NPAs cut a bank's income and capital, so it lends less; depositors and the economy suffer. Hence the recovery laws: DRTs, SARFAESI, ARCs and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.

One-line recall: what must you remember?

A loan is like a cow: it gives milk (interest). If it gives no milk for 90 days, it is "non-performing"; after a year "doubtful"; and a dead cow is a "loss".

Chapter 2 · asked 5× 🔥🔥 Important

Debts Recovery Tribunals: constitution, jurisdiction, powers, procedure and role

⚡ 30-second version · Delhi High Court Bar Assn.

📍Lawyers challenged the law that moved bank recovery suits from civil courts to new Debts Recovery Tribunals; the Delhi High Court struck it down.
❓Was Parliament competent to set up tribunals to decide bank recovery claims?
🏛️The Supreme Court upheld the Act: Parliament could create tribunals for bank recovery under its power over banking.

🪝 Banks may have their own court

📖 Read the full story 2 short paragraphs

Union of India v. Delhi High Court Bar Association · (2002) 4 SCC 275

The Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 1993 took away from civil courts, including the Delhi High Court's original side, the power to try bank recovery suits of ₹10 lakh or more (now ₹20 lakh), and gave it to DRTs.

The Delhi High Court Bar Association challenged the Act, and the Delhi High Court declared it unconstitutional. The Union of India appealed.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Background

On the Narasimham and Tiwari Committees' advice, Parliament passed the Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 1993 (renamed the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act in 2016) to create special tribunals for fast recovery of bank dues. Its validity was upheld Delhi High Court Bar Assn..

Constitution

The Central Government sets up DRTs (S.3), each with a Presiding Officer qualified to be a District Judge, and Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunals (S.8), each with a Chairperson qualified to be a High Court Judge.

Jurisdiction (S.17, S.18)

Applications by banks and financial institutions to recover debts of ₹20 lakh or more. Civil courts have no jurisdiction over such claims (S.18), except the High Courts and Supreme Court under Arts. 226 and 32. The DRT also hears borrowers' appeals under S.17 SARFAESI.

Powers

A civil court's powers for summoning, discovery, affidavits, commissions, review and ex parte orders (S.22(2)); interim orders of attachment, injunction against transfer, and appointment of a receiver (S.19).

Recovery

The DRT issues a recovery certificate; the Recovery Officer recovers it by attachment and sale of property, arrest and detention of the defendant, or appointment of a receiver (S.25, S.28).

➕ More for Part B and examples 4 more points
Procedure (S.19, S.22)

The bank files an application where the defendant resides or carries on business, or where the cause of action arose; the defendant files a written statement and may claim set-off or counter-claim. The DRT is not bound by the CPC, follows natural justice and regulates its own procedure (S.22).

Appeal

To the DRAT within 45 days (S.20); the appellant must deposit 50% of the amount determined, which the DRAT may reduce to not less than 25% (S.21).

💡 ExampleReal-world example: a bank that lent ₹5 crore to a steel unit files before the DRT; within months the DRT attaches the unit's property so it cannot be sold off, then issues a recovery certificate, and the Recovery Officer auctions the property.
Role

Faster and specialised recovery, keeping bank credit flowing and reducing NPAs. Problems remain: vacancies, backlogs and long delays, so SARFAESI and the IBC now carry much of the load.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

Yes. The Act relates to banking (Entry 45, List I), and Parliament's power over banking includes setting up tribunals to recover bank dues; Article 323B did not limit that power.

The DRT procedure is fair; the Court noted that, after the 2000 amendment, a defendant can also raise counter-claims and set-off before the DRT.

  • DRTs are valid specialised forums.
  • Speedy recovery of public money is a legitimate aim.
  • Fair hearing within the tribunal remains essential.

Chapter 3 · asked 5× 🔥🔥 Important

SARFAESI Act, 2002: securitisation, asset reconstruction and enforcement of security interest

⚡ 30-second version · Mardia Chemicals

📍Borrowers challenged SARFAESI, which let banks seize security without going to court and required a 75% deposit before a borrower could even be heard by the DRT.
❓Is SARFAESI constitutional, and is the 75% pre-deposit for a borrower's appeal valid?
🏛️The Act is valid, but the 75% pre-deposit for an appeal to the DRT was struck down as oppressive; banks must consider objections and give reasons.

🪝 Seize, but listen first

📖 Read the full story 2 short paragraphs

Mardia Chemicals Ltd. v. Union of India · (2004) 4 SCC 311 · decided 8 Apr 2004

Soon after the SARFAESI Act came into force in 2002, many borrowers, led by Mardia Chemicals, challenged it in the Supreme Court.

They argued that letting banks take possession of factories and homes without any court order was arbitrary, and that S.17(2), which required a borrower to deposit 75% of the claimed amount before the DRT would hear his appeal, made the remedy illusory.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Object

The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 lets banks and financial institutions

  1. sell bad loans to asset reconstruction companies, and
  2. enforce their security without the intervention of a court. Its validity was upheld Mardia Chemicals.
Securitisation (S.2(1)(z))

Acquisition of financial assets by an asset reconstruction company (ARC) from an originator (bank), funded by issuing security receipts to qualified buyers. The ARC holds the loans in a separate trust/scheme for each deal, which acts as the special purpose vehicle (SPV), so that the assets are ring-fenced and the receipt-holders are paid from recoveries.

Enforcement (S.13)

Where a borrower in default has his account classified as an NPA, the secured creditor gives a notice under S.13(2) to pay the full dues within 60 days. If he does not, it may under S.13(4): take possession of the secured asset (and sell or lease it), take over the management of the business, appoint a manager, or require the borrower's debtors to pay it directly. The District Magistrate / Chief Metropolitan Magistrate helps take physical possession (S.14).

Exclusions (S.31)

No SARFAESI for: liens and pledges of movables; aircraft and ships; agricultural land; security for a financial asset not exceeding ₹1 lakh; cases where the dues are less than 20% of the principal and interest.

Borrower's remedies

Application to the DRT within 45 days of the S.13(4) measure (S.17), with no pre-deposit; appeal to the DRAT (S.18) on depositing 50% (reducible to 25%). A writ petition is not the normal remedy Satyawati Tondon.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Asset reconstruction

ARCs must be registered with RBI (S.3). After acquiring a bank's loan (S.5), the ARC steps into the bank's shoes and may change or take over the borrower's management, sell or lease the business, reschedule debts, settle dues, convert debt into shares, or enforce the security (S.9).

Safeguards

The bank must consider the borrower's objections and communicate reasons for rejecting them within 15 days (S.13(3A)); sale follows the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002 (valuation, 30 days' sale notice); the borrower can redeem the asset by paying the dues before the auction notice is published (S.13(8)); in a consortium, creditors with 60% in value must agree (S.13(9)).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

The Act is valid: it was enacted to deal with the huge problem of NPAs, and enforcing security without court intervention is a reasonable measure.

But S.17(2), requiring a deposit of 75% before the borrower could approach the DRT, was struck down as unreasonable and oppressive.

The creditor must consider the borrower's reply to the S.13(2) notice and communicate reasons for rejecting it (this led to S.13(3A)).

  • Fair procedure is part of SARFAESI.
  • The borrower's first remedy, before the DRT, must be real and accessible.
  • Public money in banks justifies speedy recovery.

📚 More stories on this topic

United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon: A guarantor facing SARFAESI action went straight to the High Court under Art. 226, which stayed the bank. Held: High Courts must not entertain writ petitions against SARFAESI action when an effective remedy exists under S.17 before the DRT. 🪝 First door: DRT

Problem 1. Banban Bank is a creditor of CVC Pvt. Ltd. The company makes losses and stops paying interest. Can the bank use S.13 of SARFAESI? In what circumstances and on what procedural conditions?

Asked: Aug 2025 (Hons)

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
When and how can a bank enforce its security under S.13?
Law
S.13(1)–(4), (3A), (8), (9), S.14, S.17, S.31 SARFAESI; RBI NPA norms; Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002 Mardia Chemicals.
Apply
Conditions: (1) the bank must be a secured creditor (mortgage, hypothecation over CVC's assets); (2) CVC's account must be an NPA (overdue over 90 days); (3) the dues must not be below 20% of principal and interest, and the security not agricultural land or a pledge (S.31); (4) in a consortium, 60% in value must agree. Procedure: S.13(2) notice → 60 days → consider objections and reply in 15 days → S.13(4) possession/management → DM's help (S.14) → valuation and sale with 30 days' notice. If an insolvency process under the IBC has begun, the moratorium bars it.
Conclusion
Yes, if it is a secured creditor and the account is an NPA; it must follow the notice-objection-measure steps, and CVC can challenge the action before the DRT within 45 days. If the loan is unsecured, the bank must go to the DRT or file a suit instead.

📖 Story to quote: Mardia Chemicals

Problem 2. Bank X has many NPAs and wants to transfer some of them to a third party. How can it do so under SARFAESI?

Asked: Jan 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
How are bad loans transferred under the Act?
Law
S.2(1)(z) securitisation; S.3 registered ARCs; S.5: acquisition of financial assets by an ARC, by agreement on terms; the ARC is then treated as the lender, with S.9 and S.13 powers; S.7: issue of security receipts to qualified buyers.
Apply
Bank X can sell the NPAs to an RBI-registered ARC for cash or security receipts, usually by auction. The ARC places them in a trust (SPV) and issues security receipts to qualified buyers; it recovers by restructuring, settlement or enforcing the security. The borrowers are informed of the assignment.
Conclusion
By assigning the NPAs to a registered asset reconstruction company under S.5, which securitises them through security receipts and recovers them using S.9 and S.13 powers.

Problem 3. A bank takes possession of a guarantor's house under S.13(4). She files a writ petition in the High Court, which stays the action. Is this proper?

Asked: Practice (May 2023: effective recovery)

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is a writ the right remedy against SARFAESI action?
Law
S.17: an aggrieved person may apply to the DRT within 45 days; S.18: appeal to DRAT. High Courts should not entertain writs where this effective remedy exists, except in exceptional cases Satyawati Tondon.
Apply
She has a full remedy before the DRT, which can set aside the measure and restore possession.
Conclusion
No. The High Court should decline to interfere; she must apply to the DRT under S.17 within 45 days.

📖 Story to quote: Satyawati Tondon
