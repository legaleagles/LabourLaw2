Accountancy for Lawyers

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

A client sends her lawyer ₹50,000: ₹10,000 for court fees and stamp papers, ₹40,000 as his fee. A year later the case is decreed and ₹3 lakh is deposited in court for her; the lawyer withdraws it. Whose money is in his hands, and how much? If he cannot answer with a clear account, he is not just careless: he may be guilty of professional misconduct.

That is why "accountancy for lawyers" is part of the ethics paper. An advocate handles other people's money, runs an office with expenses and taxes, and constantly reads accounts in cases (partnership, company, matrimonial, tax disputes). Accounting is the language of money, and a lawyer must speak it.

Chapter 1 · asked 13× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Accounting: meaning, nature, functions and branches

⚡ The answer in one glance

1Meaning2Nature3Functions4Branches5Basic records6Concepts

🧠 "Many Nice Families Bring Biryani Containers": Meaning · Nature · Functions · Branches · Basic records · Concepts.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

Accounting is the art of recording, classifying and summarising in money terms the transactions of a person or business, and interpreting and communicating the results to those who need them. Book-keeping is its first stage: the day-to-day recording.

Nature
  1. It is both an art (skill in presentation) and a science (systematic rules);
  2. the "language of business";
  3. a service function that supports decision-making;
  4. deals only with transactions measurable in money;
  5. based on principles such as double entry.
Functions
  1. Recording transactions systematically;
  2. classifying them under accounts (ledger);
  3. summarising (trial balance, profit and loss, balance sheet);
  4. analysing and interpreting;
  5. communicating to owners, clients, tax authorities and courts;
  6. meeting legal requirements (tax, Bar Council rules);
  7. protecting assets and preventing fraud.
Branches
  1. Financial accounting: records and final accounts (profit and loss, balance sheet);
  2. cost accounting: cost of a product or service;
  3. management accounting: information for decisions and budgets;
  4. tax accounting: income-tax and GST;
  5. auditing: independent checking of accounts;
  6. forensic accounting: detecting fraud for litigation. For lawyers, financial, tax and forensic accounting matter most.
➕ More for Part B and examples 3 more points
💡 ExampleExample: a law firm's accountant records each fee receipt in the cash book (recording), posts it to the client's ledger account (classifying), prepares the year's profit and loss account (summarising), and the partners use it to decide fees and staff for next year (interpreting).
Basic records

Journal (first entry of each transaction), ledger (accounts of each person, income and expense), cash book and bank book, trial balance (to check arithmetic), and final accounts. For an advocate: a client ledger for every client, a fee register, receipts, and a diary linking work to fees.

Concepts

Double entry (every debit has an equal credit), business entity (the lawyer's personal money is kept apart from the practice and from clients' money), accrual v. cash basis, going concern, consistency, and prudence.

One-line recall: what must you remember?

Accounting is "R-C-S-I-C": Record, Classify, Summarise, Interpret, Communicate. Its children are financial, cost, management, tax, audit and forensic accounting.

Need for accountancy for lawyers, and the Bar Council rules on client's money

⚡ 30-second version · Harish Chandra Tiwari

📍An advocate withdrew his client's land-acquisition compensation from court, did not pay it or tell him, and later produced a false settlement affidavit.
❓What punishment fits an advocate who misappropriates his client's money?
🏛️Retaining a client's money is grave professional misconduct; the Supreme Court replaced the three-year suspension with removal of the advocate's name from the roll.

🪝 Client's money is a sacred trust

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Harish Chandra Tiwari v. Baiju · (2002) 2 SCC 67 · decided 8 Jan 2002

Baiju engaged the advocate Harish Chandra Tiwari in a land acquisition case. Compensation was deposited in court for him.

With the court's permission, the advocate withdrew the money in 1987. He neither paid it to Baiju nor told him about it.

When Baiju complained to the Bar Council, the advocate claimed he had paid after deducting fees, and produced an affidavit of a supposed settlement, which was found to be fabricated. The Bar Council of India suspended him for three years; he appealed to the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Client's money

An advocate receives fees, money for expenses (court fee, stamps, copies), and money paid to the client through him (decree amounts, compensation, settlements). This is money held in trust; accounts protect both the client and the advocate.

BCI Rules 25–32

(Part VI, Chapter II, Duty to the Client). The advocate shall keep accounts of the client's money showing amounts received, expenses incurred and fees debited, with dates (r.25); entries must show whether money was for fees or expenses, and expenses cannot be diverted to fees without the client's written consent (r.26); he must tell the client at once of money received for him (r.27); at the end he may appropriate the settled fee from unspent money (r.28), or, if the fee was unsettled, deduct the fee payable under court rules and refund the balance (r.29); he must give a copy of the account on demand (r.30); he shall not convert client funds into loans (r.31), nor lend money to his client for the case (r.32).

Running the practice

Fees, salaries of clerks and juniors, rent, library, travel: proper accounts show the true income, help in fixing fees, budgeting and partnership sharing in law firms.

Misconduct risk

Without accounts, an honest advocate cannot prove he returned money, and a dishonest one is quickly exposed. Misappropriation of client's money is among the gravest professional misconduct (S.35 Advocates Act).

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Tax compliance

Income-tax law requires professionals, including lawyers, whose receipts exceed a prescribed limit to maintain books of account and, above a higher limit, to have them audited. Under GST, legal services to business entities are taxed on reverse charge (the business client pays the tax).

Accounts in litigation

Lawyers must read balance sheets and accounts in partnership dissolution, company and insolvency matters, matrimonial maintenance (true income of a spouse), tax appeals, bank recovery and white-collar crime (forensic accounts, money trail).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

The misconduct was of the gravest kind: money received for a client is held in trust, and the advocate had kept it for years and tried to cover it up with a false document.

Suspension was too lenient. The Supreme Court ordered that his name be removed from the roll of advocates.

  • BCI Rules 25-32: keep accounts, inform the client, refund promptly.
  • Misappropriation plus deceit can lead to removal from the profession.
  • The Supreme Court can enhance punishment in an appeal under S.38.

One-line recall: what must you remember?

A lawyer's accounts answer three questions at any moment: whose money is this, what is it for (fee or expense), and has the client been told? Rules 25–32 are the answers.
