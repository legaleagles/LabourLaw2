Arrest and Rights of the Arrested

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Arrest takes away the most precious thing a person has: liberty. So the law treats arrest like a surgery: done only when necessary, by the right person, in the right way, with a written record, and with the patient (the arrested person) told what is happening and allowed to call his family and lawyer.

Joginder Kumar, a young advocate, was picked up and held for five days without reason; D.K. Basu wrote to the Supreme Court about deaths in police custody; Arnesh Kumar showed how husbands and their families were arrested routinely on dowry complaints. Each case added a safeguard, and the BNSS now writes most of them into the law.

Arrest: meaning, arrest with and without warrant, notice of appearance, procedure (S.35–43)

⚡ 30-second version · Arnesh Kumar

📍A husband feared arrest on a dowry-cruelty complaint; the Court saw that arrests in such cases had become routine.
❓Can the police arrest automatically in offences punishable with up to 7 years?
🏛️For offences up to 7 years, the police must record why arrest is necessary; Magistrates must check before authorising detention; else issue a notice to appear.

🪝 Arrest only when necessary

📖 Read the full story 2 short paragraphs

Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar · (2014) 8 SCC 273 · decided 2 Jul 2014

Arnesh Kumar's wife alleged that he and his family demanded dowry and harassed her. Fearing arrest, he applied for anticipatory bail, which was refused by the Sessions Court and the High Court.

The Supreme Court noticed that in dowry-cruelty cases, husbands and even elderly relatives were being arrested mechanically, often only to pressure them.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

Arrest is the taking of a person into custody by authority of law, by actually touching or confining his body unless he submits by word or action (S.43). Ways of bringing a person before the court: summons, warrant, arrest without warrant, proclamation.

Arrest without warrant (S.35(1))

A police officer may arrest without warrant a person who: (a) commits a cognizable offence in his presence; (b) is reasonably suspected of a cognizable offence punishable up to 7 years, only if he has reason to believe it and arrest is necessary (to prevent another offence, for proper investigation, to stop tampering or threats, or to secure attendance), recording reasons in writing; (c) is credibly accused of an offence punishable with more than 7 years or death; (d) is a proclaimed offender; (e) has stolen property; (f) obstructs a police officer or escapes custody; and others listed. For non-cognizable offences: no arrest without warrant or a Magistrate's order (S.35(2)).

Notice of appearance (S.35(3)–(6))

Where arrest is not required, the officer issues a notice to appear. A person who complies shall not be arrested unless the officer records reasons; if he fails to comply, he may be arrested. Infirm persons or those above 60 cannot be arrested for offences below 3 years without a DSP's permission (S.35(7)).

Arrest with warrant

A warrant is a written order of a court, signed and sealed, directing a police officer to arrest a named person and bring him before the court. It stays in force until cancelled or executed. It is used where the court has issued summons and the person does not appear, or for offences where the court decides to compel appearance.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Procedure of arrest

S.36: the officer wears a clear identification and prepares an arrest memo attested by a family member or local witness and countersigned by the arrested person. S.37: a designated police officer in each district and station displays names of those arrested. S.43(3): handcuffs may be used only for habitual or repeat offenders, escapees and listed grave offences. A woman is arrested only by a woman officer and, except in exceptional cases with a Magistrate's permission, not after sunset and before sunrise (S.43).

Special persons

Members of the armed forces (prior Government consent for official acts); judges: arrest of a judicial officer follows the Supreme Court's guidelines Delhi Judicial Service Assn., and no criminal case is registered against a High Court or Supreme Court judge without consulting the Chief Justice of India (K. Veeraswami v. Union of India, 1991).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. The Court granted him protection and issued directions to all States.

Police must not arrest automatically; they must be satisfied that arrest is necessary under the statutory conditions, fill a checklist, and forward it to the Magistrate. Magistrates must check the reasons before authorising detention. Where arrest is not needed, a notice of appearance must be issued within two weeks of the case.

  • Arrest brings humiliation and curtails freedom; it must not be used as a tool of harassment.
  • Officers who disobey face departmental action and contempt; Magistrates who authorise detention mechanically face action by the High Court.
  • These directions now live in S.35 BNSS.

📚 More stories on this topic

Delhi Judicial Service Association v. State of Gujarat: The Chief Judicial Magistrate of Nadiad was arrested, handcuffed and tied with a rope by the local police, and photographed. Held: The officers were guilty of contempt; the Court laid down guidelines for arresting judicial officers, with intimation to the District Judge / High Court. 🪝 Even a judge can be arrested, but with dignity

Rights of arrested persons and accused · Illegal custody · Legal aid · Self-incrimination · Confessions before a Magistrate (S.183)

⚡ 30-second version · D.K. Basu

📍A legal aid activist wrote to the Chief Justice about deaths in police custody in West Bengal; the letter was treated as a petition.
❓What safeguards must the police follow on arrest and detention, and what is the remedy for custodial violence?
🏛️Eleven requirements for every arrest were laid down; custodial torture violates Art. 21 and the State must pay compensation.

🪝 Eleven rules against torture

📖 Read the full story 2 short paragraphs

D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal · (1997) 1 SCC 416 · decided 18 Dec 1996

In 1986, D.K. Basu, Executive Chairman of Legal Aid Services, West Bengal, wrote to the Chief Justice of India about newspaper reports of deaths in police custody.

The Supreme Court treated the letter as a writ petition and examined how arrests and detentions were being carried out across India.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

1. Grounds and bail

The arrested person must be told the full particulars of the offence or grounds of arrest at once (S.47(1), Art. 22(1)); for a bailable offence, also that he is entitled to bail (S.47(2)).

2. Inform family

The officer must inform a relative or friend nominated by him, and the designated police officer, of the arrest and the place of detention (S.48), and enter it in a register.

3. Lawyer and legal aid

Right to consult a legal practitioner of his choice (Art. 22(1)); right to meet an advocate during interrogation, though not throughout (S.38). An accused who cannot afford a lawyer gets legal aid at State expense (S.341, Art. 39A); a trial without it, where it was needed, is vitiated.

4. Medical examination

By a medical officer soon after arrest; a woman by a female medical officer (S.53); at his own request to show torture (S.53, and S.51 at the police's request).

5. 24-hour production

Produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours, excluding journey time (S.58, Art. 22(2)); further custody only by a Magistrate's order (S.187).

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
6. Right to silence

No person accused of an offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself (Art. 20(3)); in police questioning he may refuse to answer incriminating questions (Nandini Satpathy v. P.L. Dani, 1978). Narco-analysis, polygraph and brain-mapping without consent violate Art. 20(3) and 21 (Selvi v. State of Karnataka, 2010).

7. Confessions (S.183)

A confession is admissible only if recorded by a Judicial Magistrate (not a police officer), after warning the accused that he is not bound to confess and that it may be used against him, and after giving him time to reflect, free from police presence; the Magistrate certifies it was voluntary. Statements of witnesses may also be recorded (compulsory for some offences, and by video where possible).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

The Court laid down eleven requirements for every arrest, including identification badges, an arrest memo attested by a witness and countersigned by the arrested person, informing a relative or friend, medical examination every 48 hours, the right to meet a lawyer during interrogation, and informing the police control room.

Failure to comply makes officers liable to departmental action and contempt. Custodial violence violates Article 21, and the State must pay compensation.

  • Custodial torture is a naked violation of human dignity.
  • Compensation under public law is in addition to other remedies.
  • Most of these requirements are now part of the BNSS (S.36, S.38, S.48, S.53, S.58).

Problem 1. A was arrested and kept in illegal custody for 7 days. What remedies does he have against the police?

Asked: Nov 2022

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
What remedies lie for illegal detention beyond 24 hours?
Law
S.58 BNSS and Art. 22(2): production within 24 hours. Art. 21. Remedies: habeas corpus (Art. 32 / 226); compensation for violation of Art. 21 (Rudul Sah v. State of Bihar, 1983; D.K. Basu); criminal complaint for wrongful confinement (BNS 127); departmental action; S.399 BNSS compensation for groundless arrest.
Apply
Seven days without production is plainly illegal. He can seek release, compensation from the State, and prosecution of the officers.
Conclusion
Habeas corpus for release, compensation under Art. 32 / 226, a criminal complaint for wrongful confinement and departmental action against the officers.

📖 Story to quote: D.K. Basu

Problem 2. A judge is involved in a murder (or corruption) case. Can the police arrest him? How?

Asked: 2016 · 2019 · 2020

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
How is a judicial officer arrested?
Law
No absolute immunity. Guidelines Delhi Judicial Service Assn.: immediate intimation to the District Judge or High Court; formal arrest only if immediate arrest is necessary; no handcuffs; no statements recorded without his lawyer and the District Judge's presence; medical examination; no police custody without the District Judge's permission. For High Court / Supreme Court judges: no case registered without consulting the Chief Justice of India (K. Veeraswami).
Apply
The police may arrest a subordinate judge in a murder case, following the guidelines; the trial is by the Court of Session (murder, BNS 103).
Conclusion
Yes, the police can arrest a subordinate judge, but only in line with the Supreme Court's guidelines and with immediate intimation to the District Judge / High Court; for superior court judges, the CJI must be consulted first.

Problem 3. Mr. X is involved in a conspiracy. Can a police officer arrest him without warrant?

Asked: 2014

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is criminal conspiracy an arrestable (cognizable) offence?
Law
BNS 61: criminal conspiracy; its punishment follows the offence conspired. S.35(1): arrest without warrant for cognizable offences, subject to necessity for offences up to 7 years. First Schedule: conspiracy to commit a cognizable offence is cognizable.
Apply
If the conspiracy is to commit a serious (cognizable) offence, the police may arrest without warrant, recording reasons where the punishment is up to 7 years; if the object is non-cognizable, a warrant is needed.
Conclusion
Yes, if the conspiracy is to commit a cognizable offence (subject to the necessity test of S.35); otherwise only with a warrant.

Problem 4. An accused is denied a lawyer during his trial. He says this violates his right to a fair trial. Decide.

Asked: Feb 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is the right to counsel part of a fair trial?
Law
Art. 22(1): right to consult and be defended by a lawyer of choice. Art. 21 and 39A: free legal aid. S.341 BNSS: legal aid at State expense where the accused has no means. (Hussainara Khatoon, 1979; Khatri v. State of Bihar, 1981.)
Apply
A trial without a defence lawyer, where the accused wanted one or could not afford one, is not a fair trial.
Conclusion
His claim is right; the trial is vitiated, and a retrial must be held with a lawyer (legal aid if needed).

📖 Story to quote: Hussainara Khatoon

Problem 5. Z was arrested for dowry harassment (cruelty). Later his wife dies due to the harassment. Who can file a case, and is he punishable for her death?

Asked: 2015

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
What offences arise, and who may set the law in motion?
Law
Cruelty by husband: BNS 85 (cognizable, non-bailable). Dowry death: BNS 80 (death within 7 years of marriage, otherwise than normal, with cruelty soon before death; minimum 7 years, up to life). Abetment of suicide where applicable. Presumption of dowry death under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam. Inquest by the Executive Magistrate (S.194).
Apply
Anyone (her parents, relatives, the police on information) can give the FIR, since the offences are cognizable. If she died within 7 years of marriage after harassment for dowry, it is dowry death.
Conclusion
Her relatives or anyone may lodge an FIR; Z can be tried for dowry death (BNS 80) besides cruelty (BNS 85), by the Court of Session.
