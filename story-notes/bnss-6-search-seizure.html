Summons, Warrants, Search and Seizure

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

The court has four ways of getting a person or a thing before it, from gentle to forceful: a summons (a polite written call), a warrant (an order to arrest and bring), a proclamation (a public notice for someone who is hiding) and attachment of the absconder's property. For things, it uses a summons to produce, and if that fails, a search.

A search enters a person's home and privacy, so the law surrounds it with witnesses, lists and records; the BNSS now adds video recording, preferably on a mobile phone. Yet even an illegal search does not make the things found inadmissible; it only makes the court look at that evidence with more care.

Chapter 1 · asked 5× 🔥🔥 Important

Summons and warrants: meaning, contents, service, when a warrant is issued; proclamation and attachment

⚡ 30-second version · A summons on the phone

📍A witness in a theft case receives a court summons by encrypted email, digitally signed by the court.
⚖️S.63: a summons is a written, signed and sealed order to appear or produce a document; the BNSS allows it in encrypted electronic form with the court's digital signature.
✅The electronic summons is valid; he must appear on the date.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Summons (S.63)

A written order of the court requiring a person to appear (or produce a document), in duplicate, signed by the presiding officer and bearing the seal of the court; the BNSS also allows it in encrypted electronic form with the court's digital signature.

Service (S.64 onwards)

Served by a police officer or court officer, personally by delivering a copy (the person signs a receipt), or by electronic communication; if he cannot be found, on an adult member of his family residing with him; if that also fails, by affixing a copy on a conspicuous part of his house. Special modes for companies, government servants and persons outside the jurisdiction.

Warrant

A written order, signed and sealed, directing a police officer to arrest and produce a named person. It remains in force until cancelled or executed. A court may issue a warrant instead of a summons (or in addition) if it believes the person has absconded or will not obey the summons, or he fails to appear without reasonable excuse after due service. A bailable warrant carries an endorsement allowing release on bail.

Proclamation and attachment (S.84, S.85)

If a person against whom a warrant is issued has absconded or is hiding, the court may publish a written proclamation requiring him to appear within at least 30 days; for offences punishable with 10 years or more, the court may declare him a proclaimed offender. It may also attach his property (S.85), and, if he still does not appear, the property may be sold. The BNSS also allows trial in absentia of a proclaimed offender in serious cases (S.356).

Chapter 2 · asked 14× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Search and seizure: search warrants, search without warrant, procedure, limitations, constitutional validity, effect of illegal search

⚡ 30-second version · M.P. Sharma

📍In a fraud investigation against a business group, warrants were issued and 34 premises were searched at once; the companies said this compelled them to incriminate themselves.
❓Do search and seizure under criminal procedure violate the right against self-incrimination in Article 20(3)?
🏛️Valid: search and seizure is not testimonial compulsion under Art. 20(3); the power to search is essential to protect society.

🪝 A search is not a forced confession

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

M.P. Sharma v. Satish Chandra · AIR 1954 SC 300 · decided 15 Mar 1954

A complaint alleged large-scale fraud and embezzlement in companies of a business group, causing loss to shareholders. During the investigation, a Magistrate in Delhi issued search warrants.

Searches were carried out at 34 places at once, and a large number of documents were seized.

The companies and their officers challenged the searches as violating Article 20(3), which protects an accused from being compelled to be a witness against himself.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

A search is the examination of a person, place or thing to find evidence of an offence; seizure is taking possession of the thing found.

Search warrant (S.96, S.97)

A court may issue a search warrant where it believes a person will not produce a document or thing when summoned (S.94), or the thing is not known to be in anyone's possession, or a general search is needed for inquiry or trial. A District or Sub-Divisional Magistrate or JMFC may issue a warrant to search a place suspected to contain stolen property, forged documents or other objectionable articles (S.97).

Search without warrant (S.185)

The officer in charge or investigating officer may search without warrant if he has reasonable grounds to believe a thing needed for investigation is in a place and cannot be obtained otherwise without undue delay. He must record his grounds in writing before the search, search in person where possible, and send copies of the record to the nearest Magistrate within 48 hours; the owner gets a free copy.

Procedure and limitations (S.103, S.105)

The occupant of a closed place must allow free entry; the officer may break open if refused. Search is made in the presence of two or more independent and respectable inhabitants of the locality; a list of things seized is prepared and signed by them; the occupant may attend, and gets a copy of the list; a woman is searched only by a woman, with decency. The BNSS adds audio-video recording of the search and seizure, preferably by mobile phone, forwarded to the Magistrate (S.105).

Seizure (S.106)

A police officer may seize any property alleged or suspected to have been stolen, or found in circumstances that create suspicion of an offence; he reports the seizure to the Magistrate at once.

➕ More for Part B and examples 1 more point
Effect of an illegal search

An irregular or illegal search does not make the seizure or the evidence inadmissible (Pooran Mal v. Director of Inspection, 1974; Radha Kishan v. State of U.P., 1963), but the court examines such evidence with great care, and the officer may face action. Under special laws such as the NDPS Act, non-compliance with mandatory safeguards can vitiate the case.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. A search and seizure is not a compelled testimonial act by the accused; it is an act of the authorities.

The power of search is an overriding power of the State for the protection of social security, regulated by law.

  • Art. 20(3) protects against compelled testimony, not against the State collecting evidence by search.
  • The view in this case that there is no fundamental right to privacy was overruled in K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017): searches must now also be lawful, necessary and proportionate.
  • Evidence from an irregular search is not excluded, but scrutinised carefully (Pooran Mal, 1974).

Problem 1. Police searched A's house without a warrant. A objects that the search is illegal, and nothing was found. Is the search legal?

Asked: May 2022 · Aug 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
When can the police search a house without a warrant, and what follows if the rules are broken?
Law
S.185: an investigating officer may search without warrant on reasonable grounds, after recording them in writing, and send copies to the Magistrate within 48 hours. S.103: two independent local witnesses; list of seized things. S.105: video recording. Art. 21 / privacy.
Apply
If the officer had recorded grounds that a thing needed for investigation was in the house and could not be got without delay, the search was legal even though nothing was found. If he did not record grounds, call witnesses, or video-record, it was illegal.
Conclusion
Legal only if S.185, S.103 and S.105 were followed; otherwise illegal, and A may complain to the Magistrate and the SP. Finding nothing does not by itself make it illegal.

Problem 2. A policeman entered B's house without a warrant, searched it and seized some material illegally. What is B's remedy?

Asked: Nov 2022

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
What remedies does a person have against an illegal search and seizure?
Law
S.185, S.103, S.105 BNSS. S.106: seizure must be reported to the Magistrate. The court may order custody or return of seized property pending inquiry or trial. Criminal trespass (BNS 329) and wrongful search by a public servant. Art. 21, writ jurisdiction. Illegally seized evidence remains admissible but is scrutinised M.P. Sharma.
Apply
B can ask the Magistrate to examine the search record and order return of the seized articles, complain against the officer, and seek a writ.
Conclusion
B can apply to the Magistrate for return of the property and action, file a complaint against the officer, and approach the High Court under Art. 226; the evidence is not automatically excluded.

📖 Story to quote: M.P. Sharma

Problem 3. The accused disappears after a warrant is issued and cannot be found for months. How can the court secure him?

Asked: (Part A/B "Summons and warrants": 2015 · 2016 · Nov 2020)

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
What processes are available against an absconding accused?
Law
S.84: proclamation requiring him to appear within at least 30 days; proclaimed offender for offences of 10 years or more. S.85: attachment of his movable and immovable property, which may be sold if he does not appear. S.356: inquiry, trial and judgment in his absence in serious cases.
Apply
After the warrant fails, the court publishes the proclamation (in a newspaper, at his house, in court) and attaches his property; if he still hides, the trial can proceed in absentia.
Conclusion
Proclamation (S.84) → attachment and sale of property (S.85) → trial in absentia (S.356).

Problem 4. Investigators need a shop's CCTV footage and a suspect's WhatsApp chats. Can they be compelled?

Asked: (Part A "Search warrant" / "Seizure")

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can documents and electronic records be compelled during investigation or trial?
Law
S.94: a court, or an officer in charge of a police station, may issue a summons or written order to produce any document, electronic communication or digital evidence. S.96: search warrant if it will not be produced. S.105: audio-video recording of seizure.
Apply
The shop owner can be asked to produce the footage; the suspect's device can be seized with a proper record if he will not cooperate.
Conclusion
Yes: an order under S.94 to produce, and if refused, a search warrant (S.96) and seizure recorded on video (S.105).
