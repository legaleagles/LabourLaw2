FIR, Complaint and Investigation

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

A crime is like a fire alarm. Someone has to ring it (the FIR at the police station, or a complaint to the Magistrate), someone has to investigate it (the police), and someone has to write the report of what they found (the charge sheet). For serious crimes (cognizable) the police can act on their own; for minor ones (non-cognizable) they need the Magistrate's permission.

The BNSS has modernised the alarm: you can now ring it from any police station (zero FIR), or online (e-FIR, signed within 3 days), get a free copy at once, and the police must video-record searches and send forensic experts in serious cases.

Chapter 1 · asked 7× 🔥🔥 Important

First Information Report (FIR): meaning, procedure, zero FIR, refusal to register, evidentiary value (S.173)

📖 The story: Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh

(2014) 2 SCC 1 · decided 12 Nov 2013

Lalita Kumari, a minor girl, was kidnapped in Uttar Pradesh in 2008. Her father went to the police, but they did not register an FIR or act on his information.

He petitioned the Supreme Court under Article 32. Because there were conflicting decisions on whether the police must register an FIR or may first make an inquiry, the matter went to a Constitution Bench.

❓ When information discloses a cognizable offence, can the police first make a preliminary inquiry before registering the FIR?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Meaning. The FIR is the first information of a cognizable offence given to the officer in charge of a police station, recorded under S.173 BNSS. It sets the criminal law in motion: the police can then investigate without a Magistrate's order.

How it is recorded (S.173(1)–(2)). Oral information is reduced to writing, read over to the informant and signed by him; it is entered in the register; a copy is given free of cost to the informant or victim at once. Information by a woman victim of certain sexual offences is recorded by a woman officer.

New in BNSS. (1) Zero FIR: information may be given irrespective of the area where the offence was committed; (2) e-FIR: by electronic communication, to be signed within 3 days; (3) preliminary enquiry for offences punishable with 3 to under 7 years, within 14 days, with a DSP's permission (S.173(3)).

If the police refuse. (1) Send the information in writing to the Superintendent of Police, who investigates or orders investigation (S.173(4)); (2) apply to the Magistrate under S.175(3) (with an affidavit) to order investigation; (3) file a complaint directly before the Magistrate (S.223); (4) writ petition.

💡 Case story Lalita Kumari: a minor girl was kidnapped in Uttar Pradesh and the police did not register her father's complaint. A Constitution Bench held: if the information discloses a cognizable offence, registration of the FIR is mandatory; a preliminary inquiry is allowed only in limited kinds of cases (family, commercial, medical negligence, corruption, long delay), within a short time. BNSS S.173(3) now builds on this.

Evidentiary value. The FIR is not substantive evidence; it can be used only to corroborate or contradict its maker, or as a dying declaration if the informant dies. Delay in lodging the FIR is not fatal if explained, but an unexplained delay invites suspicion of embellishment.

No second FIR. For the same occurrence there can be only one FIR; later information is part of the same investigation T.T. Antony. A counter-FIR by the other side (a different version) is allowed.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ended

Registration of an FIR is mandatory if the information discloses a cognizable offence; no preliminary inquiry is permitted in such a case.

If the information does not clearly disclose a cognizable offence, a preliminary inquiry may be made only to find out whether it does, in limited categories (matrimonial, commercial, medical negligence, corruption, abnormal delay), completed within a short time.

Officers who do not register an FIR in such cases must face action.

Why it matters:

  • The word "shall" in the FIR provision is mandatory.
  • Registration of an FIR protects against manipulation and ensures accountability.
  • BNSS S.173(3) now allows a preliminary enquiry, with a DSP's permission, for offences punishable with 3 to under 7 years, within 14 days.

📚 More stories on this topic

T.T. Antony v. State of Kerala: After a police firing killed five people, two FIRs were registered; years later, after a commission's report, a third FIR for murder was registered against the officers. Held: The later FIR was quashed: there can be no second FIR for the same occurrence; the police should do further investigation instead. 🪝 One incident, one FIR

Chapter 2 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Complaint (S.2, S.223–227) · Difference between FIR and complaint · Procedure for filing both

📖 The story: Going straight to the Magistrate

A woman is defamed by a relative in a family gathering. Defamation is non-cognizable, so the police cannot investigate on their own.

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

Meaning (S.2). A complaint is any allegation made orally or in writing to a Magistrate, with a view to his taking action, that some person has committed an offence; it does not include a police report. A police report on a non-cognizable case is deemed a complaint.

Procedure. (1) The Magistrate examines the complainant and witnesses on oath and records it (S.223); under the BNSS proviso he shall not take cognizance without giving the accused an opportunity of being heard. (2) He may postpone process and inquire himself or order investigation (S.225); it is compulsory where the accused lives beyond his jurisdiction. (3) If there is no sufficient ground, he dismisses the complaint with brief reasons (S.226). (4) If there is sufficient ground, he issues summons or warrant (S.227).

💡 Example: Ravi's neighbour defames him in a WhatsApp group. Defamation (BNS 356) is non-cognizable, so the police will not register an FIR. Ravi files a complaint before the Magistrate, is examined on oath, and the Magistrate issues summons to the neighbour.

A complaint (S.2) is an allegation made to a Magistrate, orally or in writing, that someone has committed an offence.

She files a complaint before the Magistrate instead of an FIR.

Chapter 3 · asked 11× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Cognizable and non-cognizable offences: meaning, distinction, examples

📖 The story: Arrest without warrant?

Police see a man breaking into a house at night. Elsewhere, a man is accused of insulting someone to provoke a fight.

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

Meaning (S.2). A cognizable offence is one for which a police officer may arrest without warrant under the First Schedule or any other law. A non-cognizable offence is one for which he has no authority to arrest without warrant.

Police powers. Cognizable: the officer records the FIR (S.173) and investigates without a Magistrate's order (S.175(1)). Non-cognizable: he enters the substance in a register, refers the informant to the Magistrate (S.174(1)), and cannot investigate without the Magistrate's order (S.174(2)). If a case has both kinds of offences, it is treated as cognizable (S.174(4)).

First Schedule. Column-wise it tells whether each BNS offence is cognizable, bailable, and which court tries it. Generally, offences punishable with 3 years or more are cognizable; lesser ones are mostly non-cognizable.

Distinction (table):

Arrestwithout warrant · only with warrant
Investigationwithout Magistrate's order · only with order
InformationFIR (S.173) · entry in register (S.174)
Seriousnessserious · less serious
Bailoften non-bailable · usually bailable
💡 Examples: cognizable: murder (BNS 103), rape (BNS 64), theft (BNS 303(2)), dowry death (BNS 80), cruelty by husband (BNS 85). Non-cognizable: simple hurt (BNS 115(2)), defamation (BNS 356), insult to provoke breach of peace (BNS 352).

Conclusion. The label decides who moves first: the police alone (cognizable), or the police only after the Magistrate's permission (non-cognizable).

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ends

A cognizable offence allows arrest without warrant; a non-cognizable one does not.

House-breaking theft: the police can arrest on the spot. The insult: they need a warrant.

Chapter 4 · asked 10× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Investigation: duties of police, case diary, police report (charge sheet) and inquest (S.175–194)

📖 The story: State of Haryana v. Bhajan Lal

1992 Supp (1) SCC 335 · decided 21 Nov 1990

A complaint alleged that Ch. Bhajan Lal, a former Chief Minister of Haryana, had acquired assets disproportionate to his known sources of income. On the Superintendent of Police's direction, an FIR was registered and investigation began.

The High Court quashed the FIR and the investigation. The State appealed to the Supreme Court.

❓ When can a High Court quash an FIR or stop an investigation at the very beginning?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Meaning (S.2). Investigation includes all proceedings for the collection of evidence by a police officer or a person authorised by a Magistrate (not a Magistrate himself).

Steps. (1) Proceed to the spot and investigate (S.176); for offences punishable with 7 years or more, a forensic expert must visit the scene and the process is video-recorded (S.176(3)); (2) search and seizure, recorded by audio-video means (S.105, S.185); (3) arrest where necessary (S.35); (4) examine witnesses (S.180); (5) medical examination (S.51–53, S.184 for a rape victim); (6) police custody up to 15 days, in parts within the first 40 or 60 days (S.187); (7) police report (S.193).

Statements. Statements to the police are not signed and cannot be used as evidence except to contradict the witness (S.181). Confessions and statements recorded by a Judicial Magistrate (S.183) can be used; confessions to the police are inadmissible.

Case diary (S.192). The investigating officer records day by day: when the information came, when he started and closed, places visited, and circumstances found. The court may use it to aid its inquiry, but not as evidence; the accused cannot demand it, except where the officer uses it to refresh memory or the court uses it to contradict him. (The general diary is the station's daily register of all events, kept under the police rules.)

Police report / charge sheet (S.193). On completion, the officer sends the report to the Magistrate: names of parties, nature of information, witnesses, whether an offence appears, whether the accused was arrested or released. He must inform the victim of progress within 90 days, supply copies to the accused, and may make further investigation even after the report (S.193(9), to be completed within 90 days unless the court extends).

💡 Case story Bhajan Lal: a complaint alleged that a former Chief Minister of Haryana had amassed disproportionate assets; the High Court quashed the FIR. The Supreme Court restored the investigation and laid down categories where an FIR may be quashed: e.g. no offence on its face, allegations absurd, legal bar, or proceedings malicious. Otherwise courts should not stop investigation at the start.

Inquest (S.194). On information of suicide, homicide, death by animal or machine, or a suspicious death (including a woman within 7 years of marriage), the officer informs the Executive Magistrate, examines the body in the presence of two or more respectable inhabitants, prepares a report on the apparent cause of death, and forwards it within 24 hours to the District or Sub-Divisional Magistrate.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ended

The Supreme Court set aside the quashing; the allegations disclosed a cognizable offence, so investigation had to proceed (it directed investigation by another officer).

It laid down illustrative categories where quashing is justified, to be applied sparingly.

Why it matters:

  • Quash where the FIR, even if taken at face value, discloses no offence.
  • Quash where the allegations are so absurd that no prudent person could believe them.
  • Quash where there is an express legal bar, or the proceedings are manifestly mala fide.
  • Otherwise courts should not interfere with the police's statutory power to investigate.

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. After receiving the FIR, the SHO decides not to investigate because the case "is not serious". Remedy of the aggrieved person?

Asked: 2018

Issue
What can be done when the police decline to investigate a cognizable offence?
Law
S.176: the officer may decline to investigate only if there is no sufficient ground, and must state his reasons and notify the informant. S.173(4): send the information to the SP. S.175(3): Magistrate may order investigation (on an application with affidavit). S.223: complaint to the Magistrate. Registration is mandatory Lalita Kumari.
Apply
If the information discloses a cognizable offence, "not serious" is not a lawful ground. The informant first approaches the SP; if nothing happens, the Magistrate.
Conclusion
Approach the SP under S.173(4), then the Magistrate under S.175(3) or by complaint under S.223; in a fit case, a writ.

📖 Story to quote: Lalita Kumari

Problem 2. Ramu told the FIR a "fair and tall" man killed Krishna; at the trial he says a "black and short" man did it. What is the value of these statements?

Asked: May 2022

Issue
What is the evidentiary value of the FIR when the informant changes his version?
Law
The FIR is not substantive evidence; it may be used to corroborate or contradict its maker under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam. The court relies on sworn evidence in court, tested by cross-examination.
Apply
The contradiction will be put to Ramu in cross-examination. A material contradiction on identity seriously damages his credibility; the court will look for other evidence (other witnesses, forensics, identification parade).
Conclusion
His court statement is the substantive evidence; the FIR only shows he contradicted himself, which weakens his testimony on identity.

Problem 3. A records a video of police misconduct during an investigation but will not hand it over, fearing the police. What are his duties and protections?

Asked: Feb 2025

Issue
Can A be required to produce the video, and how is he protected?
Law
S.94: a court or officer in charge may summon production of any document or electronic record. S.398: Witness Protection Scheme. S.105: searches are themselves to be video-recorded. Complaint to the SP or Magistrate (S.173(4), S.223).
Apply
A can be summoned to produce the video, but he need not hand it to the officers he fears: he can give it to the court or the SP, and seek protection.
Conclusion
A may be compelled to produce it, but he can produce it before the Magistrate or a senior officer and seek protection under the Witness Protection Scheme.

Problem 4. Police registered a second FIR for the same incident after an inquiry commission's report. Valid?

Asked: (standard problem; B: "law relating to FIR")

Issue
Can there be a second FIR for the same occurrence?
Law
One occurrence, one FIR; fresh material is handled by further investigation under S.193(9) T.T. Antony.
Apply
The second FIR concerns the same incident and the same accused.
Conclusion
The second FIR is invalid and will be quashed; the police should do further investigation in the first case.

📖 Story to quote: T.T. Antony
