Res Judicata and Res Sub Judice

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Two brothers fight over a field and the court decides it for the elder. Can the younger file the same case again next year, maybe before a different judge? If he could, no judgment would ever be final and the elder would never sleep in peace. Res judicata (S.11) says: a matter once decided is decided forever between the same parties.

And if the younger files a second case while the first is still pending? Res sub judice (S.10) says: wait; the second court stays its trial so that two courts do not give two different answers. Three Latin maxims carry the idea: nemo debet bis vexari pro una et eadem causa (no one should be vexed twice for the same cause), interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium, and res judicata pro veritate accipitur (a decided matter is accepted as true).

Chapter 1 · asked 9× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Doctrine of res judicata (S.11): meaning, essentials, constructive res judicata, illustrations

📖 The story: State of U.P. v. Nawab Hussain

(1977) 2 SCC 806 · AIR 1977 SC 1680 · decided 4 Apr 1977

Nawab Hussain was a confirmed Sub-Inspector of Police in Uttar Pradesh. After an inquiry, the Deputy Inspector General of Police dismissed him on 20 December 1954; his departmental appeal failed.

He filed a writ petition in the Allahabad High Court, arguing that he had not been given a reasonable opportunity and that the action was mala fide. The writ was dismissed on 30 October 1959.

In January 1960 he filed a civil suit challenging the same dismissal on a new ground: he had been appointed by the Inspector General of Police, so a DIG, a lower authority, could not dismiss him (Article 311(1)). The lower courts held the suit was not barred; the High Court decreed it. The State appealed.

❓ Can a person who lost a writ petition challenge the same order again in a suit on a ground he knew of but did not raise earlier?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Meaning. Res judicata means "a matter already judged". S.11: no court shall try a suit or issue in which the matter directly and substantially in issue was directly and substantially in issue in a former suit between the same parties (or parties under whom they claim), litigating under the same title, in a court competent to try the later suit, and has been heard and finally decided by that court.

Basis. Three maxims: nemo debet bis vexari pro una et eadem causa; interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium; res judicata pro veritate accipitur. In short: finality, no double harassment, and respect for judgments.

Essentials. (1) The matter is directly and substantially in issue in both suits (not collaterally or incidentally). (2) Same parties or their privies. (3) Same title (same capacity). (4) The first court was competent to try the later suit. (5) It was heard and finally decided on merits (not dismissed for default or withdrawn).

Constructive res judicata (Explanation IV). Any matter which might and ought to have been made a ground of attack or defence in the former suit is deemed to have been directly and substantially in issue. It stops a party from splitting his grounds and fighting in instalments.

The other Explanations. I: "former suit" means one decided earlier, whatever its filing date. II: competence is judged without regard to appeal provisions. III: the matter must have been alleged by one party and denied or admitted by the other. V: relief claimed but not expressly granted is deemed refused. VI: in representative suits (public or common rights), all persons interested are bound. VII: S.11 applies to execution proceedings. VIII: a decision of a court of limited jurisdiction, competent to decide that issue, binds even though it could not try the later suit.

Where it also applies. To writ petitions Daryao; between different stages of the same suit (an earlier order binds later stages: Satyadhyan Ghosal v. Deorajin Debi, 1960); and to co-defendants if there was a conflict between them which had to be decided. Where it does not: a decision without jurisdiction; a pure question of law about jurisdiction wrongly decided; a suit dismissed for default or withdrawn; a different capacity.

💡 Illustrations: A sues B for possession, claiming as owner, and loses. A later sues B for possession as mortgagee: barred (Expl. IV: he ought to have raised it as an alternative). C loses a suit as heir in his personal capacity; he later sues as trustee: not barred (different title). A loses a declaration suit when his adverse possession was not yet 12 years; after 12 years he sues on his newly perfected title: not barred (a fresh title that did not exist then).

Conclusion. One fair fight, one final answer: res judicata gives judgments their finality.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ended

No. The Supreme Court allowed the State's appeal and dismissed the suit.

The plea about the appointing authority was important, was within his knowledge, and could have been raised in the writ petition. Raising it later was "clearly barred by the principle of constructive res judicata".

Why it matters:

  • Constructive res judicata stops a party from fighting in instalments, with a new ground each time.
  • The general principles of res judicata, including constructive res judicata, apply to writ petitions as well as suits.
  • Otherwise a party could keep filing fresh proceedings against the same order with fresh grounds, and no order would ever be final.

📚 More stories on this topic

Daryao v. State of U.P.: Petitioners lost their writ petition under Art. 226 in the Allahabad High Court, then filed the same challenge in the Supreme Court under Art. 32. Held: Barred: a High Court decision on merits binds the parties, even against a fundamental-rights petition, unless set aside in appeal. 🪝 Even writs end once

Chapter 2 · asked 7× 🔥🔥 Important

Res sub judice (S.10) · Distinction between res sub judice and res judicata

📖 The story: Two suits about one sale agreement

A buyer sues the seller in one court for specific performance of a sale agreement. While that suit is pending, the seller sues the buyer in another court for a declaration that the same agreement stands cancelled.

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

Meaning. Sub judice = "under judicial consideration". S.10: no court shall proceed with the trial of a suit in which the matter in issue is also directly and substantially in issue in a previously instituted suit between the same parties (or their privies), litigating under the same title, where the earlier suit is pending in the same or another court in India competent to grant the relief claimed (or in the Supreme Court).

Conditions. (1) Two suits, the earlier one pending; (2) the matter in issue directly and substantially the same (courts look for the whole subject matter being identical: Aspi Jal v. Khushroo Dadyburjor, 2013); (3) same parties or privies, same title; (4) the earlier court is competent to grant the relief. Then the later suit is stayed, not dismissed. Pendency of a suit in a foreign court does not bar an Indian court (Explanation).

Object. To prevent two courts of concurrent jurisdiction trying the same matter at the same time and giving conflicting decisions, and to avoid multiplicity of suits.

Points to add. S.10 bars the trial, not the institution of the later suit, so the later court can still pass interim orders such as an injunction or a receiver (and a summary suit at the leave-to-defend stage is not a "trial": Indian Bank v. Maharashtra State Co-op. Marketing Federation, 1998). The court may also consolidate the suits, or the High Court / District Court may transfer one to the other (S.24). S.151 (inherent power) can stay a suit in cases outside S.10.

💡 Example: A sues B in Hyderabad for eviction from a house. While it is pending, he files a second suit for the same eviction in Secunderabad. The Secunderabad court must stay the trial under S.10; it may still pass urgent interim orders.

Differences (write as a table):

MatterS.10: pending in an earlier suit · S.11: already decided
Effectlater suit's trial stayed · later suit barred
Objectavoid two parallel trials and conflicting decisions · finality, no re-litigation
Stageboth suits alive · first suit concluded

Conclusion. S.10 says "wait", S.11 says "stop": together they keep one dispute in one court at a time.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ends

S.10: a court shall not proceed with the trial of a later suit where the same matter is in issue in an earlier pending suit between the same parties.

Both turn on whether the agreement is valid, so the trial of the seller's later suit is stayed.

Chapter 3 · asked 2× 🔥 Good to know

Foreign judgments (S.13, S.14) and their effect in India

📖 The story: Y. Narasimha Rao v. Y. Venkata Lakshmi

(1991) 3 SCC 451 · decided 9 Jul 1991

The couple married at Tirupati on 27 February 1975 under Hindu law, and separated in July 1978.

The husband went to the USA. Claiming 90 days' residence in Missouri, he obtained a divorce from the Circuit Court of St. Louis County on 19 February 1980, on the ground of irretrievable breakdown of marriage. The wife, in India, sent a reply under protest, refusing to accept that court's jurisdiction, and did not take part.

The husband remarried in 1981. The wife filed a complaint of bigamy. He relied on the US decree.

❓ Is a foreign divorce decree, obtained on a ground not available under the law under which the parties married, valid in India?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Meaning (S.2(6)). A judgment of a foreign court. S.13: a foreign judgment is conclusive as to any matter directly adjudicated between the same parties (or their privies), except where: (a) it was not pronounced by a court of competent jurisdiction; (b) it was not given on the merits; (c) it is founded on an incorrect view of international law or a refusal to recognise Indian law where applicable; (d) the proceedings were opposed to natural justice; (e) it was obtained by fraud; (f) it sustains a claim founded on a breach of any law in force in India.

S.14. On production of a certified copy, the court presumes the foreign court was competent, unless the contrary appears on the record or is proved. S.44A: a decree of a superior court of a "reciprocating territory" notified by the Central Government can be executed in India directly; others need a fresh suit on the foreign judgment.

Conclusion. India respects foreign judgments, but only those given fairly, on merits, by a competent court, under a law India recognises.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ended

No. The Supreme Court held the decree could not be recognised.

The Missouri court had no jurisdiction under the Hindu Marriage Act; irretrievable breakdown is not a ground under that Act; the wife did not submit to that court; and the decree rested on misrepresented jurisdictional facts. It fell within S.13(a), (b), (c), (d) and (e).

Why it matters:

  • The forum and the ground of relief must be those recognised by the matrimonial law under which the parties married.
  • Exceptions: the respondent lived permanently in that country and the ground is available under the parties' law; or she voluntarily and effectively submitted and contested; or she consented.
  • "Decided on merits" (S.13(b)) means a real contest, not an ex parte decree after a protest.
  • Residence means habitual residence, not a short stay to obtain a divorce.
  • Under S.14, a copy must be properly certified (S.86 Evidence Act) to raise the presumption.

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. Ashok sues Bhaskar for redemption, alleging a usufructuary mortgage. The mortgage is not proved; suit dismissed. Ashok now sues for possession as owner. Barred?

Asked: Aug 2023 (mirror question 2013: owner first, then mortgagee)

Issue
Is the second suit barred by res judicata, actual or constructive?
Law
S.11 Explanation IV: any matter which might and ought to have been made a ground of attack in the former suit is deemed directly and substantially in issue. Same parties, same property, competent court, decided on merits.
Apply
Ashok's claim to possession as owner was available to him when he filed the redemption suit; he could and ought to have claimed it in the alternative. Textbook illustration: a plaintiff who loses claiming in one character (owner) cannot sue again for the same property in another available character (mortgagee), and vice versa.
Conclusion
The second suit is barred by constructive res judicata (S.11, Explanation IV). (It would not be barred only if the ownership claim arose after the first suit, or the first court expressly left it open.)

Problem 2. A was a trustee. After his death B takes the trust property. C, A's son, sues B as A's heir in his individual capacity and loses. C, now appointed trustee, sues again as trustee. Barred?

Asked: May 2023

Issue
Does the earlier decision against C as an individual bar a suit by C as trustee?
Law
S.11 requires the parties to be litigating "under the same title", i.e. in the same capacity.
Apply
In the first suit C sued in his personal capacity as heir; in the second he sues in a different, representative capacity as trustee, on behalf of the trust.
Conclusion
Not barred: the title (capacity) is different, so neither res judicata nor constructive res judicata applies.

Problem 3. A's suit for declaration of ownership is dismissed. At that time he was in adverse possession, but not for 12 years. After the period is complete, he sues on title by adverse possession. Barred?

Asked: Aug 2021

Issue
Can a title perfected after the first suit be the basis of a second suit?
Law
S.11 and Explanation IV apply only to matters that existed and might and ought to have been raised in the former suit. S.27 and Art. 65 Lim. Act: title by adverse possession arises on completion of 12 years.
Apply
When the first suit was decided, A had no title by adverse possession; it came into existence later. It could not have been raised earlier.
Conclusion
Not barred: it is a new title and a new cause of action that did not exist at the time of the first suit.

Problem 4. A sues B for eviction. While it is pending, A files another suit for the same relief in another court. Is the second suit maintainable?

Asked: 2015

Issue
Can two suits on the same matter between the same parties proceed together?
Law
S.10: the court shall not proceed with the trial of a later suit where the same matter is directly and substantially in issue in an earlier pending suit between the same parties in a competent court.
Apply
Same parties, same title, same relief, earlier suit pending in a competent court.
Conclusion
The trial of the second suit must be stayed under S.10 (it is not dismissed); the court may also impose costs for abuse of process.

Problem 5. A company sues a rival company for passing off its medicine (same name, carton and colours). The rival later sues the first company in a different court with similar allegations. Advise the first company.

Asked: May 2023

Issue
How do courts handle two parallel suits on the same dispute?
Law
S.10: the later suit's trial is stayed where the matter in issue is directly and substantially the same. S.10 bars only the trial, so interim orders can still be passed. S.24: the High Court or District Court can transfer and consolidate the suits.
Apply
Both suits turn on the same question: whose trade dress is it and who is copying whom. The first suit was filed earlier.
Conclusion
The first company should apply in the later suit for a stay of trial under S.10 and, if useful, for transfer and joint trial under S.24; interim injunctions remain available in its own suit.

Problem 6. A sues B in a USA court; the suit is dismissed. Can A file a fresh suit on the same cause of action in India?

Asked: Sep 2024

Issue
Is a foreign judgment conclusive in India?
Law
S.13: a foreign judgment is conclusive between the same parties on any matter directly adjudicated, except in the six cases in S.13(a)–(f). S.14: presumption of competence from a certified copy. S.11 itself applies to Indian courts, but S.13 gives the foreign decision the same finality.
Apply
If the US court was competent, decided on the merits, followed natural justice, and was not misled by fraud, its dismissal is conclusive. If any S.13 exception applies (e.g. dismissed on a technical ground, not on merits), A can sue afresh Y. Narasimha Rao.
Conclusion
No fresh suit if the US judgment was on the merits and falls in none of the S.13 exceptions; yes, if one of those exceptions applies.

📖 Story to quote: Y. Narasimha Rao

Problem 7. A suit is dismissed for the plaintiff's default (absence). He files a fresh suit on the same cause. Is it barred by res judicata?

Asked: (standard contrast; Part A "Res judicata")

Issue
Does a dismissal for default operate as res judicata?
Law
S.11 needs the matter to have been "heard and finally decided". A dismissal for default is not a decision on merits. But O.IX r.9 CPC separately bars a fresh suit on the same cause after a dismissal under O.IX r.8; the remedy is restoration.
Apply
There was no hearing on merits, so S.11 does not apply; yet the fresh suit is barred by O.IX r.9.
Conclusion
Not res judicata, but the fresh suit is barred by O.IX r.9; he must apply to restore the first suit.

Problem 8. X's writ petition challenging his dismissal from service was dismissed by the High Court on merits. He now files a civil suit on the same grounds. Maintainable?

Asked: (Part B "Doctrine of res judicata")

Issue
Does a writ decision on merits bar a later suit?
Law
The general principles of res judicata apply to writ proceedings Daryao; a ground he could have raised is also barred (constructive res judicata) Nawab Hussain.
Apply
The same grounds were decided on merits in the writ.
Conclusion
The suit is barred by res judicata; any new ground he could have raised earlier is barred too.

📖 Story to quote: Nawab Hussain, Daryao
