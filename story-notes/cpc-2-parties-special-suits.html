Parties and Special Suits

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

A suit is like a photograph of a dispute. If someone who must be in the picture is left out, the photo is useless; if a stranger walks in, the photo is confusing. Order I decides who stands in the frame.

Some people cannot stand in the frame on their own, so the Code gives them a special door: a minor comes with a next friend, a poor person comes without paying court fee, a crowd with one interest sends a representative, the Government must first get a 2-month notice, and a stakeholder holding someone else's property can ask the claimants to fight it out between themselves (interpleader).

Chapter 1 · asked 9× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Parties to a suit: joinder, necessary and proper parties, non-joinder and misjoinder (O.I)

⚡ 30-second version · Kasturi

📍In a suit for specific performance, strangers claiming their own independent title asked to be added as defendants.
❓In a suit for specific performance of a contract for sale, can strangers to the contract who claim an independent title be added as parties?
🏛️Refused: they are neither necessary nor proper parties; adding them would turn a contract suit into a title suit.

🪝 Contract suit, contract parties

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Kasturi v. Iyyamperumal · (2005) 6 SCC 733 · AIR 2005 SC 2813

Kasturi entered into an agreement to buy land from two persons (the vendors). When they did not complete the sale, he sued them for specific performance of the agreement.

Several other people, who were not parties to the agreement, applied to be added as defendants. They said they owned and possessed the land by their own independent title, adverse to the title of the vendors.

The trial court allowed them to be added, and the High Court agreed. Kasturi went to the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Intro

A decree binds only those who are parties. So the right people must be joined: all who must be bound, and no strangers. Order I (r.1–13) deals with this.

Joinder of plaintiffs (r.1)

Persons may join as plaintiffs if

  1. the right to relief arises from the same act or transaction, or series of acts or transactions, and
  2. if they sued separately, a common question of law or fact would arise. Joinder of defendants (r.3): the same two tests apply to persons sued together.
Necessary and proper parties

A necessary party is one

  1. against whom some relief is claimed, and
  2. without whom no effective decree can be passed. A proper party is one whose presence enables the court to decide all questions completely and effectively, though no relief is claimed against him.
Non-joinder and misjoinder (r.9)

No suit shall be defeated by misjoinder or non-joinder; the court decides the rights of those before it. Proviso: this does not apply to non-joinder of a necessary party; such a suit fails.

Court's power (r.10(2))

At any stage, on application or on its own, the court may strike out a party wrongly joined or add anyone who ought to have been joined, or whose presence is necessary to decide all questions. Objection (r.13): must be taken at the earliest opportunity (before settlement of issues), else it is waived, unless the ground arose later.

Conclusion

Join everyone needed for an effective decree, object early, and remember that only the absence of a necessary party is fatal.

➕ More for Part B and examples 3 more points
Other rules worth a line each

r.4: judgment may be given for one or more plaintiffs, or against one or more defendants. r.6: persons liable on the same contract may be joined. r.7: if the plaintiff is in doubt from whom to get relief, he may join both. r.10(1): suit filed in the name of the wrong plaintiff by bona fide mistake can be corrected. r.10(4): an added defendant must be served afresh; for limitation the suit against him begins on the date he is added (S.21 Limitation Act).

Death of a party (O.XXII)

If the right to sue survives, the legal representatives are brought on record. If they are not brought within 90 days (Art. 120 Lim. Act), the suit abates as against that party; the abatement can be set aside for sufficient cause within 60 days (Art. 121). No abatement after the hearing has concluded and before judgment (r.6).

💡 ExampleExample: in a partition suit every co-sharer is a necessary party: each one's share must be fixed, so leaving out even one heir means the decree cannot work. In a suit for recovery of rent, the landlord's neighbour is neither necessary nor proper.
🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. The Supreme Court set aside the orders adding them.

A necessary party must pass two tests: some relief must be claimed against him, and without him no effective decree can be passed. These strangers passed neither.

They were not proper parties either, because their presence was not needed to decide the only real question: whether the contract should be enforced. Adding them would convert the suit into one for title and possession.

  • Necessary party: (1) a right to some relief against him must exist, and (2) no effective decree can be passed in his absence.
  • Proper party: his presence enables the court to decide completely and effectively the questions involved in the suit.
  • The plaintiff is the dominus litis (master of his suit); he cannot ordinarily be forced to fight people he has not sued, unless the court finds them necessary or proper.
  • A suit for specific performance is about the contract; a third party claiming independent title must file his own suit.

Chapter 2 · asked 9× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Suits by or against Government or public officers: S.79–82 and O.XXVII (notice under S.80)

⚡ 30-second version · Raghunath Dass

📍A trader's railway consignment of copper was lost in 1947. His S.80 notice was in his firm's name, signed as proprietor; the High Court rejected his suit over this.
❓Is a Section 80 notice invalid just because it was written in the business name of a sole proprietor, while the suit is filed in his own name?
🏛️Decree restored. The notice is read as a whole and reasonably; S.80 is not a booby trap for ordinary people.

🪝 Notice, not a booby trap

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Raghunath Dass v. Union of India · AIR 1969 SC 674 · decided 26 Jul 1968

Raghunath Dass ran a business at Gujranwala under the name "M/s. Raghunath Dass Mulkhraj". On 29 July 1947 he sent copper articles by the North Western Railway to a station near Moradabad. The goods never arrived.

Before suing the railway (the Union of India), he sent the notice required by Section 80. It was written in the firm's name, used "we" in places, and was signed "Raghunath Dass, Proprietor".

The trial court at Moradabad decreed his claim of about ₹10,206 with interest. But the Allahabad High Court dismissed the suit: it said the notice was given by one person (the firm) and the suit was filed by another (Raghunath Dass), so the notice was invalid.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

S.79: who is named

A suit by or against the Central Government is in the name of the "Union of India"; by or against a State, in the name of the "State of …" (e.g. State of Telangana).

S.80(1): notice

No suit against the Government, or against a public officer for an act done in his official capacity, until 2 months after written notice is delivered: for the Centre to the Secretary (for railways, the General Manager); for a State to the Secretary or the District Collector; for a public officer, to him personally or at his office. The notice must state the name, description and residence of the plaintiff, the cause of action and the relief claimed, and the plaint must say it was served.

Object

To give the Government a chance to reconsider and settle without litigation. So the notice is read sensibly, not as a trap Raghunath Dass.

S.80(2): urgent cases

A suit for urgent or immediate relief may be filed without notice, with leave of the court; but no relief (interim or final) is granted without giving the Government a chance to show cause. If no urgency is shown, the plaint is returned for filing after notice. S.80(3): a suit is not dismissed for a mere defect in the notice if the plaintiff is identifiable and the cause of action and relief are substantially indicated.

Conclusion

Notice first, name the State correctly, join the Government, and expect a 3-month grace before execution.

➕ More for Part B and examples 3 more points
S.81

A public officer sued for an official act is not arrested and his property is not attached before judgment (except in execution); the court may exempt him from appearing in person. S.82. A decree against Government must specify a time for satisfaction; no execution unless it stays unsatisfied for 3 months from the decree.

O.XXVII

r.1–2: pleadings are signed and verified by an officer appointed by the Government; r.4: the Government Pleader is its agent; r.5: the court gives the Government reasonable time to file its written statement; r.5A: when a public officer is sued for an official act, the Government must be joined as a party; r.5B: the court must first try to help the parties settle; r.8A: no security is demanded from the Government.

Limitation

The 2-month notice period is excluded when computing limitation (S.15(2) Limitation Act). Local bodies: a municipal corporation is not "Government", but its own Act may demand notice: e.g. GHMC needs 1 month's notice, and the suit must be filed within 6 months (S.685 GHMC Act, 1955).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. The Supreme Court allowed the appeal and restored the trial court's decree with costs.

Read as a whole, the notice showed that it came from a sole proprietor; the Government could not have been misled into thinking it came from a partnership.

  • The object of the S.80 notice is to give the Government and public officers an opportunity to reconsider the legal position and to settle the claim without litigation.
  • S.80 must be complied with, but it is "not intended to be used as booby traps against ignorant and illiterate persons".
  • The notice must be read as a whole, reasonably, and not pedantically or "divorced from common sense".
  • Today S.80(3) (added in 1976) says the same: no suit is dismissed for a mere defect in the notice if the plaintiff can be identified and the cause of action and relief are substantially indicated.

Chapter 3 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Representative suits (O.I r.8) and suits relating to public matters: public nuisance (S.91) and public charities (S.92)

⚡ 30-second version · 150 farmers, one channel

📍A factory diverts a village irrigation channel used by 150 farmers. Four of them want to sue for everyone.
⚖️O.I r.8: where numerous persons have the same interest, one or more may sue on behalf of all, with the court's permission.
✅With permission and public notice, the four sue for all 150, and the decree binds every farmer.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Representative suit (O.I r.8)

Where numerous persons have the same interest, one or more of them may sue or be sued, or defend, on behalf of all, with the permission of the court. They need not have the same cause of action; a common interest is enough.

Safeguards
  1. The court gives notice to all interested persons at the plaintiff's expense, by personal service or public advertisement (r.8(2));
  2. any of them may apply to be made a party (r.8(3));
  3. no withdrawal, abandonment or compromise without notice to all (r.8(4));
  4. a representative who is not proceeding with due diligence can be replaced (r.8(5));
  5. the decree binds all on whose behalf the suit was brought (r.8(6); also S.11 Explanation VI).
S.91: public nuisance

For a public nuisance or other wrongful act affecting the public, a suit for declaration, injunction or other relief may be filed by the Advocate General, or by two or more persons with leave of the court, even if they suffered no special damage. Their own private rights of suit are not affected.

S.92: public trusts

For an alleged breach of an express or constructive trust created for public purposes of a charitable or religious nature, or where directions are needed for its administration, the Advocate General, or two or more persons having an interest in the trust, with leave of the court, may sue in the principal civil court of original jurisdiction (or a court empowered by the State).

Conclusion

These provisions avoid multiplicity of suits and let the public protect public rights, while the leave and notice rules stop busybodies and secret compromises.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
💡 ExampleExample: 300 flat owners of a colony object to a builder blocking their common road. Three of them, with the court's permission, sue on behalf of all 300. After notice in a newspaper, the decree binds every owner, so the builder does not face 300 separate suits.
Reliefs under S.92

removing a trustee, appointing a new one, vesting property, directing accounts and inquiries, declaring shares of the property for particular objects, authorising sale or lease, settling a scheme, or any other relief the case needs. S.92(3) (cy-près): the court may alter the original purposes where they cannot be carried out, e.g. the purpose is fulfilled or impossible.

Chapter 4 · asked 8× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Interpleader suits (S.88, O.XXXV)

⚡ 30-second version · Two traders, 100 bags of rice

📍A warehouse holds 100 bags of rice. Two traders each say the rice is theirs and threaten to sue the warehouse owner if he gives it to the other.
⚖️S.88: a person who claims no interest except charges or costs may file an interpleader suit making the claimants fight it out.
✅The owner files interpleader. The court decides who owns the rice, and he cannot be sued twice.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning (S.88)

Where two or more persons claim adversely to one another the same debt, money or property from a third person, who claims no interest in it other than charges or costs and is ready to pay or deliver it to the rightful claimant, he may file an interpleader suit against all the claimants, so that the court decides to whom it should go, and he is protected.

Conditions
  1. Same debt or property;
  2. claimed adversely by two or more persons;
  3. the plaintiff claims no interest except costs or charges;
  4. no collusion with any claimant;
  5. no other suit pending in which all the rights can be properly decided.
Procedure (O.XXXV)

r.1: the plaint states that the plaintiff claims no interest, the separate claims made, and no collusion. r.2: the court may order the thing claimed to be paid or placed in court. r.3: any suit pending against the plaintiff about the same thing is stayed. r.4: at the first hearing the court may discharge the plaintiff with costs and let the claimants fight it out, or keep all parties and decide. r.6: the plaintiff's costs can be made a charge on the thing claimed.

Exception (r.5)

Agents and tenants cannot sue their principals or landlords to make them interplead with others, except with persons claiming through those principals or landlords.

Conclusion

Interpleader protects an honest stakeholder from paying twice or being sued twice.

➕ More for Part B and examples 1 more point
💡 ExampleIllustrations to r.5 (learn both): (a) A gives a box of jewels to his agent B. C says the jewels were stolen from him and claims them from B. B cannot interplead A and C, as B is A's agent. (b) A gives the box to B, then writes to C making the jewels security for a debt to C. Later A says the debt is paid; C says it is not. Both claim the jewels: B can interplead, because C claims through A.

  • Part BHeadings: Meaning → Conditions → Procedure → Agents and tenants (r.5) → Illustrations. Memory trick: "Same thing, two claimants, honest holder".

Chapter 5 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Suits by indigent persons (O.XXXIII)

⚡ 30-second version · Union Bank v. Khader

📍A limited company applied at Kochi to sue a bank as an indigent person; the bank said a company cannot be "indigent".
❓Does the word "person" in Order XXXIII CPC include a company or other juristic person?
🏛️"Person" in O.XXXIII includes a juristic person, so a company can sue as an indigent person.

🪝 A poor company is a person too

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Union Bank of India v. Khader International Construction · (2001) 5 SCC 22 · decided 8 May 2001

Khader International Construction, a limited company, wanted to sue the Union Bank of India before the Sub-Court at Kochi, but could not pay the court fee.

It applied under Order XXXIII Rule 1 CPC for permission to sue as an indigent person. The Subordinate Judge allowed it, and the High Court dismissed the bank's revision.

The bank appealed to the Supreme Court, arguing that Order XXXIII is meant only for poor human beings, not companies.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning (r.1, Explanation I)

A person is indigent if he does not have sufficient means (other than property exempt from attachment and the subject matter of the suit) to pay the court fee, or, where no fee is prescribed, is not entitled to property worth ₹1,000 (excluding the same). Such a person may sue without paying court fee first. Access to justice should not depend on money.

Procedure

r.2: the application gives the plaint's particulars plus a schedule of the applicant's movable and immovable property with values, signed and verified like a pleading. r.3: presented in person by the applicant (unless exempted). r.4: the court may examine him. r.6: notice to the opposite party and the Government Pleader, who may lead evidence on his means. r.7: hearing. r.8: if allowed, the application is numbered and registered as the plaint.

Rejection (r.5)

Not properly framed or presented; applicant not indigent; he fraudulently disposed of property within 2 months before applying; no cause of action shown; he made an agreement giving someone else an interest in the subject matter; the claim is barred by law; or a third party has agreed to finance the suit.

Conclusion

O.XXXIII is deferred payment of court fee, not a waiver: the State collects it from whoever loses.

➕ More for Part B and examples 1 more point
Afterwards

r.9: permission can be withdrawn (vexatious conduct, means appear, financing deal). r.9A: the court may assign a pleader. r.10: if he wins, the court fee is calculated and is a first charge on the subject matter of the suit, recoverable by the State. r.11: if he loses or the permission is withdrawn, the court orders him to pay the fee. r.15: a refused applicant cannot apply again for the same right, but may sue normally on paying the fee (r.15A allows time). Limitation: the time spent on the application is excluded (S.13 Lim. Act).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

Yes. The Supreme Court held that "person" in Order XXXIII includes not only a natural person but also a juristic person, so a company can maintain an application under O.XXXIII r.1.

A company is represented by a person competent to represent it. The appeal was dismissed.

  • O.XXXIII is a benevolent provision so that lack of money does not shut the door of the court.
  • "Person" is not limited to human beings; the General Clauses Act definition includes companies and associations.
  • The test is the same: does the applicant have sufficient means to pay the court fee?
  • O.XXXIII defers the court fee; it is recovered later from whoever loses (r.10, r.11).

Chapter 6 · asked 3× 🔥 Good to know

Suits by or against minors and persons of unsound mind (O.XXXII)

⚡ 30-second version · A 12-year-old's house

📍A 12-year-old inherits a house that a neighbour has occupied. The boy's mother wants to sue.
⚖️O.XXXII r.1: a minor sues in his own name through a "next friend".
✅The suit is titled "Minor X, through his mother and next friend Y". The boy is the real plaintiff.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Suit by a minor (r.1)

Filed in the minor's name by a "next friend". If filed without one, the defendant may have the plaint taken off the file, with costs against the pleader or person who filed it (r.2).

Suit against a minor (r.3)

The court appoints a "guardian for the suit" (guardian ad litem) on the plaintiff's application, after notice to the minor and his natural guardian. The order continues through appeals and execution.

Who may act (r.4)

Any person of sound mind and full age whose interest is not adverse to the minor, and who is not a defendant (for next friend) or the plaintiff (for guardian). A guardian may not be appointed without his written consent.

Minor attains majority

r.12: as plaintiff he must elect to proceed (the suit continues in his own name, next friend discharged) or abandon it (as sole plaintiff, he applies for dismissal on paying costs). r.13: as a co-plaintiff he may repudiate and be struck off. r.14: he may apply to dismiss the suit as unreasonable or improper.

Conclusion

The minor is always the real party; the next friend or guardian is only his voice, watched by the court.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Protection

r.5: every application is made through the next friend or guardian. r.6: he cannot receive money or movables for the minor without leave of the court. r.7: no compromise or agreement without leave of the court; otherwise it is voidable against everyone except the minor. r.9–11: removal or retirement of a next friend or guardian.

Persons of unsound mind (r.15)

Rules 1–14 apply to persons adjudged of unsound mind, and to those found by the court to be incapable of protecting their interests because of mental infirmity.

✍️ Turn the story into marks

  • Part AWrite the gold-edged lines above: meaning, section and one example or case.
  • Part BHeadings: Suit by minor → Suit against minor → Who may act → Protection → On majority → Unsound mind. Memory trick: "Next friend for plaintiff, guardian for defendant".

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. X failed to include a necessary party in the suit. What is the effect of non-joinder of a necessary party?

Asked: 2016 · 2019 · Nov 2020 · Feb 2024 · Feb 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Does the suit fail when a necessary party is left out, and can it be cured?
Law
O.I r.9: no suit is defeated by misjoinder or non-joinder; proviso: except non-joinder of a necessary party. O.I r.10(2): the court may add a party at any stage. O.I r.13: objection to be taken early.
Apply
Without a necessary party no effective decree can be passed Kasturi. But X can apply under r.10(2) (with an amendment of the plaint) to add him; the court may also add him on its own.
Conclusion
If the necessary party is not added, the suit must fail; if he is added under O.I r.10(2), the defect is cured. For limitation, the suit against the added party starts from the date he is added (S.21 Lim. Act).

📖 Story to quote: Kasturi

Problem 2. In a partition suit the plaintiff left out some legal heirs. Can he add them now? How?

Asked: Aug 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can omitted heirs be added as defendants mid-suit?
Law
O.I r.10(2): add any person who ought to have been joined, at any stage. O.VI r.17: amend the plaint accordingly. Every co-sharer is a necessary party in partition.
Apply
The heirs' shares must be fixed for the decree to work, so they are necessary parties. He files an impleading application under O.I r.10(2) and an amendment application; the court orders fresh summons to them (r.10(4)).
Conclusion
Yes. He can add them at any stage under O.I r.10(2), with a consequent amendment of the plaint. Without them, the partition decree would be ineffective.

Problem 3. A deposits a box of jewels with B. A pledges the jewels as security for a debt to C. A says the debt is paid; C says not. Both claim the jewels from B. Remedy for B?

Asked: Sep 2024

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can B, holding the jewels, make A and C fight it out?
Law
S.88 and O.XXXV: interpleader where two persons claim the same property adversely from a stakeholder who claims no interest. O.XXXV r.5: an agent cannot interplead against his principal, except with persons claiming through the principal (Illustration (b)).
Apply
B claims nothing for himself. C claims through A (A made the jewels security for C), so the r.5 bar does not apply. This is exactly Illustration (b) to O.XXXV r.5.
Conclusion
B may file an interpleader suit against A and C, deposit the jewels in court and seek discharge with costs (O.XXXV r.2, r.4).

Problem 4. Smt. Roja wants to sue the State Government for damages. What must she do before filing?

Asked: Aug 2023 (similar: GHMC 2017 · 2019; municipal demolition 2015)

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
What procedural steps are compulsory before suing the State?
Law
S.79: sue the "State of Telangana". S.80(1): 2 months' written notice to the Secretary to the Government or the District Collector, stating name, description, residence, cause of action and relief. S.80(2): urgent relief with leave. S.15(2) Lim. Act: notice period excluded.
Apply
She serves the notice, waits 2 months, then files, pleading that notice was served. For a local body such as GHMC, S.80 does not strictly apply, but S.685 GHMC Act requires 1 month's notice and a suit within 6 months. For a sudden demolition notice of 2 days, she may also rely on the Supreme Court's 2024 directions that demolitions need a prior show-cause notice of at least 15 days.
Conclusion
Notice under S.80 (2 months), suit against the "State of Telangana", plaint stating the notice was served; if the matter is urgent, apply for leave under S.80(2).

Problem 5. X is an indigent person who wants to sue for ₹1 lakh on a promissory note. What procedure should he follow?

Asked: 2017

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
How does a poor plaintiff sue without paying court fee?
Law
O.XXXIII r.1–8: application with property schedule, presented in person, inquiry, notice to opposite party and Government Pleader; if allowed, registered as the plaint. Art. 35 Lim. Act: 3 years; S.13 Lim. Act excludes time on the application.
Apply
X files the application (plaint particulars + list of his property), appears personally, is examined, and proves he cannot pay the court fee. If he later wins, the fee becomes a first charge on the decree amount (r.10).
Conclusion
He should apply under O.XXXIII to sue as an indigent person; once permitted, the application stands as his plaint and the suit proceeds without court fee being paid upfront.

Problem 6. A wants to sue B, who is a minor. What procedure must he adopt?

Asked: Nov 2020

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
How is a minor defendant represented?
Law
O.XXXII r.3: the court appoints a guardian for the suit on the plaintiff's application, after notice to the minor and his guardian. r.4: the guardian must be of full age, sound mind, without adverse interest, and consent in writing.
Apply
A must file the suit and an application to appoint a guardian ad litem (usually the natural guardian). A decree against an unrepresented minor is not binding on him.
Conclusion
A must apply under O.XXXII r.3 for appointment of a guardian for the suit; the suit then proceeds against the minor through that guardian.

Problem 7. A files a suit for his minor son B against C. B attains majority while the suit is pending. Advise B and C.

Asked: Sep 2024

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
What happens when a minor plaintiff becomes major mid-suit?
Law
O.XXXII r.12: he must elect to proceed or abandon; r.12(2)–(3): if he proceeds, the next friend is discharged and the suit continues in his name; r.12(4): if he abandons, as sole plaintiff he applies for dismissal on paying costs. r.14: he may apply to dismiss it as unreasonable or improper.
Apply
B decides. C cannot force B to continue, but if B abandons, C can ask for his costs.
Conclusion
B should elect: continue in his own name (A is discharged as next friend) or have the suit dismissed; C is entitled to costs if B abandons.

Problem 8. The plaintiff (or defendant) dies while the suit is pending. What happens?

Asked: 2013

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Does the suit end on the death of a party?
Law
O.XXII r.1: no abatement if the right to sue survives. r.3/r.4: legal representatives are brought on record on application within 90 days (Art. 120 Lim. Act); else the suit abates. r.9: abatement may be set aside for sufficient cause (Art. 121: 60 days). r.6: no abatement after hearing and before judgment.
Apply
For a property or money claim the right survives, so the heirs step in. For a purely personal claim (e.g. defamation), it dies with the person.
Conclusion
If the right to sue survives, the suit continues with the legal representatives brought on record within 90 days; otherwise it abates.
