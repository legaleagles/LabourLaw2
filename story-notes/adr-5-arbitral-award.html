Arbitral Award: Form, Setting Aside, Enforcement and Termination

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

After months of hearings, the arbitrator signs a forty-page award: the contractor gets ₹2 crore with interest. The losing employer cannot "appeal" it as it would a court judgment. It has only one narrow door: an application to set it aside under S.34, within three months, on limited grounds such as no fair hearing, an invalid agreement, or conflict with the public policy of India. If that fails, the award is enforced exactly like a court decree (S.36).

The idea is simple: parties chose a private judge to get a quick and final answer. If courts re-examined every award on merits, arbitration would just become another round of litigation.

Chapter 1 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Arbitral award: meaning, form and contents, kinds, interest and costs

⚖️ The law behind the story

The law, step by step:

Meaning. An arbitral award is the final decision of the arbitral tribunal on the disputes referred to it; it includes an interim award (S.2(1)(c)). It binds the parties (S.35) and is enforced like a decree (S.36).

Form (S.31(1), (2)). The award shall be in writing and signed by the members of the tribunal. With more than one arbitrator, the signatures of the majority are enough if the reason for any omitted signature is stated.

Reasons (S.31(3)). The award shall state the reasons on which it is based, unless the parties agreed that no reasons be given, or it is an award on agreed terms under S.30. Reasons must be intelligible and adequate; an unreasoned or unintelligible award can be set aside (Dyna Technologies v. Crompton Greaves, 2019).

Date, place, delivery (S.31(4), (5)). It shall state its date and the place of arbitration (seat), and is deemed made there. A signed copy is delivered to each party; time for challenge runs from receipt.

Kinds of award. Final award; interim award on any matter on which a final award can be made (S.31(6)); additional award on claims omitted (S.33(4)); award on agreed terms recording a settlement (S.30); majority award with a dissenting opinion; domestic, international and foreign awards.

Interest and costs. The tribunal may award interest for the period before the award (S.31(7)(a)); post-award interest is at the rate the award fixes, failing which 2% above the current rate (S.31(7)(b)). Costs follow S.31A: generally the unsuccessful party pays, considering conduct and offers of settlement.

💡 Example: a tribunal of three awards ₹50 lakh to a supplier; the dissenting arbitrator refuses to sign. The award is still valid because it is signed by two and records why the third signature is missing; it gives reasons, the date and "seat: Hyderabad", and post-award interest at 9% a year.

Time limit (S.29A). In domestic arbitrations, the award must be made within 12 months of completion of pleadings (pleadings within 6 months: S.23(4)); the parties may extend by 6 months; further extension only by the court, which may reduce the arbitrators' fees for delay. In international commercial arbitration, the tribunal should endeavour to keep to this. Fast-track awards: 6 months (S.29B). Corrections and interpretation: within 30 days (S.33).

Chapter 2 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Setting aside (S.34) and enforcement (S.36) of an arbitral award

📖 The story: Ssangyong Engineering and Construction Co. Ltd. v. National Highways Authority of India

(2019) 15 SCC 131 · decided 8 May 2019

Ssangyong, a Korean company, built a highway in Madhya Pradesh for NHAI. The contract had a price-adjustment formula based on the wholesale price index.

When the index series changed, NHAI issued a circular applying a new method that reduced payments. The contractor objected.

The majority of the arbitral tribunal applied NHAI's circular; a dissenting arbitrator did not. The High Court refused to interfere, and the contractor appealed.

❓ When can a court set aside an arbitral award on the grounds of public policy or patent illegality after the 2015 amendment?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Nature of the remedy. Recourse to a court against an arbitral award may be made only by an application to set it aside under S.34. It is not an appeal: the court does not re-examine facts or merits Ssangyong.

Grounds proved by the applicant (S.34(2)(a)). (i) A party was under some incapacity; (ii) the arbitration agreement is not valid; (iii) the applicant was not given proper notice of the appointment or proceedings, or was otherwise unable to present his case; (iv) the award deals with disputes beyond the scope of the reference (the bad part can be severed); (v) the composition of the tribunal or the procedure was not as agreed.

Grounds the court finds (S.34(2)(b)). (i) The subject matter is not arbitrable; (ii) the award is in conflict with the public policy of India, which (Explanation, 2015) means only: induced or affected by fraud or corruption (or breach of S.75 or S.81); contravention of the fundamental policy of Indian law; or conflict with the most basic notions of morality or justice. There is no review on merits.

Patent illegality (S.34(2A)). A purely domestic award may also be set aside for patent illegality appearing on the face of the award, but not merely for a wrong application of law or by re-appreciating evidence. Rewriting the contract or deciding without evidence is patent illegality Ssangyong.

Time and procedure. Apply within three months of receiving the award (or the S.33 decision); the court may allow 30 days more for sufficient cause, but not thereafter (S.34(3)). The court may adjourn to let the tribunal remove the defect (S.34(4)); prior notice to the other party is required (S.34(5)) and the application should be decided within one year (S.34(6)).

💡 Case story Ssangyong: in a highway contract, the tribunal applied a price-adjustment formula that NHAI had issued on its own, which the contractor never agreed to. The Supreme Court set aside the majority award: an arbitrator who rewrites the contract for the parties violates the most basic notions of justice.

Modification. A Constitution Bench has held that courts under S.34 and S.37 have a limited power to modify an award: they can sever the invalid part, correct clerical, computational or typographical errors apparent on the face of the record, and adjust post-award interest; the Supreme Court may also use Art. 142 sparingly. They cannot rewrite the award on merits Gayatri Balasamy.

Enforcement (S.35, S.36) and appeal (S.37). The award is final and binding (S.35). When the time for S.34 has expired, or the application is rejected, it is enforced under the CPC as if it were a decree (S.36(1)). Since 2015, merely filing S.34 does not stay the award; the court may grant a stay on conditions (e.g. deposit of the money); since 2021, an unconditional stay is possible where the award was induced by fraud or corruption. An appeal lies under S.37 against an order setting aside or refusing to set aside the award.

🏛️ How the story ended

After 2015, 'public policy of India' is narrow: the 'fundamental policy of Indian law' and 'basic notions of justice' grounds do not permit a review of the merits. Patent illegality applies only to domestic awards and does not cover a mere wrong application of law.

But an award that imposes on a party a contract it never agreed to, by applying a unilateral circular, breaches the most basic notions of justice. The majority award was set aside; using Art. 142, the Court upheld the minority view.

Why it matters:

  • The 2015 Explanation to S.34 applies to applications filed after 23 October 2015.
  • Re-appreciation of evidence is not allowed.
  • Deciding outside the contract or without evidence is patent illegality.

📚 More stories on this topic

Gayatri Balasamy v. ISG Novasoft Technologies Ltd.: An employee won compensation in arbitration for harassment and wrongful termination; the High Court changed the amount, and the question arose whether courts can modify awards at all. Held: Courts have a limited power to modify: sever invalid parts, correct clerical or computational errors apparent on the record, and adjust post-award interest; the Supreme Court may use Art. 142 rarely. No rewriting on merits. 🪝 Correct, do not rewrite

Termination of arbitral proceedings and of the arbitrator's mandate

⚖️ The law behind the story

The law, step by step:

Mandate ends (S.14). An arbitrator's mandate terminates, and he is substituted, if he becomes de jure or de facto unable to perform his functions or for other reasons fails to act without undue delay; or he withdraws; or the parties agree to terminate it. A dispute about this is decided by the court (S.14(2)).

Other grounds. A successful challenge for lack of independence or impartiality (S.13); withdrawal for any reason or by agreement (S.15(1)); and expiry of the time limit under S.29A without an extension by the parties or the court.

Substitution (S.15(2)–(4)). A substitute is appointed by the same rules that applied to the original appointment. Unless the parties agree otherwise, hearings may be repeated at the tribunal's discretion, and orders already made are not invalid merely because of the change.

Termination of proceedings (S.32). Proceedings end (1) with the final award, or (2) by an order of the tribunal when: (a) the claimant withdraws his claim (unless the respondent objects and the tribunal sees a legitimate interest in a final settlement); (b) the parties agree to terminate; or (c) continuation has become unnecessary or impossible. The tribunal's mandate ends with the proceedings, except for corrections (S.33) and a remand under S.34(4).

Default and settlement. If the claimant fails without sufficient cause to file his statement of claim, the tribunal terminates the proceedings (S.25(a)); if the respondent defaults, the tribunal continues (S.25(b), (c)). If the parties settle, the tribunal terminates the proceedings and, if asked, records an award on agreed terms (S.30).

💡 Example: a sole arbitrator falls seriously ill for a year and cannot hold hearings. His mandate ends under S.14; the parties appoint a substitute in the same way, and the new arbitrator may rely on the evidence already recorded.

Death and insolvency. The death of a party does not end the arbitration agreement or the mandate; it is enforced by or against his legal representatives (S.40). The death of the person who appointed an arbitrator does not end the arbitrator's authority. Insolvency: S.41 governs when the trustee or receiver adopts the contract.
