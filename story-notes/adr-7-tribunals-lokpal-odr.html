Tribunals, Lokpal, Lokayukta and Online Dispute Resolution

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Not every dispute needs a regular court. A government employee denied promotion goes to the Central Administrative Tribunal; a company in insolvency goes to the NCLT; an environmental harm goes to the National Green Tribunal. These specialised tribunals bring expertise and speed. And when the complaint is against a corrupt public servant, the citizen can go to an ombudsman: the Lokpal at the Centre or the Lokayukta in the State.

Today, disputes can even be settled online: a buyer and an e-commerce seller can negotiate, mediate or arbitrate through a portal, with video hearings and e-signed settlements. That is Online Dispute Resolution (ODR).

Tribunals: meaning, constitutional basis, kinds, merits and judicial review

⚡ 30-second version · L. Chandra Kumar

📍Laws creating administrative tribunals shut out the High Courts from service disputes, leaving only an appeal to the Supreme Court.
❓Can a law creating tribunals exclude the High Courts' power of judicial review?
🏛️The power of judicial review of the High Courts (Art. 226/227) and the Supreme Court (Art. 32) is part of the basic structure; clauses excluding it are invalid. Tribunal decisions can be challenged before a High Court Division Bench.

🪝 No tribunal is above the High Court

📖 Read the full story 2 short paragraphs

L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India · (1997) 3 SCC 261 · decided 18 Mar 1997

Article 323A and Article 323B allowed laws creating tribunals to exclude the jurisdiction of all courts except the Supreme Court under Article 136. The Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 did this for service matters.

Government employees and others challenged these exclusions, arguing that taking away the High Courts' power of review damaged the independence of the judiciary. A seven-judge bench heard the matter.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

A tribunal is a specialised adjudicating body, outside the regular hierarchy of courts, set up by statute to decide disputes in a particular field, usually with judicial and expert (technical/administrative) members.

Constitutional basis

The 42nd Amendment (1976), on the Swaran Singh Committee's advice, added Art. 323A (Parliament may set up administrative tribunals for service matters of public servants) and Art. 323B (Parliament or State Legislatures may set up tribunals for taxation, foreign exchange, industrial and labour disputes, land reforms, ceiling on property, elections, essential supplies, rent and tenancy and similar matters).

Examples

Central Administrative Tribunal (Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985); Income Tax Appellate Tribunal; NCLT and NCLAT; National Green Tribunal (2010); Debts Recovery Tribunals; Armed Forces Tribunal; Securities Appellate Tribunal; Consumer Commissions (quasi-judicial).

Judicial review

A Constitution Bench of seven judges held that the clauses excluding the jurisdiction of High Courts were invalid: judicial review under Art. 226/227 and Art. 32 is part of the basic structure; tribunals act as courts of first instance, and their decisions can be challenged before a Division Bench of the High Court L. Chandra Kumar.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Merits

Expertise; speed; simpler procedure (not bound by CPC, follow natural justice); lower cost; reduce the burden on courts. Concerns: executive control over appointments and service conditions, vacancies, short tenures; the Supreme Court has repeatedly struck down rules that weakened their independence (e.g. the Madras Bar Association cases).

💡 ExampleExample: a railway employee who is denied promotion files an application before the Central Administrative Tribunal, not the civil court. If he loses, he can go to the High Court under Art. 226 and then to the Supreme Court.
🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. The power of judicial review vested in the High Courts under Articles 226 and 227 and in the Supreme Court under Article 32 is part of the basic structure of the Constitution.

Clauses in Article 323A(2)(d), 323B(3)(d) and the Act excluding that jurisdiction were invalid to that extent. Tribunals can still decide cases, including constitutional questions about laws other than their own Act, as courts of first instance, subject to review by a Division Bench of the High Court.

  • Tribunals supplement, not supplant, the High Courts.
  • An earlier decision (S.P. Sampath Kumar) was overruled on this point.
  • Basis of later cases on tribunal independence.

Lokpal and Lokayukta: origin, composition, jurisdiction, powers and role

⚡ The answer in one glance

1Origin2Lokpal: composition and selection3Jurisdiction4Procedure and powers5Lokayukta6Role and criticism

🧠 "Old Lions Jump Past Little Rabbits": Origin · Lokpal composition · Jurisdiction · Procedure · Lokayukta · Role.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Origin

Based on the Scandinavian ombudsman (Sweden, 1809): an independent authority that inquires into citizens' complaints against public officials. The Administrative Reforms Commission (1966) recommended a Lokpal for the Centre and Lokayuktas for the States. After many failed Bills and a nationwide anti-corruption movement (2011), Parliament passed the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 (in force 16 January 2014).

Lokpal: composition and selection

A Chairperson (a former Chief Justice of India, Supreme Court judge, or eminent person) and up to eight members, of whom half are judicial and at least half are from SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women. Selection committee: the Prime Minister (chair), the Speaker, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, the Chief Justice of India (or a judge nominated by him) and an eminent jurist. The first Lokpal was appointed in 2019.

Jurisdiction

Corruption complaints (under the Prevention of Corruption Act) against the Prime Minister (except on international relations, security, public order, atomic energy and space; an inquiry needs approval of a full bench by two-thirds, held in camera), Union Ministers, MPs, Central Government officers of all groups, and bodies funded or controlled by the Centre.

Lokayukta

S.63 requires every State to set up a Lokayukta by its own law within one year. Maharashtra created one of the first (1971). In Telangana, the Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayukta function under the State's Lokayukta Act. The Lokayukta inquires into complaints against State Ministers, MLAs and officials; its powers differ from State to State.

➕ More for Part B and examples 3 more points
Procedure and powers

An Inquiry Wing and a Prosecution Wing; preliminary inquiry, then investigation (it may refer to the CBI and superintend it in such cases); powers of a civil court; search and seizure; provisional attachment of property acquired by corruption; recommendation of transfer or suspension; trial by Special Courts, to be completed within a fixed time.

💡 ExampleExample: a citizen has proof that a Central Government officer demanded a bribe to clear a licence. He can complain to the Lokpal, which can order a preliminary inquiry, have it investigated, attach the officer's ill-gotten property and send him for trial in a Special Court.
Role and criticism

They give citizens an independent forum against corruption, outside the government hierarchy. Criticisms: delays in appointments, few complaints acted upon, dependence on investigating agencies, and uneven Lokayukta laws across States.

Chapter 3 · asked 2× 🔥 Good to know

Online dispute resolution (ODR)

⚡ The answer in one glance

1Meaning2Forms3Legal support4Merits and challenges

🧠 "Mobile Friendly Legal Methods": Meaning · Forms · Legal support · Merits/challenges.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

ODR is the resolution of disputes, wholly or partly online, using technology for filing, communication, hearings and settlement, through negotiation, mediation or arbitration.

Forms

Automated/assisted negotiation (e.g. blind bidding on amounts); online mediation and conciliation by video; online arbitration with e-filing, virtual hearings and e-signed awards; e-Lok Adalats; platforms set up by regulators (e.g. SEBI's ODR portal for securities disputes) and by e-commerce firms.

Legal support

The IT Act, 2000 recognises electronic records and signatures (S.4, S.5) and contracts made electronically (S.10A); an arbitration agreement may be made by electronic communication (S.7(4)(b), A&C Act); the Mediation Act, 2023 provides for online mediation; electronic evidence is admissible (Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023). NITI Aayog's ODR Policy Plan (2021) recommends wider use.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
💡 ExampleExample: a customer in Warangal who received a damaged phone from an online seller files a complaint on an ODR platform; a mediator holds a 20-minute video call and the seller agrees to a refund the same week.
Merits and challenges

Merits: no travel, low cost, speed, suits small and cross-border disputes. Challenges: digital divide, data security and confidentiality, identity verification, enforcement of online settlements, and lack of awareness.
