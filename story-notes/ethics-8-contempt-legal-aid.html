Contempt of Court and Legal Aid

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Courts have no army and no purse. Their orders are obeyed because people respect their authority. When someone wilfully disobeys a court order, or scandalises a judge so as to shake public confidence in justice, the court can punish him for contempt. But the power is used carefully: fair criticism of a judgment is not contempt, and since 2006 truth told in the public interest is a defence.

The other side of the same coin is legal aid. Justice must be not only respected but reachable: a person who cannot afford a lawyer must still get one. For an advocate, legal aid is not charity; it is a professional duty (BCI Rule 46).

🪝 The robe does not shield contempt

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

In re Vinay Chandra Mishra · (1995) 2 SCC 584 · decided 10 Mar 1995

During a hearing in the Allahabad High Court, the judge asked the senior advocate Vinay Chandra Mishra a question about the case.

He lost his temper, shouted at the judge, and threatened to get him transferred or impeached. He was at that time the Chairman of the Bar Council of India.

The judge reported the incident, and the Supreme Court took up contempt proceedings.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Object and base

To protect the authority and dignity of courts and the administration of justice, not the personal dignity of judges. The Supreme Court and High Courts are courts of record with inherent power to punish contempt (Art. 129, 215); contempt is a ground for restricting free speech (Art. 19(2)). The Act of 1971 (on the Sanyal Committee's report) defines and limits the power.

Civil contempt (S.2(b))

Wilful disobedience of any judgment, decree, direction, order, writ or other process of a court, or wilful breach of an undertaking given to a court.

Criminal contempt (S.2(c))

Publication (by words, signs, visible representations or otherwise) or any other act which: (i) scandalises or lowers the authority of any court; (ii) prejudices or interferes with the due course of any judicial proceeding; or (iii) interferes with or obstructs the administration of justice in any other way.

Punishment and apology (S.12)

Simple imprisonment up to six months, or fine up to ₹2,000, or both. A bona fide apology may lead the court to discharge the contemner or remit the punishment; an apology must not be qualified or merely tactical.

➕ More for Part B and examples 3 more points
Defences

Innocent publication or distribution without knowledge of pending proceedings (S.3); fair and accurate report of judicial proceedings (S.4); fair criticism of a decided case (S.5); a good-faith complaint about a subordinate judge to a superior authority (S.6). Since 2006: no punishment unless the contempt substantially interferes with justice, and truth is a defence if in the public interest and the request is bona fide (S.13).

Procedure

Contempt in the face of the court is dealt with summarily (S.14). Other criminal contempt: on the court's own motion, or on a motion by the Advocate-General (or Attorney/Solicitor-General for the Supreme Court), or by any person with his written consent (S.15); a subordinate court refers it to the High Court (S.15(2)). Appeals lie under S.19. No proceedings after one year from the contempt (S.20).

Advocates and contempt

An advocate who commits contempt may be punished by the court and separately disciplined by the Bar Council (S.35, Advocates Act). Courts may also bar a contemnor-advocate from appearing before them until he purges the contempt (R.K. Anand v. Registrar, Delhi High Court, 2009). Recent example: In re Prashant Bhushan (2020): an advocate fined ₹1 for tweets held to be contempt.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

Yes. Insulting and threatening a judge in open court scandalises the court and interferes with the administration of justice: it is criminal contempt, made worse by his high position at the Bar.

The Court imposed simple imprisonment (suspended) and, under Articles 129 and 142, suspended him from practice for three years. That suspension was later overruled in Supreme Court Bar Association v. Union of India (1998).

  • The higher the position of the advocate, the higher his duty of restraint.
  • Contempt power protects the administration of justice, not the person of the judge.
  • Read with SCBA (1998) on who can suspend an advocate.

📚 More stories on this topic

Supreme Court Bar Association v. Union of India: After the Supreme Court suspended a senior advocate's licence for contempt, the Supreme Court Bar Association questioned whether the Court had that power. Held: The Supreme Court cannot, in contempt jurisdiction or under Art. 142, suspend or cancel an advocate's licence; that power belongs to the Bar Councils under the Advocates Act. The Court can punish contempt and refer the matter to the Bar Council. 🪝 Court punishes contempt; Bar Council punishes misconduct

One-line recall: what must you remember?

Contempt has two faces: civil (disobeying an order) and criminal (scandalising or obstructing). Shield: fair report, fair criticism, truth. Sword: 6 months or ₹2,000. Clock: one year.

Legal aid: meaning, constitutional and statutory basis, and the advocate's duty

⚡ The answer in one glance

1Meaning2Constitution and courts3Legal Services Authorities Act4Who is entitled5Advocate's duty

🧠 "Mangoes Can Lift Weary Ammas": Meaning · Constitution and courts · LSA Act · Who · Advocate's duty.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

Legal aid means free legal services (advice, a lawyer, court fees, copies) to persons who cannot afford them, so that justice is not denied for want of money or because of other disabilities.

Constitution and courts

Art. 39A (1976): equal justice and free legal aid. The Supreme Court made it part of Art. 21: M.H. Hoskot (1978), Hussainara Khatoon (1979), Khatri v. State of Bihar (1981, from the first production before a Magistrate), Suk Das (1986, even without asking). A trial without offering legal aid to a poor accused may be vitiated.

Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987

NALSA (national), State and District Legal Services Authorities, High Court and Taluk committees. Functions: provide legal services, organise Lok Adalats, legal literacy and awareness camps, mediation centres, the legal aid defence counsel system, victim compensation schemes, and supervise legal aid clinics in law colleges (S.4, S.7, S.10).

Who is entitled (S.12)

SC/ST members; victims of trafficking or begar; women and children; persons with disability; victims of disasters and violence; industrial workmen; persons in custody; and persons with annual income below the prescribed limit.

Advocate's duty (BCI Rule 46)

Every advocate shall bear in mind that anyone genuinely in need of a lawyer is entitled to legal assistance even if he cannot pay fully or adequately, and within his means the advocate shall render legal aid. Bar Councils run legal aid committees (S.9A, Advocates Act). A legal-aid lawyer owes the same diligence as to a paying client; neglecting such a case is misconduct.

💡 ExampleExample: an under-trial prisoner who has been in jail for two years without a lawyer writes to the District Legal Services Authority through the jail. A panel lawyer is assigned, files a bail application, and he is released the next month.
