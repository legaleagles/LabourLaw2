Lok Adalat, Legal Aid, Family Courts and S.89 CPC

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

A widow has been fighting an insurance company for motor accident compensation for six years. On a Saturday, at a National Lok Adalat, a retired judge and a social worker sit with both sides; the company offers a fair amount, she agrees, and by evening she holds an award that is final, with no appeal and the court fee refunded. That is Indian ADR at its most familiar: the "people's court".

The same idea runs through this cluster. The Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 gives free lawyers to the poor and runs Lok Adalats; Family Courts must first try to reconcile husband and wife; and S.89 CPC tells every civil judge to ask, before trial, "can this be settled outside?"

Lok Adalat: features, jurisdiction, procedure and award; Permanent Lok Adalat

📖 The story: State of Punjab v. Jalour Singh

(2008) 2 SCC 660

Claims for compensation arising out of a motor accident were pending. They were placed before a Lok Adalat.

The Lok Adalat passed an "award" enhancing the compensation, although there was no real settlement: one side had not agreed to it.

The award was challenged, and the question of how far a Lok Adalat can go reached the Supreme Court.

❓ Can a Lok Adalat decide a dispute when the parties do not agree to a settlement?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Meaning and origin. "Lok Adalat" means people's court: a forum where disputes pending in court or at the pre-litigation stage are settled amicably. Begun as camps in Gujarat (1982), it was given statutory status by the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (Chapter VI).

Organisation (S.19). Organised by the State, District and Taluk Legal Services Authorities and the Supreme Court and High Court Legal Services Committees. A bench usually has a serving or retired judicial officer and other members such as a lawyer and a social worker. National Lok Adalats are held on fixed days across India.

Jurisdiction (S.19(5)). Any case pending before any court, or any dispute at the pre-litigation stage, within the jurisdiction of the court. Not for non-compoundable offences. Typical matters: motor accident claims, bank recovery, cheque cases, matrimonial (other than divorce itself), electricity and water bills, compoundable offences.

Cognizance (S.20). A court refers a case if (i) the parties agree, or (ii) one party applies and the court sees a chance of settlement, or (iii) the court thinks it fit, after hearing the parties. The Lok Adalat acts with utmost speed, guided by justice, equity and fair play. If no settlement, the case goes back to the court (or the parties are told to approach a court).

Award (S.21). Every award is deemed a decree of a civil court; it is final and binding on all parties and no appeal lies from it. The court fee paid is refunded. An award without real consent is invalid and can be challenged only by a writ petition Jalour Singh.

💡 Example: a bank sues a farmer for ₹3 lakh. At a Lok Adalat, the bank waives part of the interest and the farmer agrees to pay ₹2.2 lakh in instalments. The award ends the suit; the farmer gets back his court fee, and neither side can appeal.

Powers (S.22). The powers of a civil court for summoning and examining witnesses, discovery, receiving evidence on affidavits and requisitioning records; its proceedings are judicial proceedings.

Permanent Lok Adalat (S.22B–22E, added 2002). For public utility services (transport, post, telephone, electricity, water, hospitals, insurance, banking and the like). It first tries conciliation; if that fails, it can decide the dispute on merits (S.22C(8)). Its award is final and binding (S.22E). Not for non-compoundable offences; there is a monetary ceiling.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ended

No. A Lok Adalat is a forum for conciliation and settlement. It has no adjudicatory function; it can only pass an award on the basis of a compromise or settlement between the parties.

If there is no settlement, it must return the case to the court. An award made without a real settlement is a nullity, and since no appeal lies, the remedy is a writ petition under Art. 226/227.

Why it matters:

  • Consent of both parties is the foundation of a Lok Adalat award.
  • Lok Adalat awards are final only when genuinely agreed (S.21 LSA Act).
  • A Permanent Lok Adalat (public utility services) is different: it can decide on merits.

Chapter 2 · asked 2× 🔥 Good to know

Free legal aid and the Legal Services Authorities

⚖️ The law behind the story

The law, step by step:

Constitutional base. Art. 39A (added in 1976): the State shall provide free legal aid so that justice is not denied by reason of economic or other disabilities. Art. 14 and Art. 22(1) (right to consult a lawyer) support it.

Supreme Court on legal aid. Free legal aid to a poor accused is part of the fair procedure under Art. 21: M.H. Hoskot v. State of Maharashtra (1978) (copy of judgment and a lawyer for appeal), Hussainara Khatoon (1979), Khatri v. State of Bihar (1981) (from the first production before the Magistrate), Suk Das v. Union Territory of Arunachal Pradesh (1986) (the accused need not even ask: the court must tell him).

Authorities. NALSA (national, headed by the Chief Justice of India as Patron-in-Chief), State Legal Services Authorities, High Court Legal Services Committees, District Legal Services Authorities and Taluk Legal Services Committees. They provide legal aid, run Lok Adalats, legal literacy camps, mediation centres, and victim compensation schemes.

Who is entitled (S.12). A member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe; a victim of trafficking or begar; a woman or a child; a person with disability; a victim of mass disaster, ethnic or caste violence, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster; an industrial workman; a person in custody; and a person whose annual income is below the prescribed limit.

💡 Example: a daily-wage worker arrested for theft has no money for a lawyer. At his first production, the Magistrate must tell him he can have a free legal-aid lawyer, and the District Legal Services Authority assigns one at no cost.

What is given. A lawyer, court fee and process fees, drafting of pleadings, copies of documents, and legal advice. The legal-aid defence counsel system now provides full-time defence lawyers in many districts.

Family Courts: object, jurisdiction, procedure and special features

⚖️ The law behind the story

The law, step by step:

Object. The Family Courts Act, 1984 sets up special courts to promote conciliation and secure speedy settlement of disputes about marriage and family affairs, in a less formal, more humane setting than ordinary courts.

Establishment and judges (S.3, S.4). The State Government, in consultation with the High Court, must set up a Family Court for every city or town with a population over ten lakh, and may elsewhere. Judges should be committed to protecting the institution of marriage and promoting the welfare of children; women are given preference. Counsellors and social welfare agencies are attached (S.5, S.6).

Jurisdiction (S.7). Suits and proceedings for nullity, restitution of conjugal rights, judicial separation and divorce; validity of a marriage or a person's matrimonial status; disputes about the property of the spouses; injunctions arising out of the marital relationship; legitimacy; maintenance; guardianship and custody of children; and maintenance proceedings under the BNSS (Chapter X).

Duty to settle (S.9). The Family Court must, in the first instance, make efforts to persuade the parties to a settlement, and may adjourn proceedings for that purpose.

Lawyers (S.13). No party is entitled as of right to be represented by a legal practitioner; the court may allow it, and may appoint a lawyer as amicus curiae. This keeps proceedings simple and less adversarial.

💡 Example: a couple files for divorce in a Family Court in Hyderabad. The judge first sends them to the court's counsellor; they agree on child custody and maintenance, and the court then records a mutual-consent decree.

Procedure and appeal. The court may lay down its own procedure for settlement (S.10(3)); proceedings may be held in camera (S.11); it may take help of medical and welfare experts (S.12); strict rules of evidence are relaxed (S.14) and only a memorandum of evidence is recorded (S.15). Appeal lies to the High Court within 30 days; no appeal from a decree passed with consent (S.19).

Chapter 4 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Section 89 CPC: settlement of disputes outside court

📖 The story: Afcons Infrastructure Ltd. v. Cherian Varkey Construction Co. (P) Ltd.

(2010) 8 SCC 24

Afcons Infrastructure had sub-contracted construction work to Cherian Varkey Construction. A money dispute arose and the sub-contractor filed a suit.

The sub-contractor asked the court to refer the dispute to arbitration under S.89. Afcons objected, and there was no arbitration agreement between them. The trial court and the High Court nevertheless referred it.

Afcons appealed to the Supreme Court, which used the case to explain the whole of S.89.

❓ Can a court refer a suit to arbitration under S.89 without the consent of both parties? How should S.89 be applied?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Text and object. Inserted by the CPC (Amendment) Act, 1999, in force from 1 July 2002. Where it appears to the court that there exist elements of a settlement acceptable to the parties, the court shall formulate the terms of a possible settlement, give them to the parties for observations, and then refer the dispute to an ADR method.

Four methods. (a) Arbitration (then governed by the 1996 Act); (b) conciliation (1996 Act); (c) judicial settlement, including Lok Adalat (LSA Act); (d) mediation (the court effects a compromise under prescribed procedure).

Procedure (O.X r.1A–1C). After framing issues, the court directs the parties to opt for one of the modes (r.1A); they appear before the chosen forum (r.1B); if the effort fails, they come back to court (r.1C).

Salem Bar and Afcons. Salem Advocate Bar Association v. Union of India (2005) upheld S.89 and approved model mediation rules. In Afcons Infrastructure the Supreme Court explained that the definitions of "judicial settlement" and "mediation" in S.89 had been mixed up and must be read interchanged; that considering ADR is mandatory after pleadings; that reference to arbitration or conciliation needs consent; and listed cases unsuitable for ADR (representative suits, election disputes, fraud and forgery, cases against the Government, criminal matters).

💡 Example: in a partition suit between three siblings, the judge sees that they mostly agree, refers them to the court's mediation centre, and they sign a settlement dividing the house; the court passes a decree in terms of it.

Effect. A settlement through Lok Adalat becomes an award (S.21 LSA Act); through mediation or judicial settlement, the court records it and passes a decree; through arbitration or conciliation, it is governed by the 1996 Act. The plaintiff gets a refund of court fee (S.16 Court-fees Act).

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ended

No. Reference to arbitration (and conciliation) under S.89 requires the consent of all parties; without an arbitration agreement or consent, the court cannot force arbitration.

The definitions of "judicial settlement" and "mediation" in S.89(2) were wrongly drafted and must be read interchanged.

The court must consider ADR in every suit after pleadings; it listed categories not suitable for ADR (e.g. representative suits, election disputes, serious fraud or forgery, suits against the Government needing a decision, criminal cases) and those well suited (commercial, family, tenancy, consumer, tort disputes).

Why it matters:

  • S.89 makes the consideration of ADR mandatory, not the use of any particular method.
  • Mediation and Lok Adalat referrals need no prior consent; arbitration and conciliation do.
  • Read with Salem Advocate Bar Association (2005).
