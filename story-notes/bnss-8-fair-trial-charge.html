Fair Trial, Charge and Double Jeopardy

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

A criminal trial is a contest between the mighty State and one individual. To keep it fair, the law gives the individual a shield: he is presumed innocent; he is told exactly what he is accused of (the charge); he is tried by an independent judge in open court, quickly, with a lawyer; and once acquitted or convicted, he is never tried again for the same offence.

In the Best Bakery case, 14 people died in a riot, the witnesses were frightened into silence and everyone was acquitted. The Supreme Court ordered a fresh trial in another State and said: a fair trial is fair to both sides, to the accused and to the victim and society.

Fair trial: meaning, constitutional basis, essential principles · Speedy trial · Presumption of innocence

⚡ 30-second version · Zahira Sheikh (Best Bakery)

📍In the 2002 Best Bakery riot case in Vadodara, 14 people died; witnesses turned hostile under threats and all accused were acquitted.
❓When witnesses are intimidated and the prosecution is half-hearted, can the court order a fresh trial?
🏛️Retrial ordered in Maharashtra: a fair trial is fair to the victim and society too; an intimidated trial is no trial.

🪝 Fair to both sides

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Zahira Habibullah Sheikh v. State of Gujarat · (2004) 4 SCC 158 · decided 12 Apr 2004

On 1 March 2002, during the riots in Gujarat, a mob attacked the Best Bakery in Vadodara; 14 people were killed.

At the trial, most eyewitnesses, including Zahira Sheikh, turned hostile, and all the accused were acquitted. Zahira later said she had been threatened into changing her evidence.

The High Court refused a retrial. Zahira and the National Human Rights Commission approached the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

A fair trial is a trial by an impartial judge, a fair prosecutor and an atmosphere of judicial calm, in which the accused has every reasonable opportunity to defend himself. It is the heart of criminal justice.

Constitutional basis

Art. 14 (equality), Art. 20 (no ex post facto punishment, no double jeopardy, no self-incrimination), Art. 21 (procedure must be fair, just and reasonable: Maneka Gandhi, 1978), Art. 22 (counsel, production), Art. 39A (legal aid).

Principles
  1. Presumption of innocence;
  2. independent, impartial judge;
  3. open court (S.366; in camera for rape cases);
  4. knowledge of the accusation (charge) and copies of documents;
  5. trial in the presence of the accused, evidence in his presence;
  6. right to a lawyer and legal aid (S.341);
  7. right to cross-examine and lead defence evidence;
  8. no self-incrimination;
  9. speedy trial;
  10. no double jeopardy;
  11. reasoned judgment and a right of appeal.
Speedy trial

A part of Art. 21 Hussainara Khatoon. No fixed outer time limit is laid down by the courts (P. Ramachandra Rao, 2002), but delay is weighed against the State. The BNSS sets timelines: charge within 60 days, judgment within 30–45 days, day-to-day trial (S.346), release of undertrials after half (one-third for first offenders) of the maximum sentence (S.479).

➕ More for Part B and examples 1 more point
Presumption of innocence

The accused is presumed innocent until proved guilty beyond reasonable doubt by the prosecution; the benefit of doubt goes to the accused. Exceptions: some laws shift a burden on the accused (e.g. presumption in dowry death, NDPS, POCSO), but only after the prosecution proves the basic facts.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

Yes. The Supreme Court set aside the acquittals and ordered a retrial by a court in Maharashtra, with protection for witnesses.

A fair trial means a trial before an impartial judge, a fair prosecutor and an atmosphere of judicial calm.

  • Fair trial is a right of the accused and also of the victim and society.
  • If witnesses are threatened, the trial is a mockery; courts must not be silent spectators.
  • Witness protection is essential to a fair trial (now the Witness Protection Scheme, S.398 BNSS).

📚 More stories on this topic

Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary, State of Bihar: Newspapers reported thousands of undertrials in Bihar jails held for years, some longer than the maximum sentence for their offence. Held: Speedy trial is a fundamental right under Art. 21; undertrials held too long must be released; free legal aid is part of a fair procedure. 🪝 Justice delayed is liberty denied

Chapter 2 · asked 10× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Charge: meaning, object, contents, alteration, joinder of charges and exceptions (S.234–247)

⚡ 30-second version · Willie Slaney

📍Slaney was charged with murder read with common intention, but convicted of murder simpliciter without a separate charge for it.
❓Does an error or omission in framing the charge make the conviction illegal?
🏛️Conviction upheld: an error in the charge does not vitiate the trial unless the accused was misled and suffered prejudice.

🪝 Substance over form

📖 Read the full story 2 short paragraphs

Willie (William) Slaney v. State of Madhya Pradesh · AIR 1956 SC 116 · decided 1955

Willie Slaney and another were tried for murder. The charge against Slaney was framed as murder read with the provision on common intention.

The trial court convicted him of murder itself, without a separate charge for murder simpliciter. He argued that the conviction was illegal because there was no proper charge.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning and object

A charge is the precise formulation of the specific accusation against the accused, framed in writing by the court and read and explained to him. Object: to tell him exactly what he must meet, so he is not taken by surprise. In summons cases no formal charge is framed; the particulars are stated (S.274).

Contents (S.234–236)

The offence and its name, the law and section, the facts making the offence; particulars of time, place and person (S.235); where needed, the manner of committing it (S.236); previous conviction if relevant for enhanced punishment.

Errors (S.238)

No error or omission in the charge is material unless the accused was in fact misled and a failure of justice resulted.

Alteration (S.239–240)

The court may alter or add to the charge at any time before judgment, reading and explaining it to the accused; it may adjourn or order a fresh trial if the change prejudices him, and witnesses may be recalled.

Basic rule (S.241)

For every distinct offence there shall be a separate charge, and each charge tried separately. Exceptions:

  1. S.242: up to five offences of the same kind within 12 months;
  2. S.243: offences in the same transaction;
  3. S.244: where it is doubtful which offence the facts make, alternative charges;
  4. S.245: conviction for a minor offence included in the charged one;
  5. S.246: persons who may be charged jointly (same offence, same transaction, abettors, theft and receivers, etc.); also the Magistrate may join with the accused's consent.
🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. The Constitution Bench upheld the conviction.

The test is whether the accused was misled and whether a failure of justice resulted; here he knew exactly what he was accused of and defended himself fully.

  • Procedure is the handmaid of justice; it should not be treated as a "fetish".
  • The real question is prejudice to the accused, judged from the whole record.
  • This principle is now in S.238 BNSS (effect of errors in charge).

Chapter 3 · asked 8× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Double jeopardy: autrefois acquit and autrefois convict (S.337 BNSS, Art. 20(2))

⚡ 30-second version · Kolla Veera Raghav Rao

📍A man convicted for a dishonoured cheque was then prosecuted for cheating on the same facts.
❓Can a person convicted under one law be tried again under another law on the same facts?
🏛️Second prosecution barred: the statutory bar applies to any offence on the same facts, wider than Art. 20(2).

🪝 Same facts, one trial

📖 Read the full story 2 short paragraphs

Kolla Veera Raghav Rao v. Gorantla Venkateswara Rao · (2011) 2 SCC 703

Kolla Veera Raghav Rao was prosecuted and convicted for dishonour of a cheque under the Negotiable Instruments Act.

Afterwards, a second prosecution was started against him for cheating, based on the same facts.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

Nemo debet bis vexari: no one should be tried twice for the same offence. Pleas: autrefois acquit (previously acquitted) and autrefois convict (previously convicted).

Art. 20(2)

No person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once. It needs a previous prosecution and punishment by a court; departmental or customs proceedings are not "prosecution" (Maqbool Hussain v. State of Bombay, 1953).

S.337 BNSS (wider)

A person once tried by a court of competent jurisdiction and convicted or acquitted shall not, while it remains in force, be tried again for the same offence, nor for any other offence on the same facts for which a different charge could have been made or of which he could have been convicted.

Exceptions (S.337(2)–(5))
  1. A distinct offence for which a separate charge could have been made may be tried later, with the State Government's consent;
  2. where the act causes consequences that happen later (e.g. the victim dies after a conviction for grievous hurt), a fresh trial for the graver offence is allowed;
  3. where the first court was not competent to try the graver offence;
  4. a discharge or dismissal of a complaint is not an acquittal.
➕ More for Part B and examples 1 more point
Illustrations to remember

(from the section): (a) convicted of grievous hurt; victim dies later: may be tried for culpable homicide. (b) convicted by the Sessions Court of culpable homicide: cannot be tried again for murder on the same facts. (c) A, B, C convicted by a Magistrate of robbing D: may later be tried for dacoity on the same facts (the Magistrate could not try dacoity).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. The second prosecution was quashed.

The statutory bar (S.300 CrPC, now S.337 BNSS) is wider than Article 20(2): it bars a second trial not only for the same offence but for any other offence on the same facts for which a different charge could have been made.

  • Art. 20(2) bars a second prosecution and punishment for the same offence.
  • The statutory provision protects also against a second trial on the same facts under a different provision.
  • Different facts or ingredients would allow a separate trial.

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. An accused acquitted for lack of evidence is prosecuted again for the same offence because new evidence is found, and convicted. Legal?

Asked: 2018 · Sep 2024

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Does new evidence allow a second trial after acquittal?
Law
Art. 20(2); S.337(1) BNSS: a person acquitted by a competent court shall not be tried again for the same offence while the acquittal stands. Remedy against a wrong acquittal is an appeal (S.419), not a fresh trial.
Apply
The acquittal by a competent court stands; new evidence is not an exception under S.337.
Conclusion
Illegal: the second trial and conviction are barred by S.337 (autrefois acquit); the State could only appeal against the acquittal.

Problem 2. R was convicted of grievous hurt to K. Later K died. Can R be tried for culpable homicide?

Asked: Nov 2022

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Does a later death allow a fresh trial?
Law
S.337 BNSS: a person convicted of an act causing consequences which together with the act make a different offence may be tried again for that offence if the consequences had not happened, or were not known to the court, at the time of the conviction (illustration (a)).
Apply
K's death happened after R's conviction for grievous hurt; it is a new consequence.
Conclusion
Yes, R can be tried for culpable homicide (BNS 105); it is an exception to double jeopardy.

Problem 3. A was convicted by the Court of Session of culpable homicide of B. Can he be tried again on the same facts for murder? / A, B, C convicted by a Magistrate of robbing D: can they be tried for dacoity?

Asked: 2019 · Aug 2023

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
When is a second trial for a graver offence on the same facts barred?
Law
S.337(1): no second trial for an offence for which a different charge could have been made at the first trial. S.337(4): a later trial is allowed if the first court was not competent to try the graver offence.
Apply
The Court of Session was competent to try murder, so a second trial for murder is barred. The Magistrate was not competent to try dacoity (a Sessions offence), so a trial for dacoity on the same facts is allowed.
Conclusion
Murder after culpable homicide: barred. Dacoity after a Magistrate's robbery conviction: allowed (the illustrations to the section say exactly this).

Problem 4. An undertrial prisoner has been in jail for three years without trial. Remedy under the BNSS?

Asked: Aug 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
What relief is available for prolonged pre-trial detention?
Law
S.479: release on personal bond after half the maximum sentence for the offence (one-third for a first-time offender), except offences punishable with death or life; S.480(6): bail if a Magistrate trial is not concluded within 60 days of the first date for evidence; S.187(3): default bail; Art. 21 speedy trial Hussainara Khatoon.
Apply
If three years is half (or one-third) of the maximum for his offence, he must be released; otherwise the delay is itself a strong ground for bail and for directions to expedite the trial.
Conclusion
Apply for release under S.479 (or bail under S.480 / S.483 citing Art. 21); if denied, approach the High Court for bail and expeditious trial, or habeas corpus.

📖 Story to quote: Hussainara Khatoon

Problem 5. A was convicted of rape and murder of a 12-year-old girl and sentenced to death (confirmed by the High Court). His appeal took 20–25 years to reach the Supreme Court. Can the sentence be commuted for delay?

Asked: Nov 2020 · Aug 2023

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Does delay in the judicial process entitle a death convict to commutation?
Law
Rape of a girl under 12: BNS 65(2) (minimum 20 years, up to life or death). Triveniben v. State of Gujarat (1989): delay during judicial proceedings is not counted; only delay after the final judgment, in disposing of mercy petitions, can be a ground. Shatrughan Chauhan v. Union of India (2014): unexplained, inordinate delay in deciding mercy petitions can justify commutation to life.
Apply
The delay was in the appeal (judicial process), so it is not by itself a ground; but if there is later unexplained delay in deciding his mercy petition, he can seek commutation.
Conclusion
No commutation merely for delay in the appeal; only unexplained delay in deciding a mercy petition after the final judgment can be a ground.

Problem 6. Four persons conspire in a hotel; one is caught with an unlicensed gun. What offence, how to frame the charge, which court?

Asked: Sep 2021 (also 2017: four persons charged with murder and robbery say they meant only to loot)

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
How are joint offenders charged?
Law
Criminal conspiracy BNS 61(2); Arms Act S.25 for the gun. S.246: persons accused of the same offence in the same transaction, or of conspiracy, may be charged and tried together. S.243: several offences in one transaction tried together. Robbery BNS 309; murder BNS 103 with common intention BNS 3(5).
Apply
All four are charged jointly with conspiracy; the gun-holder also under the Arms Act. Where robbery led to a killing, charge all four with robbery and with murder read with BNS 3(5); whether the common intention covered killing is decided on evidence.
Conclusion
Frame a joint charge under S.246 (conspiracy; robbery and murder with common intention), with a separate Arms Act charge for the gun-holder; the Court of Session tries it where any charge is a Sessions offence.
