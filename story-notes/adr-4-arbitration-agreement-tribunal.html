Arbitration Agreement, Types of Arbitration and the Arbitral Tribunal

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Every arbitration is born from a promise made before the quarrel: a clause in the contract saying "any dispute shall be referred to arbitration". That clause is the arbitration agreement. Without it, nobody can be dragged into arbitration; with it, a party who runs to a civil court is sent back to arbitration (S.8).

Next comes the arbitral tribunal: a private judge (or three) chosen by the parties. Because the parties hand over their dispute to someone they picked, the law insists on one thing above all: the arbitrator must be independent, impartial and fair to both sides (S.12, S.18). A clause that lets one party alone pick the judge is no longer allowed.

Chapter 1 · asked 9× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Arbitration agreement: meaning, essentials, form and significance

📖 The story: Jagdish Chander v. Ramesh Chander

(2007) 5 SCC 719

Partners ran a business under a partnership deed. Its dispute clause said that disputes would be mutually decided by the partners, or would be referred to arbitration if the parties so determined.

When disputes arose, one partner asked the court to appoint an arbitrator under S.11. The other said there was no arbitration agreement at all.

❓ Does a clause saying disputes "shall be referred for arbitration if the parties so determine" amount to an arbitration agreement?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Meaning (S.7(1)). An agreement by the parties to submit to arbitration all or certain disputes which have arisen or may arise between them in respect of a defined legal relationship, whether contractual or not. It may be an arbitration clause in a contract or a separate agreement (S.7(2)).

Essentials. (1) A present or future dispute; (2) a defined legal relationship; (3) a clear intention to refer to arbitration and be bound by the decision; (4) in writing; (5) parties competent to contract; (6) the dispute is arbitrable. Words like "may be referred to arbitration if the parties so agree" show no binding intention Jagdish Chander.

Written form (S.7(3), (4), (5)). It must be in writing: a document signed by the parties; an exchange of letters, telex, telegrams or electronic communications (including e-mail) that records it; an exchange of statements of claim and defence in which one alleges it and the other does not deny it; or a reference in a contract to a document containing an arbitration clause, if the reference makes it part of the contract.

Separability (S.16(1)). The arbitration clause is treated as an agreement independent of the other terms of the contract; a finding that the contract is null and void does not by itself invalidate the clause. The tribunal can rule on its own jurisdiction (competence-competence).

Reference by courts (S.8). If a party files a suit on a matter covered by an arbitration agreement, the court shall refer the parties to arbitration if the other party applies no later than its first statement on the substance, unless it finds that prima facie no valid agreement exists. Courts also give interim protection (S.9) and appoint arbitrators (S.11).

💡 Example: a software contract says "All disputes shall be finally settled by arbitration in Hyderabad". When the client sues for damages in a civil court, the vendor applies under S.8 and the court must send the dispute to arbitration.

Arbitrability. Not every dispute can be arbitrated. The Supreme Court's four-fold test excludes disputes that are actions in rem, affect third-party rights, involve sovereign or public functions, or are made non-arbitrable by statute: e.g. criminal offences, matrimonial status, guardianship, insolvency, testamentary matters, tenancies under rent control laws Vidya Drolia. An unstamped agreement is not void; stamp objections are for the tribunal (In re Interplay between Arbitration Agreements and the Stamp Act, 7 judges, 2023).

Significance. It is the foundation of arbitration: it gives the tribunal its power, fixes the scope of disputes, the number of arbitrators, the seat and the rules. It binds legal representatives (S.40) and makes the award enforceable.

🏛️ How the story ended

No. An arbitration agreement must show a clear intention to refer disputes to a private tribunal and to be bound by its decision.

A clause that requires a further consent or agreement before going to arbitration is only an agreement to agree in future; it is not an arbitration agreement under S.7.

The Court listed the attributes: intention to arbitrate, binding decision, an impartial tribunal, and a fair hearing.

Why it matters:

  • Words like 'may', 'if the parties agree' or 'subject to consent' are danger signals.
  • Use 'shall be referred to arbitration' and 'final and binding' in drafting.
  • The court will not create an arbitration agreement that the parties did not make.

📚 More stories on this topic

Vidya Drolia v. Durga Trading Corporation: A landlord-tenant dispute under a lease raised the question whether such disputes can be arbitrated at all. Held: The Court laid down a four-fold test for non-arbitrability and held that landlord-tenant disputes under the Transfer of Property Act are arbitrable (those under rent control laws are not). 🪝 Four doors closed, the rest open

Chapter 2 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Types of arbitration: ad hoc, institutional, statutory, domestic, international and fast-track

⚖️ The law behind the story

The law, step by step:

Ad hoc arbitration. Conducted by the parties and the arbitrators themselves, without any institution; the parties fix the procedure and fees. Flexible and cheap, but may suffer delays and disputes about appointment (then S.11 applies).

Institutional arbitration. Administered by an arbitral institution under its own rules: e.g. Indian Council of Arbitration, Mumbai Centre for International Arbitration, Delhi International Arbitration Centre, ICC, SIAC. The institution appoints arbitrators, fixes fees and supervises timelines. More predictable; costlier.

Statutory arbitration. Imposed by a statute, whether or not the parties agree: e.g. the arbitrator appointed by the Central Government for compensation under the National Highways Act, 1956 (S.3G), disputes under co-operative societies laws, or the Electricity Act, 2003. Part I of the 1996 Act applies so far as it is consistent with that statute (S.2(4)).

Domestic arbitration. Arbitration seated in India between parties who are not foreign (not an international commercial arbitration). Governed by Part I; the award can be set aside under S.34, including for patent illegality (S.34(2A)).

International commercial arbitration (S.2(1)(f)). A commercial dispute where at least one party is foreign: an individual habitually resident abroad, a body corporate incorporated abroad, an association managed abroad, or a foreign government. If seated in India, Part I applies (but patent illegality is not a ground to set aside); if seated abroad, Part II governs enforcement of the award in India.

💡 Example: a Hyderabad hospital and a German equipment maker agree to ICC arbitration in Singapore: this is institutional and international commercial arbitration, seated abroad, so the award will be enforced in India as a foreign award under Part II.

Fast-track and other forms. Fast-track arbitration (S.29B): on written pleadings without oral hearing (unless needed), award within six months. Documents-only arbitration; online arbitration; court-referred arbitration under S.89 CPC (with consent).

Chapter 3 · asked 11× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Arbitral tribunal: appointment, challenge, the arbitrator's relationship with the parties, natural justice and court intervention

📖 The story: Central Organisation for Railway Electrification v. ECI SPIC SMO MCML (JV)

2024 INSC 857 · decided 8 Nov 2024

A Railways construction contract had an arbitration clause under which the Railways prepared a list of its retired officers. The contractor had to suggest names from that list, and the Railways' General Manager chose the arbitrators.

A smaller bench had earlier upheld this kind of clause. Doubts arose, and the question was referred to a Constitution Bench.

❓ Is a clause valid if it lets one party appoint, or control the appointment of, the arbitrators?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Composition (S.10). The parties are free to fix the number of arbitrators, but it shall not be an even number; if they fail, there is a sole arbitrator.

Appointment (S.11). The parties may agree on a procedure. Failing that, in a three-member tribunal each party appoints one and the two appoint the third (presiding) arbitrator within 30 days. If a party or the two arbitrators fail, on application the Supreme Court (international) or the High Court (domestic), or an institution designated by it, appoints. The court only examines whether an arbitration agreement exists (S.11(6A)).

Independence and challenge (S.12, S.13). A proposed arbitrator must disclose in writing any circumstances likely to give justifiable doubts about his independence or impartiality (Fifth and Sixth Schedules). A person with a relationship listed in the Seventh Schedule (e.g. an employee or adviser of a party) is ineligible unless the parties expressly waive it in writing after the dispute arises (S.12(5)). Challenge within 15 days to the tribunal; if rejected, the tribunal continues and the point can be raised under S.34. A party with an interest in the dispute cannot unilaterally appoint a sole arbitrator, nor force the other side to choose from its own panel CORE (Railways).

Natural justice (S.18). "The parties shall be treated with equality and each party shall be given a full opportunity to present his case." This is audi alteram partem (hear the other side) and nemo judex in causa sua (no one judges his own cause) in statutory form. Also: notice of hearings and documents (S.24), the tribunal not bound by CPC or Evidence Act but by fairness (S.19), a reasoned award (S.31(3)). Breach is a ground to set aside the award (S.34(2)(a)(iii) and public policy).

Duties and relationship with parties. The arbitrator is not the agent of the party who appointed him; he is a quasi-judicial decision-maker owing duties to both: disclosure, impartiality, confidentiality (S.42A), diligence and finishing within time (S.29A: award within 12 months of completion of pleadings, extendable by 6 months by consent), deciding only within the reference, and giving reasons. Fees follow the agreement or the Fourth Schedule. His mandate ends if he cannot act or withdraws (S.14, S.15).

Court intervention (S.5). Notwithstanding any other law, no judicial authority shall intervene except where Part I so provides: reference (S.8), interim measures (S.9), appointment (S.11), termination of mandate (S.14), assistance in taking evidence (S.27), setting aside (S.34), enforcement (S.36) and appeals (S.37). This "minimal intervention" principle comes from the UNCITRAL Model Law.

🏛️ How the story ended

No. The principle of equal treatment in S.18 applies at every stage, including the appointment of arbitrators. A party with an interest in the dispute cannot unilaterally appoint a sole arbitrator, and a clause forcing the other party to choose from a panel curated by one side is invalid.

In public-private contracts, such clauses are also arbitrary under Art. 14. The independence rules in S.12(5) can be waived only by an express written agreement after the dispute arises.

To protect pending matters, the ruling on three-member tribunals applies only to appointments made after the judgment.

Why it matters:

  • Builds on TRF Ltd. (2017) and Perkins Eastman (2019).
  • Impartiality must exist and must appear to exist.
  • Government contracts must be redrafted to give both sides equal say.
