Jurisdiction and Summons

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Before Ravi can win, he must knock on the right door. Three questions decide which door: what is the dispute (civil or not, barred or not: S.9), how much is it worth (pecuniary: S.15), and where did it happen or where does the defendant live (territorial: S.16–20).

Once he is inside the right court, the court must tell Somu he has been sued. That message is the summons: delivered by hand, by post, by courier or email, pasted on the door, or published in a newspaper if Somu keeps running away. No one can be condemned unheard (audi alteram partem).

Chapter 1 · asked 8× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Jurisdiction of civil courts: S.9 "suits of a civil nature", kinds of jurisdiction, exclusion

📖 The story: Kiran Singh v. Chaman Paswan

AIR 1954 SC 340 · decided 14 Apr 1954

The plaintiffs sued for possession of about 12.5 acres of land in Bihar and for mesne profits. They valued the suit at ₹2,950. The Subordinate Judge at Monghyr dismissed it, and the District Judge dismissed their appeal.

In second appeal before the Patna High Court, the correct value was found to be about ₹9,980. At that value, the first appeal should have gone to the High Court, not the District Court.

The plaintiffs, who had themselves put the low value, now argued that the District Court's decree was a nullity, or at least that they were prejudiced because a lower court heard their appeal on facts.

❓ Is a decree passed by a court that had jurisdiction only because of a wrong valuation a nullity?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

Meaning. Jurisdiction is the authority of a court to hear and decide a case. It depends on the subject matter, the value and the place. A decision without jurisdiction is a nullity Kiran Singh.

S.9. Civil courts have jurisdiction to try all suits of a civil nature, except those expressly or impliedly barred. "Civil nature" means a dispute about private rights (property, office, contract, status). Explanation I: a suit about a right to property or to an office is civil even if it turns on religious rites or ceremonies. Explanation II: it does not matter whether fees attach to the office or whether it is tied to a place.

Bars. Express bar: a statute says civil courts shall not entertain the matter (e.g. matters given to Rent Controllers, Revenue Courts, Debts Recovery Tribunals). Implied bar: the statute creates a new right with its own complete remedy, or public policy forbids suits (e.g. a suit over political or sovereign acts).

Ganga Bai v. Vijay Kumar (1974): "There is an inherent right in every person to bring a suit of a civil nature and, unless the suit is barred by statute, one may, at one's peril, bring a suit of one's choice." Civil courts can also decide incidental questions needed to decide the suit, unless a statute reserves that question for another authority.

Kinds of jurisdiction. (1) Subject-matter (e.g. a Small Causes Court cannot try a partition suit); (2) pecuniary (value: S.6, S.15); (3) territorial (place: S.16–20); (4) original and appellate; (5) exclusive and concurrent; (6) general and special (e.g. Family Court, Commercial Court).

Pecuniary jurisdiction. S.6: no court tries a suit above its pecuniary limits. S.15: every suit is filed in the court of the lowest grade competent to try it, to reduce the load on higher courts. Value is fixed by the plaintiff in the plaint, subject to the Court-fees and Suits Valuation laws; the limits of the Junior and Senior Civil Judges are fixed by the State Civil Courts Act. Subordination: District Court above Senior Civil Judge above Junior Civil Judge; all subordinate to the High Court (S.3).

Conclusion. The door of the civil court is open to every civil claim; it closes only where the law clearly says so.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ended

No. The appeal was dismissed with costs.

Under S.11 of the Suits Valuation Act, an objection based on over- or under-valuation cannot be entertained by an appellate court unless the error prejudicially affected the decision on merits. A mere change of forum is not prejudice, and the plaintiffs could not complain of a forum they themselves chose.

Why it matters:

  • "A decree passed by a Court without jurisdiction is a nullity": its invalidity can be set up wherever it is sought to be enforced, even in execution or collateral proceedings.
  • But defects of territorial and pecuniary jurisdiction are treated as technical: S.21 CPC and S.11 Suits Valuation Act require the objection to be taken early and require prejudice or failure of justice.
  • Lack of inherent (subject-matter) jurisdiction cannot be cured by consent or waiver.

📚 More stories on this topic

Dhulabhai v. State of Madhya Pradesh: Tobacco dealers sued for refund of a sales tax that taxed tobacco from other States but not local tobacco; the State said the Act barred civil suits. Held: Suits maintainable. Exclusion of civil courts is not readily inferred; an unconstitutional levy or no adequate remedy keeps the suit alive. 🪝 The civil court door is hard to lock

  • Part CA party objects to place or value only in appeal: rejected unless taken early and prejudice shown.

Chapter 2 · asked 10× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Place of suing (S.15–21) and transfer of suits (S.22–25)

📖 The story: Patel Roadways Ltd. v. Prasad Trading Company

(1991) 4 SCC 270 · decided 6 Aug 1991

Patel Roadways, a transport company, had its principal office in Bombay and branch offices elsewhere.

In one case, cardamom was handed over at its Bodinayakanur (Tamil Nadu) branch for delivery to Delhi, and was destroyed by fire in a Delhi godown. The owner sued in the court at Periakulam. In another, pesticides booked at the Madras branch reached New Delhi damaged, and the owner sued in the City Civil Court, Madras.

Patel Roadways argued that, under the Explanation to S.20 and a contract clause, only the Bombay courts could try these suits.

❓ Where can a corporation with a head office and branch offices be sued for a cause of action arising at a branch? Can a contract clause choose Bombay?

⚖️ The law behind the story

To answer that question, the court applied this law. Read it with the story in mind:

S.16: immovable property. Suits for recovery of immovable property, partition, mortgage (foreclosure, sale, redemption), any right in it, compensation for wrong to it, or recovery of movables under attachment: filed where the property is situated. Proviso: if the relief can be obtained through the defendant's personal obedience, also where he resides or works.

S.17: property in several courts' limits. Suit may be filed in any court within whose limits any portion of the property lies, if the whole claim is within its pecuniary limits. S.18: where the local limits are uncertain, a court may record that and proceed.

S.19: wrongs to person or movables. The plaintiff may choose: where the wrong was done, or where the defendant resides, carries on business or works for gain. Illustration: A of Delhi beats B in Calcutta, or publishes in Calcutta statements defamatory of B: B may sue in Calcutta or in Delhi.

S.20: all other suits. Where the defendant (or each of several defendants) resides, carries on business or works for gain; or any of them, with leave of the court or the others' acquiescence; or where the cause of action wholly or partly arises. Explanation: a corporation carries on business at its principal office, or, for a cause of action arising where it has a subordinate office, at that place.

S.21: objections. An objection to the place of suing or to pecuniary limits is allowed in appeal or revision only if it was taken in the trial court at the earliest opportunity and there was a consequent failure of justice. S.21A: no separate suit to set aside a decree on such an objection. (Lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is different: it makes the decree a nullity.)

Transfer. S.22: where a suit may be filed in two or more courts and is filed in one, the defendant may apply, at the earliest, to transfer it to another. S.23: to which court the application goes. S.24: the High Court or District Court may, on application or on its own, transfer or withdraw any suit, appeal or proceeding pending in a subordinate court. S.25: the Supreme Court may transfer a suit from a court in one State to a court in another, if expedient for the ends of justice.

Conclusion. Property cases go where the property is; wrongs, where they happened or where the wrongdoer lives; everything else, where the defendant lives or the cause of action arose.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ended

The Supreme Court dismissed the company's appeals: the courts at the branch locations had jurisdiction, and the Bombay courts did not.

The second part of the Explanation provides "an alternative locus for the corporation's place of business, not an additional one". The contract clause was of no effect, because parties cannot confer jurisdiction on a court which has none.

Why it matters:

  • A corporation is deemed to carry on business at its principal office, or, for a cause of action arising at a place where it has a subordinate office, at that place.
  • This avoids forcing ordinary customers to travel to a distant head office to sue.
  • Parties may agree to choose one of several courts that already have jurisdiction, but cannot create jurisdiction where none exists.

Chapter 3 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Summons and modes of service (S.27–32, O.V) · Persons exempt from appearance (S.133)

📖 The story: The court calls you

A shop owner receives a sealed paper from the court telling him to appear on 15 July and answer a claim for ₹3 lakh, bringing his account books.

❓ What does the law say?

⚖️ The law behind the story

Here is the law that decides it:

Meaning. A summons is the court's written order calling the defendant to appear and answer the claim (S.27, O.V r.1), signed by the judge and sealed. It may require him to appear for settlement of issues or for final disposal (r.5), and to bring the documents he relies on (r.7). It must be served within 30 days of the institution of the suit.

Modes of service. (1) Personal service by the court's officer: on the defendant himself or his authorised agent (r.10–12), or an adult family member residing with him if he is absent and has no agent (r.15). (2) By the court through registered post, speed post, courier, fax or email (r.9), or by the plaintiff himself (r.9A). (3) Affixing on the outer door of his house if he refuses or cannot be found (r.17), with the serving officer's report verified (r.19). (4) Substituted service (r.20): where he keeps out of the way, the court orders a copy affixed in the court-house and at his house, or publication in a local daily newspaper; it is as good as personal service.

Special cases. r.21: defendant in another court's area: sent to that court. r.24: prisoner: through the jail officer. r.25: outside India with no agent: by post or courier to him abroad. r.27: public officer: sent to the head of his office. r.28: soldiers and sailors: through the commanding officer. Companies: on the secretary, director or principal officer (O.XXIX r.2). Firms: on any partner or the manager (O.XXX r.3).

Exemptions (S.133). Exempt from personal appearance: the President, Vice-President, Speaker of the Lok Sabha, Union Ministers, Supreme Court and High Court judges, Governors and Administrators, Speakers and Chairmen of State legislatures, State Ministers, and persons under S.87B. If needed as witnesses they are examined on commission (S.133(2), O.XXVI). Diplomatic agents are not obliged to give evidence as witnesses at all (Art. 31(2), Vienna Convention, given force by the Diplomatic Relations (Vienna Convention) Act, 1972).

💡 Example: the bailiff goes to Somu's house thrice; Somu locks it each time. The bailiff affixes the summons on the door and reports (r.17). The court, satisfied that Somu is avoiding service, orders publication in a Telugu daily (r.20). If Somu still stays away, the court proceeds ex parte (O.IX r.6).

Conclusion. Service is about fair notice: the Code keeps adding modes so that no defendant can defeat a suit by hiding.

Agold-edged lines are your Part A answer.

🏛️ How the story ends

A summons (S.27, O.V) is the court's signed and sealed order to appear and answer; it may ask him to bring documents (r.7). Served within 30 days of the suit.

He must appear (or send an advocate) with the account books, or risk an ex parte decree.

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. A transport company has its head office in Delhi and branches in Chennai, Jaipur and Mumbai. Sam's dispute arose from a transaction at the Chennai office, but he sues at Jaipur. How will the court decide?

Asked: May 2023

Issue
Where can a corporation be sued for a cause of action that arose at a branch?
Law
S.20(c): where the cause of action arises. S.20 Explanation: a corporation carries on business at its principal office, or, for a cause of action arising at a place with a subordinate office, at that place Patel Roadways.
Apply
The cause of action arose at Chennai, where the company has a branch. So Chennai is the place; Jaipur has neither the cause of action nor the deemed place of business for this claim.
Conclusion
The Jaipur court has no territorial jurisdiction; it will return the plaint under O.VII r.10 for presentation at Chennai.

📖 Story to quote: Patel Roadways

Problem 2. Raju wants a partition of joint family property: houses in Hyderabad and lands in Khammam. Where can he sue?

Asked: Nov 2022

Issue
Which court has jurisdiction when immovable property lies in two districts?
Law
S.16(b): partition suits where the property is situated. S.17: if the property is in the limits of different courts, in any court within whose limits any portion lies, provided the whole claim is within its pecuniary limits.
Apply
Portions of the property lie in Hyderabad and in Khammam.
Conclusion
Raju may sue either in Hyderabad or in Khammam (S.17), in the court of the lowest grade competent for the whole value (S.15).

Problem 3. X, living in Warangal, publishes in Hyderabad a defamatory statement about Y of Nizamabad. Where can Y sue for damages?

Asked: Aug 2021

Issue
Place of suing for compensation for a wrong to the person.
Law
S.19: where the wrong was done, or where the defendant resides, carries on business or works for gain; the plaintiff may choose. Illustration (b) to S.19.
Apply
The wrong (publication) was done in Hyderabad; X resides in Warangal. Y's own residence (Nizamabad) is not a ground.
Conclusion
Y may sue at Hyderabad or at Warangal, not at Nizamabad.

Problem 4. A sues B for ₹5,00,000 on a negotiable instrument in the Junior Civil Judge's court. Maintainable?

Asked: 2016

Issue
Is the suit within the pecuniary jurisdiction of the Junior Civil Judge?
Law
S.6 and S.15: lowest court competent; pecuniary limits are fixed by the State Civil Courts Act. O.VII r.10: plaint returned if filed in the wrong court.
Apply
Under the Telangana Civil Courts Act, 1972 as amended, the Junior Civil Judge's pecuniary limit has been raised over the years and is now well above ₹5 lakh. So the suit is within limits. If a claim exceeds the limit on the date of filing, the plaint is returned.
Conclusion
Maintainable, as ₹5 lakh is within the Junior Civil Judge's present pecuniary limit; always state the rule (S.15 + State Act) and the consequence (return of plaint) for full marks.

Problem 5. X wants the President of India (or a diplomatic agent) as a witness in a civil suit. Can the court summon him?

Asked: 2020 · Nov 2020

Issue
Can high constitutional functionaries and diplomats be compelled to appear?
Law
S.133(1): the President and others are exempt from personal appearance; S.133(2): if they claim the exemption, they are examined on commission (O.XXVI r.1). Diplomatic agents: Art. 31(2) Vienna Convention (Diplomatic Relations (Vienna Convention) Act, 1972): not obliged to give evidence.
Apply
The President cannot be compelled to come to court; his evidence, if relevant, is taken by commission (costs on the person claiming the exemption, unless the party seeking the evidence pays). A diplomat may testify only voluntarily.
Conclusion
No personal summons: the President is examined on commission under S.133; a diplomatic agent cannot be compelled at all.

Problem 6. A party keeps locking his house whenever the bailiff comes with summons. How can it be served?

Asked: 2015

Issue
How to serve a defendant who evades service.
Law
O.V r.17: affixing on the outer door when he cannot be found or refuses. r.19: court examines the serving officer and declares service. r.20: substituted service by affixing in the court-house and at his house, or newspaper publication; as effective as personal service.
Apply
The defendant is deliberately avoiding service, so ordinary service fails.
Conclusion
The summons can be affixed on his door (r.17) and the court can order substituted service (r.20); then, if he stays away, it proceeds ex parte.

Problem 7. In a suit for specific performance of an agreement to sell agricultural land, can the civil court decide the incidental issue "whether B is a farmer"?

Asked: 2013 (also 2018: maintenance suit in a Junior Civil Judge's court)

Issue
Can a civil court decide an incidental question that a special law may govern?
Law
S.9: civil courts try all civil suits unless expressly or impliedly barred; they may decide incidental questions needed to decide the suit Dhulabhai. If a statute gives that specific question exclusively to another authority, the civil court must leave it to that authority.
Apply
Whether B is a farmer is incidental to whether the sale can be enforced. Unless the land law makes that question exclusively triable by a revenue authority, the civil court decides it. (Similarly, a maintenance suit goes to the Family Court where one is set up for the area: Family Courts Act, S.7–8; elsewhere, to the civil court within its limits.)
Conclusion
Yes, the civil court can decide it as an incidental issue under S.9, unless a special law expressly reserves that question for another forum.

📖 Story to quote: Dhulabhai

Problem 8. A suit was filed in a court without territorial jurisdiction. The defendant took no objection at trial, lost, and now objects in appeal. Will he succeed?

Asked: (Part A "Territorial jurisdiction": 2016 · Nov 2022 · Sep 2024)

Issue
Can an objection to place of suing be raised for the first time in appeal?
Law
S.21(1): no objection as to the place of suing is allowed by an appellate or revisional court unless it was taken in the court of first instance at the earliest possible opportunity, and there has been a consequent failure of justice Kiran Singh.
Apply
He did not object at trial and shows no failure of justice; territorial defects are not "inherent".
Conclusion
No. The objection is barred under S.21; the decree stands.

📖 Story to quote: Kiran Singh

Problem 9. A suit is pending in Hyderabad, but the defendant says he cannot get a fair trial there and wants it moved to a court in another State. Who can transfer it?

Asked: (Part A "Transfer of Suits": Aug 2025; B: Nov 2022)

Issue
Which authority can transfer a civil suit to another State?
Law
S.24: the High Court or District Court can transfer within its own jurisdiction. S.25: only the Supreme Court can transfer a suit from a court in one State to a court in another State, if expedient for the ends of justice, after hearing the parties.
Apply
The transfer sought is inter-State.
Conclusion
Only the Supreme Court, on an application under S.25; within Telangana, the High Court or District Court under S.24.
