Bail

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

Bail is a promise in exchange for freedom. The accused says, "Let me go home; I will come back whenever the court calls me", and signs a bond (with or without sureties) to back that promise. Krishna Iyer J. put the whole subject in four words: "bail, not jail". A person is presumed innocent until proved guilty, so keeping him in prison before trial must be the exception.

Think of three doors. For a bailable offence the door is open as of right. For a non-bailable offence the door opens at the court's discretion. And anticipatory bail is a key you get before anyone tries to lock you in.

Chapter 1 · asked 10× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Bail: meaning, bailable and non-bailable offences, kinds of bail, bail bonds

⚡ 30-second version · Satender Kumar Antil

📍Never arrested during investigation, a man was refused bail and sent a warrant once the charge sheet was filed.
❓Must a person who was not arrested during investigation be taken into custody when the charge sheet is filed?
🏛️Bail is the rule, jail the exception; a charge sheet does not require arrest; courts must follow the arrest safeguards strictly.

🪝 Charge sheet is not a jail ticket

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI · (2022) 10 SCC 51 · decided 11 Jul 2022

Satender Kumar Antil was accused of demanding bribes, under the Prevention of Corruption Act and criminal conspiracy. The CBI investigated him but never arrested him.

After the charge sheet was filed, the court summoned him. When he appeared and asked for bail, it was refused and a warrant for his arrest was issued.

He went to the Supreme Court, arguing that courts and police routinely treat the filing of a charge sheet as a reason to arrest, even when no arrest was needed during investigation.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

For the first time, the BNSS defines bail (S.2): the release of a person accused or suspected of an offence from custody, on conditions imposed by an officer or court, on his executing a bond or bail bond. A bail bond is an undertaking with sureties; a bond is a personal bond without sureties.

Bailable v. non-bailable

A bailable offence is one shown as bailable in the First Schedule or made bailable by another law; every other offence is non-bailable. Bailable offences are generally less serious (e.g. simple hurt, BNS 115(2)); non-bailable ones are serious (e.g. murder, BNS 103; rape, BNS 64).

Bail in bailable offences (S.478)

The person shall be released on bail, by the police officer or the court, at any stage. It is a right, not a favour. If he cannot furnish a surety within a week, he is presumed indigent and released on his own bond without sureties.

Bail in non-bailable offences (S.480, S.483)

A Magistrate may release him, but not where there are reasonable grounds to believe he committed an offence punishable with death or life imprisonment, or a cognizable offence with serious previous convictions; even then a child, a woman, or a sick or infirm person may be released. The High Court and Court of Session have wider powers (S.483). Factors: nature and gravity of the charge, evidence, risk of fleeing, tampering with witnesses, antecedents.

Cancellation

A court that granted bail may order re-arrest (S.480(5)); the High Court and Sessions Court may cancel bail (S.483). Grounds: misusing liberty, tampering with evidence, threatening witnesses, committing another offence, absconding.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Kinds of bail
  1. Regular bail after arrest (S.478, S.480, S.483);
  2. interim bail while a bail application is pending;
  3. anticipatory bail before arrest (S.482);
  4. default (statutory) bail when the charge sheet is not filed within 60 or 90 days (S.187(3));
  5. bail pending appeal (S.430);
  6. release of undertrials after half (first-time offenders: one-third) of the maximum sentence (S.479).
Bonds (S.484–496)

The amount must be fixed with regard to the circumstances and shall not be excessive (S.484); the bond binds the accused to attend court; sureties declare their solvency (S.486); if the bond is forfeited, the court may recover the penalty (S.491); for a child, a bond by sureties alone may be accepted (S.494).

🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. If the accused was not arrested during investigation and cooperated, there is no need to arrest him when the trial begins.

The Court repeated that bail is the rule and jail the exception, and issued guidelines dividing offences into categories so that bail applications are decided quickly and uniformly.

  • Arrest is a serious matter; police must follow the notice-of-appearance procedure before arresting (now S.35(3) BNSS).
  • A charge sheet does not by itself justify custody.
  • Long custody with a delayed trial is a strong ground for bail.
  • Economic offences are not a single class to deny bail automatically.

Chapter 2 · asked 6× 🔥🔥 Important

Anticipatory bail (S.482): meaning, conditions, principles

⚡ 30-second version · Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia

📍A Punjab Minister accused of corruption sought anticipatory bail; the High Court laid down rigid restrictions on the power.
❓Can courts read rigid limitations into the power to grant anticipatory bail?
🏛️The restrictions were struck down: anticipatory bail is a wide discretion to protect liberty, decided case by case.

🪝 Liberty is not boxed in

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab · (1980) 2 SCC 565 · AIR 1980 SC 1632 · decided 9 Apr 1980

Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia, then a Minister in the Punjab Government, faced allegations of political corruption and feared arrest.

He and others applied for anticipatory bail. A Full Bench of the Punjab and Haryana High Court laid down strict propositions: for example, that the power is extraordinary, should be used only in exceptional cases, and not where a prima facie case is made out.

The matter went to a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

A direction that, if the person is arrested, he shall be released on bail. It is granted before arrest to a person who has reason to believe he may be arrested for a non-bailable offence. Purpose: no one should be humiliated or jailed because of a false or motivated accusation.

Who grants (S.482(1))

The High Court or the Court of Session only (not a Magistrate).

Conditions (S.482(2))

The court may impose conditions, including that he shall make himself available for interrogation by the police as and when required; shall not induce, threaten or promise anyone acquainted with the facts; shall not leave India without permission; and other conditions as under S.480(3). If he is later arrested without warrant, he is released on bail (S.482(3)).

Duration

Anticipatory bail is not limited to a fixed period and can continue till the end of the trial, unless the court limits it for special reasons Sushila Aggarwal.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Exclusions

Not available for rape of a woman under 16 (BNS 65) or gang rape of a woman under 18 (BNS 70(2)) (S.482(4)). Special laws may also bar it (e.g. the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, S.18).

Note on the BNSS change

S.482 no longer lists the old factors (gravity of accusation, antecedents, risk of fleeing) in the text; courts still consider them as part of judicial discretion. Anticipatory bail is not a blanket protection for future offences; it is offence-specific.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

No. The Supreme Court rejected the High Court's restrictive propositions.

The power is wide and must be exercised judicially, according to the facts of each case; the legislature deliberately left it to the discretion of the superior courts.

  • Anticipatory bail protects personal liberty under Article 21; restrictions not in the statute should not be read into it.
  • The applicant must show reasonable grounds for his belief that he may be arrested; vague fear is not enough.
  • The court may impose conditions, and the order should not be a blanket order for all future offences.

📚 More stories on this topic

Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi): Conflicting judgments asked whether anticipatory bail must end after a fixed period or once the court summons the accused. Held: It need not be time-bound; it can continue till the end of the trial, unless the court limits it for special reasons. 🪝 No expiry date on liberty

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Problem 1. A was arrested for a bailable offence and refused bail. What is his remedy under the BNSS?

Asked: Feb 2025 · Aug 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can bail be refused in a bailable offence, and what can A do?
Law
S.478: in a bailable offence the person shall be released on bail; if he cannot give a surety within a week, on his own bond. S.47(2): he must be told of his right to bail. S.483: the High Court and Sessions Court can direct release. Art. 21, Constitution.
Apply
Bail in a bailable offence is a right, not a discretion. Refusal by the police or Magistrate is illegal (unless he earlier broke bail conditions: S.478(2)).
Conclusion
A must be released at once; he can apply to the Magistrate under S.478, then to the Sessions Court or High Court under S.483, and if detained illegally, file habeas corpus.

Problem 2. A, who is on bail, threatens the witnesses (or commits another offence). What can be done?

Asked: 2013 · Sep 2024

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can bail be cancelled for misuse of liberty?
Law
S.480(5): the court that granted bail may direct re-arrest and commit to custody. S.483: High Court / Sessions Court may cancel. Grounds: interfering with witnesses, tampering with evidence, absconding, committing another offence.
Apply
Threatening witnesses directly interferes with a fair trial. The witnesses or prosecution apply for cancellation; a fresh offence is also a separate case.
Conclusion
The prosecution or witnesses can apply for cancellation of bail; the court will cancel it and send A to custody. The witnesses may also seek protection under the Witness Protection Scheme (S.398).

Problem 3. Laxmi, an undertrial for 6 months, is granted bail but cannot meet the conditions (or a poor man cannot pay the bond). Remedy?

Asked: 2014 · 2015

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Can poverty keep an accused in jail after bail is granted?
Law
S.478(1) proviso: an indigent person (cannot give surety within a week) may be released on his own bond. S.484: bond amount shall not be excessive; the court may reduce it. Art. 21.
Apply
Bail granted but unaffordable is bail denied. She should apply for reduction of the bond amount and release on personal bond.
Conclusion
She should apply to modify the conditions; the court can reduce the amount and release her on a personal bond without sureties.

Problem 4. Ranjith, accused of corruption, was refused bail by the trial court and the higher court. Advise him.

Asked: Feb 2024

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
What further remedies does Ranjith have?
Law
S.483: High Court's special power. Art. 136: Supreme Court. S.187(3): default bail if no charge sheet in 60/90 days. S.479: undertrial release after half (first-time offender: one-third) of maximum. A fresh application lies on changed circumstances Satender Kumar Antil.
Apply
If the High Court refused, he can approach the Supreme Court by special leave; he can also file a fresh application if circumstances change (charge sheet filed, long custody, delay in trial).
Conclusion
Apply to the Supreme Court (Art. 136), or renew the application on changed circumstances, default bail or S.479.

Problem 5. A person arrested on suspicion of terrorism: can he be released on bail?

Asked: 2014 · 2015

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is bail available in grave offences under special laws?
Law
S.480(1): no bail by a Magistrate if there are reasonable grounds to believe the offence is punishable with death or life. Special laws (e.g. UAPA S.43D(5)) add stricter conditions. But if the court finds he is not involved, there are no "reasonable grounds", and he should be released.
Apply
Once the court itself finds he was not involved, the bar does not apply; poverty cannot stop release (S.478 proviso logic, S.484).
Conclusion
Yes, he should be released, if necessary on a personal bond; if no evidence remains, the police should also seek his discharge.
