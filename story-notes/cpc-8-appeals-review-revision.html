Appeals, Review and Revision

Every topic told as a story: the case first, then the law it turned on, then how it ended, then how to write it for marks.

A losing party has four doors, and each opens for a different reason. Appeal: "the judge was wrong; let a higher court re-hear it." Review: "the same judge made an obvious mistake, or I found new evidence; please look again." Revision: "the lower court acted without jurisdiction or illegally; High Court, please supervise." Reference: "I, the lower court, have a doubt about the law; High Court, please guide me."

An appeal is a creature of statute: it exists only where a law gives it. The first appeal re-opens facts and law; the second appeal is allowed only on a substantial question of law; and revision is the High Court's supervisory eye, not another appeal.

Chapter 1 · asked 10× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Appeals: meaning, general rules, kinds (first, second, from orders) · First v. second appeal · Procedure in appeals from original decrees

⚡ 30-second version · Santosh Hazari

📍The trial court decreed a land suit; the first appellate court reversed in cryptic lines; the High Court refused to hear the second appeal.
❓What is the duty of a first appellate court that reverses the trial court, and when does a "substantial question of law" arise for a second appeal?
🏛️Remanded. A reversing first appellate court must give its own reasons; a substantial question of law is debatable, unsettled and decisive.

🪝 Reverse with reasons

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Santosh Hazari v. Purushottam Tiwari · (2001) 3 SCC 179 · decided 8 Feb 2001

In 1983 the plaintiff sued for declaration of title, possession and an injunction over about 1.09 acres in a village in Damoh, Madhya Pradesh, saying the defendant had dispossessed him of part of it in August 1981.

The defendant claimed he had been in possession since 1940–41 and pleaded adverse possession and limitation. The trial court found for the plaintiff and decreed the suit.

The first appellate court agreed the plaintiff owned the land but, in brief reasoning, held that the defendant had been in possession since 1968 and dismissed the suit. The High Court dismissed the second appeal at the threshold, saying only facts were involved.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Meaning

An appeal is the judicial examination by a higher court of a decision of a lower court, to correct errors of fact or law. The CPC does not define it. Rules:

  1. it is a creature of statute, not an inherent right;
  2. it is a vested right from the date of the suit, so later laws cannot take it away unless they say so expressly;
  3. an appeal is a continuation of the suit.
First appeal (S.96, O.XLI)

From every decree passed by a court of original jurisdiction, unless barred; an ex parte decree too (S.96(2)); but not from a consent decree (S.96(3)). It lies on questions of fact and of law; the first appellate court is the final court of facts.

Second appeal (S.100)

To the High Court from a decree passed in appeal by a subordinate court, only if the case involves a substantial question of law. The memorandum states the question; the High Court frames it and hears the appeal on it. No second appeal in money suits up to ₹25,000 (S.102). S.100A: no further (Letters Patent) appeal from a single judge.

Appeals from orders (S.104, O.XLIII r.1)

Only the orders listed are appealable, e.g. returning a plaint (O.VII r.10), refusing to set aside an ex parte decree (O.IX r.13), on temporary injunctions (O.XXXIX r.1, 2, 2A, 4), appointing a receiver (O.XL r.1). S.104(2): no appeal lies from an order passed in appeal under this section. S.105: an error in a non-appealable order can be raised in the appeal against the final decree.

Conclusion

First appeal for facts, second appeal for law, and appeals from orders only where the Code lists them.

Procedure (O.XLI)

r.1: a memorandum of appeal setting out the grounds concisely, accompanied by a copy of the judgment; in an appeal against a money decree, the disputed amount must be deposited or security given (r.1(3)). r.3: rejection or return for amendment. r.5: no automatic stay; stay of execution for sufficient cause and on security. r.11: dismissal at the admission stage after hearing the appellant. r.22: cross-objection by the respondent. r.23–25: remand. r.27: additional evidence only in limited cases. r.31: the appellate judgment states the points, the decision and the reasons. Powers (S.107): decide finally, remand, frame issues and refer them, take additional evidence.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

The Supreme Court allowed the appeal, set aside the High Court's summary dismissal and sent the second appeal back for a fresh hearing, framing a substantial question of law on adverse possession and limitation under Article 65.

A first appellate court that reverses must deal with the trial court's reasons and record its own; a cryptic reversal does not meet that duty.

  • The first appellate court is the final court of facts; its judgment must show it applied its mind to the evidence.
  • When affirming, general agreement may do; when reversing, it must engage with the trial court's reasoning.
  • A question of law is "substantial" if it is debatable, not settled by law or binding precedent, and has a material bearing on the decision.
  • It need not be of general public importance, but must arise from the pleadings and the findings of fact.

Chapter 2 · asked 8× 🔥🔥🔥 Must do

Reference (S.113), Review (S.114) and Revision (S.115): basic aspects and differences

⚡ 30-second version · Shiv Shakti

📍After the 2002 amendment, High Courts dismissed revisions against interim orders (such as injunction orders); the parties appealed.
❓After the 2002 amendment, does a revision lie against an interim order, and does the amendment apply to pending revisions?
🏛️Revision now lies only if a decision in the applicant's favour would finally dispose of the suit; no vested right in revision.

🪝 Revision only for final blows

📖 Read the full story 3 short paragraphs

Shiv Shakti Co-op. Housing Society v. Swaraj Developers · (2003) 6 SCC 659 · decided 17 Apr 2003

The CPC (Amendment) Act, 1999, in force from 1 July 2002, rewrote the proviso to Section 115, the High Court's revision power.

Applying it, High Courts held that revisions against interim orders, such as orders on applications for injunction, were no longer maintainable, because even an order in the applicant's favour would not finally dispose of the suit.

The affected parties argued that the amendment did not apply to revisions already admitted, that revision was a vested right, and that an injunction application is itself an "other proceeding" which a revision could finally dispose of.

⚖️ The law you must know Part A lines

Reference (S.113, O.XLVI)

A subordinate court may state a case and refer a doubt on a question of law to the High Court. It must refer where the case involves the validity of an Act or rule, which it considers invalid but which the High Court or Supreme Court has not yet so declared. The High Court answers; the lower court decides accordingly.

Review (S.114, O.XLVII)

A person aggrieved by a decree or order from which no appeal is allowed, or an appeal is allowed but not preferred, may apply to the same court that passed it on these grounds (O.XLVII r.1):

  1. discovery of new and important evidence which, despite due diligence, was not within his knowledge or could not be produced earlier;
  2. a mistake or error apparent on the face of the record;
  3. any other sufficient reason (analogous to the first two).
Limits of review

It is not an appeal in disguise; an error that needs a long process of reasoning is not "apparent" (Aribam Tuleshwar Sharma v. Aribam Pishak Sharma, 1979). Ordinarily the same judge hears it (r.5). No review of an order rejecting a review (r.9). Limitation: 30 days (Art. 124).

Revision (S.115)

The High Court may call for the record of a case decided by a subordinate court in which no appeal lies, if that court (a) exercised a jurisdiction not vested in it, (b) failed to exercise a jurisdiction so vested, or (c) acted illegally or with material irregularity in exercising it. Proviso (1999, in force 2002): the High Court shall not vary or reverse an order unless, had it been made in the applicant's favour, it would have finally disposed of the suit or proceeding.

Conclusion

Review corrects obvious mistakes, revision corrects jurisdictional errors, reference resolves doubts on law, and appeal corrects everything else.

➕ More for Part B and examples 2 more points
Other points on revision

It is a supervisory power, not an appeal: the High Court does not re-assess facts. S.115(2): no revision where an appeal lies to the High Court or a subordinate court. S.115(3): a revision does not operate as a stay unless the High Court stays the proceedings. Limitation: 90 days (Art. 131). Where revision is barred, Article 227 (supervisory jurisdiction) may still be invoked.

🏛️ Why the court decided so

The Supreme Court dismissed the appeals.

Revision lies only if the order, had it been made in the applicant's favour, would have finally disposed of the suit or other proceeding. The old alternative ground (failure of justice or irreparable injury) was deliberately removed.

There is no vested right in revision; S.115 is a source of the High Court's supervisory power, so pending revisions were also governed by the amendment. Article 227 remains available.

  • Test: would the order, if made in the applicant's favour, end the suit or proceeding?
  • Interim orders (injunctions, receivers, etc.) are outside revision.
  • Revision is a power of superintendence, not a right like an appeal.

Part C workshop · 10 marks each

Solve the problem like a judge

Every Part C problem is a new story with the same law. Spot the issue, state the law, apply it to the facts, and conclude. Quote the matching case story to lift your answer.

Problem 1. Mr. G is aggrieved by an order passed by the District Court in an appeal. Can he prefer any further appeal?

Asked: Aug 2025

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is a further appeal available against a District Court's decision in appeal?
Law
S.100: second appeal to the High Court from a decree passed in appeal, only on a substantial question of law. S.104(2): no appeal lies from an order passed in appeal under S.104. S.115: revision where no appeal lies. Art. 227: supervisory jurisdiction.
Apply
If the District Court's decision in appeal is a decree (an appeal from a decree under S.96), G can file a second appeal in the High Court if a substantial question of law arises. If it is an order passed in an appeal from an order (S.104/O.XLIII), no further appeal lies; G may seek revision (if the S.115 proviso is met) or invoke Article 227.
Conclusion
Second appeal under S.100 if it is an appellate decree and a substantial question of law arises; if it is an appellate order, no further appeal (S.104(2)), only revision or Art. 227.

Problem 2. A judgment was delivered against X by a lower court. He wants to file a review petition in the next appellate court. Maintainable?

Asked: 2016

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Which court hears a review?
Law
S.114 and O.XLVII r.1: the application for review is made to the court which passed the decree or order. r.5: ordinarily the same judge hears it.
Apply
The appellate court did not pass the judgment, so it cannot review it. X's remedy in the appellate court is an appeal, not a review.
Conclusion
Not maintainable: a review lies only before the same court that passed the judgment; in the higher court X must file an appeal.

Problem 3. The trial court refuses a temporary injunction; the defendant's appeal is decided; the plaintiff files a revision in the High Court against the interim order. Maintainable?

Asked: (standard problem; Part A "Revision": 2015 · Aug 2021 · Sep 2024)

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Does revision lie against an interim order?
Law
S.115 proviso (2002): revision lies only if the order, had it been made in the applicant's favour, would have finally disposed of the suit Shiv Shakti. Art. 227 remains available.
Apply
Even if the injunction were granted, the suit would continue; the order does not finally decide the suit.
Conclusion
Revision is not maintainable against such an interim order; the party may invoke Article 227 in a fit case.

📖 Story to quote: Shiv Shakti

Problem 4. The first appellate court reversed the trial court's decree in a few lines without discussing the evidence. Remedy?

Asked: (standard problem; B: "first v. second appeal")

🤔 Try it in your head, then tap to see the answer
Issue
Is a cryptic reversing judgment open to challenge in second appeal?
Law
O.XLI r.31: the appellate judgment must state the points, the decision and reasons. S.100: second appeal on a substantial question of law Santosh Hazari.
Apply
Reversing without engaging with the trial court's reasons is a failure of the first appellate court's duty, which raises a substantial question of law.
Conclusion
File a second appeal under S.100; the High Court can set aside the judgment and remand it for a reasoned decision.
