2Raghunath Dass v. Union of India

AIR 1969 SC 674 · decided 26 Jul 1968
Supreme Court · K.S. Hegde and R.S. Bachawat, JJ. (judgment by Hegde J.)
S.80 notice · object · read reasonably

In 30 seconds

Story: A trader's railway consignment of copper was lost in 1947. His S.80 notice was in his firm's name, signed as proprietor; the High Court rejected his suit over this.

Held: Decree restored. The notice is read as a whole and reasonably; S.80 is not a booby trap for ordinary people.

🧠 Notice, not a booby trap

The story

Raghunath Dass ran a business at Gujranwala under the name "M/s. Raghunath Dass Mulkhraj". On 29 July 1947 he sent copper articles by the North Western Railway to a station near Moradabad. The goods never arrived.

Before suing the railway (the Union of India), he sent the notice required by Section 80. It was written in the firm's name, used "we" in places, and was signed "Raghunath Dass, Proprietor".

The trial court at Moradabad decreed his claim of about ₹10,206 with interest. But the Allahabad High Court dismissed the suit: it said the notice was given by one person (the firm) and the suit was filed by another (Raghunath Dass), so the notice was invalid.

The question

Is a Section 80 notice invalid just because it was written in the business name of a sole proprietor, while the suit is filed in his own name?

What the court held

No. The Supreme Court allowed the appeal and restored the trial court's decree with costs.

Read as a whole, the notice showed that it came from a sole proprietor; the Government could not have been misled into thinking it came from a partnership.

Principles to quote

  1. The object of the S.80 notice is to give the Government and public officers an opportunity to reconsider the legal position and to settle the claim without litigation.
  2. S.80 must be complied with, but it is "not intended to be used as booby traps against ignorant and illiterate persons".
  3. The notice must be read as a whole, reasonably, and not pedantically or "divorced from common sense".
  4. Today S.80(3) (added in 1976) says the same: no suit is dismissed for a mere defect in the notice if the plaintiff can be identified and the cause of action and relief are substantially indicated.

Using it in the exam
