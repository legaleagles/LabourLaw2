1Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh

(2014) 2 SCC 1 · decided 12 Nov 2013
Supreme Court · Constitution Bench (judgment by P. Sathasivam CJI)
Registration of FIR is mandatory · limited preliminary inquiry

In 30 seconds

Story: Police in Uttar Pradesh did not register a father's report that his minor daughter had been kidnapped.

Held: Registration of an FIR is mandatory when information discloses a cognizable offence; a preliminary inquiry is allowed only in limited categories.

🧠 Cognizable means register

The story

Lalita Kumari, a minor girl, was kidnapped in Uttar Pradesh in 2008. Her father went to the police, but they did not register an FIR or act on his information.

He petitioned the Supreme Court under Article 32. Because there were conflicting decisions on whether the police must register an FIR or may first make an inquiry, the matter went to a Constitution Bench.

The question

When information discloses a cognizable offence, can the police first make a preliminary inquiry before registering the FIR?

What the court held

Registration of an FIR is mandatory if the information discloses a cognizable offence; no preliminary inquiry is permitted in such a case.

If the information does not clearly disclose a cognizable offence, a preliminary inquiry may be made only to find out whether it does, in limited categories (matrimonial, commercial, medical negligence, corruption, abnormal delay), completed within a short time.

Officers who do not register an FIR in such cases must face action.

Principles to quote

  1. The word "shall" in the FIR provision is mandatory.
  2. Registration of an FIR protects against manipulation and ensures accountability.
  3. BNSS S.173(3) now allows a preliminary enquiry, with a DSP's permission, for offences punishable with 3 to under 7 years, within 14 days.
