2Balfour v. Balfour

[1919] 2 KB 571
Court of Appeal, England (Atkin LJ)
Intention to create legal relations · domestic agreements

In 30 seconds

Story: A husband promised his wife £30 a month while she stayed in England; after they separated he stopped paying.

Held: No contract: agreements between husband and wife in daily life are not meant to be legally binding.

🧠 Home promises are not court promises

The story

Mr Balfour worked in Ceylon. While on leave in England, his wife fell ill and was advised to stay back for treatment. Before returning, he promised to send her £30 a month.

Later they separated and he stopped the payments. She sued him on the promise.

The question

Is a promise between husband and wife about household money a contract enforceable by law?

What the court held

No. Such arrangements are not contracts because the parties do not intend them to be attended by legal consequences.

Atkin LJ said the courts would be flooded if every domestic promise were treated as a contract; the home is a place where the King's writ does not seek to run.

Principles to quote

  1. Intention to create legal relations is an essential of a contract.
  2. In social and domestic agreements, the presumption is that there is no such intention.
  3. Contrast commercial agreements, where the intention is presumed.

Using it in the exam

Part AIntention to create legal relations; agreements that are not contracts.
Part B“All contracts are agreements but all agreements are not contracts.”
ProblemsDinner invitation and family promises: no contract.
