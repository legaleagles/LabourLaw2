2Gayatri Balasamy v. ISG Novasoft Technologies Ltd.

2025 INSC 605 · decided 30 Apr 2025
Supreme Court · Constitution Bench (5 judges, 4:1; majority by Sanjiv Khanna CJI)
Limited power of courts to modify arbitral awards

In 30 seconds

Story: An employee won compensation in arbitration for harassment and wrongful termination; the High Court changed the amount, and the question arose whether courts can modify awards at all.

Held: Courts have a limited power to modify: sever invalid parts, correct clerical or computational errors apparent on the record, and adjust post-award interest; the Supreme Court may use Art. 142 rarely. No rewriting on merits.

🧠 Correct, do not rewrite

The story

Gayatri Balasamy, a former employee of ISG Novasoft, claimed compensation for sexual harassment and wrongful termination. An arbitral tribunal awarded her a large sum.

In the challenge under S.34 and the appeal under S.37, the High Court modified the amount.

Conflicting judgments existed on whether courts can modify an award or can only set it aside. The question went to a Constitution Bench.

The question

Can a court hearing a challenge under S.34 or S.37 modify an arbitral award?

What the court held

Yes, but only within narrow limits: (1) where the invalid part can be severed from the valid part; (2) to correct clerical, computational or typographical errors apparent on the face of the record; (3) to modify post-award interest in suitable cases.

The Supreme Court may, with great caution, use Art. 142 to do complete justice, but not to rewrite the award on merits. One judge dissented, holding that courts can only set aside or remit.

Principles to quote

  1. Setting aside remains the main remedy; modification is the exception.
  2. Avoids sending parties back to fresh arbitration for small errors.
  3. Merits review is still barred.
