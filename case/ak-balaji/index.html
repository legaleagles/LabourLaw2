1Bar Council of India v. A.K. Balaji

(2018) 5 SCC 379 · decided 13 Mar 2018
Supreme Court · A.K. Goel and U.U. Lalit, JJ.
Foreign lawyers and law firms cannot practise Indian law

In 30 seconds

Story: Foreign law firms were advising clients in India and taking part in arbitrations without being enrolled under the Advocates Act.

Held: "Practice of law" covers litigation and non-litigation work; foreign lawyers cannot practise Indian law in India. They may advise on foreign law on a casual "fly in and fly out" basis, and appear in international commercial arbitrations, subject to rules.

🧠 Indian law is for Indian advocates

The story

A.K. Balaji, an advocate, challenged the presence of foreign law firms working in India without enrolment, through liaison offices and on visits.

The Madras High Court held that foreign lawyers could visit India on a casual basis to advise clients on foreign law. The Bar Council of India appealed to the Supreme Court.

The question

Can foreign lawyers and law firms practise law in India, in litigation or advisory work?

What the court held

The 'practice of law' under the Advocates Act includes both litigation and non-litigation (advisory and drafting) work. Only advocates enrolled under the Act can practise Indian law.

Foreign lawyers may come on a casual basis to advise clients on foreign law or international law; whether a visit is casual is a question of fact. They may conduct international commercial arbitration in India, but subject to the code of conduct of the Indian legal profession. BPO companies providing support services are not practising law.

Principles to quote

  1. The Bar Council of India can regulate foreign lawyers.
  2. Led to the BCI Rules for registration of foreign lawyers and law firms (notified 2023), on reciprocity.
  3. Protects the Indian profession while allowing limited foreign-law advice.
