2Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra

(2013) 6 SCC 770 · decided 3 Apr 2013
Supreme Court · T.S. Thakur and Gyan Sudha Misra, JJ.
Duty of courts to consider victim compensation

In 30 seconds

Story: In a murder case, the courts below convicted the accused but never considered compensation for the victim's family.

Held: The compensation provision casts a mandatory duty on every court to apply its mind to compensation in every criminal case.

🧠 Don't forget the victim

The story

The appellants were convicted for a murder in Maharashtra. Neither the trial court nor the High Court said anything about compensation for the family of the deceased.

When the case reached the Supreme Court, it examined whether courts can simply ignore the question of compensation.

The question

Is a criminal court bound to consider compensation for the victim?

What the court held

Yes. The provision on compensation (S.357 CrPC, now S.395 BNSS) casts a duty on every court to apply its mind to the question of compensation in every criminal case.

If a court does not award compensation, it must record its reasons.

Principles to quote

  1. Criminal justice must look after the victim, not only punish the offender.
  2. The power to award compensation is not ancillary to other sentences but in addition to them.
  3. Courts may consider the accused's capacity to pay.
