3Kasturilal Ralia Ram Jain v. State of Uttar Pradesh

AIR 1965 SC 1039
Supreme Court of India (Gajendragadkar CJ)
State liability · sovereign immunity

In 30 seconds

Story: Gold seized by the Meerut police from a jeweller was misappropriated by a head constable.

Held: The State was not liable because the seizure was in exercise of sovereign power.

🧠 Sovereign shield

The story

Ralia Ram, a jeweller, was arrested at Meerut on suspicion. The police seized gold and silver from him and kept them in the malkhana. A head constable misappropriated the gold and fled to Pakistan. When released, Ralia Ram sued the State.

The question

Is the State liable for the negligence of police officers in keeping seized property?

What the court held

No. Following P&O Steam Navigation (1861), the Court held that the power to arrest, search and seize is a sovereign power; for torts committed in its exercise, the State is not liable. The Court urged Parliament to legislate. Later cases (Nagendra Rao, 1994; Rudul Sah) have greatly narrowed the defence.

Principles to quote

  1. Art. 300: State's liability is as that of the East India Company.
  2. Sovereign v non-sovereign functions.
