1Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala

(1973) 4 SCC 225 · AIR 1973 SC 1461
Supreme Court of India (13 judges)
Basic structure doctrine · Preamble is part of the Constitution

In 30 seconds

Story: A Kerala mutt head challenged land reform laws and the amendments that protected them.

Held: Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution but cannot destroy its basic structure; the Preamble is part of the Constitution.

🧠 Amend, don't destroy

The story

Swami Kesavananda Bharati, head of a mutt in Kerala, challenged Kerala land reform laws affecting the mutt's property, and the 24th, 25th and 29th constitutional amendments that shielded such laws.

The question

Is Parliament's power to amend the Constitution under Article 368 unlimited?

What the court held

By 7:6, the Court held that Parliament may amend any provision, including fundamental rights, but cannot alter the basic structure of the Constitution — supremacy of the Constitution, republican and democratic government, secularism, separation of powers, federalism, judicial review and so on. It overruled Golak Nath and the view in Berubari that the Preamble is not part of the Constitution.

Principles to quote

  1. The Preamble is part of the Constitution and a key to its interpretation.
  2. Basic structure limits the amending power.
