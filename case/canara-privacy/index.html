3District Registrar and Collector, Hyderabad v. Canara Bank

(2005) 1 SCC 496 · decided 1 Nov 2004
Supreme Court (judgment by R.C. Lahoti CJI)
Customers' bank records and the right to privacy

In 30 seconds

Story: A State law let any person authorised by the Collector enter a bank and inspect and impound customers' documents to check stamp duty.

Held: The provision was struck down: customers' records with a bank are part of their privacy, and the power had no safeguards.

🧠 Bank records are private

The story

Andhra Pradesh amended S.73 of the Indian Stamp Act to let the Collector authorise any person to enter any premises, including banks, and inspect, take notes of and impound documents to detect evasion of stamp duty.

Officials acting under this power impounded documents held by Canara Bank. The bank challenged the law, and the High Court struck it down. The State appealed.

The question

Can officials be empowered to inspect and seize customers' documents held by a bank without safeguards?

What the court held

No. The right to privacy covers a person's documents and records even when they are in a bank's custody.

The law allowed unguided, excessive intrusion with no requirement of reasons or prior satisfaction, so it was unreasonable and invalid.

Principles to quote

  1. Banks hold customers' records in confidence; privacy goes with the papers.
  2. Any law permitting access must be reasonable, with safeguards.
  3. Strengthens the banker's duty of secrecy.
