3Union Bank of India v. Khader International Construction

(2001) 5 SCC 22 · decided 8 May 2001
Supreme Court · U.C. Banerjee and K.G. Balakrishnan, JJ. (judgment by Balakrishnan J.)
O.XXXIII · indigent person · includes a company

In 30 seconds

Story: A limited company applied at Kochi to sue a bank as an indigent person; the bank said a company cannot be "indigent".

Held: "Person" in O.XXXIII includes a juristic person, so a company can sue as an indigent person.

🧠 A poor company is a person too

The story

Khader International Construction, a limited company, wanted to sue the Union Bank of India before the Sub-Court at Kochi, but could not pay the court fee.

It applied under Order XXXIII Rule 1 CPC for permission to sue as an indigent person. The Subordinate Judge allowed it, and the High Court dismissed the bank's revision.

The bank appealed to the Supreme Court, arguing that Order XXXIII is meant only for poor human beings, not companies.

The question

Does the word "person" in Order XXXIII CPC include a company or other juristic person?

What the court held

Yes. The Supreme Court held that "person" in Order XXXIII includes not only a natural person but also a juristic person, so a company can maintain an application under O.XXXIII r.1.

A company is represented by a person competent to represent it. The appeal was dismissed.

Principles to quote

  1. O.XXXIII is a benevolent provision so that lack of money does not shut the door of the court.
  2. "Person" is not limited to human beings; the General Clauses Act definition includes companies and associations.
  3. The test is the same: does the applicant have sufficient means to pay the court fee?
  4. O.XXXIII defers the court fee; it is recovered later from whoever loses (r.10, r.11).
