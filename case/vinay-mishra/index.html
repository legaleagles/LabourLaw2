1In re Vinay Chandra Mishra

(1995) 2 SCC 584 · decided 10 Mar 1995
Supreme Court
Criminal contempt by an advocate in open court

In 30 seconds

Story: A senior advocate, then Chairman of the Bar Council of India, shouted at and threatened a High Court judge in open court when questioned during a hearing.

Held: He was guilty of criminal contempt; the Court imposed a suspended sentence and also suspended him from practice for three years (the last part was later held to be beyond its contempt power).

🧠 The robe does not shield contempt

The story

During a hearing in the Allahabad High Court, the judge asked the senior advocate Vinay Chandra Mishra a question about the case.

He lost his temper, shouted at the judge, and threatened to get him transferred or impeached. He was at that time the Chairman of the Bar Council of India.

The judge reported the incident, and the Supreme Court took up contempt proceedings.

The question

Was the advocate guilty of criminal contempt, and what punishment could the Supreme Court impose?

What the court held

Yes. Insulting and threatening a judge in open court scandalises the court and interferes with the administration of justice: it is criminal contempt, made worse by his high position at the Bar.

The Court imposed simple imprisonment (suspended) and, under Articles 129 and 142, suspended him from practice for three years. That suspension was later overruled in Supreme Court Bar Association v. Union of India (1998).

Principles to quote

  1. The higher the position of the advocate, the higher his duty of restraint.
  2. Contempt power protects the administration of justice, not the person of the judge.
  3. Read with SCBA (1998) on who can suspend an advocate.
