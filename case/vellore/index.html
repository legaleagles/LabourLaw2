2Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum v. Union of India

(1996) 5 SCC 647
Supreme Court of India (Kuldip Singh J)
Sustainable development · precautionary principle · polluter pays

In 30 seconds

Story: Tanneries in Tamil Nadu discharged untreated effluents into the Palar river, ruining farmland and drinking water.

Held: Sustainable development, the precautionary principle and polluter pays are part of Indian law; polluters must pay compensation and restoration costs.

🧠 Precaution first, polluter pays

The story

About 900 tanneries in five districts of Tamil Nadu discharged untreated effluent into the Palar river and onto agricultural land. Large areas became unfit for cultivation and drinking water was contaminated.

The question

What principles govern the balance between industry and environment?

What the court held

The Court held that sustainable development is part of customary international law and of Indian law. Its essential features — the precautionary principle (prevent harm; lack of scientific certainty is no reason to postpone measures; the burden of proof is on the developer/industry) and the polluter pays principle — are part of the law of the land (Arts. 21, 47, 48A, 51A(g)). The Court directed a Green Authority, compensation, restoration and closure of non-complying units.

Principles to quote

  1. Sustainable development · precautionary principle · polluter pays.
  2. Absolute liability for harm to environment.
