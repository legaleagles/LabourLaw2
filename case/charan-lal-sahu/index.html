6Charan Lal Sahu v. Union of India (Bhopal Act case)

(1990) 1 SCC 613
Supreme Court of India (Sabyasachi Mukharji CJ)
Bhopal gas tragedy · State as parens patriae for victims

In 30 seconds

Story: The Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster Act gave the Union Government the exclusive right to represent the victims.

Held: The Act was valid: the State could act as parens patriae for victims, but must pay interim relief and consult them.

🧠 State steps in for the victims

The story

After the Bhopal gas leak of December 1984, Parliament passed the Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster (Processing of Claims) Act, 1985, giving the Union Government the exclusive right to represent all victims in claims against Union Carbide. The Act and the 1989 settlement were challenged.

The question

Can the State represent victims exclusively in claims for an industrial disaster?

What the court held

Yes. Under the doctrine of parens patriae, the State can act on behalf of helpless victims who cannot effectively pursue their claims. The Act was constitutional, read with an obligation to pay interim maintenance and to hear the victims. Absolute liability (M.C. Mehta) applies to hazardous industries.

Principles to quote

  1. Parens patriae; MNC liability.
  2. Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 followed.
