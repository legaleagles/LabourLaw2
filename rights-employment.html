Salary & Wage Rights
Payment timelines · Minimum wages · Deduction limits
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Salary Must Be Paid by 7th/10th of Every Month
Every employer must pay wages by the 7th of every month (for establishments with fewer than 1,000 workers) or 10th (for larger establishments). Delayed payment attracts interest. Employer cannot deduct more than 50% of wages for any reason. Withholding salary is a criminal offence.
WHAT TO DO
1
Send written email/letter to employer demanding payment. Keep copy.
2
File complaint with Labour Commissioner under Payment of Wages Act.
3
Claim unpaid salary + 15% interest per year on delayed amount.
4
Labour Commissioner can order payment within days — no court fees.
5
If still unpaid — file criminal complaint under Payment of Wages Act Section 20.
Legal basis: Payment of Wages Act 1936, Sections 3, 4, 15; Minimum Wages Act 1948; Labour Commissioner jurisdiction
ENTITLEMENT
Minimum Wage is a Legal Right — Not Negotiable
Every worker is entitled to the minimum wage fixed by the State Government for their category of work. Paying below minimum wage is a criminal offence. Domestic workers, agricultural workers, construction workers are all covered under state minimum wage notifications.
Legal basis: Minimum Wages Act 1948; Code on Wages 2019 (when notified); State minimum wage schedules

Wrongful Termination Rights
Notice period · Retrenchment compensation · Standing orders
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Minimum 30 Days Notice or Pay in Lieu
All employees are entitled to minimum 30 days notice before termination (or pay in lieu of notice). Companies with 100+ workers need government permission before retrenchment. Retrenchment compensation: 15 days wages per completed year of service for workers employed 1+ year.
WHAT TO DO
1
Demand termination letter in writing with reason clearly stated.
2
Check if notice period or payment in lieu was given.
3
If employed 1+ year — claim retrenchment compensation: 15 days wages per year.
4
File complaint with Labour Commissioner for illegal termination.
5
For companies with 100+ workers — retrenchment without government approval is void.
Legal basis: Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Sections 25F, 25G, 25N; Shops & Establishments Act (state-specific)
PROHIBITED
Employer Cannot Terminate for Filing Complaints
An employer cannot terminate or victimise a worker for filing a wage complaint, labour court case, or trade union activity. Such termination is called "victimisation" and is specifically prohibited under the Industrial Disputes Act. File immediately with Labour Commissioner if this happens.
Legal basis: Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Section 25T — Unfair labour practices; Section 25U — Penalties

PF, ESI & Gratuity Rights
PF deposit · ESI medical · Gratuity after 5 years
▼
ENTITLEMENT
PF: 12% of Basic Must Be Deposited Every Month
Employer must deposit 12% of basic salary as PF contribution every month. Employer also contributes 12% (of which 8.33% goes to EPS pension). Employer cannot withhold or divert PF contributions — it is criminal misappropriation. Check your PF balance anytime on EPFO portal using UAN.
💬 What to Say
→Check PF balance at epfindia.gov.in using your UAN (Universal Account Number).
→If employer not depositing — file complaint at EPFO regional office. Employer faces criminal prosecution.
→Transfer PF on job change using UAN — don't withdraw unless absolutely necessary (tax implications).
→ESI: If your salary is ₹21,000 or below — employer must register you for ESI medical insurance.
Legal basis: Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 1952; ESI Act 1948; EPFO enforcement
ENTITLEMENT
Gratuity After 5 Years — Cannot Be Withheld
After completing 5 years of continuous service, every employee is entitled to gratuity. Formula: 15 days wages × number of years of service. Maximum: ₹20 lakhs. Employer must pay within 30 days of your leaving. Withholding gratuity attracts interest and criminal action.
WHAT TO DO
1
Send Form I (Gratuity Application) to employer in writing when leaving.
2
Employer must pay within 30 days or pay 10% interest per annum on delayed amount.
3
If employer refuses — file with Labour Commissioner under Payment of Gratuity Act.
4
Labour Commissioner can order payment with 10% interest penalty.
Legal basis: Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, Sections 4, 7, 8; Maximum ₹20 lakh (2018 amendment)

Workplace Safety & Accident Rights
Employer duty · ESI compensation · ESIC hospitals
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Safe Workplace & Accident Compensation
Every employer has a duty to provide a safe working environment. For work-related accidents: ESI-covered workers get full medical treatment at ESIC hospitals free and cash compensation during incapacity. Non-ESI workers get Workmen's Compensation under Employees' Compensation Act.
Legal basis: Factories Act 1948; Employees' State Insurance Act 1948; Employees' Compensation Act 1923; Building and Other Construction Workers Act 1996
