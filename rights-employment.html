Salary & Wage Rights
Payment timelines · Minimum wages · Deduction limits
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Salary Must Be Paid by 7th/10th of Every Month
Every employer must pay wages by the 7th of every month (less than 1,000 workers) or 10th (larger establishments). Withholding salary is a criminal offence. Employer cannot deduct more than 50% of wages for any reason.
IF SALARY IS WITHHELD
1
Send written email to employer demanding payment. Keep copy.
2
File complaint with Labour Commissioner under Payment of Wages Act.
3
Claim unpaid salary + 15% interest per year on delayed amount.
4
Labour Commissioner can order payment within days — no court fees.
5
If still unpaid — file criminal complaint under Payment of Wages Act Section 20.
Legal basis: Payment of Wages Act 1936, Sections 3,4,15; Minimum Wages Act 1948
ENTITLEMENT
Minimum Wage is a Legal Right — Not Negotiable
Every worker is entitled to the minimum wage fixed by State Government for their category of work. Paying below minimum wage is a criminal offence. Domestic workers, agricultural workers, construction workers — all covered.
Legal basis: Minimum Wages Act 1948; Code on Wages 2019; State minimum wage schedules

Wrongful Termination Rights
30 days notice · Retrenchment compensation · No victimisation
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Minimum 30 Days Notice or Pay in Lieu
All employees are entitled to minimum 30 days notice before termination (or pay in lieu). Companies with 100+ workers need government permission for retrenchment. Retrenchment compensation: 15 days wages per year of service for workers employed 1+ year.
IF WRONGFULLY TERMINATED
1
Demand termination letter in writing with reason clearly stated.
2
Check if notice period or payment in lieu was given.
3
If employed 1+ year — claim retrenchment compensation: 15 days wages per year.
4
File complaint with Labour Commissioner for illegal termination.
5
Employer cannot terminate for filing complaints — victimisation is prohibited.
Legal basis: Industrial Disputes Act 1947, Sections 25F, 25G, 25N; Shops & Establishments Act

PF, ESI & Gratuity Rights
12% PF monthly · ESI medical · Gratuity after 5 years
▼
ENTITLEMENT
PF: 12% of Basic Must Be Deposited Every Month
Employer must deposit 12% of basic salary as PF contribution every month. Employer cannot withhold PF contributions — it is criminal misappropriation. Check your PF balance on EPFO portal (epfindia.gov.in) using UAN. If salary is ₹21,000 or below — employer MUST also register you for ESI medical insurance.
💡 How to Check & Enforce
→Check PF balance at epfindia.gov.in using your UAN.
→If employer not depositing PF — file complaint at EPFO regional office. Criminal prosecution possible.
→ESI: If your salary ≤₹21,000 — employer must register you. ESIC gives free medical to you and family.
→Transfer PF on job change using UAN — do not withdraw unnecessarily (tax implications).
Legal basis: EPF & MP Act 1952; ESI Act 1948; EPFO enforcement — criminal prosecution under Section 14
ENTITLEMENT
Gratuity After 5 Years — Cannot Be Withheld
After completing 5 years of continuous service — every employee is entitled to gratuity. Formula: 15 days wages × years of service. Maximum ₹20 lakhs. Employer must pay within 30 days. Withholding attracts 10% interest and criminal action.
CLAIMING GRATUITY
1
Send Form I (Gratuity Application) to employer in writing when leaving.
2
Employer must pay within 30 days or pay 10% interest per annum on delay.
3
If employer refuses — file with Labour Commissioner under Payment of Gratuity Act.
4
Commissioner can order payment with 10% interest penalty.
Legal basis: Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, Sections 4, 7, 8; Maximum ₹20 lakh (2018 amendment)
ENTITLEMENT
Notice Period, Relieving Letter & Contract Labour Rights
Employer cannot withhold relieving letter or experience certificate unreasonably. Employment bonds requiring large sums for leaving early are generally unenforceable under Indian Contract Act — courts consistently hold unreasonable bonds void. Contract workers doing same work as permanent employees can claim regularisation after 240+ days/year.
Legal basis: Indian Contract Act 1872, Sections 27, 74 — unreasonable bonds void; Contract Labour Act 1970 — principal employer liable; Industrial Disputes Act 1947
