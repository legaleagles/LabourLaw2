Workplace Sexual Harassment (POSH)
ICC complaint · 3 months to file · Employer liable
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Internal Complaints Committee & Action
Every workplace with 10 or more employees must have an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC). You can file a complaint within 3 months of the last incident (extendable to 6 months). Complaint can be written or oral. Employer must complete inquiry within 90 days. Retaliation against complainant is prohibited and separately punishable.
WHAT TO DO
1
Document every incident — date, time, place, witnesses, any messages or emails.
2
Submit written complaint to ICC chairperson. Keep copy. Get acknowledgment.
3
If no ICC exists — file with Local Complaints Committee (LCC) at District level.
4
Also file FIR at police station under IPC Section 354A (sexual harassment).
5
If employer retaliates — file additional complaint. Retaliation is an offence under POSH.
Legal basis: Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act 2013, Sections 4, 9, 11, 13; IPC Section 354A
ENTITLEMENT
SHe-Box — Online POSH Complaint Portal
If you work for a Central Government organisation or are unsure about the ICC — file directly on SHe-Box (shebox.nic.in), the government's online portal for POSH complaints. It routes your complaint to the right authority automatically.
💬 What to Say
→Name and designation of the respondent.
→Nature of harassment — verbal, physical, visual, written.
→Date(s), time(s) and place(s) of incidents.
→Any witnesses or documentary evidence (messages, emails, CCTV request).
→Relief sought — transfer, termination of respondent, compensation.
Legal basis: POSH Act 2013, Section 9; SHe-Box Portal guidelines

Domestic Violence Rights
Stay in home · Protection order · Emergency shelter · Monetary relief
▼
URGENT — CALL 181
Right to Stay in Shared Household — Cannot Be Thrown Out
Under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005 — you have the right to remain in the shared household, even if you don't own it. You cannot be thrown out — not even by the husband or in-laws. If you are forced out, apply for a Residence Order from the Magistrate immediately.
WHAT TO DO
1
Call Women Helpline 181 or Police 100 if in immediate danger.
2
Contact Protection Officer in your district — they assist with filing and interim orders.
3
Apply for Protection Order from Magistrate — can be issued within 3 days in emergencies.
4
Apply for Residence Order — guarantees your right to stay in shared home.
5
Apply for Monetary Relief — covers medical expenses, loss of earnings, maintenance.
🚨 Emergency
If you are in immediate danger — call 112. You can also go directly to any police station and request urgent assistance. DV complaints are cognisable offences — police must register and act.
Legal basis: Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005, Sections 17 (residence right), 18 (protection order), 19 (residence order), 20 (monetary relief), 21 (custody order)

Maternity & Pregnancy Rights
26 weeks paid leave · No dismissal · Crèche · Medical bonus
▼
ENTITLEMENT
26 Weeks Paid Maternity Leave — Dismissal During Pregnancy is Illegal
Under the Maternity Benefit Act 1961 (amended 2017) — women in establishments with 10+ employees get 26 weeks paid maternity leave for first 2 children. 12 weeks for 3rd child onwards. An employer cannot dismiss a woman during pregnancy or maternity leave period — doing so is illegal and punishable.
💬 What to Say
→Give written notice to employer of expected delivery date 7 weeks in advance.
→Medical bonus of ₹3,500 if the employer does not provide free medical care.
→Crèche facility mandatory for establishments with 50+ employees within 500 metres.
→Work from home option can be availed after leave period ends by mutual consent.
→Adoption leave: 12 weeks for adopting a child below 3 months.
Legal basis: Maternity Benefit Act 1961, Sections 4, 5, 8, 11A (crèche); amended by Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017

Equal Pay & Workplace Rights
Equal pay · No discrimination · Safe working conditions
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Equal Pay for Equal Work — Gender Discrimination is Illegal
An employer cannot pay a woman less than a man for the same or similar work. This right is enshrined in the Constitution (Article 39(d)) and enforced through the Equal Remuneration Act 1976. File complaint with Labour Commissioner. Also file with National Human Rights Commission if employer is unresponsive.
WHAT TO DO
1
Collect evidence — payslips, offer letters showing pay disparity.
2
File complaint with Labour Commissioner in your district.
3
File complaint with NHRC (nhrc.nic.in) for systematic gender pay discrimination.
4
File Consumer/labour complaint if the employer is a service provider.
Legal basis: Equal Remuneration Act 1976; Article 39(d) Constitution; Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act 2013

Child Rights
Child labour prohibited · Education right · Protection from abuse
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Free Education — Ages 6 to 14
Every child between 6 and 14 years has a fundamental right to free and compulsory education under Article 21A. Private schools must reserve 25% seats for economically weaker sections. Schools cannot charge capitation fees or conduct screening tests for admission.
Legal basis: Right to Education Act 2009 (RTE Act); Article 21A Constitution; SC: Society for Un-Aided Private Schools v. Union of India — 25% reservation upheld
PROHIBITED
Child Labour is a Criminal Offence
Employment of children below 14 years is completely prohibited in all occupations. Children between 14–18 years cannot work in hazardous occupations. Employers face imprisonment up to 2 years and fines. Even engaging a child as domestic help is prohibited.
💬 What to Say
→Call Childline: 1098 — free, 24/7, for children in distress.
→File complaint with District Child Welfare Officer.
→File complaint with labour department — includes domestic workers.
Legal basis: Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act 2016; Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act 1986
