Workplace Harassment (POSH)
ICC complaint · 3 months to file · Employer liable
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Internal Complaints Committee is Mandatory
Every workplace with 10 or more employees must have an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC). You can file a complaint within 3 months of the last incident. Complaint can be written or oral. Employer must complete inquiry within 90 days. Retaliation against complainant is separately punishable.
HOW TO FILE POSH COMPLAINT
1
Document every incident — date, time, place, witnesses, any messages.
2
Submit written complaint to ICC chairperson. Get acknowledgment.
3
If no ICC exists — file with Local Complaints Committee (LCC) at District level.
4
Also file FIR at police station under IPC Section 354A.
5
If employer retaliates — file additional complaint. Retaliation is an offence under POSH.
💬 What to Include in Your Complaint
→Name and designation of the respondent.
→Nature of harassment — verbal, physical, visual, written.
→Date, time and place of each incident.
→Witnesses or documentary evidence (messages, emails).
→Relief sought — transfer, termination of respondent, compensation.
Legal basis: Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act 2013, Sections 4, 9, 11, 13; IPC Section 354A; SHe-Box Portal: shebox.nic.in

Domestic Violence Rights
Stay in home · Protection order · Emergency · Monetary relief
▼
URGENT — CALL 181
Right to Stay in Shared Household — Cannot Be Thrown Out
You have the right to remain in the shared household, even if you do not own it. You cannot be thrown out — not even by the husband or in-laws. If forced out, apply for a Residence Order from the Magistrate immediately.
IMMEDIATE STEPS
1
Call Women Helpline 181 or Police 100 if in immediate danger.
2
Contact Protection Officer in your district — they assist with filing and interim orders.
3
Apply for Protection Order from Magistrate — can be issued within 3 days in emergencies.
4
Apply for Residence Order — guarantees right to stay in shared home.
5
Apply for Monetary Relief — covers medical expenses, loss of earnings, maintenance.
🚨 Emergency
If in immediate danger — call 112. DV complaints are cognisable — police must register and act.
Legal basis: Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005, Sections 17, 18, 19, 20, 21

Maternity & Pregnancy Rights
26 weeks paid leave · No dismissal · Crèche · Medical bonus
▼
ENTITLEMENT
26 Weeks Paid Maternity Leave — Dismissal During Pregnancy is Illegal
Women in establishments with 10+ employees get 26 weeks paid maternity leave for first 2 children. 12 weeks for 3rd child. An employer cannot dismiss a woman during pregnancy or maternity leave — illegal and punishable.
💡 Additional Rights
→Give written notice to employer of expected delivery date 7 weeks in advance.
→Medical bonus of ₹3,500 if employer does not provide free medical care.
→Crèche facility mandatory for 50+ employee establishments within 500 metres.
→Work from home option can be availed after leave period ends by mutual consent.
→Adoption leave: 12 weeks for adopting a child below 3 months.
Legal basis: Maternity Benefit Act 1961, Sections 4, 5, 8, 11A; Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017

Equal Pay & Child Rights
Equal pay · Child education · Child labour prohibited
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Equal Pay for Equal Work — Gender Discrimination is Illegal
An employer cannot pay a woman less than a man for the same work. File complaint with Labour Commissioner. Also file with NHRC for systematic discrimination.
HOW TO CLAIM EQUAL PAY
1
Collect evidence — payslips, offer letters showing pay disparity.
2
File complaint with Labour Commissioner in your district.
3
File with NHRC (nhrc.nic.in) for systematic gender pay discrimination.
4
Consumer complaint if employer is a service provider.
Legal basis: Equal Remuneration Act 1976; Article 39(d) Constitution
ENTITLEMENT
Child Rights — Education & Protection
Every child between 6 and 14 years has a fundamental right to free and compulsory education under Article 21A. Private schools must reserve 25% seats for EWS. Employment of children below 14 years is completely prohibited — criminal offence. Call Childline 1098 to report child labour.
Legal basis: Right to Education Act 2009; Article 21A Constitution; Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act 2016
