Right to Information (RTI)
₹10 fee · 30 days · Any government department
▼
FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT
Any Citizen Can Demand Any Government Information
Under the RTI Act 2005 — any Indian citizen can ask any government department for any information they hold. Fee: ₹10 (BPL applicants: free). Response within 30 days (48 hours for life/liberty matters). Refusal grounds are narrow — national security, cabinet deliberations, personal privacy of third parties.
WHAT TO DO
1
Write RTI application addressing Public Information Officer (PIO) of the department.
2
State: "Under RTI Act 2005, I request the following information..."
3
Pay ₹10 by demand draft, IPO, court fee stamp, or cash (at office). BPL applicants free.
4
If no response in 30 days — file First Appeal with First Appellate Authority (same dept, senior officer).
5
Second Appeal to State/Central Information Commission if still unresolved — enforceable order.
Legal basis: Right to Information Act 2005, Sections 3, 6, 7, 19; Central Information Commission; SC: CBSE v. Aditya Bandopadhyay — RTI scope is wide

Rights When Arrested
Grounds of arrest · 24 hours · Bail · Legal aid
▼
FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT
Right to Know Grounds of Arrest — Immediately
When you are arrested, police must immediately inform you of the grounds of arrest. You have the right to inform a family member or friend. Right to consult a lawyer of your choice. Police must produce you before a Magistrate within 24 hours — no exceptions. Beyond 24 hours without Magistrate order = illegal detention.
💬 What to Say
→"What are the grounds for my arrest? I have the right to know under Article 22(1)."
→"I want to call my family/lawyer. This is my right under Article 22(1) and CrPC Section 50."
→"You must produce me before a Magistrate within 24 hours. Beyond that is illegal detention."
→Stay calm. Do not sign any document without reading it. Do not make any statement without a lawyer.
⚠️ Right to Silence
You are NOT required to answer police questions that may incriminate you. Article 20(3) of the Constitution guarantees the right against self-incrimination. Say: "I exercise my right to remain silent."
Legal basis: Article 20(3), 22 Constitution; CrPC Sections 41, 50, 56, 57; D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997 SC) — landmark guidelines on arrest procedures
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Free Legal Aid — Even in Police Custody
Every person who cannot afford a lawyer has the right to free legal representation under the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987. This applies from the moment of arrest — not just at trial. Demand this right at the police station.
💬 What to Say
→At the police station: "I cannot afford a lawyer. I request free legal aid under the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987."
→Call NALSA (National Legal Services Authority): 15100 — free, available immediately.
→District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) provides lawyers for court appearances.
Legal basis: Legal Services Authorities Act 1987; Article 39A Constitution; SC: Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar — right to legal aid is part of fair trial

Right to Privacy
Article 21 · Aadhaar · Data Protection · Phone surveillance
▼
FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT
Privacy is a Fundamental Right Under Article 21
The Supreme Court in Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017) — a 9-judge bench — unanimously held that Privacy is a Fundamental Right under Article 21. This covers: informational privacy (your personal data), bodily privacy, and decisional autonomy. No government or private entity can violate this without due process and proportionality.
💬 What to Say
→Aadhaar misuse — file complaint at uidai.gov.in or call 1947.
→Telecom data sharing without consent — file complaint with TRAI.
→Social media data breach — file complaint at cybercrime.gov.in.
→Employer surveillance beyond work scope — file complaint with Labour Commissioner + Privacy complaint.
→Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 — in force. Consent mandatory before processing personal data.
Legal basis: Article 21 Constitution; SC: Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017) — 9-judge bench; Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; IT Act 2000

Constitutional Rights — Know Your Fundamentals
6 Fundamental Rights · Writs · Enforcement
▼
FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT
6 Fundamental Rights — Directly Enforceable Against State
Articles 12–35 of the Constitution guarantee 6 Fundamental Rights: Right to Equality (Arts 14-18) · Right to Freedom (Arts 19-22) · Right Against Exploitation (Arts 23-24) · Right to Freedom of Religion (Arts 25-28) · Cultural & Educational Rights (Arts 29-30) · Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32). Violation? File a Writ Petition in High Court (Article 226) or Supreme Court (Article 32) — no fees for PILs.
💬 What to Say
→Habeas Corpus — illegal detention. File immediately in High Court.
→Mandamus — government refused to perform duty. File in High Court.
→Certiorari — quash an illegal government order.
→Prohibition — stop lower court/tribunal from exceeding jurisdiction.
→Quo Warranto — challenge a person's right to hold public office.
Legal basis: Constitution of India, Articles 12–35 (Fundamental Rights); Article 32 (right to constitutional remedies — itself a fundamental right); Article 226 (High Court writ jurisdiction)
