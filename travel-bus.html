Bus Passenger Rights India
Whether you travel by TSRTC, APSRTC, private sleeper or an online-booked bus — you have statutory rights as a passenger and as a consumer.

Cancellation & Refund Rights
State RTC · Online aggregators (RedBus, AbhiBus) · Timeline
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Refund When Bus is Cancelled by Operator
If the bus is cancelled by the operator — you are entitled to a full refund. Both state RTCs (TSRTC, APSRTC) and private operators must refund. For state RTCs — refund is governed by their internal refund policy. For private/online bookings — Consumer Protection Act 2019 applies. Aggregators like RedBus and AbhiBus cannot escape liability by calling themselves "facilitators" (National Consumer Commission in MakeMyTrip v. Bansal).
REFUND ESCALATION (Day-by-Day)
1
Day 0–7: Wait and keep cancellation SMS/email + Cancellation Reference Number (CRN). Check if refund credited.
2
Day 15: Contact state RTC complaint cell (TSRTC: tsrtconline.in · APSRTC: apsrtc.ap.gov.in). Quote PNR. Most RTCs settle within 21 days.
3
Day 21: File on National Consumer Helpline (consumerhelpline.gov.in) or call 1800-11-4000. Quote "Bus ticket refund + CPA 2019 §38."
4
Day 30: For state RTCs — file RTI at rtionline.gov.in asking for refund file notings and payment voucher number.
5
Day 45+: File at e-Daakhil (edaakhil.nic.in) — online consumer court. Claim refund + 12% interest + ₹5,000 harassment compensation.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection Act 2019, §2(11) — Deficiency in service; §38 — Consumer Commission jurisdiction up to ₹50 lakh; IT (Intermediary) Rules 2021, Rule 3(2)(a) — Aggregator liability; SC: MakeMyTrip India v. Bansal — aggregator cannot escape liability as "facilitator"
ENTITLEMENT
Online Aggregator Refund — Grievance Officer Right
Under IT Rules 2021, Rule 3(2)(a) — every aggregator (RedBus, AbhiBus, Yatra, MakeMyTrip) must have a named Grievance Officer who must acknowledge your complaint within 24 hours and resolve within 15 days. Failure removes their safe-harbour protection under IT Act 2000, Section 79 — making them directly liable.
💬 Exactly What to Write
→Email the Grievance Officer (find on their website footer): "Under IT Rules 2021 Rule 3(2)(a), I am filing a formal grievance regarding non-refund of booking [PNR]. Under the same rules, you must acknowledge within 24 hours and resolve within 15 days."
→Non-compliance removes their IT Act §79 safe-harbour. Mention this — it has teeth.

Overcharging & Service Standards
Permit fare · No extra charges · Luggage limits
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Travel at Permitted Fare — No Overcharging
Stage carriage (city/intercity bus) operators are licensed under the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 and can only charge fares approved by the Regional Transport Authority (RTA). Charging more than permitted fare is a punishable offence under MVA. Private sleeper bus operators must adhere to contract carriage permit conditions — their fares are between operator and passenger but must not include hidden charges not disclosed at booking.
💬 If Overcharged
→Note the bus registration number, route, and driver name. Demand receipt for extra payment.
→File complaint with Regional Transport Office (RTO) of that area — this is the enforcement authority.
→Also file Consumer Complaint for refund of overcharged amount + compensation.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988, §74 — Stage carriage permit conditions; §86 — Permit conditions; Consumer Protection Act 2019 — Unfair trade practice
ENTITLEMENT
Luggage Rights
Each passenger is entitled to carry reasonable personal luggage. State RTCs have specific free luggage allowances (typically 15–20 kg per ticket). Excess luggage charges must be disclosed on the permit/tariff board — hidden charges are not enforceable. Luggage lost or damaged by the bus operator — file for compensation under Consumer Protection Act 2019.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988; Consumer Protection Act 2019 §2(11)

Accident & Injury Compensation
No-fault liability · MACT · Third party insurance mandatory
▼
ENTITLEMENT — STRICT LIABILITY
Accident Compensation — No-Fault Liability (Sections 140–144)
Under Sections 140–144 of the Motor Vehicles Act 1988 — if you are injured or killed in a motor vehicle accident, the vehicle owner or insurer is liable without requiring proof of negligence. Fixed compensation: death — ₹50,000; grievous hurt — ₹25,000 (no-fault minimum). Beyond this, you can claim full compensation through the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT) based on income, age, and medical expenses — where much higher amounts are awarded.
HOW TO CLAIM ACCIDENT COMPENSATION
1
File FIR at nearest police station immediately. Note bus registration number, driver name, insurance details.
2
Collect: doctor's certificate, hospital bills, FIR copy, eyewitness contact details.
3
File claim before Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT) in your district. Courts have exclusive jurisdiction once MACT is established.
4
Under Section 164 MVA 2019 amendment — owner/insurer must pay ₹5 lakh (death) or ₹2.5 lakh (grievous hurt) within 30 days without any court order.
5
Good Samaritan protection: if you help an accident victim, you are protected from civil and criminal liability under Section 134A MVA.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Sections 140–144 (no-fault liability), Section 146 (mandatory third-party insurance), Section 165 (MACT jurisdiction), Section 164 (structured compensation), Section 162 (golden hour cashless treatment); SC: United India Insurance v. Karam Chand — occupants are third parties
ENTITLEMENT
Golden Hour — Cashless Treatment Right
Under Section 162 of the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019 — accident victims must receive cashless treatment during the golden hour (first hour after accident). No hospital can demand payment or insurance before treating a road accident victim in the golden hour. The Central Government has established a scheme and fund for this.
🚨 Know This — It Could Save a Life
→If any hospital refuses cashless treatment for an accident victim in the golden hour — call 112 immediately.
→The Motor Vehicle Accident Fund (Section 164B) covers those without insurance — hit-and-run victims are also covered.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019, Section 162 — Golden hour scheme; Section 164B — Motor Vehicle Accident Fund

TSRTC / APSRTC — State RTC Rights
Consumer forum applies · RTI available · Complaint cells
▼
ENTITLEMENT
State RTCs are Consumer Service Providers
Even though TSRTC and APSRTC are statutory bodies, they are "service providers" under the Consumer Protection Act 2019. You can file a consumer complaint against them for: cancelled bus without refund · overcharging · accident compensation · poor service. Karnataka SCDRC confirmed this in KSRTC v. Lakshmidevamma (2018) — the same principle applies across all state RTCs.
💡 RTI is a Powerful Tool Against RTCs
→Every state RTC is a "public authority" under RTI Act 2005, §2(h). You can file RTI to get internal file notings on your refund, complaint status, or accident compensation.
→RTI on TSRTC: File at rtionline.gov.in or directly at TSRTC Head Office. Mention the refund file, complaint number, and date. Response in 30 days is mandatory.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection Act 2019; Right to Information Act 2005, §2(h) — public authority; Road Transport Corporations Act 1950; SC/SCDRC precedents on RTC liability
