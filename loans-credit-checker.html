Loans & Credit Cards Reality Check

A "12% flat rate" isn't 12%. Paying the minimum due isn't actually saving you money. Find out your real numbers below, using the exact same math your lender uses.

🔍 Know Your Rights First
Why this trips up smart people too
A "12% flat rate" and a "12% reducing rate" are not the same number in disguise — they're genuinely different math, and the flat one is always more expensive for you. Nobody is lying about the 12%. They're just not explaining which 12% it is.

Key Fact Statement (KFS) — Your Legal Right
▾
Since 1 October 2024, RBI has made a standardised Key Fact Statement mandatory for every retail and MSME loan from banks, co-operative banks, and NBFCs. It must show the Annual Percentage Rate (APR) — the true all-in cost including fees — plus a full amortisation schedule, in plain, simple language.
How to use it: Ask for the KFS before signing anything. If a lender can't or won't provide one, that itself is a serious red flag for a regulated loan.
📐
Flat Rate vs Reducing Rate — The Real Difference
▾
A flat rate charges interest on your full original loan amount for the entire tenure, even as you repay the principal. A reducing rate charges interest only on what you still owe, which shrinks every month. The same headline percentage means a much bigger real cost under flat rate.
How to check: Use the calculator below — enter your flat rate and it computes the true reducing-rate equivalent, using the exact same EMI math your lender uses.
🔓
Foreclosure Charges — Floating vs Fixed Matters
▾
Effective 1 January 2026, RBI banned foreclosure and prepayment charges on floating-rate loans to individuals, for any purpose, and on qualifying MSE business loans — no lock-in period allowed, and charges can't be applied retrospectively. Fixed-rate loans are NOT covered by this protection — lenders can still charge prepayment penalties on those.
How to check: Confirm in writing whether your loan is floating or fixed rate before signing. If foreclosure flexibility matters to you, that single detail changes your rights completely.
⏳
The Cooling-Off Period on Digital Loans
▾
After receiving your KFS on a digital loan, you get a mandatory reflection window — at least 24 hours, up to 3 days for longer-tenure loans — during which you can cancel and repay only the principal plus proportionate interest for the days you held the money, with no penalty.
How to check: If you accepted a loan too quickly and have second thoughts about the terms, check whether you're still inside this window before assuming you're stuck.
📱
Is the Lending App Even Authorised?
▾
RBI maintains a public directory of authorised Digital Lending Apps (DLAs). Instant-loan apps that aren't on it are operating outside regulation entirely — a common source of harassment, illegal recovery tactics, and predatory terms.
How to check: Before installing any instant-loan app, check whether it's linked to an RBI-regulated bank or NBFC and appears in the official DLA directory.
💳
Minimum Due — What It Actually Costs
▾
Since December 2022, RBI requires the minimum amount due to be the higher of (a) 100% of that month's interest, fees and taxes, or (b) 5% of the total outstanding — designed to stop the balance from silently growing forever. But paying only the minimum still means paying serious interest for years.
How to check: Use the calculator below to see exactly how many months and how much extra interest "just paying the minimum" actually costs on your real balance.
🎁
Interest-Free Period — One Missed Rule Cancels It
▾
The interest-free period on a credit card only applies if you pay the ENTIRE outstanding balance by the due date. Paying even the minimum due — leaving any balance unpaid — forfeits the grace period on that cycle, and interest gets charged from the original transaction date, not from the due date.
How to check: If you're carrying any balance forward, don't assume new purchases that month are still interest-free — check your specific issuer's terms, since this is where a lot of surprise interest comes from.

Your Legal Backing
RBI Key Fact Statement Guidelines, April 2024
Mandatory since 1 October 2024 for all retail/MSME loans from banks, co-op banks and NBFCs — must show APR and full amortisation schedule in plain language.
RBI Pre-payment Charges on Loans Directions, 2025
Effective 1 January 2026 — bans foreclosure/prepayment charges on floating-rate loans to individuals (any purpose) and qualifying MSE loans. No lock-in period. Fixed-rate loans are not covered.
RBI Credit Card Rules — Minimum Due & Interest
Since December 2022, minimum due must be the higher of 100% of interest/fees/taxes or 5% of outstanding. Late fees only apply after 3 days overdue, calculated on the overdue amount only.
