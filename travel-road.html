Auto-Rickshaw Rights
Meter rights · Refusal to ply · Overcharging · How to complain
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Travel by Meter — Auto Cannot Quote Flat Rate
Auto-rickshaws operating on contract carriage permits in city areas are legally required to use their meter and charge approved RTA fares. Refusing to use meter and quoting flat rates is a punishable offence under the Motor Vehicles Act. Overcharging is also punishable. An auto with "For Hire" flag raised is legally obligated to take you.
💬 What to Do When Auto Refuses Meter
→Note the auto registration number (on front and back). If they refuse — don't argue. Note the number, take photo, and file complaint with RTO.
→Hyderabad: File complaint at RTA Telangana app or 1800-425-0022. Record auto number + location + time.
→Multiple complaints against the same auto lead to permit cancellation — this is the most effective deterrent.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988, §74 — Permit conditions for contract carriage; Central Motor Vehicles Rules 1989, Rule 129 — Taxi meter requirement; State Motor Vehicle Rules — overcharging punishable; MVA §177 — penalty for rule violations
ENTITLEMENT
Auto Cannot Refuse to Ply — Refusal is a Punishable Offence
An auto-rickshaw with the "For Hire" flag raised cannot legally refuse to take a passenger to their destination within the permitted area. Refusal to ply is a violation of permit conditions — the driver can be fined and the auto impounded. The Motor Vehicles Act treats this seriously because auto permits are public interest licenses.
HOW TO FILE COMPLAINT
1
Note auto registration number + driver name (if visible on permit card in auto).
2
Telangana: Call RTA helpline 1800-425-0022 or file at rta.telangana.gov.in.
3
Andhra Pradesh: File at ap.meeseva.gov.in or call 1902.
4
Also file Consumer Complaint — refusal to provide contracted service is deficiency under CPA 2019.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988, §86 — Permit conditions; Consumer Protection Act 2019 §2(11) — Service deficiency; State RTA enforcement rules

Taxi & Cab Aggregator Rights (Ola, Uber, Rapido)
Cancellation · Surge pricing · Safety · Complaints
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Cab Aggregators are Regulated — Motor Vehicle (Amendment) Act 2019
The Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019 formally recognises cab aggregators (Ola, Uber, Rapido, Namma Yatri) as a category requiring licensing. State governments set fare bands — aggregators cannot charge above the maximum or below the minimum set by State Transport Authority. Surge pricing has a legal ceiling per state guidelines.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019, Section 93 — Aggregators; State guidelines on cab fare regulation; Consumer Protection Act 2019 — unfair trade practice if surge price disclosed deceptively
ENTITLEMENT
Refund When Cab Cancels After Accepting
When a cab driver accepts your booking and then cancels — any cancellation fee charged to you is not payable (you didn't cancel). The app must refund any amount deducted. Driver-side cancellation after acceptance is a service deficiency under Consumer Protection Act 2019.
COMPLAINT AGAINST OLA / UBER
1
Report in-app immediately — take screenshot of the cancelled booking showing driver accepted.
2
If app doesn't resolve — email their Grievance Officer (mandatory under IT Rules 2021).
3
File at National Consumer Helpline (1800-11-4000) or consumerhelpline.gov.in.
4
For larger issues (safety, overcharging pattern) — file with State Transport Authority or RTO.
SAFETY RIGHT
Safety Rights in Cab — SOS, Driver Verification
All cab aggregators must provide: SOS button in the app (links to emergency services) · Driver identity verification before cab starts · Share trip option. If a driver deviates from route or behaves dangerously — use SOS, call 112, and screenshot the app showing route deviation.
🚨 Safety Protocol
→Always share your trip with a trusted contact before starting. This creates a live tracking record.
→Note cab registration number before boarding. Match it with the app display.
→If driver starts wrong route — say clearly "This is not the right route. I am ending this trip." Screenshot the map showing deviation before exiting.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019 — Aggregator guidelines; Consumer Protection Act 2019 — safety standards; IT Rules 2021 — Grievance Officer mandate

Road Accident — Your Rights as Victim
No-fault compensation · MACT · Hit-and-run · Golden hour
▼
ENTITLEMENT — NO FAULT REQUIRED
Accident Compensation — Strict No-Fault Liability
Under Sections 140–144 of MVA 1988 — you do NOT need to prove the driver was at fault to get initial compensation. Fixed amounts: death — ₹50,000; permanent disablement — ₹25,000 (no-fault minimum). For full compensation — file at MACT and court determines amount based on income and injury. Under 2019 amendment Section 164 — ₹5 lakh for death and ₹2.5 lakh for grievous hurt must be paid within 30 days.
IMMEDIATE STEPS AFTER ACCIDENT
1
Call 112. Note vehicle registration number of the vehicle that hit you.
2
Get medical treatment — under Section 162, hospitals must provide golden hour cashless treatment. Do not let them delay treatment for payment.
3
File FIR at nearest police station. Note the FIR number.
4
Collect: doctor's certificate, hospital bills, FIR copy, witness contacts, vehicle insurance details.
5
File compensation claim at Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT). No time limit for filing but earlier is better. No-fault claim within 6 months.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Sections 140–144 (no-fault), Section 146 (mandatory insurance), Section 162 (golden hour cashless treatment), Section 164 (structured compensation 2019 amendment), Section 165 (MACT jurisdiction); Article 21 Constitution — right to life includes accident compensation
ENTITLEMENT
Hit-and-Run Accident — Solatium Scheme
Even if the vehicle that hit you flees — you are not left without compensation. Under the Solatium Scheme (Section 161 MVA): death in hit-and-run = ₹2 lakh compensation · grievous hurt = ₹50,000. The Motor Vehicle Accident Fund (Section 164B) covers all road users including those without insurance.
HIT-AND-RUN CLAIM PROCESS
1
File FIR at police station mentioning "hit-and-run" and vehicle unknown.
2
Apply to Claims Enquiry Officer (at District Collector's office or RTO) with FIR copy and medical records.
3
Compensation paid from the Solatium Fund maintained by General Insurance Council.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Section 161 — Hit-and-run compensation; Section 164B — Motor Vehicle Accident Fund; Solatium Scheme Rules
GOOD SAMARITAN PROTECTION
Help an Accident Victim — You Are Protected by Law
Under Section 134A of MVA 1988 — any person who helps a road accident victim in good faith is protected from civil and criminal liability even if the victim dies. You cannot be detained by police, forced to be a witness, or made to bear medical costs. This law was enacted specifically to encourage people to help accident victims.
💡 Know This — It Could Save Someone's Life
→If police try to detain you for helping — state: "I am a Good Samaritan under Section 134A of the Motor Vehicles Act. I acted in good faith and am legally protected."
→Supreme Court guidelines (Savelife Foundation v. Union of India) further protect Good Samaritans and direct police not to reveal helper's identity.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Section 134A — Good Samaritan; SC: Savelife Foundation v. Union of India (2016) — Good Samaritan guidelines mandatory

Road Safety — Rights of Pedestrians & Cyclists
Footpath right · Pedestrian crossings · Driver duties
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Pedestrians Have Priority at Zebra Crossings
Drivers must stop and give way to pedestrians at designated zebra crossings. Failure to do so is a traffic violation. Footpaths are for pedestrians — vehicles (including two-wheelers) driving on footpaths violate MVA and can be fined. If you are injured on a footpath by a vehicle — the driver has clear liability and you have a strong MACT claim.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles Act 1988 §§ 111–114 — Traffic rules; Central Motor Vehicles Rules — pedestrian priority; MVA 2019 amendment — increased penalties for footpath driving
ENTITLEMENT
National Road Safety Board — Citizen Grievance Right
The MVA 2019 amendment established the National Road Safety Board to advise on traffic management. Citizens can raise road safety concerns — dangerous road design, broken footpaths, missing zebra crossings — through local municipal bodies and state transport departments. RTI is a powerful tool to demand road infrastructure improvements.
Legal basis: Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019 — National Road Safety Board; RTI Act 2005 — municipal road maintenance accountability
