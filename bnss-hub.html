Nyaya Metro — the subject as a metro map

Units are metro lines and key topics are stations, sized by how often they were asked — FIR, arrest, bail, trial, juvenile justice, probation. Tap a station for its one-line meaning, BNSS and CrPC section, leading case and memory hook. The repeat Part C problems (juvenile age, arresting a judge, probation for murder, double jeopardy…) are drawn as yes/no route maps.

Best for
Seeing the whole subject at once, linking topics, quick revision, Part C practice.
How to use it
  1. Open the map on your phone and ride one line (unit) at a time.
  2. Big stations were asked the most — learn those tickets first.
  3. Before the exam, walk each Part C route map and say the answer aloud.
  4. Print the PDF and pin it near your desk.

Final Exam Cluster Notes

The exam-writing notes. The syllabus grouped into 14 clusters of topics asked together, with ready-to-write Part A (6-mark), Part B (15-mark) and Part C (10-mark) answers, cases told as short stories, key points in bold, and a BNSS ⇄ CrPC section finder.

Best for
The last two weeks, writing practice, students who want answers in exam shape.
How to use it
  1. Start with the clusters the Analysis marks as asked most — juvenile justice, bail, FIR and arrest.
  2. Read a model answer, close the PDF, write it from memory, then compare.
  3. Learn both numbers for the important sections — BNSS first, CrPC in brackets.

Story Notes — one crime, start to finish

All five units as one story: one evening at a market, a shopkeeper is stabbed; a 16-year-old and a friend are involved. Tools & reference

Default-bail day counter

Enter the date of the first remand and the kind of offence — it shows the last day for filing the charge sheet (60 or 90 days) and the day from which the accused can claim default bail under S.187(3) BNSS (S.167(2) CrPC).

Best for
Part C problems on 'charge sheet not filed — bail?', and understanding how the days are counted.
How to use it
  1. Pick the offence type: up to 10 years, or death / life / 10 years or more.
  2. Enter the date of the first remand order. The remand day itself is counted (Enforcement Directorate v Kapil Wadhawan, 2023).
Enter the date to see the result.
Bare Act — old CrPC 1973 (full text)

Every section of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 in full text, searchable by number or word. The past papers use these numbers; the Cluster Notes and the Nyaya Metro give the matching BNSS sections.

Best for
Reading the exact words of a section asked in an old paper.
How to use it
  1. Search the CrPC number from the question (e.g. 167).
  2. Find its BNSS number in the Cluster Notes' section finder and quote both.
