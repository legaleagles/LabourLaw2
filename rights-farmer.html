Crop Insurance Rights (PMFBY)
2% premium Kharif · Claim in 2 months · 72-hour damage report
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Crop Loss Compensation — Mandatory Within 2 Months
Under Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) — farmers are entitled to insurance compensation for crop loss due to natural calamities, pests, and diseases. Premium: only 2% (Kharif), 1.5% (Rabi), 5% (commercial crops). Banks cannot add PMFBY premium to KCC without your consent. Claims must be settled within 2 months of harvest.
WHAT TO DO
1
Enroll before cut-off date at pmfby.gov.in or through your bank/CSC.
2
Report crop loss within 72 hours by calling the Insurance Company helpline or informing gram panchayat.
3
If claim not settled within 2 months — file complaint with District Agriculture Officer.
4
If claim rejected — file with IRDAI Insurance Ombudsman at irdai.gov.in — free, binding.
5
Check claim status at pmfby.gov.in using your policy number or Aadhaar.
⚠️ 72-Hour Rule is Critical
You MUST report crop damage within 72 hours of loss to be eligible for compensation. This is the most common reason valid claims are rejected. Report immediately even if loss estimate is uncertain.
Legal basis: Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana 2016; IRDAI Crop Insurance Guidelines; Insurance Act 1938 — claim must be settled in 2 months

Kisan Credit Card & Farm Loan Rights
4% interest · Bank cannot refuse KCC · Moneylender regulation
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Every Eligible Farmer Has Right to Kisan Credit Card
All scheduled banks must offer Kisan Credit Card (KCC) to eligible farmers. Effective interest rate after interest subvention: 4% per annum. Banks cannot arbitrarily refuse KCC. Credit limit based on land holding, cropping pattern and scale of finance. KCC is revolving credit — repay after harvest.
WHAT TO DO
1
Apply in writing to Branch Manager. Collect acknowledgment.
2
If refused within 30 days — appeal to District Lead Bank Manager.
3
File complaint with RBI at sachet.rbi.org.in or call 14448.
4
File complaint with NABARD regional office — NABARD oversees agricultural credit.
5
File RTI to bank asking for reasons for rejection and KCC policy.
Legal basis: RBI Guidelines on Agricultural Credit; KCC Scheme 2004 (revised 2019); NABARD guidelines; RBI Master Circular on Interest Subvention Scheme
ENTITLEMENT
Moneylender Regulation — Excessive Interest is Illegal
State Moneylenders Acts regulate the maximum interest moneylenders can charge. Charging above the regulated rate is illegal. Harassment and coercion by moneylenders is a criminal offence. If a moneylender has seized your property without court order — file FIR immediately.
💬 What to Say
→File complaint with District Collector under state Moneylenders Act.
→File FIR if threatened, assaulted, or property illegally seized.
→Apply for debt restructuring through NABARD Debt Relief Schemes.
→Contact Farmers' Commission or District Agriculture Officer for mediation.
Legal basis: State Moneylenders Acts (AP Moneylenders Act 1957; Telangana Moneylenders Act); IPC Sections 384-389 (extortion, robbery); Article 39(b) Constitution

Water & Irrigation Rights
Canal allocation · Bore well · WUA · RTI for records
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Irrigation Water Allocation
Farmers have the right to water allocation from irrigation canals as per their registered land and crop type. Revenue/Irrigation authorities cannot arbitrarily deny water turns. Water User Associations (WUAs) have legal standing to demand canal water. File RTI to get water allocation records for your village.
WHAT TO DO
1
Contact your Water User Association (WUA) — they have legal powers to represent farmers.
2
File written complaint to Executive Engineer (Irrigation) of your district.
3
File RTI to Irrigation Department asking for water allocation records.
4
Contact District Collector — DM has power to resolve inter-village water disputes.
5
File Writ Petition in High Court if irrigation authorities fail to act.
Legal basis: AP Water, Land and Trees Act 2002; Telangana Water Resources Development Corporation Act; Article 21 SC: water is part of right to life; State Irrigation Acts

MSP & Agricultural Market Rights
Minimum Support Price · APMC · Fair price · No distress sale
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Fair Price — MSP as Benchmark
The government announces Minimum Support Price (MSP) for 23 crops every season. While MSP is not always legally enforceable in private markets, government procurement agencies (FCI, state agencies) must procure at MSP. If forced to sell below MSP to a government agency — file complaint with District Collector and state Agriculture Marketing Board. APMC markets must provide proper weighment and payment receipts.
💬 What to Say
→Demand proper weighment receipt (katta pucharika) at APMC — it's your right.
→Payment must be made within the time specified by state APMC Act.
→Commission agents cannot deduct more than the notified commission rate.
→File complaint with APMC Secretary for any market malpractice.
→File RTI to Agriculture Marketing Board for MSP procurement data.
Legal basis: Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) Acts; National Food Security Act 2013; Essential Commodities Act 1955; Agricultural Marketing and Farmer Empowerment Policy
