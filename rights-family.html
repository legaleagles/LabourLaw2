Divorce & Separation Rights
Grounds · Mutual consent · Where to file · Free legal aid
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Grounds for Divorce Under Hindu Law
Under the Hindu Marriage Act 1955, divorce can be filed on grounds of: Cruelty (most common) · Desertion (2+ years) · Adultery · Conversion to another religion · Unsoundness of mind · Leprosy or venereal disease · Renouncement of world · Presumed death (7+ years missing). Wife has additional grounds: rape, sodomy, bestiality by husband; marriage solemnised before 15 years of age.
WHAT TO DO
1
Mutual consent: both file joint petition at Family Court — 6 months cooling period (waivable by court).
2
Contested: file petition at Family Court with grounds. Collect evidence.
3
Interim maintenance can be sought immediately on filing — court can order within days.
4
Wife can file in her place of residence, place of marriage, or where husband resides.
5
Contact Legal Aid (15100) if you cannot afford a lawyer — free legal aid is a right.
Legal basis: Hindu Marriage Act 1955, Sections 13, 13A, 13B; SC: Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli (2006) — irretrievable breakdown; Special Marriage Act 1954 (civil marriage)

Maintenance & Alimony Rights
All religions · CrPC 125 · Interim maintenance · Enforcement
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Maintenance Under CrPC Section 125 — Applies to All Religions
Any wife, legitimate or illegitimate child, or parent unable to maintain themselves can claim maintenance under CrPC Section 125 — regardless of religion. Courts can order interim maintenance within days of filing. The amount is based on the husband's/father's means and standard of living. Non-payment can result in imprisonment.
WHAT TO DO
1
File application at Magistrate Court under Section 125 CrPC — minimal fee, no lawyer required.
2
Also apply for interim maintenance — court can order within days.
3
Also claim in family court under personal law (Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act for Hindus).
4
If maintenance order is not paid — file execution petition. Court can imprison defaulter.
5
Under DV Act — also claim monetary relief including rent and medical expenses.
Legal basis: CrPC Section 125 (all religions); Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act 1956; Protection of Women from DV Act 2005, Section 20; Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act 1986

Child Custody Rights
Welfare paramount · Interim custody · Visitation rights · Habeas corpus
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Child's Welfare is Paramount — Courts Follow This Strictly
In all custody matters, Indian courts apply the "welfare of the child" principle — the child's best interests, not parental rights, determine custody. Factors: age (young children typically with mother), child's stated preference (if mature enough), financial capacity, moral fitness. Both parents retain visitation rights by default regardless of custody decision.
WHAT TO DO
1
File Guardianship petition at Family Court under Guardians and Wards Act 1890.
2
Apply for interim custody immediately — court decides temporary custody while case proceeds.
3
Gather evidence: school records, medical records, daily care documentation.
4
If child taken away illegally — file Habeas Corpus petition in High Court for immediate return.
5
Child can express preference to judge in chambers (in camera) without parents present.
Legal basis: Guardians and Wards Act 1890; Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act 1956; SC: Gaurav Nagpal v. Sumedha Nagpal (2009) — welfare paramount; Nil Ratan Kundu v. Abhijit Kundu (2008)

Inheritance & Succession Rights
Equal rights for daughters · Will contest · Legal heir certificate
▼
LANDMARK — SC 2020
Daughters Have Equal Ancestral Property Rights — Retroactively
The Supreme Court in Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020) — a 3-judge bench — settled the law: daughters are coparceners by birth in Hindu ancestral property, equal to sons, retroactively — even if the father died before the 2005 amendment. A married daughter's rights are identical to an unmarried daughter. She can demand partition at any time.
💬 What to Say
→File a Civil Suit for Partition at your district Civil Court.
→If excluded from will — contest within 12 years limitation period in Civil Court.
→Apply for Legal Heir Certificate at District Tehsildar (for bank account, property transfer).
→For movable assets (shares, FD) — apply for Succession Certificate in Civil Court.
→Muslim succession: daughters get half share of son. Governed by personal law.
Legal basis: Hindu Succession Act 1956, Section 6 (amended 2005); SC: Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020) — retroactive; Indian Succession Act 1925 (non-Hindus); Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Act 1937
