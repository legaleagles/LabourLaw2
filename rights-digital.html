Cyber Fraud & Financial Scams
Report in 24 hrs · 1930 · cybercrime.gov.in · Bank reversal
▼
URGENT — REPORT WITHIN 24 HRS
Cyber Fraud — Speed is Everything
For any online financial fraud (UPI, net banking, OTP scam, fake website) — report within 24-48 hours. The faster you report, the higher the chance of freezing the fraudster's account before money moves. Call 1930 immediately. File online at cybercrime.gov.in simultaneously.
WHAT TO DO
1
Call 1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline) — available 24/7.
2
File online complaint at cybercrime.gov.in — select "Financial Fraud."
3
Call your bank simultaneously — request transaction freeze/reversal.
4
Keep all evidence: screenshots, transaction IDs, UPI IDs, phone numbers.
5
If bank refuses reversal — file Banking Ombudsman complaint at bankingombudsman.rbi.org.in.
Legal basis: IT Act 2000, Sections 43 (unauthorised access), 66 (computer-related offences), 66C (identity theft), 66D (cheating using computer); IPC Sections 419-420; RBI Zero Liability Circular 2017

Online Harassment & Fake Accounts
Social media · Morphed images · Stalking · Threats
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Online Harassment is a Criminal Offence
Online harassment — threatening messages, morphed photos, fake accounts in your name, cyberstalking, trolling with criminal intent — are all criminal offences under the IT Act and IPC. File FIR at police station or online. Platforms must remove reported content within 24 hours (sexually explicit) or 72 hours (other harmful content).
WHAT TO DO
1
Screenshot ALL abusive content first — platforms sometimes delete posts after reporting.
2
Report in-app (violation of community standards) — this creates a record.
3
File cyber complaint at cybercrime.gov.in or nearest cyber police station.
4
For women: file with NCW Cyber Cell at ncwapps.nic.in for faster response.
5
If platform doesn't act in 72 hours — escalate to IT Ministry grievance portal.
Legal basis: IT Act 2000, Sections 66E (privacy violation), 67 (obscene content), 67A; IPC Sections 354D (cyberstalking), 499-500 (defamation), 503, 507 (criminal intimidation); IT Rules 2021 — platform takedown obligations

Data Privacy Rights
Consent · Access · Correction · Erasure · DPDP Act 2023
▼
NEW LAW 2023
Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 — Your Data Rights
The DPDP Act 2023 gives you rights over your personal data: Right to know what data is collected · Right to correction and erasure · Right to grievance redressal · Right to nominate a person to exercise rights on your behalf. Data Fiduciaries (companies holding your data) must obtain clear consent and can be penalised up to ₹250 crore for breaches.
💬 What to Say
→Contact company's Data Protection Officer (DPO) — mandatory to appoint under DPDP Act.
→Request: "Under DPDP Act 2023, I request access to/correction of/erasure of my personal data."
→If denied — file complaint with Data Protection Board of India (when notified).
→For Aadhaar data misuse specifically — file with UIDAI: uidai.gov.in or call 1947.
Legal basis: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, Sections 11-14 (data principal rights); IT Act 2000 Section 43A; SC: Puttaswamy 2017 — privacy fundamental right

Right Against Government Surveillance
Interception must be lawful · Proportionate · No blanket surveillance
▼
FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT
Phone/Internet Surveillance Must Follow Due Process
The government can legally intercept phone calls and internet traffic only under specific authorisation under Section 69 IT Act or Section 5(2) Telegraph Act — by specific designated officers, for specific national security/public order reasons. Blanket mass surveillance is unconstitutional (Puttaswamy 2017). If you believe you are illegally surveilled — file writ petition in High Court.
Legal basis: IT Act 2000, Section 69; Indian Telegraph Act 1885, Section 5(2); SC: PUCL v. Union of India (1997) — interception guidelines; SC: Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017) — surveillance must be proportionate
