Consumer Rights India
Every purchase is a contract. Every service is a commitment. The Consumer Protection Act 2019 gives you powerful rights — and a fast, free way to enforce them.

Defective Product Rights
Replacement · Repair · Refund · Compensation
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Right to Replacement, Repair or Refund for Defective Product
Under the Consumer Protection Act 2019, if any product you buy is defective, you can demand: replacement of the product · repair at no charge · full refund of purchase price · compensation for loss caused by the defect. You do not need a lawyer. You do not need to pay high filing fees. District Consumer Commission handles claims up to ₹50 lakhs.
HOW TO CLAIM
1
Send written complaint to seller/manufacturer with photos of the defect. Keep copy.
2
If no response in 30 days — file at consumerhelpline.gov.in or call 1800-11-4000.
3
For claims up to ₹50 lakh — file at District Consumer Commission. Minimal fee.
4
Keep original bill, warranty card, all communication, and photos as evidence.
5
Claim: replacement/repair/refund PLUS compensation for inconvenience.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection Act 2019, Section 2(10) — Defect; Section 38 — Complaint jurisdiction; Consumer Protection Rules 2020
ENTITLEMENT
Manufacturer Liable Even if Purchased from Dealer
The manufacturer, dealer, and seller are all jointly liable for product defects. You can sue any or all of them. The burden of proof for manufacturing defects shifts to the manufacturer — you don't need to prove how it broke.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection Act 2019, Section 84 — Product liability; Section 85 — Manufacturer liability

Deficiency in Service Rights
Hotels · Hospitals · Insurance · Banks · Telecom
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Any Service Below Promised Standard is Actionable
Deficiency in service covers: hospitals, insurance companies, banks, telecom operators, hotels, transport operators, educational institutions, and any paid service. If the service falls short of what was promised — you can claim refund + compensation.
💬 Common Service Deficiency Examples
→Insurance claim wrongly rejected — file Consumer Complaint + IRDAI complaint.
→Bank deducted charges without notice — file Consumer Complaint + RBI Banking Ombudsman.
→Hospital denied promised treatment — file Consumer Complaint + State Medical Council.
→Hotel room not as advertised — file Consumer Complaint + file with Tourism Ministry.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection Act 2019, Section 2(11) — Deficiency in Service; SC: Indian Medical Association v. VP Shantha (1995) — medical services covered

E-Commerce Rights
Online shopping · Returns · Wrong delivery · Platforms
▼
ENTITLEMENT
E-Commerce Platform is Liable — Not Just the Seller
Under Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 — the platform itself is liable for: failing to display return/refund policy · allowing counterfeit products · not facilitating grievance redressal. The platform cannot say "we are just a marketplace" to escape liability if they exercise control over listings.
IF YOUR ORDER GOES WRONG
1
Photograph the product at delivery — condition at receipt is your evidence.
2
Raise return request within platform policy window. Screenshot the request.
3
If platform refuses — file complaint at consumerhelpline.gov.in naming the platform.
4
For fraud (item not delivered, money taken) — file at cybercrime.gov.in.
5
File at e-Daakhil (edaakhil.nic.in) for consumer court online — fast and free.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020; Consumer Protection Act 2019; IT Act 2000
ENTITLEMENT
Grievance Officer — 24 Hour Acknowledgement Right
Every e-commerce platform must have a named Grievance Officer who must acknowledge complaints within 24 hours and resolve within 15 days. Their name and contact must be displayed on the website. Non-compliance removes their legal protection under IT Act Section 79.
💬 What to Write to Grievance Officer
→"Under Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, I am filing a formal grievance regarding [issue]. Under IT Rules 2021, you must acknowledge within 24 hours and resolve within 15 days."
→Non-compliance = loss of IT Act §79 safe-harbour protection. Mention this explicitly.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, Rule 4; IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules 2021, Rule 3(2)(a)

Bank & UPI Fraud Rights
Zero liability · 3-day rule · Chargeback · Ombudsman
▼
URGENT — ACT WITHIN 3 DAYS
Fraudulent Bank Transaction — Zero Liability if Reported in 3 Days
If money is debited from your account without your authorisation — report to your bank within 3 working days for zero liability. After 3 days, liability increases. After 7 days, you may bear partial or full loss. The RBI Circular on Limiting Liability (2017) mandates this.
IMMEDIATE STEPS
1
Call bank helpline immediately — request transaction block and reversal. Note time and name.
2
Send written email to bank with transaction ID within 3 working days.
3
File cyber complaint at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
4
If bank refuses reversal — file Banking Ombudsman complaint at bankingombudsman.rbi.org.in.
5
Credit card fraud: initiate chargeback with your bank simultaneously.
Legal basis: RBI Circular DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18 — Limiting Liability; IT Act 2000; Consumer Protection Act 2019

Misleading Advertisement Rights
CCPA · Celebrities liable · ₹50 lakh penalty
▼
ENTITLEMENT
Misleading Ads are a Criminal Offence — Endorsers Also Liable
Under CPA 2019 — misleading advertisements are punishable. The advertiser, endorser (including celebrities), and platform are all liable. Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) can impose penalties up to ₹10 lakh (first offence) and ₹50 lakh (repeat). Celebrities who endorse without due diligence face penalties too.
HOW TO FILE
1
Screenshot or record the advertisement with date and platform.
2
File complaint with CCPA at ccpa.gov.in — select "Misleading Advertisement."
3
Also file consumer complaint for any financial loss caused by the misleading claim.
4
For health products with false claims — also file with FSSAI or Drug Controller.
Legal basis: Consumer Protection Act 2019, Sections 2(28), 21 — Misleading advertisements; CPA Section 89 — Punishment; CCPA Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements 2022
